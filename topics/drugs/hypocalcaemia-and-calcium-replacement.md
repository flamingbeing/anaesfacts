# Hypocalcaemia and Calcium Replacement

Low corrected calcium causes neuromuscular irritability and QT prolongation; treat with slow IV calcium.

## Definition

- Total corrected serum calcium below 8.5 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L)

## Severity

- Mild to moderate: 1.9-2.2 mmol/L
- Severe: below 1.9 mmol/L

## Causes

- PTH-dependent (PTH normal or low)
  - Hypoparathyroidism
    - Idiopathic or autoimmune
    - Iatrogenic: after thyroidectomy
    - Tertiary
  - Low magnesium
  - Inherited or genetic causes
- PTH-independent (PTH raised)
  - Hyperphosphataemia
    - Chronic renal failure
    - Cell lysis
  - Pancreatitis (calcium loss)
  - Nutritional (e.g. osteoporosis risk factors)
  - Vitamin D deficiency
    - Chronic renal failure
    - Malabsorption
    - Nephrotic syndrome: loss of vitamin D binding protein
    - Hereditary vitamin D resistant rickets type II
  - Sepsis or severe illness: impaired PTH secretion plus reduced calcitriol production
  - Massive transfusion (citrate toxicity)
- Hypomagnesaemia (causes PTH resistance)

## Clinical features

- Mnemonic "CATs go NUMB"
  - Convulsions
  - Arrhythmias: prolonged QT, torsades de pointes
  - Tetany and cramps, including vocal cords (breathlessness)
    - Chvostek's sign (tap facial nerve, CN VII)
    - Trousseau's sign (carpal spasm)
    - Hyperreflexia
    - Muscle weakness
  - Paraesthesia, perioral numbness (resembles local anaesthetic toxicity)
- Laryngospasm with stridor
- Delirium

## Management

- Correct the underlying cause
- Calcium gluconate 10%: 10 ml IV over 10 min (slow bolus)
  - 100 mg/ml, 1 g per 10 ml vial
  - Contains 8.9 mg/mL elemental calcium
  - Never as a fast bolus: risk of cardiac arrest (heart stops in systole)
- Alternative: calcium chloride 10% IV
  - Contains 27.2 mg/mL elemental calcium, 3 times that of gluconate
  - More irritant (osmolality 2,000 mosm/kg H2O); extravasation more likely to cause tissue necrosis
  - Give through a free-flowing central venous catheter where possible
- Mild or maintenance
  - Increase dietary calcium by 1000 mg/day, e.g. calcium carbonate 1.25 g BD
  - With or without calcitriol 0.25-0.5 mcg OM (regimens vary)
  - If PTH recovery is not expected: long-term calcitriol and calcium
