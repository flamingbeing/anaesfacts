# Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

Endogenous catecholamine from the adrenal gland acting on α and β receptors: β effects at low doses, α1 effects at high doses.

## Presentation

- Name: "epi-nephr-ine" and "ad-renal" both mean above the kidney; the two names are the same drug
- Clear solution 0.1–1 mg/ml for IV use
- Usual ampoule 1mg in 1ml (1:1000)
- 1% eye drops (open-angle glaucoma)
- Metered dose inhaler giving 280 mcg (anaphylaxis from insect stings or drugs)
- Mixed with local anaesthetic at 1 in 80 000–200 000

## Administration

- Not given orally (inactivated)
- Subcutaneous absorption is slower than IM
- Tracheal absorption is unreliable; use only in emergencies without IV access
- Infusion made up in normal saline
  - 0.3mg in 50mls (6 mcg/ml): 1ml/kg/h = 0.1mcg/kg/min
  - 0.3 x body weight (mg) in 50mls: 1ml/h = 0.1mcg/kg/min
- Nebulised: 0.5ml of 1:1000 diluted to 4ml; can be diluted in lignocaine instead
- With halothane, limit to 100 mcg per 10 minutes to avoid arrhythmias

## Dosage

- Cardiac arrest (ACLS)
  - IV/IO: 1mg (1:10,000, i.e. 10ml) every 3-5mins
  - Paediatrics: 10mcg/kg (0.1ml/kg of 1:10,000)
  - ETT: 0.1mg (1:1000)
- Hypotension: 10-100mcg (1:10,000)
- Anaphylaxis in theatre
  - 100-300mcg IV, repeat as needed; monitor closely, as fatal overdoses have occurred
  - Then infusion 0.05-0.1mcg/kg/min (or 1–4 mcg/min), which may avoid repeat boluses
- Bronchospasm: 10mcg (1:100,000)
- Infusion: 0.02-0.2mcg/kg/min (usually 1–2 mcg/min)
- Critically ill with circulatory failure: 0.01–0.5 mcg/kg/min

## Mechanism of action

- Acts on α and β adrenoceptors
  - Low doses: β effects predominate
  - High doses: α1 effects predominate
- Receptor signalling
  - α1: Gq, activates phospholipase C; PIP2 is split, IP3 released, intracellular Ca2+ rises
  - α2: Gi, inhibits adenylate cyclase, cAMP falls
  - β: Gs, activates adenylate cyclase, cAMP rises, with site-specific phosphorylation

## Effects

- Cardiovascular
  - Dose-dependent: β1 = β2 > α1 = α2; at high plasma levels α = β
  - Low-dose infusion (β effects)
    - β1: heart rate, contractility and cardiac output increase; myocardial oxygen use increases
    - β1: coronary vasodilation
    - β1: conduction speeds up (dromotropic) and arrhythmia threshold falls
    - β2: peripheral vascular resistance and diastolic pressure may fall
    - MAP may change little, as effects oppose
    - More potent β agonist than dopamine: larger rises in stroke volume and heart rate at comparable doses
  - High-dose infusion, or 1 mg bolus in cardiac arrest (α1 effects)
    - SVR and blood pressure rise, overriding β2 vasodilation
    - So: vasodilation at low concentrations (β2), vasoconstriction at high (α)
    - Vasoconstriction is uneven: strongest in skin, kidney and gut circulations
  - High, prolonged doses can be directly toxic to the heart
- Respiratory
  - Small rise in minute volume
  - Potent bronchodilator (β2 relaxes bronchial smooth muscle); secretions may thicken
  - Pulmonary vascular resistance rises
- Renal
  - Renal blood flow falls moderately
  - Na+ reabsorption rises: direct tubular effect plus renin and aldosterone release (RAAS)
  - Bladder sphincter tone rises, which can make passing urine difficult
- Metabolic
  - Basal metabolic rate and plasma lactate rise
  - Hyperglycaemia: glycogenolysis (liver and muscle), gluconeogenesis, and less glucose uptake by muscle, heart and fat
    - Insulin release rises at first (β2) but is usually overridden by α inhibition of insulin release
    - Glucagon release rises
  - Lipolysis (β3) raises free fatty acids, driving hepatic fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis
  - Potassium: brief rise as the liver releases K+, then K+ moves into cells (β2)
  - Stimulates ACTH
- CNS
  - Increases MAC
  - Raises the peripheral pain threshold
- Other
  - Uterine smooth muscle relaxes (β 2)
  - Gut smooth muscle strongly inhibited (α and β); gut sphincters contract (α)
  - Skeletal muscle (β): slow red fibre twitch tension falls, fast white fibre twitch tension rises; tremor with β2 activity (likely from more muscle spindle firing)
  - Mast cell histamine release is inhibited (probably β2)
  - Platelet aggregation increases (α2)
  - α1 contracts the iris radial muscle, vas deferens and splenic capsule

## Adverse effects

- Septic shock: as effective as other catecholamines but more unwanted cardiac stimulation (harmful in coronary disease)
- Gut hypoperfusion can damage the bowel mucosal barrier
- Extravasation can cause tissue necrosis
- Do not infiltrate where end arteries supply the tissue (risk of ischaemia)
- Overdose can be fatal

## Indications

- Cardiac arrest (asystole), as IV bolus
- Anaphylaxis: drug of choice for circulatory support in anaphylactic shock
- Circulatory failure in the critically ill, by infusion
- Acute upper airway obstruction: nebulised, vasoconstriction briefly reduces swelling
- With local anaesthetic, for vasoconstriction before surgical dissection
- Open-angle glaucoma (eye drops)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Metabolised by mitochondrial MAO and COMT in liver, kidney and blood
- Products: inactive 3-methoxy-4-hydroxymandelic acid (vanillylmandelic acid, VMA) and metadrenaline, conjugated with glucuronide or sulfate, excreted in urine
- Half-life about 2 minutes
