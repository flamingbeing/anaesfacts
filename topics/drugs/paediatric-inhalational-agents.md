# Paediatric inhalational agents

Children need more volatile (MAC peaks around 6 months) and induce and wake faster; sevoflurane is the usual induction agent.

## Effects
- MAC is higher in children
  - Full-term neonate: same as adult
  - Rises from one month, peaks at 6 months (50% higher), then falls by about 6% per decade
  - Highest MAC between 1 to 6 months of age
  - Premature babies: 20-30% lower
  - Memory aid: infant MAC about 50% above adult, child MAC about 25% above adult
- Faster induction and emergence because of
  - High alveolar ventilation relative to FRC
  - Larger share of cardiac output to vessel-rich organs
- Otherwise similar to adults
  - Same indications, contraindications and metabolism
  - All depress the cardiovascular system to some degree
  - All lower airway resistance by relaxing bronchial smooth muscle

## Dosage
- MAC by age
  - Desflurane: neonate 9.1, infant 9.4, child 8.6, adult 6.0
  - Sevoflurane: neonate 3.2, infant 3.2, child 2.5, adult 2.0
  - Isoflurane: preterm 1.3, neonate 1.6, infant 1.9, child 1.6, adult 1.16
  - Halothane: neonate 0.9, infant 1.2, child 0.9, adult 0.76

## Indications
- Sevoflurane is the commonest inhalational induction agent; halothane is still used, especially for airway surgery or a potentially difficult airway
- Isoflurane and desflurane are unsuitable for inhalational induction

## Sevoflurane
- Not pungent, so 8% can be used from the start, speeding induction
- High concentrations cause marked respiratory depression or apnoea
- Low potency: a child may be apnoeic yet still too light for airway instrumentation
- Emergence delirium and distress sometimes follow sevoflurane maintenance
- Unlike the other volatiles, not metabolised to trifluoroacetate (which triggers antibodies and immune hepatitis)

## Desflurane
- Useful for maintenance in long cases with IPPV
- Too irritant for induction or spontaneous breathing in most children

## Isoflurane
- Coronary vasodilation and coronary steal are of little or no concern in children

## Halothane
- Potent and smooth: useful for the potentially difficult airway and airway endoscopy
- At high concentrations, dysrhythmias are much less of a problem in young children than adults
- Strong bronchodilator: useful in severe asthma or airways irritable after a viral infection

## Nitrous oxide
- Used as a carrier gas for volatiles, as in adults
- Traditionally used to cut volatile requirement in young children, who are prone to volatile-induced myocardial depression and bradycardia: "the heart is sensitive, the brain is resistant"
- Mild cardiovascular depression in neonates
- Faster uptake in infants and young children than adults
- Procedural analgesia as a 50% mix with oxygen (Entonox, Equanox)
- Use is declining
  - Diffuses into air spaces: raises pressure in rigid spaces (middle ear) and expands compliant ones (pneumothorax, ETT cuff)
  - Causes PONV
  - Prolonged use can be toxic via methionine synthetase and vitamin B12
  - Relatively contraindicated in laparoscopic, bowel, middle ear and some eye surgery, and emphysema
  - Piped air is readily available

## Flashcards
- Q: At what age does MAC peak, and by how much?
  - A: About 6 months, roughly 50% above adult values
- Q: MAC of sevoflurane in a neonate, child and adult?
  - A: 3.2, 2.5 and 2.0
- Q: How does MAC in premature babies compare?
  - A: 20-30% lower
- Q: Why can sevoflurane be started at 8%?
  - A: It is not pungent, so high concentrations are tolerated and induction is faster
