# Oxycodone

Strong opioid used as an alternative to morphine, about 1.5-2x as potent; also combined with naloxone (Targin).

## Presentation
- IV: 10mg in 1ml
- Oral: immediate release (5-10mg) and sustained release (OxyContin)
- Targin (oxycodone with naloxone): fixed prolonged-release tablets
    - 5 mg/2.5 mg, 10 mg/5 mg, 20 mg/10 mg, 40 mg/20 mg

## Administration
- IV: dilute to 10ml to make 1mg/ml (as for morphine); give 1-2mg boluses
- Oral

## Dosage
- Paediatric oral: 0.1-0.2mg/kg Q6-8H (a bit less than morphine)

## Mechanism of action
- Opioid agonist, 1.5-2x more potent than morphine
- Targin: naloxone has heavy first-pass metabolism, so no systemic effect, but acts locally on the gut (before the liver) to reduce constipation; oxycodone still acts systemically

## Effects
- Profile similar to morphine; some patients tolerate one better than the other
- Cochrane 2017 (cancer pain): pain relief and overall adverse events similar to other strong opioids including morphine

## Indications
- Alternative to morphine for moderate to severe pain
- Targin: severe pain with resistant constipation
    - May help postoperatively where constipation is a particular problem (haemorrhoidectomy, pelvic floor repair), despite being prolonged release

## Pharmacokinetics
- Oral bioavailability about 50% (85% in the Targin section of the note)
- Metabolised to noroxycodone and oxymorphone

## Flashcards
- Q: Potency of oxycodone relative to morphine?
  - A: 1.5-2x more potent
- Q: Paediatric oral oxycodone dose?
  - A: 0.1-0.2mg/kg Q6-8H
- Q: How does Targin reduce constipation without reversing analgesia?
  - A: Naloxone acts locally on the gut but has heavy first-pass metabolism, so no systemic effect
- Q: Targin dose combinations?
  - A: 5 mg/2.5 mg, 10 mg/5 mg, 20 mg/10 mg, 40 mg/20 mg
