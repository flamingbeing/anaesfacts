# Midazolam

Water-soluble, short-acting benzodiazepine for premedication, co-induction and sedation (Dormicum, Hypnovel, Versed).

## Presentation

- Clear solution at pH 3.5, 1 mg/ml in 5 ml ampoules; given neat
- Midazolam sugar syrup for oral use
- Mnemonic: Versed = VERy SEDated
- Structure depends on pH
  - At pH 3.5 the diazepine ring is open: ionised, water-soluble, so no pain on injection
  - Above pH 4 the ring closes: unionised, lipid-soluble
- pKa 6.5: at physiological pH 89% is unionised and crosses membranes

## Administration

- IV, IM, oral or intranasal
- Oral: the IV solution is often used; bitter, so mix with a little diluting juice
- Intranasal (children): via an atomiser; allow for its dead space of 0.1 ml
- Respiratory depression and fall risk: monitor closely if given before surgery

## Dosage

- IV
  - Premedication: 0.02–0.04 mg/kg (e.g. 1–2 mg)
    - Start with 2 mg, then 1 mg every 2–3 minutes
    - Usually up to 5 mg; take care with higher doses
  - Co-induction: 1–2 mg bolus, or 2–5 mg
  - Induction: 0.1–0.2 mg/kg
  - With an opioid or other CNS depressant, reduce the dose to about 50%
  - Onset 45–60 sec: slower than fentanyl and even ketamine
  - Duration 15–30 min, longer than other induction agents
    - Usually under 2 hours, but up to 6 hours in some; longer with bigger doses
    - Anterograde amnesia lasts about 20–40 minutes after one IV dose
- IM
  - 0.05 to 0.15 mg/kg (up to 10 mg)
  - Premedication without loss of consciousness: 0.05 mg/kg
  - Onset 5–15 min, peak 15–60 min
  - Duration 2 hours (range 1–6 h); amnesia about 1 hour
- Oral
  - Anxiolysis in children before surgery: 0.5 mg/kg (up to 20 mg)
  - Conscious sedation: 0.5–0.75 mg/kg (up to 20 mg)
  - Onset 20 min, peak 40 min; good effect usually by about 20–30 min
  - Duration 2–6 hours; 60–90 min for conscious sedation
- Intranasal (children)
  - 0.2–0.3 mg/kg, maximum 10 mg per dose
  - Onset 10–15 min, duration up to 60 min

## Mechanism of action

- Acts at the GABAA receptor; both wanted and unwanted effects come from this

## Effects

- Cardiovascular: fairly cardiostable, but SVR and BP fall at induction doses
- Respiratory: respiratory depression, worse with opioids
- CNS
  - Reduces cerebral metabolic rate and cerebral blood flow
  - No EEG burst suppression
  - Anticonvulsant (useful in local anaesthetic toxicity)
  - Strong amnesia
  - Loss of balance: fall risk
- No analgesia, so combine with other agents
- Variable response; some show paradoxical excitement

## Indications

- Premedication for anxiety
- Co-induction (not as a sole induction agent)
- Sedation for minor procedures and ventilated patients

## Pharmacokinetics

- Oral bioavailability about 40%
- About 95% protein bound
- Lipid-soluble at physiological pH: reaches the CNS quickly; short action is due to redistribution
- Hydroxylated to the active metabolite 1-α hydroxymidazolam, then glucuronidated and excreted by the kidneys
  - Less than 5% becomes oxazepam
- Clearance falls in both hepatic and renal failure
- Elimination half-life 1.5–3.5 h (1–4 hours)
- Clearance 6–10 ml/kg/min, higher than diazepam and lorazepam, so it wears off faster after infusion
- Shares hepatic P450 3A3/4 with alfentanil: given together, both may last longer

  - A: 0.02–0.04 mg/kg (e.g. 1–2 mg)
- Q: Oral midazolam dose for anxiolysis in children?
  - A: 0.5 mg/kg (up to 20 mg)

  - A: 0.2–0.3 mg/kg, maximum 10 mg per dose
- Q: Why is IV midazolam painless on injection?
