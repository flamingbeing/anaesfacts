# Tramadol

Weak mu agonist that also blocks noradrenaline and 5-HT reuptake; a racemic cyclohexanol derivative whose two enantiomers act differently.

## Presentation
- Tablets, capsules or sachets, 50–400 mg (including modified release)
- Injection for IV or IM use: 100 mg in 2 ml

## Administration
- Oral: onset 1 hr, peak 2-3 hrs, duration of action 6 hrs
- IV or IM

## Dosage
- Oral: 25-100 mg TDS-QDS
  - Weight-based: 1-2 mg/kg Q6H (PAN app)
- IV: 50-100 mg 6-hourly after a 100 mg loading dose
  - Weight-based: 1-2 mg/kg after a 2 mg/kg loading dose (PAN app)
- Advanced CKD: suggested maximum 50 mg BD orally

## Mechanism of action
- Weak mu agonist; acts at all opioid receptors, mainly mu
- Inhibits 5-HT and noradrenaline reuptake and promotes presynaptic 5-HT release
  - Gives a second, non-opioid route to analgesia through the descending inhibitory pathways in the spinal cord
- Not classified as a controlled drug (as recorded)
- Equivalence: 100 mg tramadol is roughly 5-15 mg morphine
  - Oral morphine to oral tramadol potency ratio 1:4 to 1:10, so 400 mg tramadol a day is about 40-100 mg oral morphine (Palliative Care Formulary)
  - Parenteral morphine to parenteral tramadol ratio 1:10, so 100 mg parenteral tramadol is about 10 mg parenteral morphine (Palliative Care Formulary)

## Adverse effects
- At equianalgesic doses, less respiratory depression and constipation than morphine; otherwise similar effects
- Naloxone reverses respiratory depression and analgesia
- Nausea and vomiting: the commonest adverse effect, about as frequent as with other opioids (some feel more frequent)
- Interactions with TCAs and SSRIs: psychomotor impairment, CNS depression, serotonin syndrome, seizures

## Contraindications
- Avoid with a history of seizures

## Pharmacokinetics
- Oral bioavailability 70%, rising above 90% with repeated doses
- Hepatic metabolism by CYP2D6, CYP3A4 and CYP2B6, then glucuronidation
- Only one metabolite is analgesic: O-desmethyltramadol (made in the liver, excreted by the kidneys)
  - Its average 5-h half-life may double in advanced CKD
  - High levels can cause respiratory depression and lower the seizure threshold
- Metabolites excreted in urine
- Volume of distribution 4 l/kg
- Elimination half-life 5–6 hours, oral or parenteral

## Flashcards
- Q: Besides weak mu agonism, how does tramadol produce analgesia?
  - A: Inhibits noradrenaline and 5-HT reuptake (and releases 5-HT), acting on descending inhibitory pathways
- Q: Adult IV tramadol dosing?
  - A: 50-100 mg 6-hourly after a 100 mg loading dose
- Q: Oral morphine to oral tramadol potency ratio?
  - A: 1:4 to 1:10 (400 mg tramadol a day is about 40-100 mg oral morphine)
- Q: Suggested maximum tramadol dose in advanced CKD?
  - A: 50 mg BD orally
- Q: Which patients should avoid tramadol?
  - A: Those with a history of seizures, and caution with SSRIs or TCAs (serotonin syndrome, seizures)
