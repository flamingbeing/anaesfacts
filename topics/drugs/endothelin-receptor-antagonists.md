# Endothelin receptor antagonists

Oral pulmonary vasodilators for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) that block endothelin receptors; bosentan is the main example.

## Background

- Endothelin-1 (ET-1) is a strong pulmonary vasoconstrictor, produced in excess in PAH
- ETA receptors: ET-1 constricts vascular smooth muscle and drives its proliferation, mainly through these
- ETB receptors: trigger prostacyclin and NO release from endothelium
- Several endothelin antagonists are licensed for primary PAH

## Bosentan

### Dosage
- Start 62.5 mg twice daily for 4 weeks
- Maintenance 125 mg twice daily

### Mechanism of action
- Dual endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA): blocks both ETA and ETB

### Effects
- Lowers pulmonary and systemic vascular resistance with no rise in heart rate
- Improves symptoms and mortality in primary PAH

### Adverse effects
- Usually well tolerated
- Abnormal liver function tests: check liver aminotransferases monthly
- Anaemia, headache, peripheral oedema, nasal congestion
- Class effect: testicular atrophy and infertility
- Potentially teratogenic

### Indications
- PAH in adults and children

### Pharmacokinetics
- Oral bioavailability 50%
- Induces its own metabolising enzymes (CYP2CY and CYP3A4), so plasma levels fall by 50% with ongoing use
- Excreted in bile

## Ambrisentan

- Selective ETA antagonist (LETAIRIS)
- Oral, once daily, 5-10 mg
- Efficacy and side effects similar to bosentan
- Monthly liver aminotransferase checks also needed

## Sitaxsentan

- Selective ETA antagonist (THELIN)
- Withdrawn after reports of fatal liver injury in PAH patients
