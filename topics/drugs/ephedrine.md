# Ephedrine

Mixed direct and indirect sympathomimetic (alpha and beta): raises blood pressure and heart rate; prone to tachyphylaxis.

## Presentation

- Injection 30 mg/ml (30 mg in 1 ml vial)
- Also tablets, elixir and nasal drops
- Plant-derived originally, synthesised for medical use
- Four isomers exist; only the L-isomer is active

## Administration

- IV boluses
- Diluting a 30 mg vial:
  - 30 mg in 10 ml = 3 mg per ml
  - 30 mg in 6 ml = 5 mg per ml
- Onset: seconds
- Peak: quoted as 78-90 sec or 2-5 min (a little slower than phenylephrine)
- Duration: 20-60 min (a little longer than phenylephrine)

## Dosage

- IV: 3-5 mg boluses

## Mechanism of action

- Indirect: taken up into presynaptic nerve terminals and releases noradrenaline, giving alpha-mediated vasoconstriction
- Direct: mixed alpha and beta agonist, raising heart rate and cardiac output
- Beta2 effect gives mild bronchodilation
- Also inhibits MAO breakdown of noradrenaline
- Tachyphylaxis: noradrenaline stores in sympathetic nerve endings become depleted

## Effects

- Cardiovascular: cardiac output, heart rate, blood pressure, coronary blood flow and myocardial oxygen consumption increase
- Respiratory: respiratory stimulant; bronchodilation
- Renal: renal blood flow and GFR fall
- Net effect: vasoconstriction and faster heart rate, so suited to patients with both low blood pressure and low heart rate

## Adverse effects

- Arrhythmias
- Tachyphylaxis
- Obstetrics: poorer cord gas pH than purer alpha agonists
- Interaction: use with extreme caution in patients on MAOIs

## Indications

- Hypotension with regional anaesthesia
  - Still widely used in obstetrics because pure alpha agonists can cause significant maternal hypertension
- Bronchospasm
- Nocturnal enuresis
- Narcolepsy

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed orally, IM and subcutaneously
- Not broken down by MAO or COMT (unlike adrenaline), so it lasts longer
- Elimination half-life 4 hours
- Partly metabolised in the liver; 65% excreted unchanged in urine

## Flashcards
- Q: How do you make 3 mg/ml and 5 mg/ml ephedrine from a 30 mg vial?
  - A: 30 mg in 10 ml = 3 mg/ml; 30 mg in 6 ml = 5 mg/ml
- Q: Why does ephedrine show tachyphylaxis?
  - A: Its indirect action depletes noradrenaline stores in sympathetic nerve endings
- Q: What is the usual IV bolus dose of ephedrine?
  - A: 3-5 mg
- Q: Which drug class makes ephedrine dangerous?
  - A: MAOIs; use with extreme caution
