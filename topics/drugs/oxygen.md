# Oxygen

Medical gas made by fractional distillation of air; supplied from cylinder banks or liquid stores, toxic at high partial pressures.

## Presentation
- Medical grade: 99-99.5% pure
- Made by fractional distillation of liquefied air, or by an oxygen concentrator
    - Concentrator: zeolite mesh adsorbs N2, leaving about 97% oxygen
- Hospital supply: gas at room temperature and pressure, or refrigerated liquid
    - Gas: 2 banks of H cylinders (7080L each) on a manifold
        - Only 1 bank in use at a time
        - Manifold reduces cylinder pressure to line pressure and switches banks automatically when one is empty
    - Liquid: stored well below the critical temperature of -119°C (a gas can only be liquefied by pressure below its critical temperature)
- Local backup: E cylinder (680L), fits most anaesthesia machines

## Administration
- Cylinder contents are judged by pressure, which falls in proportion to use
    - 1000psig: E cylinder about half full (330L)
- Bourdon gauge: flexible tube straightens under pressure, driving a gear that moves the pointer
- Safety: pressure-relief valve set to rupture at 3300 psig (E cylinder walls withstand >5000); the yoke has parts made of Wood's metal, a low melting point alloy

## Effects
- Cardiovascular
    - Correcting hypoxaemia improves all cardiovascular variables
    - Prolonged 100% oxygen slightly lowers cardiac output and constricts coronary arteries
    - Lowers pulmonary vascular resistance and pulmonary artery pressure
- Respiratory
    - Mild respiratory depression in healthy people at high concentration
    - In patients truly dependent on hypoxic drive, even modest concentrations may be fatal

## Adverse effects
- Toxicity is due to free radicals
- CNS: anxiety, nausea and seizures above 200 kPa partial pressure
- Lung: lipid peroxidation of the alveolar capillary membrane; areas of collapse
- Neonates: retrolental fibroplasia, possibly from vasoconstriction of developing retinal vessels

## Flashcards
- Q: Purity of medical grade oxygen?
  - A: 99-99.5%
- Q: Capacity of an E oxygen cylinder, and contents at 1000 psig?
  - A: 680L; about half full (330L)
- Q: Critical temperature of oxygen?
  - A: -119°C
- Q: Partial pressure above which CNS oxygen toxicity occurs?
  - A: 200 kPa
