# Flecainide

Vaughan Williams class 1 (subclass Ic) antiarrhythmic and amide local anaesthetic, given orally or IV.

## Presentation

- Oral and IV forms

## Dosage

- Oral: 100 mg bd (maximum 400 mg daily)
- IV: 2 mg/kg over 10–30 minutes (maximum 150 mg)
  - Can follow with an infusion: initially 1.5 mg/kg/h, then reduce to 100–250 mcg/kg/h for up to 24 hours
  - Maximum 600 mg in 24 hours

## Mechanism of action

- Blocks fast Na+ influx into cardiac tissue; prolongs phase 0 of the action potential
- No effect on action potential duration or refractory period
- Effect is most marked on the conducting pathways

## Adverse effects

- Cardiac:
  - Can unmask pre-existing conduction disease; take care with SA or AV node disease or bundle branch block
  - Paradoxical rise in ventricular rate in AF or atrial flutter
  - Higher mortality when used to suppress ventricular ectopics after myocardial infarction
  - Negative inotrope: can cause cardiac failure
  - Raises the pacing threshold
- Non-cardiac: dizziness, paraesthesia, headache

## Indications

- Atrial and ventricular tachyarrhythmias, including WPW syndrome

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed; oral bioavailability 90%
- About 50% plasma protein bound
- Volume of distribution 6–10 l/kg
- Hepatic metabolism to active metabolites; these and unchanged drug are excreted in urine
