# Alfentanil

Phenylpiperidine µ agonist related to fentanyl: least potent, fastest onset and shortest acting of the fentanyl group.

## Presentation
- Colourless solution, 1mg in 2ml (0.5mg/ml)
- Can be diluted to 10 ml (1 mg) to give 100mcg per ml

## Administration
- IV bolus or infusion

## Dosage
- Sedation: 5mcg/kg, then 2.5mcg/kg top-ups every 10-15min as needed
  - Some use 50-100mcg boluses for sedation
- Induction of anaesthesia: 10 mcg/ kg IV

## Mechanism of action
- µ-receptor agonist
- 4 to 10 times less potent than fentanyl (1/4th to 1/10th as potent)

## Effects
- Fast onset; duration 5–10 min, a third of fentanyl's (1/3rd)
- More cardiostable than fentanyl, with fewer cardiovascular effects

## Adverse effects
- Greater risk of respiratory depression than fentanyl
- Interactions
  - Midazolam shares the hepatic enzymes (CYP3A3/4): giving both markedly prolongs both elimination half-lives
  - Erythromycin can prolong its action by inhibiting hepatic CYP450

## Indications
- Short bursts of fast analgesia, e.g. during a nerve block or head pinning
- Infusion for sedation, though duration then lengthens considerably

## Pharmacokinetics
- pKa 6.5: 89% unionised at pH 7.4, so faster onset than fentanyl at equipotent doses
  - Fentanyl pKa 8.4: only 9% unionised at pH 7.4
- Much smaller volume of distribution than fentanyl (lower lipid solubility, high protein binding)
  - So shorter elimination half-life despite lower clearance
- Hepatic N-demethylation to noralfentanil; metabolites conjugated and excreted in urine
