# Analgesic adjuncts

Non-opioid drugs that add to analgesia, grouped by the mnemonic "NMDA" plus a few others.

## Mnemonic: NMDA
- N: NMDA antagonists
    - Ketamine
    - Magnesium sulfate
- M: Membrane stabilisers
    - Lidocaine
    - Gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin)
    - Carbamazepine
- D: Antidepressants and antipsychotics
    - Antidepressants: SSRI (fluoxetine), SNRI (duloxetine), NaSSA (mirtazapine), SARI (trazodone)
    - Tricyclics (amitriptyline, nortriptyline)
    - MAOIs (e.g. selegiline)
    - Antipsychotics
- A: Alpha-2 agonists
    - Clonidine
    - Dexmedetomidine

## Antispasmodics and muscle relaxants
- Orphenadrine (e.g. Anarex)
- Baclofen
- Diazepam
- Eperisone (Myonal)

## Topicals
- Lidocaine
- Capsaicin
- Menthol patch (Salonpas)
- Methyl salicylate

## Sleep aids
- Melatonin (e.g. Circadin) and melatonin receptor agonists
- Hydroxyzine (first-generation H1 antihistamine)
- Benzodiazepines
- Drugs with sedation as a side effect: Anarex, gabapentin, amitriptyline

## Other targeted adjuncts
- Nifedipine: painful oesophageal spasm and tenesmus from gastrointestinal tumours; can ease odynophagia
- Clodronate: blocks osteoclast bone resorption; malignant bone pain and its hypercalcaemia
- Pinaverium (antispasmodic): shortens pain episodes in irritable bowel syndrome
- Corticosteroids: pain from central nervous system tumours
- Oxybutynin (anticholinergic): painful bladder spasms

## Flashcards
- Q: What does the mnemonic NMDA stand for in analgesic adjuncts?
  - A: NMDA antagonists, Membrane stabilisers, antiDepressants and antipsychotics, Alpha-2 agonists
- Q: Which adjunct treats malignant bone pain and hypercalcaemia?
  - A: Clodronate
- Q: Which calcium channel blocker eases painful oesophageal spasm from GI tumours?
  - A: Nifedipine
