# Phenylephrine

Direct-acting pure α1 agonist: raises SVR and blood pressure with a reflex bradycardia; no β effect.

## Presentation
- Clear solution, 10 mg in 1 ml
- Also 1000 mcg in 10 ml (100 mcg/ml), given neat

## Administration
- IV boluses
- IM or SC for a longer effect
- IV infusion

## Dosage
- IV bolus 50–100 mcg
- IM or SC 2–5 mg
- Infusion: started at 500 mcg/hr in a ward patient

## Mechanism of action
- Direct-acting sympathomimetic amine
- Pure α1 agonist; no β-adrenoceptor action

## Effects
- Cardiovascular
  - SVR and blood pressure rise, with reflex bradycardia, so cardiac output falls
  - Not arrhythmogenic
  - Coronary and cerebral perfusion pressure increase; coronary and cerebral blood flow change little
- CNS: no stimulation
- Renal: blood flow falls, as with noradrenaline
- Uterus: now the first-line vasopressor for spinal hypotension at caesarean section (2018 international consensus); gives a better cord gas profile

## Adverse effects
- Cardiac output can fall: use with caution in ischaemic heart disease

## Indications
- Low SVR from spinal anaesthesia or drugs
- Low blood pressure with a high heart rate
- Reversing a left-to-right intracardiac shunt that general anaesthesia has turned by lowering SVR
- Nasal decongestant and mydriatic
- Possibly SVT with hypotension (limited role)

## Pharmacokinetics
- IV: rapid rise in blood pressure lasting 5–10 minutes
- IV bolus timings: onset immediate, peak 27-40 sec (faster than ephedrine), duration 15-20 min
- IM or SC: onset 15 minutes, lasts up to 1 hour
- Hepatic metabolism by MAO; metabolites and their elimination route are not known

## Flashcards
- Q: Usual IV bolus dose of phenylephrine?
  - A: 50–100 mcg
- Q: Which receptor does phenylephrine act on?
  - A: α1 only; no β effect
- Q: Effect of phenylephrine on heart rate and cardiac output?
  - A: Reflex bradycardia; cardiac output falls
- Q: Peak effect after an IV bolus?
  - A: 27-40 sec
