# Sotalol

Non-selective β-blocker without intrinsic sympathomimetic activity, which also has class III (and some class I) antiarrhythmic action.

## Presentation

- Tablets, and solution 40 mg in 4 ml
- Racemic mixture: D-isomer gives class III action; L-isomer gives class III and class II (β-blocking) action

## Dosage

- Oral 80–160 mg twice daily
- IV 50–100 mg over 20 minutes

## Mechanism of action

- Lengthens the action potential, so the effective refractory period of conducting tissue is prolonged
- Non-selective β-blockade

## Adverse effects

- Cardiac
  - Torsades de pointes: the most serious; rare, under 2% of patients treated for sustained VT or VF
  - More likely with high doses, long QT and electrolyte disturbance
  - Can precipitate heart failure
- Non-cardiac (all rare): bronchospasm, masked hypoglycaemia symptoms, visual disturbance, sexual dysfunction

## Contraindications

- Committee on Safety of Medicines: not for angina, hypertension, thyrotoxicosis or around myocardial infarction

## Indications

- Ventricular tachyarrhythmias
- Prophylaxis of paroxysmal SVT, including after DC cardioversion
- Better than other β-blockers at keeping sinus rhythm after DC cardioversion of AF; rate stays well controlled if AF recurs

## Pharmacokinetics

- Fully absorbed; oral bioavailability over 90%
- Not protein bound; not metabolised
- About 90% excreted unchanged in urine, the rest in bile
- Clearance falls markedly in renal impairment
