# Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors

Drugs that block acetylcholinesterase (AChE), so more acetylcholine (ACh) is available at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ) and other cholinergic sites.

## Mechanism of action

- AChE normally hydrolyses ACh; inhibitors slow this, so ACh builds up
- Three groups by mechanism, which also match duration
  - Easily reversible inhibition (short-acting): edrophonium
  - Carbamylated enzyme complex (medium-acting): neostigmine, physostigmine, pyridostigmine (medium to long)
  - Irreversible inactivation (long-acting): ecothiopate, organophosphates

### Carbamylated enzyme complex

- AChE hydrolyses both ACh and carbamate esters
- ACh leaves an acetylated enzyme; carbamates leave a carbamylated enzyme that is hydrolysed far more slowly
- While carbamylated, AChE cannot break down ACh
- Also called acid-transferring or time-dependent AChE inhibitors

### Irreversible inactivation (organophosphates)

- Very toxic; used as insecticides (tetraethylpyrophosphate, TEPP) and nerve agents (sarin, GB)
- Very lipid soluble, so rapidly absorbed through skin
- Phosphorylate the esteratic site of AChE
- The complex is very stable: resists hydrolysis and reactivation, unlike carbamates
- Recovery depends on making new enzyme
- Also inhibit plasma cholinesterase
- Ecothiopate iodide has been used for glaucoma: contracts the ciliary muscle and opens trabecular meshwork drainage
- Toxicity causes cholinergic crisis

## Uses

- Reversal of non-depolarising muscle relaxants (e.g. neostigmine)
  - Less ACh breakdown at the NMJ, so excess ACh outcompetes the blocker for receptors and allows depolarisation
- Myasthenia gravis: pyridostigmine for treatment; edrophonium for diagnosis
- Glaucoma, lowering intraocular pressure: physostigmine, ecothiopate
- Cognitive symptoms (mainly memory and learning) of Alzheimer's disease, Lewy body dementias and Parkinson's disease: rivastigmine, donepezil, galantamine
- Antidote to anticholinergic poisoning
- Insecticides (e.g. malathion) and nerve agents

## Adverse effects

- AChE exists beyond the NMJ, so muscarinic effects occur
  - Bradycardia, asystole, hypotension
  - Bronchospasm
  - Nausea and vomiting, diarrhoea
  - Urination (may be involuntary)
  - Miosis, loss of accommodation
  - Cholinergic crisis
- Give an anticholinergic (atropine or glycopyrrolate) at the same time to prevent muscarinic overactivity (bradycardia, salivation)

## Tacrine (tetrahydroaminacrine hydrochloride)

- Short-acting; crosses the blood-brain barrier, so has central effects
- Formerly used to prolong succinylcholine
- Used in the USA for Alzheimer's disease

## Physostigmine

- Natural alkaloid from the Calabar bean
- Carbamate without a quaternary ammonium group, so crosses the blood-brain barrier
- Uses: anticholinergic toxicity, glaucoma

## Edrophonium

### Presentation

- Clear colourless injection, 10 mg/ml
- Phenolic quaternary amine

### Dosage

- Myasthenic versus cholinergic crisis: IV 2mg
  - Myasthenic crisis: power improves; cholinergic crisis: worsens
  - May give up to 10mg if no deterioration
- Reversal of competitive block: 0.5mg/kg (to 0.7mg/kg) slow IV, after a suitable anticholinergic dose (e.g. atropine) to cover muscarinic effects
- Onset faster than neostigmine; peak 1-2min; short duration (10 min)

### Mechanism of action

- The only easily reversible inhibitor
  - Quaternary amine binds the anionic site of AChE; hydroxyl group hydrogen-bonds at the esteratic site
  - This keeps ACh off the active site, but no covalent bond forms, so ACh can compete
- Also increases ACh release; part of the effect is prejunctional
- 12–16 times less potent than neostigmine, and less predictable for reversing profound block

### Adverse effects

- Few muscarinic effects, but can still cause bradycardia and salivation

### Pharmacokinetics

- Low lipid solubility (quaternary amine)
  - Not absorbed orally
  - Does not cross the blood-brain barrier or placenta
- Up to 65% excreted unchanged in urine; the rest glucuronidated in the liver and excreted in bile
- Not hydrolysed by cholinesterases

- Q: Which AChE inhibitor is the only easily reversible one?
  - A: Edrophonium
- Q: Edrophonium dose to distinguish myasthenic from cholinergic crisis?
  - A: 2 mg IV (up to 10 mg if no deterioration)
- Q: Edrophonium dose for reversal of competitive block?
  - A: 0.5 mg/kg (to 0.7 mg/kg) slow IV after an anticholinergic
