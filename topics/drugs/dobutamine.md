# Dobutamine

Synthetic catecholamine and inodilator: mainly β1, some β2 (about 3:1), weak α.

## Presentation

- 250 mg in 20 ml (with sodium metabisulfite), or 250 mg in 5 ml (with ascorbic acid)

## Administration

- Dilute 250 mg in 50 ml (5 mg/ml), as for dopamine
- IV infusion only; no loading dose
- Often needs to be combined with noradrenaline

## Dosage

- Start at 3–5 mcg/kg/min, increase in steps of 3–5 mcg/kg/min
- Usual range 5–20 mcg/kg/min; up to 200 mcg/kg/min reported
- Titrate to haemodynamic targets

## Effects

- Contractility, stroke volume and cardiac output increase
- Heart rate usually rises by 5–15/min, sometimes by more than 30/min
- Mild vasodilation at about 5 mcg/kg/min; little change in SVR up to about 15 mcg/kg/min; vasoconstriction at higher doses
- Blood pressure often unchanged
- Myocardial oxygen demand increases
- Tolerance develops within days

## Adverse effects

- Arrhythmias at any dose
- Faster ventricular rate in AF or atrial flutter

## Contraindications

- Aortic stenosis
- Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy
- Cardiac tamponade

## Indications

- Low cardiac output after myocardial infarction or cardiac surgery (0.5–20 mcg/kg/min)
- Cardiogenic shock
- Septic myocardial depression, with a vasoconstrictor
- Pharmacological stress testing
- In decompensated heart failure, other inodilators are often preferred

## Pharmacokinetics

- Metabolised by COMT to inactive metabolites
- Half-life about 2 min
