# Isoflurane

Pungent halogenated ethyl methyl ether volatile with MAC about 1.2%, little metabolism and a favourable cerebral profile.

## Presentation
- Halogenated ethyl methyl ether; structural isomer of enflurane (same formula, different structure)

## Dosage
- MAC approximately 1.2%
- MAC40: 1.17% at age 40 (BJA 2003 iso-MAC charts)

## Effects
- Cardiovascular
  - Mainly lowers SVR
  - Can cause tachycardia (less than desflurane)
  - Only small falls in contractility and cardiac output
  - Coronary steal proposed (dilated normal arterioles divert flow from areas fed by diseased vessels), but later work suggests it does not occur if coronary perfusion pressure is kept up
  - Possible myocardial protection via ATP-dependent potassium channels
- Respiratory
  - Depresses ventilation more than halothane, less than enflurane
  - Minute volume falls; respiratory rate and PaCO2 rise
  - Pungent: irritates the upper airway (coughing, breath-holding), so rarely used for induction
  - Still some bronchodilation
- Central nervous system
  - Best balance among volatiles of lower cerebral oxygen demand with minimal rise in cerebral blood flow
  - Cerebral autoregulation preserved up to 1 MAC
- Hepatic
  - Total hepatic blood flow falls, but is preserved better than with other inhalational agents

## Adverse effects
- Its –CHF2 group can react with dry soda lime (or baralyme) to make carbon monoxide
  - Reported when circle systems were left with dry gas flowing over a weekend
  - Enflurane and desflurane also have –CHF2 groups and can do the same

## Pharmacokinetics
- Only 0.2% metabolised; no metabolite linked to toxicity

## Flashcards
- Q: MAC of isoflurane?
  - A: Approximately 1.2% (1.17% at age 40)
- Q: How much isoflurane is metabolised?
  - A: Only 0.2%
- Q: Up to what concentration is cerebral autoregulation preserved with isoflurane?
  - A: 1 MAC
- Q: Which structural feature lets isoflurane produce carbon monoxide with dry soda lime?
  - A: The –CHF2 group (shared with enflurane and desflurane)
