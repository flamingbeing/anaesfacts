# Types of Fluid Replacement

Crystalloids, colloids and blood differ in what they contain and where the volume goes, which sets how much is needed to replace blood loss.

## Crystalloids
- Water with dissolved electrolytes
- Much cheaper than colloids

## Colloids
- Water with macromolecules that exert oncotic pressure
- Their sodium chloride content gives them tonicity and osmolality like crystalloids; the macromolecules add oncotic pressure
- Thought to support intravascular volume and perfusion better, with less weight gain and oedema than crystalloids
- Side effects: anaphylaxis, coagulopathy

## Blood
- Transfused blood adds volume to both the intracellular space and the extracellular (intravascular) space

## Replacing blood loss
- Crystalloid: give about 3x the estimated blood loss
- Colloid: an equal volume works acutely
  - Colloids have short half-lives, so over time the total colloid volume needed approaches that of crystalloid

## Flashcards
- Q: Volume of crystalloid needed to replace blood loss?
  - A: About 3x the estimated blood loss
- Q: Volume of colloid to replace blood loss acutely?
  - A: About equal to the blood lost, though more is needed later as colloids have short half-lives
- Q: Two main side effects of colloids?
  - A: Anaphylaxis and coagulopathy
