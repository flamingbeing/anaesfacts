# Ropivacaine

Amide local anaesthetic, pure S-enantiomer related to bupivacaine: slightly less potent, less cardiotoxic, with more sensory than motor block.

## Presentation
- Amide of the pipecoloxylidide group
  - Like bupivacaine and mepivacaine, but with a propyl group on the piperidine nitrogen (bupivacaine butyl, mepivacaine methyl)
- Pure S-enantiomer (as is levobupivacaine)
- Three strengths (2, 7.5 and 10 mg/ml), two volumes (10 and 100 ml)
- No vasoconstrictor combination, as it does not change duration or tissue uptake

## Administration
- Epidural or perineural infusions are usually 0.2% ropivacaine
- Adding adrenaline or sodium bicarbonate does not seem to change onset or duration

## Dosage
- Toxic dose 3 mg/kg
- Onset 10-15 min
- Duration 3-8 hrs

## Mechanism of action
- pKa and protein binding match bupivacaine, so onset and duration are similar
- Slightly lower lipid solubility than bupivacaine: penetrates large motor fibres less

## Effects
- Differential block: for a given dose, sensory block exceeds motor block
  - Lower lipid solubility means less early penetration of large myelinated Aβ motor fibres, which are initially spared
  - Motor block is slower, less dense and shorter than with an equivalent bupivacaine dose
  - In theory better than bupivacaine for epidural infusion (sensory/motor separation, higher clearance)
  - With continuous infusion the motor fibres are blocked too, giving similar block in Aβ and small unmyelinated C fibres
- Slightly less potent than bupivacaine: in peripheral nerve block, 0.5% bupivacaine equals 0.6% ropivacaine
- Mild vasoconstriction at low concentrations (unlike bupivacaine)

## Adverse effects
- Less neurotoxic and cardiotoxic than bupivacaine; CNS toxicity appears before cardiac toxicity
  - Leaves cardiac Na+ channels faster and depresses the myocardium less
  - Lower lipid solubility may also help
  - S-enantiomers act less on myocardial Na channels, so less cardiotoxic (the dextro-enantiomer of bupivacaine selectively blocks cardiac Na channels)
- The S-enantiomer is described as more potent and less toxic

## Pharmacokinetics
- Hepatic metabolism by aromatic hydroxylation
  - Mainly to 3-hydroxyropivacaine, also 4-hydroxyropivacaine; both have some local anaesthetic activity
- Higher clearance than bupivacaine

## Flashcards
- Q: Toxic dose of ropivacaine?
  - A: 3 mg/kg
- Q: Usual concentration for epidural or perineural infusion?
  - A: 0.2%
- Q: Ropivacaine concentration equipotent to 0.5% bupivacaine in nerve block?
  - A: 0.6%
- Q: Why is motor block less dense than with bupivacaine?
  - A: Lower lipid solubility reduces early penetration of large myelinated motor fibres
