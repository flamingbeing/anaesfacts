# Hyoscine Hydrobromide

Hydrobromide salt of hyoscine: a lipid-soluble antimuscarinic with mainly central effects (antiemetic, sedative, antisialagogue).

## Presentation

- Hydrobromide salt of hyoscine
- Hyoscine is a racemic mixture; only l-hyoscine is active

## Administration

- Transdermal use is effective for PONV and motion sickness despite very low plasma levels

## Dosage

- 0.3-0.65mg
- Onset <1min; duration 2hrs

## Mechanism of action

- Antimuscarinic; mainly central effects because it is highly lipid soluble

## Effects

- Peripheral anticholinergic effects (some clinically useful)
  - Tachycardia rare: slight vasotonic effect, so less tachycardia than glycopyrrolate
- Drowsiness and mild sedation (crosses the blood–brain barrier); useful for airway procedures
- Amnesia and analgesia

## Adverse effects

- Central anticholinergic syndrome: excitement, ataxia, hallucinations, behaviour change, drowsiness

## Indications

- Potent antisialagogue
- Potent antiemetic (crosses the blood–brain barrier)
- Premedication, traditionally with an IM opioid; shown to reduce PONV
- Sedation and amnesia
- Motion sickness (transdermal)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Variable absorption; oral bioavailability 10% to 50%
- Extensively metabolised by liver esterases; little excreted unchanged in urine
- Shorter acting than atropine
