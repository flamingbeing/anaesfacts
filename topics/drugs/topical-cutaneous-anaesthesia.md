# Topical Cutaneous Anaesthesia

Skin numbing before venous cannulation and venepuncture: EMLA cream, Ametop (tetracaine) gel and ethyl chloride spray.

## Overview
- Routine before cannulation and venepuncture, especially in children
- Example order: Ametop applied on call to theatre

## EMLA: presentation
- 5% eutectic mixture: crystalline bases of 2.5% lidocaine and 2.5% prilocaine in a white oil-in-water emulsion
  - Each gram has 25 mg lidocaine and 25 mg prilocaine
- Tubes of 5 g or 30 g
- Eutectic: two compounds mixed behave with one set of physical properties
  - The mixture melts at 16°C, below either drug alone, so at room temperature it is an oil that crosses intact skin (each alone would be a crystalline solid)

## EMLA: administration
- Licensed from 1 year of age
- Apply to intact skin over visible veins under an occlusive dressing
- Uses: before vascular cannulation, or before harvesting skin grafts

## EMLA: onset and duration
- Needs time: apply for at least 60 minutes
  - Depth of analgesia grows with application time; after 120 min it exceeds mean skin thickness
  - After 1 hour it works in 65% of children; better if left 90-120 min
- Lasts 30-60 min after removal

## EMLA: effects
- Biphasic action on skin vessels
  - Vasoconstriction, maximal at 1.5 hours; can make venepuncture harder
  - Then vasodilatation and erythema after 2-3 hours
- Mainly vasoconstricts, unlike Ametop (vasodilates)

## EMLA: cautions and contraindications
- Methaemoglobinaemia from o-toluidine, a prilocaine metabolite
- Not on broken skin or mucous membranes (rapid systemic absorption)
- Caution with class I antiarrhythmics (e.g. tocainide, mexiletine): additive, possibly synergistic toxicity
- Avoid in congenital or idiopathic methaemoglobinaemia
- Higher risk with methaemoglobin-inducing drugs (e.g. sulfonamides, phenytoin)

## EMLA: methaemoglobinaemia in infants
- Neonatal red cells lack methaemoglobin reductase
- Preterm neonates at extra risk: thinner stratum corneum, more absorption
- Caution under 3 months
- BNF (2003): infants 1–12 months unlicensed, single application on intact skin under specialist supervision; under 1 month not recommended
- Safe doses have been established: 1 g on the prepuce for 1 h before circumcision in term neonates in their first week gave MetHb similar to placebo (mean 1.3%, monitored for 18 h)

## Ametop (tetracaine / amethocaine)
- 4% tetracaine for topical use
- More lipophilic than lidocaine and prilocaine; crosses the stratum corneum more easily
- Licensed from 1 month of age
- Efficacy equal to or better than EMLA
- Apply under an occlusive dressing, as for EMLA
- Faster onset (40 min) and longer action (about 4 hours after removal)
- Forms a depot in the stratum corneum that releases slowly: limits systemic uptake and prolongs action
- Adverse effects and cautions
  - Contraindicated on broken skin
  - Higher systemic toxicity than EMLA
  - Erythema in 30-40% after 40 min (more with longer use): expected local vasodilatation, not a true side effect
  - Itch in 10%, local oedema in 5%

## Ethyl chloride
- Instant skin analgesia before venepuncture or cannulation
- Evaporates on the skin, briefly cooling it to between -10°C and -20°C, causing short-lived anaesthesia
- Main advantage: immediate onset when there is no time to wait for a cream
- Avoid on broken skin
- Technique
  - Clean with an alcohol wipe
  - Let the alcohol dry fully: wet alcohol plus the spray can cause severe skin irritation
  - Spray for 3 to 7 seconds (never over 10 seconds) until the skin blanches
  - Hold the can about 6 inches (one hand span) away
  - Numbness lasts 1 minute: cannulate or inject straight away while the skin is still white and cold

## Flashcards
- Q: Composition of EMLA?
  - A: 5% eutectic mix: 2.5% lidocaine and 2.5% prilocaine (25 mg of each per gram)
- Q: Minimum EMLA application time and duration after removal?
  - A: At least 60 min (better 90-120 min); lasts 30-60 min after removal
- Q: Ametop onset and duration?
  - A: Onset 40 min; lasts about 4 hours after removal
- Q: Why is EMLA risky in neonates?
  - A: Prilocaine metabolite o-toluidine causes methaemoglobinaemia, and neonatal red cells lack methaemoglobin reductase
- Q: How long to spray ethyl chloride?
  - A: 3 to 7 seconds, never over 10 seconds, until the skin blanches
