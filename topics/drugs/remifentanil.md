# Remifentanil

Ultra-short-acting synthetic pure mu agonist (a phenylpiperidine related to fentanyl), broken down by non-specific esterases, so its offset stays fast however long it runs.

## Presentation

- White crystalline powder in glass vials of 1, 2 or 5 mg remifentanil hydrochloride
- Contains glycine: not licensed for spinal or epidural use

## Administration

- IV, usually by continuous infusion; ask for a syringe driver if you plan to use it
- Reconstitute and dilute with 5% dextrose, 0.9 or 0.45% saline; stable for 24 hours
- Common mix: 1-2 of the 1 mg vials in 50 ml water (20-40 mcg/ml)

## Dosage

- Potency: similar to fentanyl in some sources, about **twice as potent as fentanyl** in others
- Bolus: 0.1-1 mcg/kg (may cause bradycardia and muscle rigidity)
- Infusion range: **0.05–2 mcg/kg/min**, titrated to effect (like propofol mcg/kg/min doses, but 1000 times smaller)
- Manual (non-TCI) induction
  - 0.5 –1 mcg/kg/min
  - With or without an initial **1 mcg/kg bolus**, given over at least 30 seconds
  - Further **0.5-1 mcg/kg every 2-5 min** for light anaesthesia or brief intense stimulation
  - No bolus needed if intubation will be more than 8 to 10 minutes after starting the infusion
- Manual maintenance
  - High dose: 0.2-2 mcg/kg/min
  - Low dose with spontaneous ventilation: **0.02-0.2 mcg/kg/min**
    - = 1.2-12 mcg/kg/hr
    - = 60-600 mcg/hr, or 3-30 ml/hr, for a 50 kg patient
- TCI: Minto model (effect-site)
  - BJA Education: 3-8 ng/ml
  - ATOTW: 3-10 ng/ml; low 0.5-3 ng/ml, high 6-12 ng/ml
  - Higher targets may be needed depending on surgery; titrate to response and to the hypnotic dose
  - Induction: 2 ng/ml for older adults
  - Maintenance: **avoid going below 2 ng/ml in an unparalysed patient**, to keep spontaneous breathing suppressed
  - Emergence: may continue at **1–2 ng/ml to smooth extubation** (BJA Education)
  - Minto's ke0 comes from EEG studies; an EEG effect is not the same as onset of analgesia
- Children
  - Minto TCI only if **weight over 30 kg** (for lean body mass) and **age over 12 yr**
  - Under 30 kg: manual infusion, as no TCI model is available yet
  - Under 8 years: **less sensitive**; tolerate bigger doses breathing spontaneously and need more for a given antinociception (AAGBI)
- Timing
  - Onset 1 min, peak 1.5-2 min; allow at least 2-3 minutes to see the effect of a dose change
  - Duration 5-10 min, then little or no opioid effect remains
  - Context-sensitive half-time about 3-4 min
  - Spontaneous breathing resumes about 6-12 minutes after stopping or reducing to a low effect-site concentration

## Mechanism of action

- Pure mu-opioid receptor agonist

## Effects

- **Synergistic with volatiles and propofol** for loss of consciousness and for blocking movement and haemodynamic responses to noxious stimuli
  - Can cut propofol dose by up to 50% (AoA 2018)
- Offset so quick that **postoperative analgesia must be set up before surgery ends**
- PONV seems less common, given the very short action

## Adverse effects

- **High doses typically cause bradycardia and hypotension**; reversible with **glycopyrrolate**
  - ATOTW: consider prophylactic glycopyrrolate for high-dose remifentanil (8 ng/ml)
- Bolus: bradycardia and muscle rigidity
- **Opioid-induced hyperalgesia** and tolerance with high doses (**target above 5 ng/ml** or infusion above 0.2 mcg/kg/min)
- More postoperative shivering, unrelated to intraoperative hypothermia; dose-related, probably from acute opioid tolerance and NMDA receptor stimulation

## Indications

- Intense intraoperative analgesia at high dose, without prolonged respiratory depression afterwards
- When **large swings in stimulation are expected** (fast titration)
- To **avoid postoperative opioid effects**
- To **avoid neuromuscular blockers** for intubation and maintenance
  - Neuromonitoring
  - Myasthenia gravis, other neuromuscular disorders, or wherever relaxants are a disadvantage
- Can also facilitate intubation or nerve blocks

## Pharmacokinetics

- Hydrolysed by **non-specific plasma and tissue esterases**: elimination half-life 3–10 minutes
- **Offset depends on metabolism, not redistribution** (unlike alfentanil and fentanyl)
- Esterases are plentiful, so it does not accumulate: **context-sensitive half-time barely changes** with infusion length
- Poor substrate for plasma cholinesterase: unaffected by cholinesterase deficiency or anticholinesterases
- Main metabolite: a carboxylic acid, essentially inactive (1/4600th the potency), excreted in urine; half-life 2 hours in healthy adults
- Effect not prolonged by liver or renal impairment

## Flashcards

- Q: Remifentanil infusion range and low-dose spontaneous-ventilation range?
  - A: 0.05–2 mcg/kg/min overall; 0.02-0.2 mcg/kg/min with spontaneous ventilation
- Q: Why is remifentanil's offset independent of infusion duration?
  - A: Rapid hydrolysis by non-specific plasma and tissue esterases; context-sensitive half-time about 3-4 min
- Q: Minimum Minto effect-site target in an unparalysed patient?
  - A: Do not go below 2 ng/ml
- Q: What dose thresholds are linked with opioid-induced hyperalgesia?
  - A: Target above 5 ng/ml or infusion above 0.2 mcg/kg/min
- Q: Minto TCI limits in children?
  - A: Weight over 30 kg and age over 12 yr; otherwise manual infusion
