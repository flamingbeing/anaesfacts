# First-generation H1 antihistamines

Sedating H1 antagonists that cross the blood-brain barrier; used for itch, sleep, nausea and vomiting.

## Class features

- Cross the blood-brain barrier
- Generally sedating

## For itch

- Chlorpheniramine
  - PO 5 mg 6 hourly
- Diphenhydramine
  - IV 25–50 mg or 1 mg/kg, slow bolus, usually no faster than 25 mg/min
  - IM 100 mg
  - Maximum 400 mg/day

## For sleep

- Hydroxyzine (Atarax)
  - PO 10 mg at night for sleep, increased to 25 mg three or four times daily, up to 100 mg four times daily
  - Children: 2 mg/kg/day orally in divided doses

## For nausea and vomiting

- Promethazine (Phenergan)
  - Phenothiazine derivative, structurally different from the antipsychotic phenothiazines
  - Common second-line antiemetic, especially for vestibular causes such as motion sickness
  - Non-selective antihistamine; causes drowsiness, so take care where sedation is risky (sleep apnoea, opioids or other respiratory depressants)
  - IM 25 mg 6 to 12 hourly
- Cyclizine
  - Piperazine derivative
  - Injection contains lactic acid, pH 3.2, so IM and IV injection can be painful
  - Uses: motion sickness, radiotherapy, PONV, opioid-induced emesis, Ménière's disease symptoms
  - Mechanism: H1 antagonist; its anticholinergic action may add much to the antiemetic effect
  - Effects
    - Increases lower oesophageal sphincter tone
    - Mild anticholinergic effects; heart rate may rise after IV injection
    - Rarely extrapyramidal effects and sedation
  - Kinetics: well absorbed orally, bioavailability about 75%; little else is known
- Dimenhydrinate
  - PONV: IV 0.5 mg/kg, up to 25 mg
- Cinnarizine
  - Antiemetic for vestibular causes

## Others

- Orphenadrine: analogue of diphenhydramine, used as a muscle relaxant (Anarex)
- Triprolidine: combined with pseudoephedrine for allergies
