# Isoprenaline

Very potent synthetic catecholamine acting on β1 and β2 receptors, with no alpha effect.

## Presentation

- Clear solution, 1 mg/ml, for IV infusion
- Metered dose inhaler, 80 or 400 mcg per dose
- 30 mg tablets (very rarely used)

## Dosage

- IV: 0.5–10 mcg/min

## Mechanism of action

- β1 and β2 agonist; no alpha action

## Effects

- Cardiovascular
  - β1: heart rate, contractility, automaticity and cardiac output increase
  - Blood pressure effect varies
  - β2 vasodilation can lower SVR so much that the rise in cardiac output fails to hold blood pressure
  - Myocardial oxygen delivery may fall a lot: tachycardia shortens diastolic filling and low diastolic pressure cuts coronary perfusion; some coronary vasodilation offsets this
- Respiratory
  - Potent bronchodilator; inhibits lung histamine release; improves mucus flow
  - Increases anatomical dead space and V/Q mismatch, which may cause hypoxaemia
- CNS: stimulant
- Splanchnic: mesenteric and renal blood flow increase
- Metabolic: blood glucose and free fatty acids rise

## Indications

- IV for severe bradycardia from AV block or beta blockers
- No longer used for asthma or reversible airway obstruction
  - It was among the first widely used bronchodilators, but β1 cardiac effects were blamed for increased asthma deaths; β2-selective drugs (e.g. salbutamol) replaced it

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed orally, but extensive first-pass metabolism by hepatic COMT gives low oral bioavailability
- A large fraction is excreted unchanged in urine, with conjugated metabolites
