# Dexmedetomidine

Highly selective full alpha-2 agonist (Precedex) giving rousable sedation and analgesia without respiratory depression; watch for bradycardia and slow waking.

## Presentation

- 200 mcg in 2 ml (100 mcg/ml)
- The active D-stereoisomer of racemic medetomidine

## Administration

- Dilute to 20 ml for 10 mcg/ml
  - Or dilute to 50 ml (4 mcg/ml) to use a conversion table
- Onset slow: 5-10 min, slower still without a loading dose
  - Peak effect 15–30 min after IV loading
- Duration 60-120 min (dose-dependent)
- Switch off 1-2 h before end of surgery to avoid delayed waking
- Licensed for less than 24 hrs only
  - Beyond 24 hours: tolerance and tachyphylaxis
  - Abrupt stop after long use may cause withdrawal, as with clonidine (not specifically studied)
- Only in a monitored setting (risk of hypotension, bradycardia, sinus arrest)

## Dosage

- IV loading: 0.5-1 mcg/kg over 10-20 mins
- Sedation: 0.2-0.7 mcg/kg/hr
- Analgesia: 0.1-0.2 mcg/kg/hr
- Alternative regimen: 1 mcg/kg load over 10 min, then 0.8 mcg/kg/hr
- Intranasal premedication: 1-2 mcg/kg
- Intrathecal and epidural use also described
- Oral route limited by first-pass metabolism (bioavailability 16%)

## Mechanism of action

- Like clonidine but more potent, with 8 times the alpha-2 affinity (alpha-2:alpha-1 ratio 1600:1)
- Full agonist at alpha-2 receptors

## Effects

- Sedation ("cooperative sedation")
  - Patients rouse easily, then drift back to a sleep-like state when left alone
  - Often more cooperative and communicative than with standard ICU sedation
  - Supplements sedation in ventilated critically ill patients; cuts propofol needs
  - Anxiolytic at low doses, anxiogenic at high doses
  - Up to 50% reduction in MAC of volatile agents
- Analgesia (spinally mediated)
  - Acute and chronic pain; long-lasting, no respiratory depression
  - Synergy with opioids; IV use reduces postoperative opioid needs
  - Suppresses opioid withdrawal symptoms
  - Spinal or epidural use gives analgesia without motor or sensory block
- Cardiovascular
  - Initial transient rise in BP, lasting up to 10 minutes
    - Post-synaptic alpha-1 stimulation: vasoconstriction
    - Peripheral alpha-2B stimulation: vasoconstriction
  - Then a longer fall in BP from central sympatholysis
    - PR interval lengthens, AV nodal conduction slows
    - Baroreflex sensitised: lower heart rate for a given rise in BP
    - CO well maintained despite bradycardia
  - Ceiling effect at higher doses, limited by rising alpha-1 stimulation
  - Coronary effects mixed: direct vasoconstriction offset by lower sympathetic tone and local nitric oxide
  - Blunts cardiovascular responses to perioperative stimuli
- Respiratory: no respiratory depression, unlike opioids
- Renal: diuresis (several mechanisms, including reduced ADH release)
- Endocrine
  - Inhibits the stress response to surgery
  - Inhibits insulin release, though blood sugar rarely rises
- Haematological
  - No platelet aggregation at therapeutic doses, despite platelet alpha-2 receptors
  - Blocks adrenaline-induced platelet aggregation
- Antisialogogue

## Adverse effects

- Bradycardia, hypotension, sinus arrest
- Delayed awakening
- Rebound hypertension if stopped suddenly
  - From peripheral vasoconstriction and higher plasma catecholamines
  - Worse with beta-blockade (unopposed vasoconstriction)

## Indications

- ICU and procedural sedation
- Premedication, sedation and anxiolysis before surgery
- Reducing anaesthetic requirements
- Analgesic adjunct (acute and chronic pain)
- Opioid withdrawal
- Hypertension (sympatholysis)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Oral bioavailability only 16% (extensive first-pass metabolism)
- Distribution half-life 6 minutes; terminal elimination half-life 120 minutes
- Context-sensitive half-life: 4 minutes after a 10-minute infusion, 250 minutes after an 8-hour infusion
- Hepatic metabolism to inactive metabolites, excreted renally
  - Consider dose reduction in hepatic impairment
