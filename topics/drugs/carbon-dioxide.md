# Carbon dioxide

Colourless medical gas, pungent at high concentration; used mainly for laparoscopic insufflation.

## Presentation

- Stored as a liquid at 51 bar at 20°C
- Grey cylinders: size C 450 litres up to size E 1800 litres
- Boiling point −78.5°C
- Critical temperature 31°C; critical pressure 73.8 bar

## Effects

- Cardiovascular (via sympathetic stimulation)
  - Heart rate, blood pressure and cardiac output increase
  - Coronary vasodilation
  - Arrhythmias more likely when PaCO2 is high
- Respiratory
  - Raised PaCO2 acts on the respiratory centre and peripheral chemoreceptors: minute volume increases
  - Bronchodilation
  - PaCO2 above 10 kPa can cause respiratory depression
- Central nervous system
  - Cerebral blood flow and intracranial pressure rise with PaCO2
  - Narcosis possible above 10 kPa

## Indications

- Insufflation gas for laparoscopy
- Occasionally to stimulate breathing after general anaesthesia
- Cryotherapy
