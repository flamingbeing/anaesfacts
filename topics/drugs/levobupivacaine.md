# Levobupivacaine

Amide local anaesthetic; the S-enantiomer of (racemic) bupivacaine, with less cardiotoxicity.

## Presentation

- Solutions of 2.5, 5 and 7.5 mg/ml

## Dosage

- Toxic dose 2.5 mg/kg (slightly higher than bupivacaine)
- Maximum single dose 150 mg; maximum over 24 hours 400 mg

## Mechanism of action

- Bupivacaine is a racemic mix of S- and R-enantiomers; levobupivacaine is the S-enantiomer alone
- The R (dextro) enantiomer selectively blocks cardiac Na channels; the S (levo) enantiomer is less potent there, so less cardiac toxicity

## Effects

- Equally potent as bupivacaine
- Onset 10-15 min
- Duration 3-8 hrs
- More vasoconstriction and less motor block than bupivacaine

## Adverse effects

- Potentially less cardiotoxic and neurotoxic than bupivacaine and other local anaesthetics:
  - Myocardial depression (via cardiac K+ channel block) needs a higher dose than with bupivacaine
  - CNS excitation and convulsions appear at lower doses with bupivacaine than with levobupivacaine

## Indications

- Used much as bupivacaine is
- Not licensed for subarachnoid block

## Flashcards

- Q: What is the toxic dose of levobupivacaine?
  - A: 2.5 mg/kg
- Q: What are the maximum single and 24-hour doses?
  - A: 150 mg single dose; 400 mg in 24 hours
- Q: Why is levobupivacaine less cardiotoxic than bupivacaine?
  - A: It is the S-enantiomer, less potent at cardiac Na channels than the R-enantiomer
- Q: Is levobupivacaine licensed for subarachnoid block?
  - A: No
