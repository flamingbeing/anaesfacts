# Neostigmine

Reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (quaternary amine), mainly used to reverse non-depolarising neuromuscular block.

## Presentation
- Tablets, and solution for IV injection
- Also as a fixed-dose combination with glycopyrrolate
- IV: clear colourless solution, 2.5 mg in 1 ml (2.5 mg/ml)

## Administration
- Give IV slowly, together with an anticholinergic
  - Glycopyrrolate matches its time course better than atropine

## Dosage
- IV reversal: 0.05 mg/kg (up to 0.07 mg/kg), i.e. 50 mcg/kg, 5 times the glycopyrrolate dose
  - Usually 2.5 mg (some give 1.25 mg)
  - Do not exceed 0.07 mg/kg (ceiling effect)
  - Onset 1 min, peak 10 min, duration 20–30 min
- Oral (myasthenia gravis, urinary retention): 15–30 mg every 2-4 hours
  - Effects last up to 4 hours

## Mechanism of action
- Forms a carbamylated enzyme complex (reversible inhibition)
  - Its strongly basic carbamyl group binds the anionic site of acetylcholinesterase, then moves to the esteratic site
  - The carbamylated enzyme is hydrolysed far more slowly than acetylcholine (minutes rather than microseconds)
- No effect on phase I block from depolarising agents; antagonises phase II block, but only briefly

## Effects
- Muscarinic
  - Cardiovascular: variable, depends on the background autonomic tone
    - Given alone, causes bradycardia; cardiac arrest reported after high doses
    - Small role in supraventricular tachycardia (prolongs conduction time)
  - Respiratory: more bronchial secretions; may trigger bronchospasm, especially in asthma
  - Gut
    - Increases PONV
    - More salivation
    - More intestinal motility, possibly abdominal cramps; some evidence of more anastomotic breakdown
- Nicotinic
  - Small doses act directly on skeletal muscle, causing contraction
  - High doses may block neuromuscular transmission and cause weakness

## Adverse effects
- Paradoxical weakness, even at therapeutic doses
  - Looks like a phase 1 depolarising block: single-twitch depression without fade
  - Mechanisms: depolarising block, desensitisation block, open-channel block
  - Peaks 5 to 10 min after IV dose
  - Unnecessary doses may be common, as most anaesthetists do not use quantitative neuromuscular monitoring
- Inhibits plasma cholinesterase, so may prolong suxamethonium

## Indications
- Reversal of non-depolarising neuromuscular block (main use)
- Paralytic ileus
- Myasthenia gravis
- Urinary retention

## Pharmacokinetics
- Quaternary ammonium group (N+(CH3)3) makes it lipid insoluble
  - Poor oral absorption, bioavailability 1-2%
  - Does not cross the blood-brain barrier to any real extent
- Minimal protein binding, small volume of distribution
- Elimination half-life 15-80 min
- Mainly metabolised by plasma esterases to a quaternary alcohol; some hepatic metabolism with biliary excretion
- 60% excreted unchanged in urine
  - Renal impairment reduces clearance and prolongs half-life
