# Disopyramide

Class Ia antiarrhythmic used second line for supraventricular and ventricular tachyarrhythmias; marked anticholinergic effects.

## Presentation

- Tablets (including slow release)
- Solution 10 mg/ml

## Dosage

- Oral: up to 800 mg daily in divided doses
- IV: 2 mg/kg over 30 minutes (maximum 150 mg), then infusion 1 mg/kg/h up to 800 mg/day

## Mechanism of action

- Blocks Na+ channels: slower rate of rise of phase 0 of the action potential
- Raises threshold potential
- Lengthens refractory period, so action potential duration increases
- Anticholinergic

## Adverse effects

- Cardiac (rising with plasma level)
  - QT prolongation, sometimes torsades de pointes
  - Contractility falls while ventricular excitability rises; may favour re-entry arrhythmias
  - Rarely cardiac failure and cardiogenic shock
- Anticholinergic: blurred vision, dry mouth, sometimes urinary retention; often poorly tolerated

## Indications

- Second line for SVT and ventricular tachyarrhythmias
- In AF or atrial flutter, control ventricular rate first with beta blockers or verapamil

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed; oral bioavailability 75%
- Partly metabolised (in the kidney, per the note); most excreted unchanged in urine
- Elimination half-life about 5 hours; much longer in renal or cardiac failure
