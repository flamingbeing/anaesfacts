# Rocuronium

Aminosteroid non-depolarising muscle relaxant with rapid onset due to low potency; reversed by anticholinesterases or sugammadex.

## Presentation
- Colourless solution, 50 mg in 5 ml
- Aminosteroid, monoquaternary amine; derived from vecuronium, differing at only four positions

## Administration
- IV, given neat
- Infusion or top-up boluses for maintenance

## Dosage
- ED95: 0.3mg/kg
- Intubating dose: 0.6mg/kg (range 0.6-1.2mg/kg)
    - 0.6 mg/kg: intubating conditions in 100–120 seconds
    - 0.9–1.2 mg/kg: down to about 60 seconds; used instead of suxamethonium for rapid sequence intubation
- Onset 1-2min; satisfactory intubating conditions within 60–90 s (about 1 min after an induction dose)
- Intermediate duration: 30-75min, similar to vecuronium
- Infusion: 5-15mcg/kg/min, or top-up boluses of 5-10mg

## Mechanism of action
- Competitive non-depolarising blocker at the neuromuscular junction
- 6–8 times less potent than vecuronium
- Low potency explains the fast onset: a larger dose means more molecules, a steeper plasma-to-NMJ gradient and faster diffusion

## Effects
- No direct sympathomimetic effect
- Minimal cardiovascular effects, like vecuronium
- Mildly vagolytic at high doses: heart rate may rise during rapid intubation doses

## Adverse effects
- More likely to cause anaphylactoid reactions than pancuronium or vecuronium

## Indications
- Intubation, including rapid sequence intubation in higher doses
- Maintenance of neuromuscular block

## Pharmacokinetics
- More lipophilic than vecuronium: mostly taken up by the liver and excreted unchanged in bile; less in urine
- Mainly hepatic clearance
- Minimal metabolism (<5%); some de-acetylated metabolites
    - 17-desacetylrocuronium, the only plasma metabolite, is 20 times less potent and unlikely to add to block
- Renal failure: half-life increases by 37%, but Miller states single and repeat dose duration is not much changed
- Duration may be prolonged in hepatic and renal failure

## Flashcards
- Q: Rocuronium ED95 and standard intubating dose?
  - A: 0.3mg/kg; 0.6mg/kg
- Q: Dose for rapid sequence intubation and time to intubating conditions?
  - A: 0.9–1.2 mg/kg, about 60 seconds
- Q: Why does rocuronium have a fast onset?
  - A: Low potency: a larger dose gives a steeper concentration gradient to the NMJ
- Q: Rocuronium infusion rate?
  - A: 5-15mcg/kg/min

  - A: Unchanged in bile (hepatic uptake), less in urine
