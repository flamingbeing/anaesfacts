# Epoprostenol

Prostacyclin (PGI2): a natural prostaglandin made by pulmonary endothelium; pulmonary vasodilator and platelet inhibitor.

## Administration

- Continuous IV infusion via an infusion pump, because of its very short half-life

## Dosage

- Anticoagulation of a haemofiltration circuit: 2–12 ng.kg-1.min-1, infused into the extracorporeal circuit

## Mechanism of action

- Platelets: stimulates adenylate cyclase, raising cAMP, which lowers intracellular Ca2+ and inhibits platelet adhesion and aggregation
- May also be fibrinolytic
- Pulmonary vessels: raises cAMP in vascular smooth muscle, relaxing it directly

## Adverse effects

- From vasodilation: hypotension, tachycardia, facial flushing, headache

## Indications

- Pulmonary arterial hypertension (primary PAH): IV infusion lowers pulmonary artery pressure, improves exercise tolerance and prolongs survival
- Keeping the haemofiltration circuit patent

## Pharmacokinetics

- Half-life less than 5 minutes

## Prostacyclin analogues

- Developed because continuous IV epoprostenol is awkward and very costly; analogues are more stable
- Treprostinil: half-life about 4.5 hours
  - Continuous subcutaneous infusion, or inhaled: 4 sessions a day, 9 breaths per session
- Iloprost: half-life about 30 min; stable analogue given by nebuliser 6 to 9 times a day
  - Vasodilator side effects as for prostacyclin, including syncope
  - Cough and bronchoconstriction, as it sensitises airway sensory nerves
