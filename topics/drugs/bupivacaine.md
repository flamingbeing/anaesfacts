# Bupivacaine

Long-acting amide local anaesthetic (Marcaine); racemic, with marked cardiotoxicity.

## Presentation

- 0.25% and 0.5% solutions, plain or with 1:200 000 adrenaline
- Heavy bupivacaine 0.5% for subarachnoid block
  - Contains glucose 80 mg/ml, specific gravity 1.026
- Racemic mixture

## Dosage

- Toxic dose: 2 mg/kg
  - For a 50 kg patient: 20 ml of 0.5%

## Effects

- Onset intermediate to slow, clearly slower than lidocaine
- Duration 3-8 hrs

## Adverse effects

- Cardiotoxicity: binds the sodium channel more tightly than lidocaine, especially in cardiac muscle
  - Dissociation time constant from cardiac sodium channels 10x longer than lidocaine
  - CC/CNS ratio 3 (lidocaine 7)
  - The dextro-enantiomer is the most cardiotoxic (selective cardiac sodium channel block)
  - This led to the levo-enantiomers levobupivacaine and ropivacaine

## Indications

- Still the main drug for labour and postoperative epidural infusions, despite cardiotoxicity concerns and newer agents (ropivacaine, levobupivacaine)
- Subarachnoid block (heavy preparation)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Most highly protein-bound of the amide local anaesthetics
- Hepatic metabolism by dealkylation to pipecolic acid and pipecolylxylidine
