# Anticonvulsants

Diverse drugs for epilepsy, psychiatric illness and neuropathic pain; overview plus phenobarbital, lamotrigine and vigabatrin.

## Drugs in the class
- Carbamazepine, sodium valproate, phenytoin, phenobarbital, lamotrigine, levetiracetam, gabapentin, pregabalin

## Uses
- Epilepsy
- Psychiatric disorders
- Pain
  - Trigeminal neuralgia (carbamazepine)
  - Post-herpetic neuralgia (gabapentin, pregabalin)
  - Diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia (pregabalin)
  - Migraine prophylaxis (divalproex, topiramate)

## Mechanisms
- Very varied: voltage-gated calcium and sodium channels, GABA, glutamate receptors

## Administration
- Use a single agent for epilepsy where possible: avoids interactions, and a second agent rarely helps

## Phenobarbital
- Heavy sedation limits use
- Typically used when benzodiazepines and phenytoin fail to stop status epilepticus
- Among the longest-acting barbiturates
- Hepatic enzyme inducer: interacts with warfarin, oral contraceptives and other anticonvulsants

## Lamotrigine
- Mechanism
  - Unrelated chemically to other anticonvulsants
  - Inactivates voltage-sensitive sodium channels on neurones releasing glutamate and aspartate
  - Has effects beyond classic sodium channel blockers such as phenytoin, so may act in other ways
- Indications
  - Adjunct for generalised and focal epilepsy in adults and children from 2 years
  - Bipolar disorder; mood stabiliser
- Adverse effects
  - Hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, nausea
  - Life-threatening skin reactions
  - Rare neuroleptic malignant syndrome
  - Teratogenicity: among the safest in pregnancy; cardiac and facial defect risk between 2 and 5%

## Vigabatrin
- Dosage: adjunct for complex partial seizures, maximum 1.5 g bd
- Mechanism: irreversibly inhibits GABA-transaminase (which breaks down GABA); acts for 24 hours
- Pharmacokinetics
  - Excreted unchanged in urine
  - Lowers phenytoin levels by up to 25% (mechanism unknown)
  - Elimination half-life 6 hours

## Flashcards
- Q: When is phenobarbital typically used in status epilepticus?
  - A: After benzodiazepines and phenytoin have failed
- Q: Maximum dose of vigabatrin?
  - A: 1.5 g bd
- Q: Vigabatrin effect on phenytoin?
  - A: Lowers its level by up to 25%
- Q: Main serious skin risk of lamotrigine?
  - A: Life-threatening skin reactions
