# Mexiletine

Lidocaine analogue used for ventricular tachyarrhythmias; narrow therapeutic ratio.

## Presentation

- Clear, colourless solution: mexiletine hydrochloride 250 mg in 10 ml
- Oral form, including modified release

## Mechanism of action

- Blocks Na+ channels: slows the rate of rise of phase 0 and raises the threshold potential
- Shortens phase 3 repolarisation, so action potential duration and refractory period fall

## Adverse effects

- Common; low therapeutic ratio
- Cardiac: sinus bradycardia, supraventricular and ventricular tachyarrhythmias
- Gastrointestinal: troublesome nausea and vomiting and change in bowel habit in up to 40%
- Neurological: confusion, diplopia, seizures, tremor, ataxia
- Others reported: thrombocytopenia, rash, jaundice

## Indications

- As for lidocaine, especially arrhythmias linked to ischaemia or digoxin

## Pharmacokinetics

- Oral bioavailability 90%: well absorbed in the upper small bowel, first-pass metabolism only about 10%
- Protein binding 65%
- Volume of distribution 6–13 l/kg
- Hepatic metabolism to several inactive metabolites
- Up to 20% excreted unchanged in urine
