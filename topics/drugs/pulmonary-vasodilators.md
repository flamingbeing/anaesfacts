# Pulmonary Vasodilators

Overview of drug classes that lower pulmonary artery pressure, and why few work well.

## Why so few work
- Most pulmonary vascular receptors are not lung-specific, so drugs act everywhere and side effects are unacceptable
- In lung disease, abolishing hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (HPV) removes the main compensation for poor gas exchange
  - Example: sublingual nifedipine in severe airways disease lowers pulmonary pressure but worsens hypoxaemia
- Inhaled delivery helps with both problems, especially if the drug is inactivated before reaching the systemic circulation

## Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO)
- Selective pulmonary vasodilator in severe lung disease; no systemic effect because haemoglobin inactivates it quickly
- Raises flow to well-ventilated lung and away from poorly ventilated lung, so V/Q mismatch improves and oxygenation rises
- With oxygen it oxidises to NO2; rate depends on oxygen concentration and on NO concentration squared
  - NO2 forms nitric and nitrous acids with water, causing severe pneumonitis and pulmonary oedema
  - Keep oxygen and NO concentrations, and their contact time, as low as possible
- Benefit may be short-lived
- Abrupt stop causes rebound (probably suppressed endogenous NO): oxygenation falls and PA pressure rises; wean in slow steps
- In some acute lung injury patients it improves oxygenation but not survival

## Prostacyclin (PGI2) and analogues
- IV prostacyclin is used for pulmonary hypertension and to lower PA pressure in critical illness, but is not lung-selective, so adverse effects are significant
- Inhaled: the lung barely metabolises it and some is absorbed systemically, but the dose needed is tiny, so significant side effects are minimal

## Phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors
- Block breakdown of cAMP and cGMP, boosting pulmonary artery smooth muscle relaxation via several pathways, including all NO-mediated ones
- Used IV and inhaled to lower pulmonary pressure
- Type 5 PDE selective inhibitors (e.g. sildenafil, tadalafil) are most relevant: type 5 mainly breaks down pulmonary cGMP

## ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers
- ACE inhibitors lower PVR in pulmonary hypertension due to lung disease, but only with long-term use; may also reduce vascular remodelling
- Losartan (angiotensin II receptor antagonist) lowers PA pressure within hours without worsening oxygen saturation

## Calcium channel blockers
- Nifedipine and others lower secondary pulmonary hypertension dose-dependently by blocking L-type calcium channels in pulmonary artery smooth muscle
- Abolishing HPV can worsen hypoxaemia in some patients
- The high doses often needed are negatively inotropic and can worsen right heart failure

## Endothelin receptor antagonists
- Competitive antagonists at ETA and ETB receptors; clinically ETB effects seem to dominate and PA pressure falls
- Endothelin contributes to pulmonary vascular remodelling in chronic hypoxia, which these drugs may slow
- Bosentan, ambrisentan and macitentan in current use; macitentan (longer-acting) has been licensed for pulmonary arterial hypertension since 2013

## Flashcards
- Q: Why is inhaled nitric oxide selective for the pulmonary circulation?
  - A: Haemoglobin inactivates it rapidly, so it does not reach the systemic circulation
- Q: Why must iNO be weaned slowly?
  - A: Abrupt withdrawal causes rebound pulmonary hypertension and falling oxygenation
- Q: What toxic product forms from NO and oxygen?
  - A: NO2, which forms nitric and nitrous acids causing pneumonitis and pulmonary oedema
- Q: Which PDE type is targeted for pulmonary vasodilation?
  - A: Type 5 (pulmonary cGMP breakdown)
