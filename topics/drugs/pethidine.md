# Pethidine

Synthetic phenylpiperidine opioid with anticholinergic and anti-shivering effects; its metabolite norpethidine is neurotoxic.

## Presentation
- Tablets
- Injection 10–50 mg/ ml; commonly 50mg in 1ml
    - Can be diluted to 5ml (10mg/ml)

## Administration
- IV, IM or oral; titrate to effect as with all opioids

## Dosage
- IV or IM 0.5–1.0 mg/kg, repeated 2–3 hourly
- Equivalence: 1mg morphine = 10mg pethidine
- Recovery: 20mg IV boluses for postoperative pain
- Onset 2min, peak 5-7min, duration 2-3hr (120–150 minutes)

## Mechanism of action
- Opioid agonist; originally designed as an anticholinergic, later found to be analgesic
- Anti-shivering action mainly from lowering the shivering threshold, probably via kappa receptors

## Effects
- Shares the usual opioid effects of morphine, with these differences
- Anticholinergic: less miosis (possibly mydriasis), dry mouth, sometimes tachycardia (structure resembles atropine)
- Possibly less biliary spasm than morphine, of unclear clinical importance
- Uniquely effective among opioids at stopping or reducing shivering; alfentanil, morphine and fentanyl work less well for postoperative shivering
- Histamine release

## Adverse effects
- Renal failure: norpethidine accumulates, causing oversedation, hallucinations and grand mal seizures, not reversed by naloxone
- Serious interaction with MAOIs (contraindicated with type A MAOIs)
    - Mechanism: pethidine blocks serotonin reuptake while the MAOI reduces amine breakdown, giving central serotonergic overactivity
    - Coma, labile circulation, convulsions, hyperpyrexia
    - The note states other opioids are safe with MAOIs

## Contraindications
- Patients on type A MAO inhibitors

## Indications
- Postoperative shivering
- Postoperative pain
- Labour analgesia

## Pharmacokinetics
- More lipid-soluble than morphine, so faster onset
- Oral bioavailability 50%
- Liver metabolism
    - Ester hydrolysis to inactive pethidinic acid
    - N-demethylation to norpethidine, with half the analgesic activity of pethidine
- Norpethidine elimination half-life 14–21 hours, longer than pethidine; accumulates in renal failure
- Urine excretion of norpethidine, pethidinic acid and a little unchanged pethidine
- Labour
    - High lipid solubility: large amounts cross the placenta
    - Less lipid-soluble norpethidine builds up in the fetus, peaking about 4 hours after the maternal IM dose
    - Slower fetal clearance prolongs the half-lives of pethidine and norpethidine by a factor of three
