# NSAIDs in children

Ibuprofen and diclofenac are the usual perioperative NSAIDs in children; often used off licence, avoided in neonates.

## Presentation
- Ibuprofen: oral
- Diclofenac sodium: oral, rectal suppository, IV

## Dosage
- Diclofenac
  - Oral 1 mg/kg 8-hourly (maximum 50 mg)
  - Rectal 1-2 mg/kg (maximum 100 mg); usually 1mg/kg suppository
  - IV 1 mg/kg
  - Maximum by all routes 3 mg/kg/day or 150 mg/day
- Ibuprofen
  - Oral 5-10 mg/kg 6-hourly (usually 10mg/kg three times daily)
  - Maximum 400 mg single dose or 40 mg/kg/day
  - Often given before surgery as analgesic premedication

## Effects
- Probably more potent analgesics than paracetamol

## Adverse effects
- Very few with short-term perioperative use in healthy children

## Contraindications
- Broadly as in adults
- Aspirin: not used under 12 years of age (Reye's syndrome)
- Neonates: not used for analgesia, due to effects on renal prostaglandins and immature kidney perfusion
- Licensing (often used off licence)
  - Ibuprofen: not licensed below 5 kg or 3 months; minimum age 6 months also quoted
  - Diclofenac: not licensed below 1 year; suppositories not licensed under 6 years

## Indications
- Perioperative analgesia; ibuprofen and diclofenac sodium are commonest

## Flashcards
- Q: Oral ibuprofen dose in children and maximum?
  - A: 5-10 mg/kg 6-hourly; max 400 mg per dose or 40 mg/kg/day
- Q: Maximum daily diclofenac in children?
  - A: 3 mg/kg/day or 150 mg/day by all routes
- Q: Why are NSAIDs avoided in neonates?
  - A: Effects on renal prostaglandins and immature kidney perfusion
- Q: Below what age is aspirin avoided, and why?
  - A: 12 years, because of Reye's syndrome
