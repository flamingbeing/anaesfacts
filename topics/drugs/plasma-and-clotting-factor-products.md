# Plasma Products and Clotting Factor Concentrates

FFP, cryoprecipitate, fibrinogen concentrate, PCC and single-factor products: contents, indications and doses.

## General

- AB+ is the universal plasma donor

## Fresh frozen plasma (FFP)

- Presentation: 250ml bags
- Dose: 15 ml/kg
- Indications
  - Loss of several clotting factors (e.g. sepsis, DIC, dilution, TTP/HUS, liver disease)
  - PT/PTT above 1.5x normal, INR above 1.5
  - Balanced transfusion in major haemorrhage (e.g. after 4-6 units of packed red cells)
  - Emergency warfarin reversal in life-threatening bleeding

## Cryoprecipitate

- Made by thawing and centrifuging FFP and keeping the precipitate
- Contains fibrinogen, plus vWF, factor VIII and factor XIII
- Indications
  - Fibrinogen deficiency (fibrinogen below 1.0) in major haemorrhage, trauma, DIC
  - vWF, factor VIII or factor XIII deficiency, but not for haemophilia or vWD unless no alternative exists
- Presentation: 1 unit = 20-40ml; supplied as 5U bags
- Dose
  - Usually 1 unit/10kg; typical adult dose 10 units (more after fibrinolytic therapy)
  - One unit per 10 kg should raise fibrinogen by 0.5–1.0 g/L
  - 10U contains about 2g fibrinogen

## Fibrinogen concentrate

- Purified fibrinogen
- Dose: 30-50mg/kg
  - Dose to reach a target rise in FIBTEM A5: target increase x body weight / 160
  - A single 70 mg/kg dose raises plasma fibrinogen by about 125 mg/dL
- Paediatric cardiac: 20mg/ml

## Prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC)

- Lyophilised concentrate of vitamin K-dependent factors II, VII, IX and X from large pooled plasma
- Two forms: four-factor (4F-PCC) and three-factor (3F-PCC)
  - 4F-PCC has more factor VII than 3F-PCC, plus anticoagulant proteins (protein C, protein S, antithrombin, heparin)
- Advantages over FFP: smaller volume, faster to give
- Indications
  - Reversal of warfarin or vitamin K deficiency when bleeding or needing urgent surgery (within 6 h)
  - Vitamin K takes more than 6 hours to correct the INR
- Dose
  - Emergency warfarin reversal: usually 25-50 units/kg, or 1500-2000U; check INR 15 minutes after the infusion ends
  - Bleeding: 15-30U/kg
  - Paediatric cardiac: 10U/kg
- Complication: thrombosis

## Factor VIII concentrate

- Factor VIII deficiency (haemophilia A)

## Factor IX concentrate

- Factor IX deficiency (haemophilia B)

## Humate-P

- Purified human factor VIII with von Willebrand factor
- Indications: von Willebrand disease, haemophilia A

## Recombinant activated factor VII (rFVIIa)

- Licensed for haemophilia A or B with inhibitors
- Used off label to control bleeding in many settings
- Already activated (unlike most factor products, which are inactive precursors), so higher thrombosis risk
- Expensive

## Flashcards

- Q: Dose of FFP?
  - A: 15 ml/kg
- Q: Expected rise in fibrinogen from cryoprecipitate 1 unit per 10 kg?
  - A: 0.5–1.0 g/L (10U contains about 2g fibrinogen)
- Q: PCC dose for emergency warfarin reversal?
  - A: 25-50 units/kg or 1500-2000U; check INR 15 minutes after
- Q: Factors in PCC?
  - A: Vitamin K-dependent factors II, VII, IX and X
- Q: Main complication of rFVIIa?
  - A: Thrombosis (it is already activated)
