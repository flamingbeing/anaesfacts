# Dopamine

Endogenous catecholamine acting on dopamine, β and α receptors in a dose-dependent way.

## Presentation

- Clear solution: 200 or 800 mg in 5 ml water, with sodium metabisulfite
  - Commonly 200 mg in 5 ml

## Administration

- IV only, preferably via a central vein
- Dilute 200 mg in 50 ml NS (4 mg/ml)
- Onset within 5 minutes; duration 10 minutes
- Needs adequate preload, as with other inotropes, to help limit tachycardia

## Dosage

- Infusion 2-20 mcg/kg/min

## Mechanism of action

- Acts on α, β and dopamine receptors depending on dose
- D1 (Gs) and D2 (Gi) receptors act via adenylate cyclase, raising or lowering cAMP
- In some brain cells and autonomic ganglion interneurones, dopamine is not turned into noradrenaline and is released as a neurotransmitter itself

## Effects

- Cardiovascular effects depend on infusion rate and vary between patients:
  - Low dose (below 3 mcg/kg/min): dopamine receptors in renal and splanchnic beds
    - Renal blood flow increases; urine output may rise
    - Direct action on renal tubular cells increases natriuresis and urine output, independent of renal blood flow
    - Raises urine output but is not really renoprotective
  - Moderate dose (3-10 mcg/kg/min): β effects on heart and periphery
    - Contractility and heart rate increase, with peripheral vasodilatation
  - High dose (above 10 mcg/kg/min): dose-dependent α activation in systemic and pulmonary vessels
    - Progressive systemic and pulmonary vasoconstriction; PVR increases
    - Higher afterload can blunt the stroke volume gain seen at lower doses
- Less arrhythmogenic than adrenaline
- Splanchnic: D1-mediated mesenteric vasodilatation; any rise in urine output may be wholly from blocked proximal tubule Na+ reabsorption plus better CO and BP
- CNS: does not cross the blood-brain barrier
- Gut: nausea and vomiting (chemoreceptor trigger zone); gastric transit time increases

## Adverse effects

- Sinus tachycardia and AF in 25% of patients on infusions
- Extravasation can cause tissue necrosis
- Interaction: effects can be much greater and longer during MAOI therapy, even though it is direct acting

## Indications

- Cardiogenic and septic shock, though other options are favoured (mechanical assist devices for cardiogenic shock, noradrenaline for septic shock)
  - Useful where renal protection matters, e.g. kidney transplants
- To increase urine output; low-dose dopamine is NOT recommended as treatment for acute renal failure

## Pharmacokinetics

- Metabolised by MAO and COMT in liver, kidney and plasma to inactive compounds (3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid and homovanillic acid, HVA), excreted in urine as sulfate and glucuronide conjugates
- About 25% of a dose becomes noradrenaline in sympathetic nerve terminals
- Half-life about 3 minutes
