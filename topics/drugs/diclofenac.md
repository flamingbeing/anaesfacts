# Diclofenac

Phenylacetic acid NSAID for mild to moderate pain and opioid sparing; carries a cardiovascular risk similar to COX-2 inhibitors.

## Presentation

- Oral, rectal and parenteral forms
- Suppositories 50mg, also 12.5mg and 25mg
- Parenteral form is highly irritant

## Administration

- IM injection may be very painful and damages muscle
- IV injection causes local thrombosis
- Suppositories not licensed in children under 6 years

## Dosage

- Adult maximum by all routes: 150 mg/d in divided doses
- Oral: 25-50mg tds
- IM: 75mg stat, at most bd
- Paediatric maximum by all routes: 3 mg/kg/day
  - Oral: 1 mg/kg TDS (maximum 50mg) for pain after minor surgery (tonsillectomy, inguinal herniotomy)

## Adverse effects

- Cardiovascular: raised coronary event risk with chronic use, similar to COX-2 inhibitors; not a first-line agent
- Renal: may cause renal impairment, especially after major surgery
- Gut: less gastric irritation than indomethacin or aspirin
- Injection pain and tissue damage (see Administration)
- Interactions
  - Raises lithium and digoxin levels
  - Usually no effect on oral anticoagulants or oral hypoglycaemics; isolated reports support close monitoring

## Indications

- Mild to moderate postoperative pain, alone
- Opioid sparing in severe pain
- Especially useful for renal colic
- Side effects limit use in major surgery

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed orally
- Highly protein-bound (99%); small volume of distribution (0.15 l/kg)
- Hepatic hydroxylation and conjugation to inactive metabolites
- Excreted in urine (60%) and bile (40%)
- Elimination half-life (stated in the note for aspirin): 15–30 min with first-order kinetics; much longer when enzymes saturate and kinetics become zero order
