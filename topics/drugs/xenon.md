# Xenon

Inert noble gas anaesthetic with very low blood solubility, fast onset and offset, analgesic, but very expensive.

## Presentation
- Inert, odourless gas; 0.0000087% of the atmosphere
- No occupational or environmental hazards
- Made by fractional distillation of air, at about 2000 times the cost of N2O

## Dosage
- MAC 71%

## Effects
- Cardiovascular
  - No change in myocardial contractility
  - May slightly lower heart rate
- Respiratory
  - Unlike other inhaled agents, slows respiratory rate while tidal volume rises, so minute volume is unchanged
  - Denser (×3) and more viscous (×1.5) than N2O; could raise airway resistance at high concentrations, though probably of little clinical importance
  - No diffusion hypoxia like N2O despite high concentrations
- Central nervous system
  - Used to enhance brain CT and to measure cerebral blood flow
  - Raises cerebral blood flow variably in humans; not recommended for neuroanaesthesia
- Significant analgesia

## Pharmacokinetics
- Blood:gas partition coefficient very low (0.14): onset and offset faster than desflurane and N2O
- Not metabolised; eliminated by the lungs

## Flashcards
- Q: MAC of xenon?
  - A: 71%
- Q: Blood:gas partition coefficient of xenon?
  - A: 0.14, giving faster onset and offset than desflurane and N2O
- Q: Effect of xenon on breathing pattern?
  - A: Rate falls, tidal volume rises, minute volume unchanged
- Q: Why is xenon not recommended for neurosurgery?
  - A: It raises cerebral blood flow variably
