# Entonox

Premixed 50% oxygen and 50% nitrous oxide for inhaled analgesia; separates if cooled below its pseudocritical temperature.

## Presentation

- Compressed gas: 50% oxygen and 50% nitrous oxide by volume
- French blue cylinders with white and blue checked shoulders, at 137 bar
  - G = 3200 litres; J = 6400 litres
- Large cylinders have a dip tube ending in the liquid phase, so liquid is used first and oxygen below 20% is not delivered

## Administration

- Two-stage pressure demand regulator fitted to the cylinder
- Gas flows only while the patient inhales through mask or mouthpiece; stops at the end of each breath
- Storage before use
  - Horizontal for about 24 hours at 5°C or above (larger diffusion area)
  - Can be used sooner than 24 hours if well mixed by repeated inversion
  - Manufacturer standard: store at 10°C for 24 hours, then invert three times so the mixture is uniform
- Scavenging needed to protect staff
- Avoid prolonged use (nitrous oxide marrow effects), especially in the critically ill

## Effects

- Analgesia

## Adverse effects

- Separation (liquefaction) below the pseudocritical temperature of -7°C (Poynting effect)
  - Most likely at 117 bar; higher or lower pressure makes it less likely
  - In pipelines at 4.1 bar the pseudocritical temperature is below –30°C
  - Gives a liquid of mostly nitrous oxide with about 20% oxygen dissolved, under a gas phase rich in oxygen
- Using a separated cylinder
  - 1) First the gas is mostly O2: little analgesia
  - 2) As the cylinder empties the liquid boils off: N2O concentration rises, the mixture gets stronger and becomes hypoxic (below 20% oxygen near empty)
- Rewarming and mixing cylinder and contents reverses separation
- Bone marrow effects of nitrous oxide with prolonged use

## Indications

- Analgesia in the emergency department, labour ward and pre-hospital care

## Flashcards

- Q: Pseudocritical temperature of Entonox in a cylinder?
  - A: -7°C (below –30°C at pipeline pressure of 4.1 bar)
- Q: Entonox cylinder colour and pressure?
  - A: French blue with white and blue checked shoulders at 137 bar
- Q: Danger of using a separated Entonox cylinder?
  - A: Little analgesia at first, then a hypoxic N2O-rich mixture below 20% oxygen as it empties
- Q: Storage before use?
  - A: 10°C for 24 hours then invert three times
