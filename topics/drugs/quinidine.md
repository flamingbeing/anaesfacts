# Quinidine

Class Ia sodium channel blocker, now little used because better-tolerated drugs exist; side effects are frequent.

## Mechanism of action

- Class Ia: blocks Na+ channels, slowing the upstroke (phase 0) of the action potential
- Raises threshold potential
- Lengthens the refractory period without changing action potential duration
- Vagolytic (antagonises vagal tone)

## Effects

- ECG: longer PR interval, wider QRS, longer QT interval

## Adverse effects

- Common; intolerable in up to 30% of patients
- Cardiac
  - New arrhythmias: heart block, sinus tachycardia (vagolytic), ventricular arrhythmias
  - In AF or flutter, AV conduction may speed up until the ventricular rate matches the atrial rate: first slow AV conduction with a beta-blocker, Ca2+ channel blocker or digoxin
  - Hypotension from alpha-blockade or direct myocardial depression; worse with hyperkalaemia
- Non-cardiac
  - **Cinchonism** (CNS toxicity): tinnitus, blurred vision, impaired hearing, headache, confusion
- Interactions
  - Displaces digoxin from binding sites: digoxin levels increase
  - Phenytoin lowers quinidine levels (enzyme induction)
  - Cimetidine raises quinidine levels (enzyme inhibition)
  - Potentiates both depolarising and non-depolarising muscle relaxants

## Indications

- SVT, including atrial fibrillation and flutter
- Ventricular ectopic beats

## Pharmacokinetics

- Well absorbed; oral bioavailability about 75%
- About 90% protein bound
- Hepatic metabolism to active metabolites, mostly excreted in urine
- Elimination half-life 5–9 hours

## Flashcards

- Q: What is cinchonism?
  - A: Quinidine CNS toxicity: tinnitus, blurred vision, impaired hearing, headache, confusion
- Q: Why pretreat with an AV nodal blocker before quinidine for AF or flutter?
  - A: Vagolytic action can speed AV conduction so the ventricular rate approaches the atrial rate
- Q: Effect of quinidine on digoxin and on muscle relaxants?
  - A: Raises digoxin levels (displacement) and potentiates both depolarising and non-depolarising relaxants
- Q: Oral bioavailability and half-life of quinidine?
  - A: About 75%; 5–9 hours
