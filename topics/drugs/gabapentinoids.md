# Gabapentinoids

Gabapentin and pregabalin: anticonvulsants acting on the alpha-2-delta-1 (α2δ-1) subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels, used for neuropathic pain and as opioid-sparing adjuncts.

## Gabapentin

### Administration
- Start low (100 mg to 300 mg total per day) and increase by 100–300 mg every 1–3 days to effect
  - Example: 300 mg PO on day one, 300 mg PO twice a day on day two, then 300 mg PO three times a day on day three
- Give the first dose in the evening
- Effect usually seen in the first week; sometimes a month before clear improvement
- Stop by tapering over more than seven days: abrupt withdrawal can cause withdrawal symptoms or seizures
- Titrate up and down at the start and end of treatment

### Dosage
- Maximum for postherpetic neuralgia: 1800 mg per day (600 mg TDS)
- Maximum for epilepsy: 3600 mg PO per day in 3 divided doses (e.g. 1200 mg TDS)
- Focal seizures: 900–1200 mg/day
- Neuropathic pain: 1800 mg/day
- Single preoperative oral dose for postoperative pain: 300–1200 mg
- Children
  - Start at 5 mg/kg/dose PO
  - Titrate up over 3–7 days
  - Maintenance 15–30 mg/kg/dose
- Renal impairment
  - CrCl greater than 60 ml/min: 300 to 1200 mg PO three times a day
  - CrCl 15 to 59 ml/min: 300 mg at night
  - CrCl 15 ml/min: 300 mg on alternate days
  - CrCl less than 15 ml/min: reduce the daily dose in proportion to CrCl, based on the dose for CrCl 15 ml/min

### Mechanism of action
- Binds the presynaptic α2δ-1 subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels, reducing excitatory neurotransmitter release and neuronal hyperexcitability
- May have some NMDA antagonist activity
- Structurally like GABA but does not bind GABA receptors or alter GABA synthesis or uptake

### Effects
- Opioid sparing: lower opioid requirement and fewer opioid side effects
- Given before surgery, may prevent central sensitisation and later hyperalgesia or allodynia, with little effect on normal nociception
- Possibly anxiolytic (uncertain)

### Adverse effects
- Generally mild
- Drowsiness or somnolence is the most common, mostly during initial titration
- Dizziness, ataxia, nausea, headache
- Psychiatric effects such as depression
- Associated with viral infections

### Indications
- Mainly an anticonvulsant: focal seizures
- Neuropathic pain (e.g. postherpetic neuralgia, diabetic peripheral neuropathy); often used off-label for other pain
- Adjuvant analgesic to reduce postoperative pain

### Pharmacokinetics
- Oral bioavailability about 60%, falling as the dose rises
- Not protein bound and not metabolised, which makes combining it with other anticonvulsants easier
- Elimination half-life 6 hours
- Excreted only by the kidneys, with no active metabolites; reduce the dose in renal impairment
- Crosses the blood–brain barrier readily

## Pregabalin

- Designed as the successor to gabapentin
- More potent: roughly a 1:6 ratio

### Mechanism of action
- Binds the α2δ-1 subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels in cortical neurones, like gabapentin

### Adverse effects
- Dizziness and drowsiness are common
- Also weight gain, confusion, ataxia, GI upset, erectile dysfunction

### Indications
- Adjunct for partial seizures in adults
- Anxiety

### Pharmacokinetics
- Rapid absorption; oral bioavailability 90%, not dose dependent; absorption delayed by food
- Not protein bound and not metabolised; excreted in urine
