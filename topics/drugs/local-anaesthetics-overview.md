# Local Anaesthetics Overview

Local anaesthetics split into two chemical groups: amides and esters.

## Amides
- Memory aid: most amides have an "i" before "caine"
- Lignocaine (lidocaine, Xylocaine)
- Bupivacaine
- Levobupivacaine
- Ropivacaine
- Prilocaine

## Esters
- Cocaine
- Chloroprocaine
- Procaine
- Amethocaine (tetracaine, Ametop)
- Benzocaine

## Related areas
- General properties: potency, onset and duration; side effects
- Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)
- Topical skin anaesthesia (EMLA cream, Ametop, ethyl chloride)
- Local anaesthetics in children
- Analgesic adjuncts

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the memory aid for telling an amide from an ester local anaesthetic?
  - A: Most amides have an "i" before "caine" (e.g. lignocaine, bupivacaine).
- Q: Is chloroprocaine an amide or an ester?
  - A: An ester.
- Q: Name the ester in Ametop.
  - A: Amethocaine (tetracaine).
