# Cocaine

Ester local anaesthetic used topically for anaesthesia and vasoconstriction, notably in the nose.

## Presentation
- From the leaves of Erythroxylon coca, native to Bolivia and Peru
- Paste from 1% to 4%
- Moffat's solution: 2 ml 8% cocaine, 2 ml 1% sodium bicarbonate, 1 ml 1:1000 adrenaline
  - Used in the nasal cavities; less popular now because of side effects

## Dosage
- Maximum 1.5 mg/kg or 100 mg

## Mechanism of action
- Blocks uptake 1 and MAO
- Also stimulates the CNS

## Effects
- Combined actions make hypertension and arrhythmias more likely
- Causes hyperthermia

## Adverse effects
- High doses: confusion, hallucinations, seizures, arrhythmias, cardiac rupture

## Indications
- Topical anaesthesia
- Local vasoconstriction

## Pharmacokinetics
- Well absorbed across mucous membranes
- About 95% protein bound
- Unlike other esters, significant hepatic hydrolysis to inactive products, excreted in urine

## History
- 1884: first used for eye anaesthesia; the experimenter applied it to his own eye and pricked it with pins
- 1885: nerve-block and peridural anaesthesia first demonstrated with cocaine
- 1898: first used for spinal anaesthesia
