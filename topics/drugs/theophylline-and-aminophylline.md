# Theophylline and Aminophylline

Methylxanthine bronchodilators with a narrow therapeutic range; less used now because of side effects and modest efficacy, but still useful in severe asthma and COPD.

## Presentation
- Aminophylline: ethylenediamine salt of theophylline (80% theophylline, 20% ethylenediamine); ethylenediamine has no therapeutic effect but improves solubility at neutral pH
- Aminophylline injection 25mg/ml
- Aminophylline tablets, usually slow release (half-life about 6 hours); slow-release aminophylline and theophylline are equally effective
- Theophylline (Neulin) tablets, standard and slow release (SR)

## Dosage
- Acute severe asthma, IV aminophylline
  - Loading dose 6 mg/kg over 20 minutes
  - Then infusion 0.5 mg/kg/h (or 25mg/h)
  - Halve both doses if already taking theophylline or clearance is reduced, and check levels more often
- Oral aminophylline for asthma: 450 to 1250 mg daily
- Theophylline PO 125mg tds-qds; SR PO 125mg bd
- Target plasma concentration 10–20 mcg/ml
  - Low doses aiming for 5-10 mg/L largely avoid side effects and interactions

## Mechanism of action
- Not fully understood; proposed mechanisms
  - Non-selective inhibition of all five phosphodiesterase isoenzymes, so cAMP (and possibly cGMP) rises
  - Adenosine receptor antagonism at therapeutic levels
    - Adenosine bronchoconstricts asthmatic airways via histamine and leukotriene release
    - A1 receptor antagonism may cause the serious effects: arrhythmias and seizures
  - Direct noradrenaline release from sympathetic nerves; synergy with catecholamines that raise cAMP via adrenoceptors

## Effects
- Respiratory
  - Bronchodilation; works well with β2 agonists because cAMP is raised by a different route
  - Improves diaphragm contractility
  - Increases respiratory centre sensitivity to CO2
  - Growing evidence of anti-inflammatory effects in asthma
- Cardiovascular
  - Mild positive inotropy and chronotropy; some coronary and peripheral vasodilation
  - Lowers the arrhythmia threshold (especially ventricular), particularly with halothane
- CNS: the alkyl group at the 1-position (shared with caffeine) gives CNS stimulation and a lower seizure threshold
- Renal: the same 1-position alkyl group gives a weak diuretic effect; less tubular Na+ reabsorption causes natriuresis and possible hypokalaemia

## Adverse effects
- Side effects track plasma level and tend to start above Cp > 15 mg/L
- Toxicity: above 35 mcg/ml hepatic enzymes saturate and kinetics switch from first to zero order
  - Cardiac: tachyarrhythmias up to VF
  - CNS: tremor, insomnia, seizures (especially after rapid IV injection)
  - Nausea and vomiting
  - Rhabdomyolysis

## Indications
- Severe asthma and COPD
- Acute asthma: IV aminophylline is less effective than nebulised β2 agonists, so keep it for patients not responding to or intolerant of β agonists
- Chronic asthma: adding low-dose theophylline to low- or high-dose inhaled steroid controls symptoms and lung function better than doubling the steroid; less effective than β2 agonists and steroids, though a few patients benefit unexpectedly
- COPD: inhaled anticholinergics and β2 agonists preferred; theophylline added in more severe disease, with extra benefit on top of a long-acting β2 agonist
- Central apnoea in premature neonates (fewer episodes)
- Very occasionally, heart failure

## Pharmacokinetics
- Theophylline absorbed rapidly and completely; aminophylline oral bioavailability >90%
- About 50% protein bound
- Hepatic metabolism by cytochrome P450 (mainly CYP1A2) to inactive metabolites
  - Large variation between patients, so levels may need measuring
  - Low hepatic extraction ratio: metabolism does not depend on liver blood flow
- About 10% excreted unchanged in urine
- Interactions
  - Smoking increases clearance
  - Enzyme inhibitors (cimetidine, erythromycin, ciprofloxacin, oral contraceptives) slow elimination: reduce the dose
  - Fluvoxamine (an SSRI): avoid, levels can rise sharply
  - Enzyme inducers (phenytoin, carbamazepine, barbiturates, rifampicin) increase clearance: dose may need increasing
