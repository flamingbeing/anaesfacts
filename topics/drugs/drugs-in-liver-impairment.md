# Drugs in liver impairment

How cirrhosis and liver failure change drug handling, and which anaesthetic, analgesic and antimicrobial drugs to choose or adjust.

## Summary

- Adjust the dose of almost everything
- Paracetamol <2g/day; no NSAIDs
- Prefer short-acting opioids

## Hepatic clearance

- Total hepatic clearance is described by Rowland's "well-stirred" model
- Depends on:
  - How well blood is delivered to the liver
  - How well the liver removes drug from blood: the hepatic extraction ratio (ER), the fraction removed in one pass

## Drugs by hepatic extraction ratio

- High ER (>0.7): flow-limited clearance, set by hepatic blood flow
  - Examples: propofol, ketamine, fentanyl, morphine, lidocaine
  - Clearance changes with cardiac output and hepatic blood flow (e.g. shock)
  - Enzyme induction or inhibition makes little difference
  - High first-pass metabolism, so low oral bioavailability; sensitive to portosystemic shunting
- Intermediate ER
- Low ER (<0.3): capacity-limited clearance, set by enzyme activity and free fraction
  - Examples: rocuronium, diazepam, phenytoin
  - Sensitive to enzyme induction and inhibition
  - Sensitive to protein binding changes ("capacity-limited, binding-sensitive" drugs)
  - Clearance does not depend on blood flow
  - Low first-pass metabolism, so high oral bioavailability

## Changes in cirrhosis

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: portosystemic shunting reduces first-pass metabolism
- Distribution:
  - Larger Vd for hydrophilic drugs (ascites, oedema), e.g. neuromuscular blockers, so higher loading doses
  - Higher free fraction from low albumin and displacement by bilirubin
- Metabolism:
  - Less hepatic blood flow
  - Lower CYP450 (phase I) activity; glucuronidation (phase II) largely preserved until end-stage
  - Less biliary excretion
  - Low plasma (pseudo)cholinesterase, so suxamethonium lasts longer
- Excretion: hepatorenal syndrome lowers renal clearance

### Pharmacodynamics
- CNS: blood-brain barrier more permeable; more sensitive to GABA-acting drugs (benzodiazepines, induction agents), which can lead to encephalopathy

## Anaesthetic drugs

- Sedatives:
  - Propofol: clearance slightly reduced
  - Midazolam: clearance much reduced; adjust dose
  - Dexmedetomidine: clearance much reduced; adjust dose
- Neuromuscular blockers:
  - Atracurium and cisatracurium: drugs of choice (Hofmann elimination, ester hydrolysis)
  - Rocuronium and vecuronium: last longer (less hepatic uptake and biliary excretion)
  - Suxamethonium: slightly longer, rarely relevant unless enzyme levels < 25%
- Reversal:
  - Neostigmine: longer elimination half-life (similar to rocuronium in cirrhosis)
  - Sugammadex: effect unchanged by liver failure; safe and reliable
- Local anaesthetics: amides (lidocaine, bupivacaine) are metabolised in the liver; clearance much reduced

## Analgesics

- Paracetamol: safe at low doses
  - FDA guidance: short-term (<14 days) and low dose (max 2g/day, e.g. 500mg QDS)
  - Glutathione depletion or accidental overdose can quickly cause severe liver injury, especially in chronic alcohol use
- NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors: contraindicated (variceal bleeding, renal impairment)
- Opioids:
  - Remifentanil: ideal (organ-independent)
  - Fentanyl: fine as boluses but clearance reduced in cirrhosis; highly protein bound, so reduce dose if albumin is low
  - Morphine: reduced clearance; use with caution
  - Oxycodone: reduced clearance; use with caution
  - Codeine: prodrug activated to morphine by CYP2D6, which is reduced in liver disease, so poor effect and accumulation; avoid
  - Pethidine: prolonged half-life; avoid
  - Tramadol: prodrug; may work less well in liver failure; use with caution
  - Targin (oxycodone/naloxone): avoid, as first-pass clearance of naloxone may be lost (e.g. shunts)
- Adjuncts:
  - Gabapentin: safe, no dose change needed
  - Tricyclics (amitriptyline, nortriptyline): reduced clearance; adjust dose

## Pain management

- Typical patients:
  - After liver transplant
  - After liver resection
  - Occasionally pain referrals in liver failure or biliary obstruction, usually palliative
- Mild liver disease: treat as for normal patients

## Antimicrobials

- Metronidazole: longer half-life; halve the dose (reduce by 50%) in Child-Pugh C
- Clindamycin: longer half-life; avoid or reduce dose in severe disease

## Flashcards
- Q: What extraction ratio makes clearance flow-limited, and give examples?
  - A: ER >0.7: propofol, ketamine, fentanyl, morphine, lidocaine
- Q: Which NMBs are drugs of choice in liver failure?
  - A: Atracurium and cisatracurium
- Q: What is the paracetamol limit in liver impairment?
  - A: Max 2g/day, short term (<14 days)
- Q: Which opioids should be avoided in liver impairment?
  - A: Codeine, pethidine and oxycodone/naloxone (Targin); remifentanil is ideal
- Q: How should metronidazole be adjusted in Child-Pugh C?
  - A: Reduce the dose by 50%
