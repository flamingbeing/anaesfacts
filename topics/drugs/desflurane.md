# Desflurane

Fluorinated ethyl methyl ether volatile with the fastest onset and offset, given via a heated pressurised vaporiser; pungent and a potent greenhouse gas.

## Presentation
- Fluorinated ethyl methyl ether
- MAC about 6.0%; MAC40 6.6% at 40 years (BJA 2003 iso-MAC charts)
- Boiling point 23.5°C: very volatile, unsafe in a conventional vaporiser
- Vapour pressure 669 mmHg at 20°C, close to atmospheric, so it nearly boils at room temperature
- Blood:gas partition coefficient 0.42
- Was slow to come into use because of difficulties in preparation and delivery

## Administration
- Electronic Tec 6 vaporiser heats it to 39°C at 2 atmospheres
- The vaporiser heats and pressurises the vapour, then adds a set fraction to the fresh gas flow; built differently from isoflurane and sevoflurane vaporisers
- Not suitable for induction (pungent)

## Mechanism of action
- As for other volatile anaesthetics

## Effects
- Fastest onset and offset of the volatiles (low blood:gas solubility)
  - Faster emergence than propofol TIVA and sevoflurane
- Minimal tissue uptake: quick wake-up after long cases and in obesity
- Cardiovascular: broadly like isoflurane
  - Does not sensitise the heart to catecholamines
  - Lowers cerebral and coronary vascular resistance
  - Above 1 MAC, or with rapid increases in concentration: transient sympathetic activation with tachycardia and hypertension
- Respiratory
  - Similar to other agents; respiratory depression greater than halothane but less than isoflurane and enflurane
  - PaCO2 rises and minute ventilation falls as concentration increases
  - Pungent: coughing, breath-holding, airway irritation, laryngospasm

## Adverse effects
- Greenhouse effect much worse than sevoflurane or isoflurane
- Airway irritation and laryngospasm
- Sympathetic stimulation (tachycardia, hypertension); use with care in ischaemic heart disease
- More PONV than TIVA
- Carbon monoxide production

## Contraindications
- Inhalational induction
- Asthma
- When sympathetic stimulation must be avoided

## Indications
- Day case surgery (rapid emergence)
- Need for a quick, clean intraoperative wake-up: scoliosis surgery, awake craniotomy
- Bariatric surgery: little tissue uptake allows rapid waking in patients sensitive to sedatives and opioids
- Prolonged surgery where low tissue uptake helps

## Pharmacokinetics
- Very low metabolism: less risk of fluoride renal toxicity and "halothane" type hepatitis
