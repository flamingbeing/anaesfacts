# Phosphodiesterase inhibitors

Drugs that stop phosphodiesterases breaking down cAMP and cGMP; effects depend on which isoenzyme is blocked.

## Overview
- Block hydrolysis of cyclic 3,5 adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and cyclic 3,5 guanosine monophosphate (cGMP)
- Poor specificity: most act on several isoenzymes, and most tissues carry several
    - Explains their wide side-effect profile, which still limits use

## PDE3
- Breaks down the cAMP made after beta-receptor stimulation; the cardiac and smooth muscle form
- PDE3 inhibitors raise and prolong intracellular cAMP, amplifying beta-mediated sympathetic effects
- Heart: increases contractility (inotropy), heart rate (chronotropy) and conduction velocity (dromotropy)
- Smooth muscle: relaxation, by
    - Inhibiting calcium release from intracellular stores
    - Protein kinase A phosphorylating actin-myosin regulatory proteins
- Platelets: higher cAMP reduces aggregation

## PDE5
- Breaks down cGMP formed in response to nitric oxide (NO)
- PDE5 inhibitors boost NO-mediated vasodilation (e.g. Viagra in penile erectile tissue)
- Uses: primary pulmonary hypertension, persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn, erectile dysfunction
- Higher cGMP is also anti-platelet: reduced aggregation

## PDE4 and PDE7
- PDE4 inhibitors may help inflammatory disease (e.g. reactive airway disease) and some cancers where cAMP is low
- Lung contains PDE3, PDE4 and PDE7; asthma drugs such as theophylline are non-specific
- Anti-inflammatory lung effects occur at lower blood levels than bronchodilation and are mediated by PDE4 and PDE7
    - Possible future approaches: low-dose non-specific inhibitors (fewer side effects) or specific PDE4 or PDE7 inhibitors (in development)

## Drugs
- Non-selective: methylxanthines (theophylline, aminophylline)
- PDE3 selective (3 main types relevant to anaesthesia)
    - Imidazolone: enoximone, a potent inotrope with marked peripheral vasodilation
    - Bipyridine: milrinone and amrinone
        - Milrinone largely replaced amrinone in the 1980s; similar pharmacodynamics, 30 times more potent
    - Others: anagrelide, cilostazol
- PDE4 selective: ibudilast
- PDE5 selective: sildenafil, dipyridamole

## Flashcards
- Q: Which PDE isoenzyme do milrinone and enoximone inhibit, and what are the main effects?
  - A: PDE3: raised cAMP gives inotropy, chronotropy, dromotropy, vasodilation and reduced platelet aggregation
- Q: What does PDE5 break down?
  - A: cGMP formed in response to nitric oxide
- Q: How much more potent is milrinone than amrinone?
  - A: 30 times
- Q: Name two clinical uses of PDE5 inhibitors besides erectile dysfunction.
  - A: Primary pulmonary hypertension and persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn
