# Paracetamol

Central-acting analgesic and antipyretic with almost no anti-inflammatory action; very safe at normal doses, hepatotoxic in overdose.

## Presentation
- Oral: 500 mg tablets, alone or combined; paediatric elixir 120 mg in 5 ml
- Combination: Anarex (paracetamol with orphenadrine)
- Rectal: suppositories 125 mg and 1 g
- IV: 1 g in 100 ml (10mg/ml); also 500 mg in 100ml

## Administration
- Oral: not absorbed in the stomach, so delayed gastric emptying lowers and delays the peak level
- Rectal: absorption slower and more variable than oral or IV; high initial doses needed, so not the preferred route
- IV: ideally infuse over 15mins (rapid bolus may cause phlebitis)
  - Onset 5-10 minutes; peak 1 hour; duration 4-6 hours

## Dosage
- General: about 15mg/kg every 4-8hrs, max 90mg/kg/day
  - Neonates and infants up to 3 months: 60 mg/kg/day
- Oral
  - Adults: 0.5-1g every 6-8 hours or as needed; max 4g/day
  - Children: 15 mg/kg every 6 hours
  - Neonates: 7.5-10mg/kg every 6 hours
  - Anarex: 2 tablets three times daily or as needed
- Rectal (children): loading 40 mg/kg (20 mg/kg in neonates), then 20 mg/kg 6-hourly
- IV
  - >50kg: 1g every 6 hours, max 4g daily
  - <50kg and children: 15mg/kg every 6 hours, max 90 mg/kg/d
  - Neonates: 7.5-10mg/kg every 6 hours
  - At least 4 hours between doses
  - Up to 6 g daily used in palliative care, and for 3 to 5 days by specialist acute pain services
- Before surgery: ask when the last dose was given on the ward, and whether the patient took analgesics at home before a same-day admission
- Premedication of 2 g oral before day surgery (if over 50 kg) is used in some centres, then 1 g 6-hourly after

## Mechanism of action
- Not fully understood; mainly central antinociception
  - Inhibits central cyclo-oxygenases (proposed COX-3, a COX-1 variant); this central prostaglandin inhibition explains the antipyretic effect
  - Activates the endocannabinoid system
  - Activates spinal serotonergic pathways
  - Blocks prostaglandin production at the transcription level
- Almost no effect on COX in vitro; grouped with NSAIDs for its analgesic and antipyretic effect
- No anti-inflammatory activity

## Adverse effects
- Very safe; fewer side effects than NSAIDs; no gastric irritation
- Hepatotoxicity
  - Contraindicated with raised transaminases
  - Caution in liver disease or after resection, though it can be used safely in liver disease
  - Consider LFTs with prolonged use

## Overdose
- Toxic dose saturates normal conjugation, so more NAPQI forms and exhausts hepatic glutathione
- NAPQI then binds hepatocyte sulfhydryl groups, causing cell death and centrilobular necrosis
- Hepatotoxicity possible above 150 mg/kg within 24 hours; rarely from as little as 75 mg/kg within 24 hours
- Treatment replenishes glutathione
  - Oral methionine: boosts glutathione synthesis
  - Acetylcysteine (oral or IV): hydrolysed to cysteine, a glutathione precursor; IV preferred as vomiting is common
- Accidental extra dose
  - An extra 2 g in an adult is not hepatotoxic and does not need an overdose pathway
  - Manage differently if <33 kg or liver function already impaired
  - Still a drug administration error: report as an incident

## Pharmacokinetics
- Well absorbed from the small bowel; oral bioavailability 80%
- Low protein binding (10%); larger volume of distribution than other NSAIDs
- Hepatic metabolism, mainly to glucuronide, also sulfate and cysteine conjugates; actively excreted in urine, little unchanged
- Small amounts of the toxic metabolite NAPQI form at therapeutic doses and are quickly conjugated with hepatic glutathione

## Flashcards
- Q: Adult IV paracetamol dose over 50 kg?
  - A: 1 g every 6 hours, max 4 g daily
- Q: Paediatric rectal loading dose?
  - A: 40 mg/kg (20 mg/kg in neonates), then 20 mg/kg 6-hourly
- Q: Dose threshold for possible hepatotoxicity?
  - A: More than 150 mg/kg within 24 hours (rarely 75 mg/kg)
- Q: How does acetylcysteine work in overdose?
  - A: Hydrolysed to cysteine, a glutathione precursor, replenishing glutathione
- Q: Neonatal IV paracetamol dose?
  - A: 7.5-10 mg/kg every 6 hours
