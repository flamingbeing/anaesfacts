# Remimazolam

Ultrashort-acting benzodiazepine, the rapid-offset equivalent of midazolam.

## Administration

- Potential uses
  - GA and TIVA
  - Sedation
  - ICU sedation
  - With remifentanil ("double remi")

## Dosage

- Induction: 0.1-0.3 mg/kg bolus
- Maintenance: 0.75-3 mg/kg/hr
- Paediatrics (Japanese case reports presented at the 3rd ASPA meeting)
  - Induction at high dose 12 mg/kg/hr or moderate dose 6 mg/kg/hr, then 1 mg/kg/hr maintenance
  - RSI in a 5 kg baby: remimazolam 1 mg bolus, fentanyl 50 mcg, rocuronium

## Mechanism of action

- Structure similar to midazolam; acts on the GABA-A receptor in a similar way

## Effects

- Rapid onset and rapid offset
- Stable haemodynamics, as with midazolam
- Reversed by flumazenil; rebound sedation unlikely

## Pharmacokinetics

- Ester bond: hydrolysed by tissue esterases
- No active metabolites
- No accumulation: context-sensitive half-time stays under 10 min (unlike propofol and midazolam)
- Kinetic profile resembles remifentanil
  - But volume of distribution is about twice and clearance about half that of remifentanil, so elimination is slower

## Flashcards
- Q: Remimazolam induction bolus dose?
  - A: 0.1–0.3 mg/kg
- Q: Remimazolam maintenance infusion rate?
  - A: 0.75–3 mg/kg/hr
- Q: How is remimazolam metabolised?
  - A: Ester hydrolysis by tissue esterases; no active metabolites
- Q: Context-sensitive half-time of remimazolam?
  - A: Stays under 10 min
- Q: Can remimazolam be reversed?
  - A: Yes, by flumazenil; rebound sedation unlikely
