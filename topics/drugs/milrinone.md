# Milrinone

Bipyridine phosphodiesterase III inhibitor: an inodilator that raises cAMP, giving inotropy, vasodilation and better lusitropy.

## Presentation
- 10 mg in 10 ml (1 mg/ml), yellow solution, stored at room temperature
- Class recorded as bipyridine / biguanide

## Administration
- IV only; dilute before use
- One dilution: 10 mg in 50 ml (recorded as giving 0.5 mg/kg)
- Onset 5-15 mins

## Dosage
- Loading dose 50 mcg/kg (hypotension is most likely during the bolus)
- Infusion 0.25/0.375-0.75 mcg/kg/min
- Reduce the dose in renal failure

## Mechanism of action
- Inhibits PDE III, so less cAMP is broken down and cAMP rises (similar to Gs activation)
- More protein kinase activity and protein phosphorylation, leading to net Ca2+ influx into muscle
- Effects:
  1) Positive inotropy
  2) Arterial and venous dilation
  3) Increased lusitropy (faster ventricular relaxation)

## Effects
- Better RV lusitropy lowers CVP
- Works in patients on beta-blockers or with beta-receptor downregulation (as in chronic CHF)
- Compared with beta-agonists: smaller rise in myocardial O2 consumption and less tachycardia, with lower filling pressures and lower PVR
- Titratable infusion, unlike long-acting levosimendan

## Adverse effects
- Hypotension, especially during the initial bolus
- Arrhythmias
- Long-term oral use in severe heart failure was linked to higher mortality

## Contraindications
- Avoid in renal failure (renally eliminated)

## Indications
- Short-term IV treatment of low cardiac output states and cardiac failure (first line for some, rarely used by others)
- To lower CVP during hepatectomy

## Pharmacokinetics
- Vd 0.38 L/kg; 70% protein bound
- Minimal metabolism; 80% excreted unchanged in urine
- Half-life recorded variously as 2.3 hrs, 2.7 hrs (elimination half-time) and 1–2.5 hours
- Effect stops about 8 hours after the infusion ends
