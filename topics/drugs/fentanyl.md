# Fentanyl

Synthetic phenylpiperidine opioid, a highly lipid-soluble mu agonist about 100 times as potent as morphine.

## Presentation
- Colourless solution, 100mcg in 2ml (50mcg/ml), given neat
- Transdermal patches releasing 25 to 100 mcg per hour for 72 hours
- Lozenges releasing 200 mcg to 1.6 mg over 15 minutes

## Administration
- IV, intrathecal, epidural, intranasal, transdermal, transmucosal (lozenge)
- IV low dose: give in 25-50mcg boluses, about 25-150mcg in total in adults
- Intranasal: used as premedication in children
- Patch: slow, takes about 24hrs to work
    - 12h to minimum effective serum concentration
    - 36h to maximum serum concentration
    - 17h for serum concentration to halve after removal

## Dosage
- Doses vary widely with the length of analgesia and sedation needed
- Equivalence: 100mcg = 10mg morphine
- Low dose IV: 1-2mcg/kg
    - Onset 1-2min, peak 3-5min
    - Duration 30-60min
- High dose IV: 50–100 mcg/kg
    - For opioid-based anaesthesia (a hypnotic is still needed)
    - Duration extends to about 6 hours
- Sedation infusion: 25-200mcg/hr
    - After prolonged infusion only the elimination half-life matters, so the effect lasts much longer
- Intranasal (paediatric premedication): 1 – 2 mcg/kg, maximum 100 mcg; onset 10 – 15 minutes
- Neuraxial, added to local anaesthetic
    - Spinal: 10–25 mcg (also given elsewhere in the note as 10–15 mcg)
    - Epidural: 25–100 mcg

## Mechanism of action
- Mu-receptor agonist
- 100 times more potent than morphine
- Mu receptor dissociation constant 1.9 (morphine 2.0): binding affinity almost the same, so lipid solubility explains most of the difference between the two

## Effects
- Shares morphine's mu-agonist effects
- Less histamine release than morphine
- Intrathecal: no delayed respiratory depression at typical doses, because it moves quickly from CSF into the cord
    - Morphine enters the cord slowly, so some rides CSF bulk flow up to the midbrain
    - Respiratory depression does occur with epidural infusion or repeated epidural boluses

## Adverse effects
- Chest wall rigidity and impaired ventilation at high doses (1000mcg), more often than with morphine
- Fentanyl-induced cough
    - Fentanyl, sufentanil and alfentanil cause a brief cough in up to 50% of patients after IV bolus, despite opioids normally suppressing cough
    - Possible mechanisms (unclear)
        - Sympathetic inhibition, vagal dominance, reflex bronchoconstriction, mucosal deformation and irritant receptor stimulation
        - Pulmonary chemoreflex via C-fibres (J-receptors) or rapidly adapting irritant receptors
        - Airway histamine release
    - Prevention
        - Inject more slowly: incidence falls markedly
        - Lidocaine 1.5 mg/kg 1 minute before (lowest effective dose 0.5mg/kg)
        - Fentanyl 25 mcg 1 minute before the main bolus (125 or 150 mcg)
        - Also effective: propofol, alpha-2 agonists (clonidine, dexmedetomidine), inhaled beta-2 agonists (terbutaline, salbutamol), NMDA antagonists (ketamine, dextromethorphan)

## Indications
- Intraoperative analgesia; blunts the sympathetic response to intubation at induction
- Adjunct to local anaesthetic in spinal and epidural anaesthesia
- Paediatric premedication (intranasal)
- Sedation infusion
- Chronic pain (patch)

## Pharmacokinetics
- Very lipid soluble (600 times more than morphine), one of the most lipid-soluble pure mu agonists
    - Fast onset; short action at low doses
- Low doses (<3 mcg/kg IV): short action is due only to rapid redistribution
- High doses or prolonged dosing: tissues saturate and action is much longer
- Liver: N-demethylated to norfentanyl; both then hydroxylated; inactive metabolites excreted in urine
- Clearance similar to morphine; elimination half-life longer (higher lipid solubility and volume of distribution)
- Ion trapping in the stomach: more than 99.9% ionised in acid; becomes unionised and absorbable in the alkaline small bowel, but rapid hepatic first-pass metabolism keeps systemic levels from rising much

## Flashcards
- Q: Fentanyl equianalgesic to 10 mg morphine?
  - A: 100 mcg
- Q: Intranasal fentanyl premedication dose and onset in children?
  - A: 1 – 2 mcg/kg, maximum 100 mcg; onset 10 – 15 minutes
- Q: How long does a fentanyl patch take to reach minimum effective and maximum serum concentration?
  - A: 12h and 36h
- Q: Lidocaine dose to prevent fentanyl-induced cough?
  - A: 1.5 mg/kg 1 minute before (lowest effective 0.5mg/kg)
- Q: Why is intrathecal fentanyl unlikely to cause delayed respiratory depression?
  - A: High lipid solubility: it moves quickly from CSF into the cord rather than spreading up to the midbrain
