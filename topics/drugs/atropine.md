# Atropine

Competitive muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist that blocks parasympathetic activity.

## Presentation

- 0.6mg in 1ml (0.6mg/ml)
- Racemic mixture; only l-atropine is active

## Administration

- Given IV undiluted

## Dosage

- 0.3mg-0.9mg
- 15-20mcg/kg (about twice the glycopyrrolate dose)

## Mechanism of action

- Competitive antagonist at muscarinic acetylcholine receptors

## Effects

- Central nervous system
  - Less likely to cause central anticholinergic syndrome than hyoscine, and less sedating
- Cardiovascular
  - Tachycardia from parasympathetic block
  - Small IV doses can cause initial bradycardia: possibly central vagal effect or partial agonism at cardiac muscarinic receptors
- Respiratory
  - Bronchodilation, increasing dead space (more than hyoscine)
  - Bronchial secretions reduced
- Gastrointestinal and urinary
  - Reduced salivary secretions; weaker antisialagogue than hyoscine
  - Lower oesophageal sphincter tone falls; gastric acid secretion falls slightly
  - Urinary retention
- Eye
  - Pupil dilation
  - Topical use may raise intraocular pressure: important in glaucoma
- Other
  - Inhibits sweating; may cause pyrexia in children

## Indications

- Bradycardia
- Antisialagogue
- Formerly routine premedication to reduce bronchial and salivary secretions and vagal bradycardia at induction; rarely needed with modern techniques
- Blocking muscarinic side effects of anticholinesterases
- Not used for PONV because of its cardiovascular effects

## Pharmacokinetics

- Onset 30-45sec; duration 30min
- Rapid but unpredictable gut absorption
- 50% protein bound
- Extensively metabolised by liver esterases; excreted in urine, very little unchanged
