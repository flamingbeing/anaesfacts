# Terlipressin

Long-acting synthetic vasopressin analogue (prodrug) selective for V1 receptors, given as intermittent IV boluses.

## Administration
- IV intermittent boluses (possible because of its long action)
- Can also be given as a continuous infusion

## Dosage
- Refractory intraoperative hypotension: single 1mg dose
- Refractory septic shock or bleeding varices: 1-2mg every 4-6 hours
- A single IV dose of 1–2 mg can raise blood pressure for 5 hours

## Mechanism of action
- Triglycyl-lysine vasopressin; prodrug converted to the active form, hence long half-life
- More selective for V1 receptors than vasopressin, so avoids effects of the other vasopressin receptors
- Constricts systemic and splanchnic vessels (arteriolar constriction in the splanchnic bed)
- Also dilates intrahepatic vessels

## Effects
- MAP increases
- Vasopressor effect lasts about 5-8 hrs

## Adverse effects
- Cardiovascular: vasoconstriction, higher afterload, CO falls
- Ischaemia (less with continuous infusion): skin, reduced coronary and mesenteric flow
  - Higher splanchnic ischaemia risk than vasopressin, and ischaemic effects cannot be reversed for 5 hours after a dose
- Respiratory: hypoxia, pulmonary oedema
- Hyponatraemia (V2-mediated antidiuresis)

## Indications
- Refractory intraoperative hypotension
- Refractory septic shock
- Bleeding varices (potent splanchnic vasoconstrictor)
- Hepatorenal syndrome
- Evidence: Intensive Care Medicine 2018 multicentre double-blind RCT, terlipressin vs noradrenaline infusion in septic shock
  - First trial powered for mortality, organ dysfunction and safety
  - China, 21 ICUs: 684 screened, 617 randomised, 526 analysed (14% lost, withdrawn or excluded after randomisation)
  - 28-day mortality not different: 40% vs 38% (p=0.633)
  - No difference in days alive and free of vasopressors, or in SOFA fall from D0 to D7
  - Stopped early for futility at interim analysis
