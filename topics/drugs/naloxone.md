# Naloxone

Pure opioid antagonist, titrated to restore adequate breathing; short acting, so watch for renarcotisation.

## Presentation
- 0.4mg in 1ml
  - Dilute 1 ml to 10ml for 40mcg/ml

## Administration
- IV preferred; intranasal, subcutaneous or IM if IV access is delayed
  - These routes absorb more slowly and clear later, so titration is much harder
- Aim for adequate ventilation, not a normal conscious level
- Onset: IV 1-2mins (as fast as fentanyl); IM or subcutaneous 2-5mins

## Dosage
- IV 0.04-0.4mg every 2min until respiratory rate is 12 or greater, guided by respiratory and cardiovascular state
- Breathing spontaneously: start with 0.04mg (40mcg) IV
  - Children: 0.002mg/kg/dose (2mcg/kg/dose)
- Apnoeic: higher starting dose, 0.4mg
  - Children: 0.01mg/kg/dose (10mcg/kg/dose)
- Cardiorespiratory arrest from possible opioid overdose: at least 2mg initially
  - Children: 0.1mg/kg/dose
- No response after 5 to 10 mg: reconsider the diagnosis
- Pruritus and urinary retention from opioids: infusion 0.1-0.25mcg/kg/hr
  - Above 0.3mcg/kg/hr, analgesia is lost
- Repeat doses may be needed after ventilation recovers, depending on the amount and duration of the opioid

## Mechanism of action
- Competitive antagonist at µ, κ and δ receptors; highest affinity for µ

## Adverse effects
- Sudden loss of analgesia (pain)
- Opioid withdrawal if the dose overshoots in an opioid-dependent patient
  - Manage withdrawal supportively, not with more opioid: large doses are needed to overcome naloxone, and they cause worse toxicity once naloxone wears off
- Occasionally tachycardia, hypertension, arrhythmias and pulmonary oedema
- Weigh these against the risks of intubation and short-term ventilation
- Renarcotisation: any agonist with a longer half-life than naloxone can cause it, especially after bolus dosing

## Pharmacokinetics
- Short duration: 30–40 minutes; half-life quoted from 30-120min
- Shorter acting than morphine and high-dose fentanyl
- Observe until symptom-free for at least 2 hours after the last dose; many use 4 hours to be safe

## Flashcards
- Q: Starting IV naloxone dose in a spontaneously breathing adult?
  - A: 40 mcg (0.04 mg), repeated every 2 min
- Q: Initial naloxone dose in suspected opioid cardiac arrest?
  - A: At least 2 mg (children 0.1 mg/kg)
- Q: Duration of action of naloxone?
  - A: 30-40 minutes
- Q: Naloxone infusion for opioid pruritus?
  - A: 0.1-0.25 mcg/kg/hr; above 0.3 mcg/kg/hr analgesia is lost
- Q: How long to observe after naloxone?
  - A: At least 2 hours symptom-free, commonly 4 hours
