# Ibuprofen

Propionic acid NSAID with fewer side effects than most of its class; low doses carry no added prothrombotic risk.

## Presentation

- Tablets, oral suspension, topical preparations
- No parenteral or rectal form (per the note)
- Racemic mixture of 2 enantiomers (chiral centre, like other propionates)

## Dosage

- Adults: 400 mg TDS, can increase to 800 mg TDS
  - The lower dose does not raise prothrombotic risk, the side effect that reshaped NSAID prescribing
- Children: 5-10 mg/kg every 6-8 hours (Q6-8H, up to 400mg per dose), maximum 40mg/kg/day
  - Minimum age 6 months

## Effects

- Analgesic
- Weaker anti-inflammatory action; below 1.2 g/d may not work in chronic inflammatory disease

## Adverse effects

- Fewer than many other NSAIDs; use has grown as other NSAIDs' risks emerged

## Indications

- Analgesia

## Pharmacokinetics

- Rapid gut absorption; highly protein-bound
- Much of the inactive R enantiomer is converted by an isomerase to the active S enantiomer (dexibuprofen) in gut and liver
- Hepatic cytochrome P450 metabolism to inactive hydroxy and carboxy metabolites; glucuronidation is minor
- All metabolites excreted renally
