# Magnesium sulfate and hypomagnesaemia

Main uses: torsades, AF, asthma, pre-eclampsia, pain and blood pressure control; watch for hypotension and prolonged neuromuscular block.

## Presentation

- 20% or 50% solution
- 10 ml vial of 50% = 5 g = 20 mmol

## Administration

- IM or IV
- Cellular uptake of Mg2+ is slow, so repletion needs sustained correction

## Dosage

- Hypomagnesaemia: 2 g over 20 minutes, until the plasma level is normal
- Pre-eclampsia: 4 g over 10 minutes, then 1 g/hour
  - Therapeutic range 2–3.5 mmol/l
  - Monitor closely for respiratory depression; hyporeflexia may come first
- Acute asthma: 2 g over 20 minutes
- Pain: 10 mmol given slowly (e.g. into a drip)

## Mechanism of action

- One of the main cations; key role in neurotransmission and neuromuscular excitability
- Antagonised by calcium
- Normal serum range 0.7–1.0 mmol/l

## Effects

- Hypotension
- Prolongs neuromuscular block

## Adverse effects

- Toxicity depends on plasma concentration:
  - Above 4 mmol/l: nausea, hyporeflexia, slurred speech
  - Above 6 mmol/l: muscle weakness, respiratory depression, bradycardia
  - Above 10 mmol/l: cardiac arrest
- Treat toxicity with calcium gluconate

## Indications

- Hypomagnesaemia
- Arrhythmias: AF, torsades de pointes
- Pre-eclampsia
- Acute asthma
- Pain (analgesic adjunct)
- Blood pressure control

### Hypomagnesaemia

- Definition: serum magnesium below 1.7 mg/dL (0.70 mmol/L)
- Causes:
  - GI loss: starvation or malabsorption, vomiting or diarrhoea, alcoholism, acute pancreatitis
  - Excess renal loss: secondary hyperaldosteronism (cirrhosis, CHF), hyperglycaemia, hypokalaemia, hypercalcaemia, loop and thiazide-type diuretics, nephrotoxic drugs, proton-pump inhibitors
- Clinical features: seizures, paresis, Chvostek and Trousseau signs, arrhythmias including torsades de pointes
  - ECG: widened QRS, prolonged PR, T-wave abnormalities
- Treatment:
  - Treat the cause
  - Give Mg2+ IM or IV
  - Stop diuretics; if a diuretic is needed, use a K+-sparing one to limit magnesium loss in urine
- Hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia will not correct until magnesium is replaced
