# Ketamine

NMDA receptor antagonist giving dissociative anaesthesia and strong analgesia, with sympathetic stimulation that usually preserves blood pressure.

## Presentation

- Racemic mixture: roughly equal S and R isomers
- Also as the single S(+) enantiomer (esketamine): 2 to 3 times as potent as R(–), with greater NMDA affinity and selectivity
- Water soluble; acidic solution, pH 3.5–5.5
- 100 mg in 2 ml (50 mg/ml)
- Small lipophilic molecule; crosses the blood–brain barrier fast, so onset is rapid
- In short: good for trauma and severe postoperative pain; avoid in IHD, poorly controlled hypertension, raised IOP or ICP

## Administration

- IV
  - Dilute 100 mg in 2 ml to 10 ml for 10 mg/ml
  - Standard 10 mg/ml for infusion: a 5 times (1:5) dilution, e.g. 2 ml (100 mg) plus 8 ml normal saline, or 50 mg/ml stock with 0.9% NaCl
  - Postoperative infusions on a CADD pump with lock
- IM: very useful, e.g. in combative patients
- Oral (sedation), rectal (sedation)
- Intrathecal and epidural (analgesia)

## Dosage

- IV induction: 1-2 mg/kg (1 mg/kg for co-induction)
  - Onset 45-60 sec, a little slower than other IV induction agents
  - Does not act within one arm–brain circulation time; central effects seen at about 90 seconds
  - Anaesthesia lasts 10-15 min (a little longer than other agents); reorientation at 20-30 min
  - To extend: half the original dose as a further bolus, infusion 20-40 mcg/kg/min, or a volatile
  - Analgesia outlasts anaesthesia
- IV procedural sedation: 0.5-1 mg/kg
- IV analgesic boluses: 0.1-0.3 mg/kg (e.g. 5-20 mg), subdissociative
  - e.g. to help position patients with fractures before a regional block
- IV analgesic infusion: 0.3 mg/kg/h
- IM
  - Induction 5-10 mg/kg
  - Sedation 2-5 mg/kg (e.g. 4 mg/kg)
  - Onset 3-4 min; anaesthesia 15-30 min; reorientation over 60 min
- Oral sedation: 3-6 mg/kg
- Sub-anaesthetic (analgesic) range: under 1 mg/kg IV bolus, under 0.5 mg/kg/h IV infusion
- Intraoperative analgesia
  - Initial 0.5 mg/kg slow bolus
  - Then 0.25 mg/kg every 30 min, or infusion 0.5 mg/kg/h
  - Procedures over 2 h: stop at least 60 min before the end of surgery to avoid slow recovery
  - Halve doses for less painful procedures
- Postoperative analgesia
  - Loading 0.1–0.3 mg/kg IV bolus
  - Maintenance 0.05–0.3 mg/kg/h IV (sub-anaesthetic)
  - Monitor hourly for the first 8 hours, then 4-hourly: sedation score, respiratory rate, BP, HR, pain score

## Mechanism of action

- NMDA receptor antagonist: most potent NMDA channel blocker in clinical use
  - Phencyclidine (PCP) derivative; found during PCP research in the early 1970s
  - Binds the phencyclidine site when the channel is open (activated)
  - Also binds a second membrane site that does not need an open channel, reducing how often the channel opens
- Inhibits nitric oxide synthase, lowering nitric oxide (a central and peripheral pain transmitter)
- Some opioid receptor activity
- NMDA–glutamate receptor
  - A calcium channel; central to dorsal horn (central) sensitisation
  - At normal resting membrane potential it is blocked by magnesium and inactive
  - Prolonged excitation shifts membrane potential, the block lifts, opioid response falls, allodynia and hyperalgesia develop
  - Probably mediated by intracellular nitric oxide

## Effects

- CNS
  - Dissociative anaesthesia: EEG shows dissociation between thalamocortical and limbic systems
  - Limbic system links thalamic sensory input with memory and emotion; ketamine stops pain signals being processed or given emotional meaning, so the patient is unaware of surroundings
  - Alpha rhythm replaced by theta and delta activity
  - Potent analgesia, including at sub-anaesthetic doses; low-dose infusion can cut morphine use without side effects
  - Amnesia
  - Cerebral blood flow increases, so ICP rises; CMRO2 increases
  - No anticonvulsant properties
  - Muscle tone increases; may cause limb jerking (yet used safely in myopathies and malignant hyperthermia)
- Cardiovascular
  - Stimulant via sympathetic activation: blocks noradrenaline reuptake at nerve endings, releasing endogenous catecholamines; not dose-dependent
  - Usually prevents hypotension; MAP may rise, hypertension possible
  - Heart rate rises markedly, so cardiac output increases
  - Preload and SVR maintained
  - Mild direct myocardial depression; S(+) depresses less than R(−) in vitro
  - Myocardial oxygen demand increases
  - Wide variation between patients
- Respiratory
  - Minimal respiratory depression compared with thiopentone and propofol; rate may increase
  - Brief fall in ventilation possible after a bolus
  - Airway usually, not always, maintained
  - Laryngeal and pharyngeal reflexes preserved
  - Bronchodilator
  - Pulmonary vascular resistance increases
- Eye: IOP rises slightly
- Uterus: variable effects on tone
- No proven adverse hepatic or renal effects
- Perioperative analgesic benefits (NMDA antagonism)
  - Potentiates opioids, opioid-sparing: severe acute, neuropathic and "opioid-resistant" pain
  - Reduces opioid-related nausea and vomiting
  - Prevents acute opioid tolerance and hyperalgesia; reduces central sensitisation, wind-up and opioid-induced hyperalgesia (OIH)

## Adverse effects

- Emergence phenomena (10-20%): the main drawback
  - Perceptual distortion, emergence delirium, vivid unpleasant dreams, nightmares, hallucinations
  - Avoid when emergence will follow soon after a dose
  - S(+) form: less intense but just as frequent
  - Less troubling in children; less common if left to recover undisturbed
  - Reduced by concurrent midazolam; postoperatively, lower the dose or give a low-dose benzodiazepine
- Increased salivation and airway secretions
  - Consider an antisialogogue: atropine 20 mcg/kg or glycopyrronium 5-10 mcg/kg
- Laryngospasm: often quoted, rarely seen
  - Noisy breathing is usually from head position; fix by repositioning
  - True laryngospasm usually from instrumentation or secretions touching the cords
- Nausea and vomiting, more than after thiopental or propofol
- Unpleasant side effects historically limited use as an induction agent
- Sedation and respiratory depression only if dosing strays into the anaesthetic range
- High-dose recreational use: severe interstitial cystitis, sometimes needing cystectomy

## Contraindications

- Poorly controlled cardiovascular disease: hypertension, ischaemic heart disease, heart failure, arrhythmias (sympathomimetic)
- Raised ICP; open eye injury or raised IOP (controversial if ventilated)
- Active psychosis or unstable psychiatric illness (worsens psychotomimetic effects)
- Moderate to severe hepatic dysfunction (heavy hepatic metabolism)
- Pregnancy (crosses placenta; possible fetal neurotoxicity or depression)

## Indications

- Induction
  - Reactive airway disease
  - Haemodynamically unstable patients
- Analgesic adjunct at low doses, especially with a chronic pain element
  - Second line when opioids fail or requirements escalate (tolerance)
  - Opioid-tolerant or opioid-dependent patients, chronic pain or acute-on-chronic flares
  - Major painful surgery: major spine, thoracotomy, major laparotomy, polytrauma
  - Refractory acute pain after failed multimodal or opioid regimens
- Procedural sedation

## Pharmacokinetics

- Least protein-bound IV anaesthetic (about 25%)
- Plasma level falls bi-exponentially
  - Initial fall: distribution across lipid membranes
  - Slower phase: hepatic metabolism
- Clearance depends on hepatic blood flow
- Demethylated by hepatic P450 (CYP2B6, CYP3A4) to active norketamine (30% as potent)
  - Then to inactive glucuronides, excreted in urine
- Consider dose reduction in severe hepatic impairment

## Flashcards

- Q: IV and IM induction doses of ketamine?
  - A: IV 1-2 mg/kg; IM 5-10 mg/kg
- Q: Analgesic (subdissociative) bolus dose of ketamine?
  - A: 0.1-0.3 mg/kg IV (e.g. 5-20 mg)
- Q: Incidence of ketamine emergence phenomena and what reduces them?
  - A: 10-20%; concurrent midazolam and recovering undisturbed
- Q: Main contraindications to ketamine?
  - A: Poorly controlled cardiovascular disease, raised ICP or IOP, active psychosis, moderate to severe hepatic dysfunction, pregnancy
- Q: Postoperative ketamine maintenance infusion range?
  - A: 0.05–0.3 mg/kg/h IV
