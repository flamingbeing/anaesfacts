# Parecoxib and valdecoxib

Parenteral COX-2 inhibitor; an inactive prodrug of valdecoxib (oral valdecoxib itself was withdrawn).

## Presentation

- Powder for injection; reconstitute with 0.9% saline

## Administration

- IM or IV

## Dosage

- 40 mg first dose, then 20–40 mg every 6–12 hours
- Maximum 80 mg/day

## Mechanism of action

- Parecoxib has no activity of its own; valdecoxib inhibits COX-2
- COX-1:COX-2 inhibition ratio 1:61

## Effects

- Onset 15 min, peak effect 2 h

## Adverse effects

- Valdecoxib: severe skin and hypersensitivity reactions (exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, angioedema), mainly in people with sulfonamide allergy
  - Reported in about 8 per million patients
  - Valdecoxib withdrawn in April 2005 for this reason
- Parecoxib has not shown the same skin reactions, probably because it is used only short term
- Interactions
  - Fluconazole (CYP2C9 inhibitor): reduce the dose
  - Ketoconazole and midazolam (CYP3A4): no adjustment needed
  - Valdecoxib inhibits other cytochrome P450 enzymes and may raise levels of:
    - Flecainide and metoprolol (CYP2D6)
    - Omeprazole, phenytoin and diazepam (CYP2C19)

## Contraindications

- Sulfonamide allergy (for valdecoxib)
  - Parecoxib's sulfonamide group is non-aromatic, like that of furosemide (frusemide) and tolbutamide, which are not contraindicated in sulfonamide allergy
- Severe hepatic impairment (not recommended)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Parecoxib is hydrolysed by liver enzymes to valdecoxib; plasma half-life 20 minutes
- Valdecoxib: hepatic metabolism by CYP2C9, CYP3A4 and glucuronidation to many metabolites, one of which also inhibits COX-2
- Valdecoxib elimination half-life 8 hours
- Renal impairment does not change kinetics (hepatic elimination)

  - A: 40 mg first dose, then 20–40 mg every 6–12 hours; maximum 80 mg/day
- Q: What is the active metabolite of parecoxib?
  - A: Valdecoxib

  - A: Severe skin and hypersensitivity reactions (e.g. SJS, TEN), mainly with sulfonamide allergy
