# Pyridostigmine

Quaternary amine anticholinesterase, mainly for myasthenia gravis; longer acting with fewer autonomic effects than neostigmine.

## Presentation

- Extended-release tablets 180 mg

## Dosage

- Highly individual: guided by response and tolerance of side effects
- Usually start 30mg three times daily, then maintenance 60mg four times daily while awake
- Maximum usually 600 mg/day; more than this raises the risk of cholinergic crisis
  - Rarely up to 750mg/day (Leventhal criteria)
- Extended-release 180 mg once or twice daily, at least 6 hours apart
- Conversion from oral
  - Oral 60mg pyridostigmine = IV 2mg pyridostigmine (oral to IV is 1/30th); preferred because it lasts longer
  - Oral 60mg pyridostigmine = 1mg neostigmine IV/IM (neostigmine 1.5-2 times more potent); rarely first choice as it is much shorter acting with more muscarinic side effects

## Indications

- Myasthenia gravis (preferred over neostigmine)

## Pharmacokinetics

- Slower onset than neostigmine (>16 min)
- Longer duration (6hrs)
- More dependent on renal elimination than neostigmine: 75% excreted unchanged
- Does not cross the blood–brain barrier
- Compare physostigmine: tertiary amine, well absorbed orally, crosses the blood–brain barrier; formerly used for anticholinergic poisoning
