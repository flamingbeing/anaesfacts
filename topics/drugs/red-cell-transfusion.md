# Red cell transfusion

Packed red cells (PCT) restore oxygen-carrying capacity; use restrictive Hb triggers, choose the right match for the urgency, and know when to ask for leukodepleted, irradiated or washed units.

## Principles

- Stays in the intravascular compartment
- Replace blood loss ml for ml
- Not for volume expansion: use crystalloid for that

## Dosage

- 1 unit raises Hb by about 1 g/dL (or Hct by 3%) in a 70 kg adult

## Transfusion triggers (restrictive strategy)

- Practice has moved to restrictive thresholds (eg **TRICC trial**) to cut transfusion-related morbidity
- **Hb below 7 g/dL**: transfuse most patients
- **Hb below 8 g/dL**: consider with
  - Pre-existing cardiovascular disease
  - Postoperative states (eg orthopaedic, cardiac surgery)
- **Hb 8–10 g/dL**: usually not indicated; consider only in **active bleeding** (Hb lags behind acute haemorrhage)
- **Hb above 10 g/dL**: not indicated
- Trauma, massive transfusion or ongoing loss: Hb is unreliable; activate the massive transfusion protocol on haemodynamics and severity (eg shock index)

## Physiology

- Oxygen delivery: DO2 = CO x CaO2
- As Hb falls, CO **increases** (lower viscosity, sympathetic drive) to maintain DO2
- Decompensation only when this is exhausted, typically Hb below 5 g/dL in healthy people
- Healthy patients usually tolerate Hb down to **5 g/dL** before ischaemic ECG changes

## Products

- PCT, with or without leukocyte reduction or irradiation
- Whole blood: massive transfusion and trauma; rarely used
- Frozen blood: rare groups, military use

## Storage and administration

- Stored at 4ºC
- Transfuse within 35 days of collection; older cells lyse and can cause hyperkalaemia
- Give each unit over 2 h, maximum 4 h
- Warm before transfusing
- Blood giving-set filter: typically 200 um (microns), removes debris
- Other filters for comparison
  - Epidural filters: 0.2 um
  - HME filters: 0.2 um
  - IV drug filters (eg phenytoin): 0.22 um
  - Filter needles: 5 um

## Matching

- Group and crossmatch (GXM): valid for 72 hrs
  - Group: recipient ABO and Rh status
  - Crossmatch: recipient blood mixed with donor blood, checking for agglutination
- Fully matched: 30-45 min wait
- Rapid match (same ABO and Rh group as recipient): 15-30 min wait
- Unmatched emergency ("e-bloods"): 10 min to immediate
  - O negative for Indian and Caucasian patients (especially women of reproductive age)
  - O positive for Chinese and Malay patients
- O negative is the universal red cell donor
- Alloantibodies are commoner in women, from exposure to non-self red cell antigens in pregnancy

## Specialised products

- **Leukocyte-reduced (leukodepleted)**: white cells removed
  - Fewer **febrile non-haemolytic reactions**: for patients who have had them
  - Less **HLA alloimmunisation**: multiply transfused patients or possible stem cell transplant recipients
  - Less **CMV transmission**: immunocompromised patients
  - In Singapore, CMV-safe products come from leukodepletion rather than seronegative donors, as CMV seropositivity is high
- **Irradiated**: lymphocytes can no longer proliferate
  - Prevents **transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease (TA-GVHD)** in high-risk groups
    - Stem cell transplant recipients
    - Antithymocyte globulin (ATG) or purine analogue chemotherapy (eg fludarabine)
    - Hodgkin's lymphoma
- **Washed red cells**: most plasma proteins, antibodies and electrolytes removed
  - **Anaphylaxis**: especially **IgA-deficient** patients with anti-IgA antibodies
  - **Neonatal and intrauterine transfusion**: less potassium and preservative
- **CMV-seronegative** (rarely used in Singapore)
  - From donors without CMV antibodies
  - Rarely needed or available because leukodepletion works so well

## 2,3-DPG in stored blood

- Storage below **6°C** slows glycolysis; despite preservatives (CPD, adenine), 2,3-DPG is **negligible within 2 weeks**
- After transfusion, regeneration is limited by **DPG mutase** reactivation
  - **50% recovery**: about 7 hours
  - **Full recovery**: about 48 hours
- Depleted blood has a **lower P50** (left shift of the oxyhaemoglobin curve): higher affinity, theoretically worse tissue unloading
- **Clinically minimal** in routine transfusion; even in massive transfusion P50 rarely changes by more than 0.5 kPa (3.8 mmHg)

## Flashcards

- Q: Expected Hb rise from 1 unit of packed cells in a 70 kg adult?
  - A: About 1 g/dL (Hct about 3%)
- Q: Restrictive Hb triggers?
  - A: Below 7 g/dL for most; below 8 g/dL with cardiovascular disease or after orthopaedic or cardiac surgery; 8–10 only if actively bleeding
- Q: How long can a unit of red cells run?
  - A: Over 2 h, maximum 4 h
- Q: Indications for irradiated blood?
  - A: Prevent TA-GVHD: stem cell transplant, ATG or purine analogue (fludarabine) therapy, Hodgkin's lymphoma
- Q: Who needs washed red cells?
  - A: IgA-deficient patients with anti-IgA antibodies, and neonatal or intrauterine transfusion
