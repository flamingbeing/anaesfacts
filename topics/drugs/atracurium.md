# Atracurium

Intermediate-acting non-depolarising benzylisoquinolinium muscle relaxant, broken down in plasma independent of liver and kidney.

## Presentation

- Colourless solution, 10 mg in 1 ml, in 2.5, 5 and 25 ml vials
- Store at 4°C
- Racemic mixture of 10 stereoisomers and geometric isomers, from four chiral centres

## Administration

- IV, given neat
- Inject slowly to limit histamine release

## Dosage

- ED95: 0.23 mg/kg (BJA CME 2004)
- Intubating dose: 0.5 mg/kg (range 0.3–0.6 mg/kg)
- Onset: about 2–3 min to intubating conditions; within 90–120 seconds at 0.5 mg/kg
- Duration: intermediate, 25–30 min
  - Adult example: about 30 mg at induction, then 10 mg top-ups every 30 min
- Infusion: 0.3 to 0.6 mg/kg/hour

## Adverse effects

- Histamine release, local at the injection site or generalised: hypotension, bradycardia, bronchospasm; mostly after fast injection
- Laudanosine (a tertiary amine from Hofmann degradation) can cause seizures at high levels
  - Not reported in humans during general anaesthesia; clinical levels are lower than those needed
- Critical illness myopathy, as with vecuronium

## Pharmacokinetics

- Two plasma pathways, both largely organ-independent, so renal or liver impairment does not prolong its action
- Only about 10% excreted unchanged in urine
- Ester hydrolysis
  - By non-specific plasma esterases (not plasma cholinesterase)
  - About 60% of metabolism
  - Products: quaternary alcohol, quaternary acid, laudanosine
  - Faster in acidic conditions, but clinical pH changes probably make little difference
- Hofmann elimination
  - Spontaneous breakdown at body temperature and pH; stable at pH 4 and 4°C
  - Products: laudanosine and a quaternary monoacrylate
  - Slowed by acidosis and hypothermia
- Laudanosine: glycine antagonist, no neuromuscular blocking action, cleared by the kidneys
