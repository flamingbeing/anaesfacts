# Lidocaine

Achiral amide local anaesthetic (lignocaine, Xylocaine) that is also a class Ib antiarrhythmic and an IV analgesic adjunct.

## Presentation

- Lidocaine hydrochloride, clear colourless solution 0.5–2%, plain or with adrenaline 1 in 80 000–200 000
  - IV antiarrhythmic use: 1% or 2% (10–20 mg/ml)
  - Dental cartridges: 2% with adrenaline 1:80000, 1.8 ml
- 2% gel; 5% ointment
- Spray giving 10 mg per dose; 4% solution for mucous membranes
  - High concentrations are used as sprays for mucosa (1% = 10 mg/ml)

## Administration

- Local anaesthesia:
  - Rapid onset (5-10 min)
  - Skin infiltration works almost at once: small unmyelinated fibres are penetrated quickly
  - Large, thick, well-myelinated nerves (e.g. sciatic) take longer
  - Duration of action 1-2 hr
- Antiarrhythmic: IV only

## Dosage

- Maximum (toxic) dose for local anaesthesia: 3 mg/kg plain, 7 mg/kg with adrenaline
- Before propofol, to reduce injection pain: 0.5 mg/kg
- Arrhythmia: 1-1.5 mg/kg IV bolus, then infusion of 1-3 mg/min in adults
  - Slow the infusion when hepatic blood flow is reduced
- IV infusion (analgesia): 1.5 mg/kg loading dose over 10 min, then 1.5 mg/kg/hr
  - Caution in hepatic and renal dysfunction
  - See perioperative infusion below

## Mechanism of action

- Nerve block and neuraxial anaesthesia: blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels (VGSC, NaV), reducing neuronal excitability and stopping peripheral nerve conduction
- IV analgesic adjunct ("membrane stabiliser"):
  - Partly sodium channel blockade
  - Also acts on other voltage-gated and ligand-gated ion channels in spinal cord neurones, shifting ion balance and membrane potential and so neuronal excitability
  - Also acts on GPCRs and many intracellular signalling pathways
- Antiarrhythmic (class Ib):
  - Na+ channel block slows the rate of rise of phase 0 and raises the threshold potential
  - Shortens phase 3 repolarisation, so action potential duration and refractory period fall

## Adverse effects

- Among the least toxic amides, probably because of relatively low protein binding (prilocaine is the least toxic)
- CNS toxicity above 4 mcg/ml: circumoral tingling, dizziness, paraesthesia
- Above 5 mcg/ml: confusion, coma, seizures
- Cardiovascular toxicity above 10 mcg/ml: AV block and refractory hypotension from myocardial depression; partly from central medullary depression

## Indications

- Local infiltration, nerve blocks, neuraxial anaesthesia
- IV analgesic adjunct
- Sustained ventricular tachyarrhythmias, especially with ischaemia (where inactivated Na+ channels predominate) or re-entry

## Pharmacokinetics

- 70% protein bound, to alpha1-acid glycoprotein (AAG)
- 33% unionised
- Extensive hepatic metabolism by dealkylation to monoethylglycine-xylidide and acetaldehyde; little unchanged drug in urine
  - Further hydrolysis and hydroxylation give the main metabolite, 4-hydroxy-2,6-xylidine, excreted in urine
  - Some metabolites are antiarrhythmic; others may enhance lidocaine-induced seizures
  - Metabolites may accumulate in renal failure
- High hepatic extraction ratio:
  - Clearance depends on hepatic blood flow, little affected by enzyme activity
  - Hepatic perfusion is the rate-limiting step
  - Clearance falls with low cardiac output and reduced hepatic blood flow (relevant in critical illness)
- Elimination half-life about 90 minutes; without a loading dose, steady state takes about 6 hours with normal liver function

## Perioperative IV lidocaine infusion for analgesia

### Indications
- Surgery: major open abdominal or colorectal surgery (less paralytic ileus, lower opioid need)
- Patient: severe chronic pain, opioid tolerance or dependence, severe opioid side effects

### Contraindications
- Cardiac conduction defects (AV block), severe heart failure
- Epilepsy, seizures, reduced level of consciousness
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR <30)
- Severe hepatic impairment (reduced clearance)
- Low body weight
- Allergy or hypersensitivity
- Acute porphyria
- Concurrent regional anaesthesia:
  - No local anaesthetic bolus within 4hrs before or 4hrs after the IV infusion
  - Wound or epidural catheter infusions (without boluses) can start 30 minutes after the IV infusion stops

### Dose
- Loading: <1.5 mg/kg over 10 minutes
- Maintenance: <1.5 mg/kg/h, max 120 mg/h
- Duration: <12 hours (context-sensitive half-life rises markedly with time)
- Use ideal body weight (IBW) to avoid toxicity in obesity

### Safety
- Standard concentration 10 mg/ml; take care when diluting
- Dedicated, labelled pump and IV cannula, with anti-siphon/anti-reflux valves
- Monitor for local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST); have lipid emulsion 20% ready
- Stop at the end of surgery, before leaving theatre; not for unmonitored ward use

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the maximum dose of lidocaine with and without adrenaline?
  - A: 3 mg/kg plain; 7 mg/kg with adrenaline
- Q: At what plasma levels do CNS and cardiovascular toxicity appear?
  - A: CNS above 4 mcg/ml (seizures above 5 mcg/ml); cardiovascular above 10 mcg/ml
- Q: What is the perioperative IV lidocaine infusion regimen?
  - A: Load <1.5 mg/kg over 10 minutes, then <1.5 mg/kg/h (max 120 mg/h) for <12 hours, dosed on ideal body weight
- Q: How long must a local anaesthetic bolus be separated from an IV lidocaine infusion?
  - A: No bolus within 4 hours before or after
- Q: What limits lidocaine clearance?
  - A: Hepatic blood flow (high extraction ratio)
