# Sympathetic Drugs

Sympathomimetics and sympatholytics: drugs that raise or block sympathetic effects on vessels, heart rate and contractility.

## Overview

- Used to support the circulation, especially blood pressure
- Act on one or more of
  - Arterial vasoconstriction
  - Heart rate (chronotropy)
  - Contractility (inotropy)
- Short-term vasopressors are often needed in anaesthesia because
  - Most anaesthetic agents depress the cardiovascular system
  - Many surgical patients are dehydrated, systemically unwell or have cardiovascular disease

## Sympathomimetics

- Act on adrenoceptors or dopamine receptors
  - Direct-acting: bind and activate the receptors themselves
  - Indirect-acting: cause noradrenaline release, which then acts on the receptors
- Structure: benzene ring with amine side chains at C1
  - Hydroxyl groups at C3 and C4 make it a catecholamine (3,4-dihydroxybenzene is catechol)

### Classes

- Natural catecholamines: adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine
  - Linked synthesis pathways; act on adrenergic and dopaminergic receptors
- Synthetic agents
  - Only isoprenaline, dobutamine and dopexamine are catecholamines (hydroxyls at the 3- and 4- positions)
- Other inotropes: non-selective phosphodiesterase inhibitors

### By receptor

- Alpha-1 agonists
  - Metaraminol: alpha-1 agonist, weak beta activity
  - Noradrenaline: strong alpha-1, moderate beta-1, minimal beta-2
  - Methoxamine
  - Phenylephrine; ephedrine (indirect)
- Alpha-2 agonists
  - Clonidine: partial alpha-2 agonist (alpha-2:alpha-1 = 200:1)
  - Dexmedetomidine
- Beta-1 agonists
  - Dobutamine
  - Isoprenaline (isoproterenol): non-selective beta-1 and beta-2
- Beta-2 agonists
  - Salbutamol
  - Ritodrine
  - Isoprenaline (non-selective)
  - Dopexamine: potent beta-2 agonist, used in heart failure
- Mixed agonists: adrenaline

## Sympatholytics

- Block the sympathetic system at three levels
  - Peripheral: alpha and beta antagonists block noradrenaline at the heart or vessels
  - Ganglionic: block transmission at sympathetic ganglia
  - Central: reduce sympathetic outflow from the brain

### Alpha-1 antagonists

- Prazosin: highly selective post-synaptic alpha-1 antagonist
- Phenoxybenzamine: long-acting, irreversible, non-selective; higher affinity for alpha-1 than alpha-2
- Phentolamine: cardiovascular effects like phenoxybenzamine, but shorter and reversible with alpha agonists
- Tolazoline: mainly a pulmonary vasodilator

### Beta antagonists

- Non-selective (beta-1 and beta-2)
  - Propranolol (prototype)
  - Oxprenolol
  - Sotalol (antiarrhythmic)
  - Timolol (mainly glaucoma)
- Cardioselective (beta-1)
  - Atenolol, bisoprolol, metoprolol
  - Esmolol
- Mixed alpha and beta antagonists
  - Carvedilol
  - Labetalol

### Ganglion blockers

- Block the post-synaptic action of acetylcholine at autonomic ganglia
- Trimetaphan is the only one still in clinical use

## Flashcards
- Q: What makes a sympathomimetic a catecholamine?
  - A: Hydroxyl groups at C3 and C4 of the benzene ring
- Q: Which synthetic agents are catecholamines?
  - A: Isoprenaline, dobutamine and dopexamine
- Q: Clonidine alpha-2:alpha-1 selectivity?
  - A: 200:1 (partial alpha-2 agonist)
- Q: Which alpha antagonist is long-acting and irreversible?
  - A: Phenoxybenzamine
- Q: Only ganglion blocker still in clinical use?
  - A: Trimetaphan
