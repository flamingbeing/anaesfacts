# Paediatric opioids

Doses of morphine, fentanyl and remifentanil in children; reduce morphine and fentanyl under 3 months.

## Morphine
- Neonates: lower clearance and longer half-life
  - Immature liver metabolism and blood-brain barrier: the same plasma level gives higher CNS levels in neonates and young infants than in older children and adults
- Intraoperative: 100-150 mcg/kg IV; 25-50 mcg/kg IV if under 3 months
- Postoperative
  - Infusion 10-40 mcg/kg/hour; 5-20 mcg/kg/hour if under 3 months
  - PCA bolus 20 mcg/kg
  - Oral 200-300 mcg/kg 2-4-hourly (maximum 15 mg)

## Fentanyl
- Alternative to morphine intraoperatively
- Rarely used IV or orally after surgery
- Common additive to local anaesthetic for epidural infusion
- Intraoperative: 2-4 mcg/kg IV; 1-2 mcg/kg IV if under 3 months
- Infusion 5-10 mcg/kg/hour; largely replaced by remifentanil for infusion
- Intranasal use as premedication

## Remifentanil
- Intraoperative infusion, or bolus to aid tracheal intubation
- Broken down by plasma and tissue esterases (no glucuronidation needed), so offset is fast and predictable even in neonates
- Intraoperative infusion 0.25-0.5 mcg/kg/minute
- Bolus 0.1-1 mcg/kg (may cause bradycardia and muscle rigidity)

## Flashcards
- Q: Intraoperative IV morphine dose in a child, and under 3 months?
  - A: 100-150 mcg/kg; 25-50 mcg/kg under 3 months
- Q: Postoperative morphine infusion rate in children?
  - A: 10-40 mcg/kg/hour (5-20 mcg/kg/hour under 3 months)
- Q: Intraoperative fentanyl dose in a child?
  - A: 2-4 mcg/kg IV (1-2 mcg/kg under 3 months)
- Q: Why does remifentanil wear off predictably even in neonates?
  - A: Esterase metabolism, no glucuronidation needed
