# Opioid prescribing and conversion

Starting doses, breakthrough dosing, side-effect management and conversion factors to oral morphine.

## Dosage
- Common starting doses
  - PO tramadol 25-50 mg three times daily
  - PO morphine 2.5-5 mg 4-6 hourly plus as needed
  - PO oxycodone 2-5 mg 4-6 hourly plus as needed
  - SC fentanyl 25 mcg 4 hourly as needed
- Breakthrough oral dosing
  - Immediate-release dose = 1/6th or 10-20% of the total daily dose, every 4-6 hours
  - Common palliative order: PO morphine X mg every 4 hours plus every 4 hours as needed (regular and PRN doses can be the same)

## Administration
- Switching to a transdermal patch
  - For persistent severe chronic pain with stable requirements in opioid-tolerant patients (e.g. oral morphine equivalent dose, OMED, of at least 30 mg/day for more than 72 hours)
  - Continue the previous analgesia for 12 hours
  - Breakthrough: immediate-release 1/6th of total daily dose every 4 hours to 1/10th every 2 hours (q4H to q2h)
  - Increase patch strength only after 2x 72 hour applications (once levels have stabilised)

## Adverse effects
- Nausea and vomiting
  - Mild: one PRN agent is usually enough
    - IV ondansetron 4-8 mg 8 hourly
    - IV metoclopramide 10 mg 6-8 hourly
  - Moderate to severe: regular round-the-clock dosing of at least one agent; alternatives include dexamethasone and prochlorperazine
  - Previous severe nausea and vomiting: regular dosing of at least one agent for the first 24 hours, reviewed daily
- Ileus and constipation
  - Laxatives
  - Targin may help opioid-related ileus (often colicky pain)
- Pruritus
  - Mild: calamine lotion
  - Moderate to severe: first-generation antihistamines
    - PO chlorpheniramine 4 mg 6 hourly
    - IM promethazine 25 mg 6 to 12 hourly

## Pharmacokinetics
- Golden rule: when switching opioids, always convert to oral morphine first
- Oral opioids, factor to PO morphine
  - PO codeine 0.13
  - PO tramadol 0.2 (PO tramadol 50 mg x 0.2 = PO morphine 10 mg)
  - PO morphine 1
  - PO oxycodone 1.5 (PO oxycodone 5 mg x 1.5 = PO morphine 7.5 mg)
- IV opioids, factor to PO morphine
  - IV tramadol 0.3
  - IV pethidine 0.4
  - IV morphine 3 (IV morphine 1 mg x 3 = PO morphine 3 mg)
  - IV oxycodone 3
  - IV fentanyl 300 (IV fentanyl 10 mcg = 0.01 mg x 300 = PO morphine 3 mg)
    - In chronic use the factor is closer to 120-200 (IV fentanyl 10 mcg = PO morphine 1.2-2 mg)
- Transdermal patches, equivalent PO morphine per day
  - Fentanyl 12 mcg/h: 30 - 60 mg
  - Fentanyl 25 mcg/h: 60 - 130 mg
  - Fentanyl 50 mcg/h: 130 - 220 mg
  - Quick fentanyl rule: multiply by 3 (fentanyl 25 mcg/h x 3 = PO morphine 75 mg/day)
  - Buprenorphine 5 mcg/h: 9-14 mg
  - Buprenorphine 10 mcg/h: 18-28 mg
  - Quick buprenorphine rule: multiply by 2 (5 mcg/h x 2 = PO morphine 10 mg/day)
- Transdermal fentanyl worked conversion
  - 100 mg oral morphine per day is about 1 mg (1000 mcg) fentanyl per day (transdermal or IV)
  - So 60 mg oral morphine per day is about 0.6 mg (600 mcg) fentanyl per day
  - 600 mcg over 24 hours = 25 mcg/h
  - So 60 mg oral morphine per day is about a 25 mcg/h fentanyl patch

## Flashcards
- Q: Conversion factor from IV morphine to PO morphine?
  - A: 3 (IV morphine 1 mg = PO morphine 3 mg)
- Q: Oral breakthrough dose as a fraction of total daily dose?
  - A: 1/6th (10-20%) every 4-6 hours
- Q: Oral morphine equivalent of a 25 mcg/h fentanyl patch?
  - A: About 60 mg/day (table range 60 - 130 mg)
- Q: When can a fentanyl patch strength be increased?
  - A: Only after 2x 72 hour applications
- Q: PO oxycodone to PO morphine factor?
  - A: 1.5
