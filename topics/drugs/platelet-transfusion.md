# Platelet transfusion

Platelets for low counts or poor function; targets depend on bleeding, brain injury or procedure.

## Presentation
- Pooled platelets (from 4-6 donors), e.g. CSP (cell separated platelets)
  - Raise platelet count by about 15,000/mm3
- Single-donor (apheresis) platelets: equivalent to 4-6 random-donor units
  - Raise platelet count by 40-60,000/mm3
- Stored at 22ºC on an agitator; use within 5 days
  - Room-temperature storage carries a higher infection risk

## Administration
- Do not warm platelets (current guidance, though evidence is scarce)
  - Extreme warming (above 43-45 degrees C) impairs aggregation and alters cytoskeletal membrane components

## Dosage
- 5-10 ml/kg
- 1 CSP raises ROTEM A5 EX (EXTEM A5) by 8-10 mm
- Paediatric cardiac: 15-20 ml/kg (higher than expected)

## Indications
- Targets
  - 100k in bleeding patients
  - 80k in brain injury or trauma
  - 50k for surgery or line insertion
- Abnormal platelet function (not ITP)
- Emergency reversal of antiplatelet drugs
- Pooled platelets: thrombocytopenia with bleeding; for platelet dysfunction, 1 unit may be enough as a catalyst
- Apheresis platelets: potential bone marrow transplant recipients (lower risk of refractoriness)

## Adverse effects
- Platelet refractoriness: little or no rise after transfusion (e.g. under 5000)
  - Causes: autoantibodies (ITP), alloantibodies, consumption (bleeding, sepsis), hypersplenism

## Flashcards
- Q: Usual platelet dose?
  - A: 5-10 ml/kg
- Q: Platelet targets for bleeding, brain injury and surgery or lines?
  - A: 100k, 80k and 50k
- Q: Platelet storage conditions?
  - A: 22ºC on an agitator, use within 5 days
- Q: Expected rise from single-donor apheresis platelets?
  - A: 40-60,000/mm3
