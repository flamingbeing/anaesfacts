# Local anaesthetics in children

Neonates and infants under 6 months have more free drug and slower clearance, so amide doses are halved; toxicity in anaesthetised children often shows first as cardiovascular collapse.

## Differences from adults
- Less α-1-acid glycoprotein (and albumin) in neonates and infants <6months: lower protein binding, higher free fraction
  - Bupivacaine, levobupivacaine and ropivacaine doses are cut in neonates and infants up to 6 months
  - The reduction is arbitrary: 50% of the dose for older children
- Immature hepatic enzyme clearance
- Larger volume of distribution and longer half-life than adults
  - Lower peak level after a single dose, but more accumulation with repeat doses or infusions
- Toxicity
  - Awake patients (e.g. postoperative epidural infusion): CNS signs usually come before cardiovascular collapse
  - Most blocks are done under anaesthesia, so the first sign is often cardiovascular

## Specific agents
- Prilocaine: avoid in infants <3 months
  - Metabolite 4,6-hydro-xytoluidine (strong oxidant) causes methaemoglobinaemia while methaemoglobin reductase activity is low
  - Full enzyme activity at about 3 months of age

## Maximum doses
- Levobupivacaine
  - Bolus 2.5 mg/kg (<6mths: 1.25 mg/kg)
  - Infusion 0.4 mg/kg/hour (<6mths: 0.25 mg/kg/hour)
- Ropivacaine
  - Bolus 3.0 mg/kg (<6mths: 1.5 mg/kg)
  - Infusion 0.4 mg/kg/hour (<6mths: 0.25 mg/kg/hour)
- Bupivacaine
  - Bolus 2.5 mg/kg (<6mths: 1.25 mg/kg)
  - Infusion 0.4 mg/kg/hour (<6mths: 0.25 mg/kg/hour)
- Lidocaine
  - Bolus 3.0-7.0 mg/kg (<6mths: 1.5 mg/kg)
- Prilocaine
  - Bolus 6-10 mg/kg (<6mths: 3-5 mg/kg)

## Flashcards
- Q: Maximum bupivacaine bolus in a child, and under 6 months?
  - A: 2.5 mg/kg; 1.25 mg/kg under 6 months
- Q: Maximum ropivacaine infusion rate in children, and under 6 months?
  - A: 0.4 mg/kg/hour; 0.25 mg/kg/hour under 6 months
- Q: Why are amide doses halved under 6 months?
  - A: Low α-1-acid glycoprotein raises the free fraction
- Q: Why avoid prilocaine under 3 months?
  - A: Methaemoglobinaemia, as methaemoglobin reductase is immature
- Q: First sign of LA toxicity in an anaesthetised child?
  - A: Often cardiovascular collapse
