# Propofol

The most commonly used IV induction agent: a phenol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) acting at GABA-A receptors, with fast onset and offset, marked cardiovascular and respiratory depression, and antiemetic effects.

## Presentation

- 1% (10 mg/ml) white aqueous emulsion: 200 mg in 20 ml ampoule; also 2% and 50 ml bottles
- Water insoluble; emulsion contains 1.2% egg lecithin, 2.25% glycerol and 10% soybean oil
- 1.1 kcal/ml; long, high-dose ICU infusions may cause hypertriglyceridaemia
- pH 7, pKa 11 (weak organic acid): almost fully unionised at pH 7.4
- Disodium edetate added since 1996 to inhibit bacterial growth; this reduced, but did not abolish, infections from contaminated propofol

## Administration

- IV only, given neat
- Rapid onset, short action and easy titration suit it to TCI, for sedation or as part of balanced or total IV anaesthesia (see the TIVA and TCI topic)
- Lipid emulsion supports bacterial growth, and contamination rises quickly with time
  - Disinfect ampoules or vials before opening; this greatly cuts contamination
  - Strict asepsis; inspect for particles, discolouration or a separated emulsion, and do not use if contaminated
  - Draw up or spike immediately before use, one patient only, and start the infusion straight away
  - FDA: syringes or vials must start within 6 hours of opening or filling; a large-volume (eg 100 mL) vial infused directly must be used within 12 hours of opening or spiking
  - Disinfect the rubber stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol
  - Discard leftovers within 6 hours of filling a syringe or 12 hours of spiking a large vial

## Dosage

- Induction: **1.5-2.5 mg/kg**
  - Children: **2.5-5 mg/kg**
  - Older or high-risk patients: reduce to 1 mg/kg or less and titrate to effect
- TCI targets
  - TIVA maintenance: **2-5 mcg/ml** in most patients
  - Sedation under local or regional anaesthesia: **0.5-1.5 mcg/ml**
  - Most patients wake at 1–2 mcg/ml
- Sedation infusion: 25-100 mcg/kg/min
  - = **1.5-6 mg/kg/hr**, or 0.15-0.6 ml/kg/hr of 1%
  - Alternative: 20-200 mg/hr (can start at 50 mg/hr)
- Maintenance infusion: 50-200 mcg/kg/min
  - = **3-12 mg/kg/hr**, or 0.3-1.2 ml/kg/hr of 1%
- Timing
  - **Onset 30-45 sec**
  - **Peak 2 min** (watch the heart rate)
  - **Duration 3-8 min**; reorientation in 10-20 min
  - Infusions over hours to days accumulate in fat and slow recovery
- No dose change in renal or liver failure; it does not accumulate there

## Mechanism of action

- Enhances transmission at the GABA-A receptor

## Effects

- Cardiovascular: the greatest depression of any induction agent; **profound hypotension**
  - Contractility falls
  - SVR falls markedly
  - Venodilation markedly reduces preload
  - Heart rate falls (bradycardia); reflex tachycardia is rare
  - Cardiac output falls
  - MAP falls markedly, mainly from the fall in SVR
- Respiratory: potent depression; **apnoea is common**
  - Blunts the ventilatory response to hypoxia and to hypercarbia
  - More respiratory depression than thiopentone
  - Avoid when spontaneous ventilation must be kept
  - Blunts upper airway reflexes; cough or laryngospasm after it is rare
- CNS
  - Intracranial pressure, cerebral blood flow and CMRO2 all fall
  - Large doses may still reduce cerebral perfusion pressure, because systemic pressure falls more
  - Moderate anticonvulsant; used in status epilepticus
  - Less residual cognitive impairment than other induction agents; pleasant dreams
- Antipruritic (unlike opioids), whether used for induction, maintenance or in subhypnotic doses after surgery; D2 antagonism may explain it
- Antiemetic (unlike opioids)

## Adverse effects

- Pain on injection in up to 67%; less with lignocaine or a larger vein
- Excitatory movements (myoclonus, twitching, hiccups) in up to 10%
  - Probably not cortical seizures but a subcortical excitatory-inhibitory imbalance
  - Dystonic, with choreiform movements and opisthotonos
- Allergy
  - Egg lecithin is a purified phosphatide and is not thought to matter in egg allergy (egg anaphylaxis is driven by egg-white proteins: ovalbumin, ovomucoid, conalbumin)
  - Reactions to propofol seem due to its isopropyl or phenol groups, not the lipid
  - Soya allergy: not thought to matter, as protein is removed from the oil
- Prolonged infusion: fat overload syndrome (hyperlipidaemia with fatty infiltration of heart, liver, kidneys and lungs)
- Green urine from a phenolic metabolite: with infusions, rarely after one dose; benign and resolves on stopping
- Propofol-related infusion syndrome (PRIS)
  - First seen as unexpected deaths in a few children ventilated in ICU for respiratory infection: progressive **metabolic acidosis**, resistant **bradycardia**, **lipaemic** serum
  - Features: new **metabolic (especially lactic) acidosis** (86%), **cardiac dysfunction such as bradycardia** (88%), **rhabdomyolysis** of cardiac and skeletal muscle (45%), **renal failure** (37%), **hypertriglyceridaemia** (15%)

## Contraindications

- Caution or avoid in haemodynamic instability
- CSM: contraindicated for ICU sedation in children aged 16 years and under

## Indications

- Induction and maintenance of anaesthesia (most commonly used agent)
- Sedation
- Reactive airway disease
- Status epilepticus

## Pharmacokinetics

- 98% bound to albumin
- Largest volume of distribution of the induction agents: 4 L/kg
- Short action after a bolus because plasma levels fall fast as it redistributes to well-perfused tissues
- Mainly **hepatic** metabolism, all to inactive products excreted in urine
  - About 40% conjugated to a glucuronide
  - About 60% to a quinol, then excreted as glucuronide and sulfate
- Clearance exceeds hepatic blood flow, so some extrahepatic metabolism occurs; plasma levels fall faster than thiopental after distribution
- Terminal elimination half-life 5–12 hours; approaches 60 hours if sampled beyond 24 hours, probably slow release from fat
- **Context-sensitive half-time rises with prolonged infusion**, but careful titration still allows fairly quick waking

## Flashcards

- Q: Adult and paediatric induction doses of propofol?
  - A: 1.5-2.5 mg/kg in adults; 2.5-5 mg/kg in children; 1 mg/kg or less in older or high-risk patients
- Q: Typical TCI targets for TIVA maintenance and for sedation?
  - A: 2-5 mcg/ml for TIVA; 0.5-1.5 mcg/ml for sedation; most wake at 1-2 mcg/ml
- Q: How soon must a propofol infusion from a syringe or vial start after drawing up (FDA)?
  - A: Within 6 hours; within 12 hours for a large-volume vial infused directly
- Q: Main features of PRIS?
  - A: Metabolic (lactic) acidosis, cardiac dysfunction such as bradycardia, rhabdomyolysis, renal failure, hypertriglyceridaemia, lipaemic serum
- Q: Main cause of the fall in MAP after propofol?
  - A: Fall in SVR (plus venodilation and reduced contractility)
