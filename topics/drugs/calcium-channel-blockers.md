# Calcium Channel Blockers

Drugs of varied structure that all block Ca2+ entry through L-type calcium channels.

## Members

- Dihydropyridines: amlodipine, nifedipine, nicardipine, nimodipine
- Non-dihydropyridines: verapamil, diltiazem

## Mechanism of action

- Block L-type Ca2+ channels; T-, N- and P-type channels are spared
- L-type channels
  - Found widely in the cardiovascular system
  - Carry the slow inward current of the cardiac action potential plateau
  - Trigger release of intracellular Ca2+
  - Regulated by cAMP-dependent protein kinase
- T-type channels
  - Structurally like L-type
  - Mainly in cardiac cells without T-tubules (SA node) and some vascular smooth muscle
  - Absent from ventricular myocardium
- N-type channels: nerve cells only

## Effects

- Each drug has a different affinity for L-type channels in myocardium, nodal tissue and vascular smooth muscle, so effects vary between drugs

## Contraindications

- Non-dihydropyridines: heart block, low ejection fraction

## Indications

- All: essential hypertension
- Non-dihydropyridines also: angina and arrhythmias
