# Drugs in renal impairment

How renal impairment and dialysis change drug handling, which anaesthetic and analgesic drugs to choose, and renally adjusted antimicrobial doses.

## Two patient groups

- Renal dysfunction (drug excretion):
  - CrCl >60 is usually enough to clear drugs and metabolites, with no dose change
  - Exception: NSAIDs
- Haemodialysis:
  - Dose changes
  - Is the drug dialysable?

## Principles

### Pharmacokinetics
- Distribution:
  - Larger Vd with fluid overload; smaller Vd after dialysis
  - Low albumin and acidosis reduce protein binding, raising free drug levels
    - Reduce doses of benzodiazepines, barbiturates, propofol and local anaesthetics (especially bupivacaine)
- Metabolism and excretion: renal clearance falls in line with CrCl
  - Renally excreted (usually hydrophilic) drugs may need dose reduction or avoidance
    - Reduce rocuronium dose
    - Others affected: penicillins, neostigmine
  - Some liver-metabolised drugs have active metabolites cleared by the kidney: benzodiazepines, morphine, pethidine

### Pharmacodynamics
- Uraemia increases CNS sensitivity to sedatives and opioids

### Other
- Hyperkalaemia: suxamethonium may be contraindicated
- Avoid nephrotoxins such as NSAIDs

## Anaesthetic drugs

### Induction and maintenance
- IV agents:
  - Propofol, thiopentone, etomidate: reduce dose by about 30% (less protein binding, more CNS sensitivity)
  - Benzodiazepines: reduce dose; midazolam and diazepam have active metabolites excreted by the kidney
  - Ketamine: generally safe; no dose change
- Volatiles:
  - Safe: isoflurane, desflurane
  - Avoid: enflurane (fluoride nephrotoxicity)
  - Caution: sevoflurane (theoretical risk from compound A and fluoride); consider keeping below 2 MAC-hours in significant impairment

### Neuromuscular blockers
- Best choice: atracurium or cisatracurium (Hofmann elimination, independent of renal function)
- Reduce dose: rocuronium, vecuronium, mivacurium (longer action)
- Avoid: pancuronium (85% renal excretion; high risk of residual paralysis)
- Contraindicated: suxamethonium if K > 5.5mmol/L

### Reversal
- Neostigmine: no problem; its clearance falls more than that of the NMBs, so recurarisation is unlikely
- Sugammadex: not recommended if eGFR < 30 mL/min (complexes are renally excreted; removal by dialysis unpredictable)

### Local anaesthetics
- Less protein binding and lower seizure threshold in uraemia
- Reduce the maximum dose by 25%

### Antibiotics (general)
- Loading dose unchanged
- Maintenance: lower dose or longer interval, adjusted to CrCl
- Penicillins and cephalosporins need maintenance dose adjustment
- Contraindicated: gentamicin, with or without vancomycin

### Drugs causing hyperkalaemia
- Suxamethonium
- ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone
- NSAIDs

## Analgesics

### Simple analgesics
- Paracetamol: safe
- NSAIDs: avoid, even in anuric patients
  - Prostaglandin inhibition removes intrinsic renal vasodilation and worsens hypertension, ischaemic heart disease, oedema, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia and upper GI bleeding

### Opioids (CNS more sensitive)
- Safe:
  - Fentanyl, sufentanil, alfentanil: hepatic metabolism, no active metabolites; good choices, no dose change
  - Remifentanil: broken down by non-specific plasma esterases to inactive metabolites; no dose change
  - Methadone: faecal (10-45%) and urinary (20-50%) excretion, no active metabolite; generally safe, no dose change
  - Buprenorphine: extensive hepatic metabolism; no dose reduction currently advised
- Caution or reduce dose:
  - Oxycodone: active metabolite oxymorphone accumulates; better than morphine but not as good as fentanyl
  - Tramadol: active metabolite O-desmethyltramadol accumulates, causing respiratory depression and lower seizure threshold; avoid in seizure disorders
    - Adjust dose: e.g. start 25mg BD if eGFR <30, not more than 50mg BD
- Avoid:
  - Morphine: metabolised to normorphine (4%), morphine-3-glucuronide (55%) and the active morphine-6-glucuronide (10%), all renally excreted
    - M6G accumulates, causing respiratory depression, hypotension and narcolepsy
    - Avoid in CKD (CrCl <30 mL/min); M6G is removed by haemodialysis but not peritoneal dialysis
  - Codeine: unpredictable hepatic metabolism to codeine-6-glucuronide (81%), norcodeine (2%), morphine (0.6%), M3G (2%), M6G (0.8%) and morphine (2.4%)
    - Active metabolites: morphine and M6G
    - Avoid in CKD (CrCl <30 mL/min)
  - Pethidine (meperidine): active metabolite normeperidine accumulates; neurotoxic, lowers seizure threshold
    - Contraindicated in CKD and dialysis
  - Hydromorphone: hepatic metabolism to the active metabolite hydromorphone-3-glucuronide (36.8%), which accumulates and is neuroexcitatory; thought to have no analgesic action
    - Hydrocodone: parent drug and active metabolite accumulate in renal failure; metabolised to hydromorphone, then on to hydromorphone-3-glucuronide

### Adjuncts
- Gabapentin and pregabalin: safe, but adjust dose
  - Gabapentin: start 100mg at night, max 300mg daily in renal failure
- Tricyclics: no dose change needed

## Pain management in renal failure

- Considerations:
  - Neuropathic pain is common (diabetic neuropathy, critical limb ischaemia)
  - Account for comorbidities
  - CNS more sensitive to opioids in severe CKD
- Management:
  - Avoid nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs)
  - Check current renal function (eGFR) and dose accordingly
  - Watch for opioid sedation
  - Biopsychosocial approach
  - Multimodal analgesia; regional techniques where possible

## Antimicrobials on renal replacement therapy

- Drugs cleared only by the liver need no change
- All others need a lower dose, a longer interval, or both
- Vancomycin and macrolides may need peak and/or trough levels
- Examples:
  - No change: ciprofloxacin, rifampicin, fusidic acid, metronidazole, azole antifungals, azithromycin
  - Lower dose: aztreonam (by 50–70%), aciclovir, zidovudine
  - Longer interval: cephalosporins, teicoplanin, erythromycin, clarithromycin
  - Both: meropenem, imipenem/cilastatin

## Renally adjusted antimicrobial doses

"<30" etc. = renal function threshold (ml/min); "6H" = every 6 hours.

### Beta-lactams
- IV cloxacillin: 2g 6H
- IV ampicillin: 1 to 2g 6H; <30: 1 to 2g 12H; <10: 1 to 2g 24H
- IV Augmentin: 1.2g 8H; <30: 1.2g 12H; <10: 1.2g 24H
- Oral Augmentin: 625mg 8H (500mg amoxicillin + 125 clavulanate); <30: 625mg 12H; <10: 625mg 24H
- Oral cefalexin: 0.5 to 1g 6H; <30: 0.5 to 1g 8H; <10: 0.5 to 1g 12H
- IV cefazolin: 1 to 2g 8H; <30: 1 to 2g 12H; <10: 1 to 2g 24H
- IV ceftriaxone: 1 or 2g 24H (no renal adjustment)
- IV ceftazidime: 2g 8H; <50: 2g 12H; <30: 2g 24H; <10: 1g 24H
- IV cefepime: 2g 8H; <50: 2g 12H; <30: 2g 24H; <10: 1g 24H
- IV Tazocin: 4.5g 6-8H; <40: 2.25g 6H; <20: 2.25g 8H; ESRF on haemodialysis: 2.25g 8-12H
- IV ertapenem: 1g 24H; <50: 1g 24H; <30: 500mg 24H
- IV imipenem: 500mg 6H; <50: 250mg 6H; <30: 250mg 8H; <10: 250mg 12H
- IV meropenem: 1g 8H; <50: 1g 12H; <30: 500mg 12H; <10: 500mg 24H

### Vancomycin (IV)
- Load 30mg/kg
- 15-20mg/kg 12H; <50: 15mg/kg 24H; <30: 15mg/kg 48H; <10: 15mg/kg 96H

### Others
- Oral clindamycin: 300-450 mg QDS (no renal adjustment)
- IV clindamycin: 600 -900 mg 6-8H (no renal adjustment)
- IV levofloxacin: 750mg 24H; <50: 750mg 48H; <30: 500mg 48H
- Oral levofloxacin: 500mg 24H; <50: 500mg 48H; <30: 250mg 48H
- IV ciprofloxacin: 400mg 12H; <30: 400mg 24H
- Oral ciprofloxacin: 500mg 12H; <30: 500mg 24H
- Oral clarithromycin: 500mg 12H; <30: 250mg 12H
- Oral or IV doxycycline: 100mg every 12 hours (Q12H) (no renal adjustment)
- IV tigecycline: 100mg loading, then 50mg Q12H
- IV metronidazole: 500mg 8H (no renal adjustment)
- Oral metronidazole: 400-600 mg 8H (no renal adjustment)
- IV gentamicin: 5mg/kg 24H; <50: 3mg/kg 24H; <30: 4mg/kg 48H; <10: 2mg/kg 72H
- IV amikacin: 15mg/kg 24H; <50: 5mg/kg 24H; <30: 5mg/kg 48H; <10: 3mg/kg 72H

## Flashcards
- Q: Above what CrCl is dose adjustment usually unnecessary?
  - A: CrCl >60 (except NSAIDs)
- Q: When is sugammadex not recommended in renal impairment?
  - A: eGFR < 30 mL/min
- Q: Above what potassium is suxamethonium contraindicated?
  - A: K > 5.5 mmol/L
- Q: By how much should the maximum local anaesthetic dose be reduced in renal impairment?
  - A: By 25%
- Q: Which opioids are safe without dose change in renal failure?
  - A: Fentanyl, sufentanil, alfentanil, remifentanil (also methadone, buprenorphine)
