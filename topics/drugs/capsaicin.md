# Capsaicin

Topical analgesic adjunct; TRPV1 agonist that first excites, then exhausts, nociceptive fibres.

## Presentation

- Patch 8%
- Cream

## Administration

- Patch applied for 60 minutes
- Expensive, and application is quite painful

## Mechanism of action

- Potent, highly selective agonist at TRPV1 (transient receptor potential vanilloid 1)
  - Found mainly on peripheral endings of nociceptive C and A-delta fibres
- Initial excitation: nerve depolarises and releases substance P (pain transmission, neurogenic inflammation)
  - Causes the early burning feeling
- Desensitisation: repeated or high-dose exposure (such as the 8% patch) exhausts the fibres
  - Pain transmission falls, giving relief

## Flashcards
- Q: What receptor does capsaicin act on?
  - A: TRPV1 (transient receptor potential vanilloid 1), as a selective agonist
- Q: How long is the 8% capsaicin patch applied?
  - A: 60 minutes
- Q: Why does capsaicin first cause burning?
  - A: Initial excitation releases substance P
- Q: How does capsaicin give pain relief?
  - A: Repeated or high-dose exposure desensitises and exhausts nociceptive fibres
