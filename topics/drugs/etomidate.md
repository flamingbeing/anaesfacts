# Etomidate

Imidazole induction agent with the least cardiovascular depression, at the cost of transient adrenal suppression.

## Presentation

- 0.2% solution at pH 4.1 with 35% v/v propylene glycol (improves stability, reduces injection irritation)
- Lipid emulsion formulation also available (e.g. Lipuro)
- 20mg in 10ml (2mg/ml); given neat
- Still used occasionally in the UK; remains widely available and used in North America, e.g. for emergency RSI

## Dosage

- Induction: 0.2-0.3mg/kg
  - 0.3mg/kg is the usual dose, e.g. 20mg for a 70kg adult
  - Other sources: 0.2-0.5mg/kg
- Onset 30-45sec (rapid)
- Duration 5-10min (short); time to reorientation 20min

## Mechanism of action

- Imidazole ester (imidazole derivative)
- Enhances GABA transmission

## Effects

- Cardiovascular: least disturbance of the common IV induction agents; less hypotension
  - Contractility and blood pressure largely unchanged
  - Peripheral vascular resistance may fall slightly (less than with other agents)
  - Myocardial oxygen supply largely unchanged
  - Safer than propofol in haemodynamic instability: trauma, elderly, severe hypovolaemia, patients on vasopressors
- Respiratory: less respiratory depression; less blunting of upper airway reflexes; may cause hiccups
- CNS: may lower seizure threshold (relevant in ECT, where it may lengthen seizures)

## Adverse effects

- Nausea and vomiting
- Myoclonus and excitatory movements (20%)
- Pain on injection (up to 25%)
- Transient adrenal suppression
  - Inhibits 11β-hydroxylase and 17α-hydroxylase, blocking cortisol and aldosterone synthesis
  - Infusions for sedation in septic ICU patients were linked to higher mortality
  - Single doses affect adrenal function but probably matter little in otherwise fit patients
  - Its best haemodynamic profile is in sick patients, who are also the most harmed by steroid suppression; rarely used for elective induction
- May precipitate a porphyric crisis
- Hypersensitivity less common than with other agents; histamine release rare
- Its side effects have kept its role limited despite its attractive profile

## Contraindications

- Avoid in sepsis (adrenal suppression)

## Indications

- Induction in haemodynamically unstable patients

## Pharmacokinetics

- 75% bound to albumin
- Effect ends by rapid redistribution to tissues and rapid hepatic metabolism
- Elimination by hepatic metabolism and renal excretion
  - Non-specific hepatic esterases (and possibly plasma cholinesterase) hydrolyse it to ethyl alcohol and a carboxylic acid metabolite
  - May inhibit plasma cholinesterase
- Kinetics not altered in renal or hepatic disease
