# Failed Regional Anaesthesia

Pause, find the cause and the surgical stage, then repeat, supplement or convert to GA.

## Causes
- Anaesthetic: inadequate block (patchy or incomplete)
- Surgical: surgery outlasts the block (single-shot spinal), e.g. unexpected technical difficulty
- Patient
  - Cannot keep still despite a working block: delirious, uncooperative, anxious
  - Loss of consciousness from a complication

## Management
- Tell the surgeon; pause the operation
- Find the reason: no block, patchy block, slow onset, receding block, anxiety
  - ABC; exclude complications such as LAST or high spinal
  - Ask where the pain is
- Consider the surgical stage: if closure is near, local infiltration or a little IV supplement may do
- Options
  - Repeat the block if surgery has not started
    - Check the maximum LA dose: risk of LA toxicity
    - A repeat neuraxial dose may spread too far (high spinal): consider CSE instead
    - Consider another technique, e.g. spinal for a failed peripheral nerve block
  - Supplement with IV analgesia, e.g. ketamine, fentanyl
    - Reassurance or sedation if the problem is distress rather than pain
  - Surgeon adds LA to a patchy block, staying under the maximum dose
  - Convert to GA
    - Positioning may be hard once set up for surgery
    - Neuraxial sympathectomy plus GA worsens hypotension
    - Plan postoperative analgesia
- Communicate with surgeon and patient: keeps trust and lowers PTSD risk
- Document for medicolegal reasons

## Failed spinal
- Anaesthetic mechanisms
  - Lumbar puncture failure: subarachnoid space not reached
  - Injection failure: wrong needle tip position or migration, e.g. orifice into the epidural or subdural space
- Patient mechanisms
  - Spread failure from anatomical barriers: kyphosis, scoliosis, spinal stenosis, septae
  - Tarlov cysts (incidence up to 9%): CSF aspirates but no block follows
- Psychological: anxiety or distress mistaken for block failure
- Prevention
  - Higher LA doses
  - CSE when duration is unpredictable
  - Sensory testing; test the skin before incision

## Failed peripheral nerve block
- Often from not waiting long enough
- Injection outside the paraneural sheath, or into a vessel or a fascial plane not connected to the target nerve
- Too little volume for fascial plane blocks
- Anatomical variation: accessory nerves, e.g. obturator sparing in some lumbar plexus blocks

## Flashcards
- Q: First steps when a block fails intraoperatively?
  - A: Tell the surgeon, pause, find the cause (exclude LAST or high spinal, ask where the pain is) and the surgical stage.
- Q: Risk of repeating a spinal after a failed one, and an alternative?
  - A: Excessive spread (high spinal); consider CSE instead.
- Q: What is the incidence of Tarlov cysts and why do they matter?
  - A: Up to 9%; CSF can be aspirated without a block following.
- Q: Most common cause of a failed peripheral nerve block?
  - A: Not waiting long enough.
