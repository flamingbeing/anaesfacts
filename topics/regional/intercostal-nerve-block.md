# Intercostal Nerve Block

Block of the intercostal nerve under the rib, lateral to the costal angle; gives ipsilateral sensory and motor block at each level injected, with high LA absorption.

## Anatomy
- Nerve runs in the neurovascular bundle just below the rib; it is the lowest structure in the bundle
- Lateral to the costal angle there are 3 muscle layers: external, internal and innermost intercostal
  - Bundle runs between the internal and innermost intercostal muscles
  - From the costal angle onwards, the main bundle lies in the costal groove (lower edge of the upper rib); a collateral bundle runs along the upper edge of the lower rib
  - Main bundle order from top to bottom: vein, artery, nerve (VAN)
  - Innermost intercostal (intercostalis intimus) is flimsy and lets LA diffuse readily
  - Parietal pleura lies deep to the innermost layer
- Medial to the costal angle there is only the external intercostal muscle and the internal intercostal membrane (no internal intercostal muscle), so block lateral to the costal angle
- Lateral cutaneous branch arises at the midaxillary line, so block proximal to it
- Costal groove is deepest just lateral to the rib angle, so in theory this is the safest site

## Indications and efficacy
- Sensory and motor block at the levels injected, ipsilateral only
- Covers skin, muscle and parietal peritoneum; upper abdominal surgery needs extra cover for visceral pain
- Suits thoracic and upper abdominal procedures
- Bupivacaine or lidocaine with adrenaline lasts on average 12 hours

## Complications
- Pneumothorax (<1%)
- LAST: absorption from this site is high; add adrenaline to reduce systemic uptake
- Haematoma
- Spinal or epidural spread

## Landmark technique
- Position: sitting (lateral or prone also possible)
- Count to the right intercostal spaces
- Find the angle of the rib, usually about 7 cm from the midline; the block can be done anywhere proximal to the midaxillary line
- Pull the skin from the groove up over the rib
- 22G, 50-mm needle at 20° cephalad; contact rib within about 1 cm
- Walk off the lower edge of the rib, keeping the 20° cephalad angle
- Nerve usually lies less than 3 mm deeper than the rib; a pop is often felt with a short bevel
- Inject 5 mL per level; stay within the maximum dose of the chosen LA

## Ultrasound technique
- Helps find the spaces, especially in obesity or difficult anatomy
- High-frequency linear probe placed vertically (sagittal) on the back to see rib, space and pleura
- Scan lateral to the costal angle but posterior to the posterior axillary line, before the bundle divides
- Sonoanatomy
  - Bundles (main and collateral) lie between internal and innermost intercostal muscles
  - Innermost layer is often not seen, so find the internal intercostal muscle as the key layer
  - Nerves themselves are often not visible; collateral bundle vessels at the top of the lower rib may show on colour Doppler
  - Parietal pleura: bright hyperechoic line sliding with breathing, with comet tail artefacts deep to it
- Needle: 50mm, in-plane, caudal to cephalad, into the space lateral to the costal angle and behind the posterior axillary line
  - Pass through external and internal intercostal muscles
  - Best endpoint: within the internal intercostal muscle, keeping the tip superficial to the pleura
- 2ml is usually enough

## Flashcards
- Q: Where should an intercostal block be done along the rib?
  - A: Lateral to the costal angle (about 7 cm from midline) and proximal to the midaxillary line
- Q: Order of structures in the main intercostal bundle, top to bottom?
  - A: Vein, artery, nerve (VAN)
- Q: Landmark needle and volume?
  - A: 22G 50-mm needle at 20° cephalad, walked off the lower rib edge; 5 mL per level
- Q: Incidence of pneumothorax?
  - A: Less than 1%
- Q: Why add adrenaline to intercostal blocks?
  - A: High systemic absorption from this site; adrenaline reduces it
