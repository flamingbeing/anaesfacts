# Intrathecal Local Anaesthetics, Opioids and Adjuncts

Spinal drugs by duration, how intrathecal opioids behave, and the common adjuncts, with doses.

- All intrathecal drugs are preservative-free, as preservatives are neurotoxic

## Long-acting local anaesthetics
- For procedures >90 min

### Tetracaine
- Longest-acting spinal local anaesthetic
- Similar to bupivacaine but with denser motor block
- Vasoconstrictors prolong it
- Onset slow: 5-10 min
- Duration: 90-150 min

### Bupivacaine
- Onset slow: 5-10 min (isobaric may be slower)
- Duration: 90-150 min, i.e. 1.5-2.5 hr (depends on dose and block height)
  - 2-dermatome regression: 90-140 min
  - Complete resolution: 240-380 min
  - In practice often lasts 2-3 hrs
  - Low dose (e.g. 5 mg) recovers about as fast as lidocaine
- Dose: 5-20 mg
  - Lumbar or lower limb: 4-8 mg
  - T10: 10-15 mg
  - T4: 12-20 mg
- Preparations
  - Plain: isobaric at 24°C, slightly hypobaric at 37°C (Miller)
  - Hyperbaric: with 8% dextrose
- Advantage: low rate of transient neurological symptoms (TNS)

### Levobupivacaine
- Pure S-isomer; less cardiotoxic, but little benefit for spinals
- Slightly less potent than bupivacaine

### Ropivacaine
- E.g. obstetric CSE
- Diluted in water it becomes hypobaric
- About 0.6 the potency of bupivacaine for spinals (Miller)

## Short to intermediate-acting local anaesthetics
- For procedures <90 min

### Prilocaine
- 2% hyperbaric solution (Takipril)
- Faster offset than bupivacaine; duration similar to lidocaine
- Less TNS than lidocaine
- Dose: same volume as you would use for bupivacaine
  - Saddle block: 0.5-1 ml
  - Below the umbilicus or T10: 2-3 ml (40-60 mg); average duration 100-130 min
    - Between 40 and 60 mg suits lower limb and lower abdominal surgery lasting up to 90 min
  - Above T10: 3 ml
  - Maximum recommended: 4 ml
- Not recommended in children

### Lidocaine
- Onset fast: 3-5 min
- Duration: plain 60-75 min; with adrenaline 60-90 min
- Dose: lower limb 25-50 mg; lower abdomen 50-75 mg; upper abdomen 75-100 mg
- Avoid intrathecally because of TNS
  - Low back pain and dysaesthesia radiating to buttocks, thighs and legs after the block wears off
  - About 14% of patients after lidocaine spinal

## Short-acting local anaesthetic
### Procaine
- Onset fast: 3-5 min
- Duration: plain 45 min; with adrenaline 60 min
- Dose: lower limb 75 mg; lower abdomen 125 mg; upper abdomen 200 mg
- Advantage: less TNS than lidocaine
- Limitations
  - More nausea and vomiting
  - Higher failure rate
  - Neurotoxicity concerns limit use
  - Slow full recovery despite short action

## Pharmacokinetics in the subarachnoid space
- Effect needs uptake into nerve tissue (mainly nerve roots and superficial cord), which depends on
  - (1) CSF concentration: dose and distance from the injection site
  - (2) Surface area of nerve tissue bathed in CSF
  - (3) Lipid content: heavily myelinated tissue takes up more
  - (4) Diffusion of the drug
- Spread within the subarachnoid space sets block height
- Elimination by vascular absorption
  - Depends on perfusion of the subarachnoid space and nerve tissue; reduced by adrenaline and by some local anaesthetics (e.g. bupivacaine, lidocaine)

## Intrathecal opioids
### Kinetics
- Give high, dose-dependent CSF levels immediately
- Spinal cord penetration depends on lipid solubility
  - Less lipophilic drugs (morphine): slower onset, longer action
- Morphine is not metabolised in the CNS
- Elimination mainly by reabsorption through the arachnoid granulations
  - Morphine leaves the intrathecal space more slowly than after epidural or IM dosing, prolonging analgesia
  - Some vascular uptake occurs but matters little clinically

### Morphine versus fentanyl
- Morphine enters the cord slowly and lingers in CSF, so much of it moves cephalad
  - Analgesia covers more dermatomes
  - Delayed respiratory depression from brainstem binding
- Fentanyl enters the cord quickly, so little moves cephalad

### Clinical effects
- Analgesia
- Respiratory depression (prolonged with morphine)
- Nausea
- Pruritus: spinal 46% versus epidural 8.5%
  - Often around the nose and face
  - Severity rises with intrathecal morphine dose but not epidural dose
  - Treat with naloxone 20 mcg, then 40 mcg every 5 min titrated; should not bring back pain, as the ED50 for itch is far lower than for analgesia

### Fentanyl
- Dose: 10-25 mcg
- Onset: 5 min (Chestnut)
- Duration: 10 min (Chestnut)
- Duration of action: 2-3 hrs (Chestnut)
- Better intraoperative analgesia; larger doses needed to lengthen the block
- Respiratory depression risk very low and brief: suitable for day cases

### Morphine
- Dose: 50-200 mcg for caesarean section; higher doses used for major surgery
- Onset: 30-60 min (Chestnut), faster than epidural
- Peak: 60-90 min (Chestnut), same as epidural
- Duration of action: up to 24 hrs (Miller)
- Good postoperative analgesia but risk of delayed respiratory depression (not for day cases)

### Intrathecal morphine without local anaesthetic
- Advantages
  - Reliable, superior analgesia after major surgery
  - Little sensory, motor or autonomic effect compared with epidural; no high-dependency care needed for an epidural
  - In long surgery may give some intraoperative analgesia and haemodynamic stability
- Disadvantages
  - Risks of dural puncture, including PDPH
  - Delayed respiratory depression, pruritus; avoid in day surgery, caution in the elderly

## Other adjuncts
### Alpha-2 agonists
- Act on dorsal horn alpha-2 receptors
- Clonidine 25-50 mcg
  - Prolongs block by about 1 hr and improves postoperative analgesia
  - Sedation peaks within 1-2 hrs and lasts up to 8 hrs
- Dexmedetomidine 3-5 mcg (about 10-fold more selective)
  - 3 mcg can prolong the block without haemodynamic upset
- Side effects: hypotension, bradycardia, sedation

### Vasoconstrictors
- Adrenaline (0.1–0.2 mg) or phenylephrine (2–5 mg) prolong the block by reducing local anaesthetic uptake
- Adrenaline also adds analgesia via alpha-2 agonism
- Theoretical risk of spinal cord ischaemia with adrenaline in spinals
- Phenylephrine raises TNS risk and may lower block height

### Steroids
- Dexamethasone 4 mg

### Others
- Neostigmine 10-50 mcg
  - Anticholinesterase; raises spinal cord acetylcholine (antinociceptive)
  - Prolongs block and improves analgesia, but side effects limit use: nausea and vomiting, bradycardia, anxiety or agitation
- Midazolam 1-2 mg
- Ketamine

## Flashcards
- Q: Spinal hyperbaric bupivacaine dose for a T4 block?
  - A: 12-20 mg (T10: 10-15 mg; lower limb 4-8 mg)
- Q: Prilocaine 2% hyperbaric dose for surgery below T10, and maximum?
  - A: 2-3 ml (40-60 mg); maximum 4 ml
- Q: Why avoid intrathecal lidocaine?
  - A: Transient neurological symptoms in about 14%
- Q: Naloxone regimen for neuraxial opioid itch?
  - A: 20 mcg, then 40 mcg every 5 min titrated
- Q: Intrathecal clonidine dose and effect?
  - A: 25-50 mcg; prolongs block by about 1 hr; sedation up to 8 hrs
