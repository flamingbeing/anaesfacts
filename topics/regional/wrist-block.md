# Wrist Block

Blocks of the median, ulnar and superficial radial nerves at the wrist or forearm for hand and finger surgery.

## Overview
- Indication: hand and finger surgery
- Probe: transverse at the wrist crease or distal third of the forearm
- Goal: LA beside the median and ulnar nerves and the superficial (sensory) branch of the radial nerve
- Total volume: 10–15 mL
- Anaesthetises the whole hand except the deep radial branch territory
- Often done 5–10 cm above the wrist crease:
  - More space; the wrist is crowded and bounded by bone on three sides
  - Catches the palmar branches of the median and ulnar nerves, which leave a few centimetres above the crease
- In-plane or out-of-plane; choose by ergonomics
- Ulnar and radial nerves run with arteries: arterial puncture can cause haematoma
- Circumferential spread usually not needed: nerves are small with thin epineurium, so LA diffuses in quickly
- 3–4 mL per nerve is enough if placed right next to the nerve

## Median nerve
- Course: medial to the brachial artery at the elbow; deep to flexor digitorum superficialis mid-forearm; becomes superficial near the wrist; enters the carpal tunnel under the flexor retinaculum with the long flexor tendons (FDP, FDS, flexor pollicis longus)
- At the wrist crease: a cluster of oval bright structures; nerve and tendons are easily confused
- Slide 5–10 cm proximally on the volar forearm: tendons give way to muscle, leaving the median nerve on its own; trace back down if wanted
- Often simplest to block at mid-forearm, where it is easiest to recognise

## Ulnar nerve
- From mid-forearm to wrist it lies on the ulnar (medial) side of the ulnar artery: a useful landmark
- At the wrist crease: bright ulna surface with shadow; just lateral to it and very superficial, the triangular or oval bright nerve beside the pulsating artery
- Flexor carpi ulnaris tendon lies superficial to the nerve here; scanning proximally separates them
- Slide up and down the forearm following the artery to confirm the nerve on its ulnar side

## Radial nerve (superficial branch)
- Splits into terminal branches at the wrist, so ultrasound is of little help there
- At the wrist: subcutaneous field block around the radial styloid works well
- With ultrasound: at the elbow or mid-forearm, a thin bright structure lateral to the radial artery, above the radius
- Leaves the antebrachial fascia between the brachioradialis and extensor carpi radialis tendons

## Flashcards
- Q: Total LA volume for a wrist block, and per nerve?
  - A: 10–15 mL total; 3–4 mL per nerve.
- Q: Why block 5–10 cm above the wrist crease?
  - A: More room, and it catches the palmar branches of the median and ulnar nerves.
- Q: Which area does a wrist block miss?
  - A: The territory of the deep branch of the radial nerve.
- Q: Where is the ulnar nerve relative to the ulnar artery in the forearm?
  - A: On its ulnar (medial) side.
- Q: Best way to block the radial nerve at the wrist?
  - A: Subcutaneous field block around the radial styloid.
