# Ilioinguinal and Iliohypogastric Nerve Block

Block of the two L1 nerves between internal oblique and transversus abdominis near the ASIS, for inguinal surgery; landmark (mainly children) or ultrasound-guided.

## Indications
- Herniotomy and inguinal hernia repair
- Orchidopexy (with a low scrotal incision, add infiltration or a pudendal block)
- Varicocele ligation
- Hydrocele
- Other inguinal surgery; analgesia after suprapubic incision

## Contraindications
- General contraindications to regional blocks
- Relative: obstructed hernia

## Anatomy
- Both nerves come from the ventral primary ramus of L1; iliohypogastric runs above ilioinguinal
- At the level of the ASIS they lie between internal oblique and transversus abdominis
- At or anterior to the ASIS, the iliohypogastric pierces internal oblique to lie under external oblique
- Distance from ASIS to the nerves does not depend on age or weight; cadaver and ultrasound studies put the nerves closer to the ASIS than most traditional techniques assume

## Landmark technique (mainly children)
- Many described methods and a 10-25% failure rate: not an easy block, partly due to anatomical variation
- Supine
- Needle perpendicular to skin, 1cm medial and inferior to the ASIS (or 1 of the patient's fingerbreadths)
  - Alternative: 2.5 mm medial along the line from ASIS to umbilicus
- Two clicks (external then internal oblique) may be felt, but often only one
  - External oblique is often already aponeurotic here; 50% of patients have only two muscles at the ASIS level
  - Nerve to peritoneum distance averages 3.3 mm, so beware intraperitoneal injection
- Inject 0.25% levobupivacaine 0.4 (0.3-0.5) mL/kg slowly
  - Ultrasound allows smaller volumes, possibly lowering plasma LA levels and the risk of femoral nerve block
- Keep anaesthesia deep until peritoneal, spermatic cord and testicular handling is over: the block does not cover these stimuli

## Ultrasound-guided technique (anterior TAP block)
- Probe oblique on the abdomen, on the line from ASIS to umbilicus
- Identify the three abdominal wall muscles (as for lateral TAP)
  - External oblique may be aponeurotic
  - Iliacus sometimes visible below transversus abdominis
  - Transversus abdominis lies just above the deep circumflex iliac artery
- Needle lateral to medial, tip between internal oblique and transversus abdominis
- Aim: spread in that plane close to the two nerves
- Volume: 10-15 mL per side in adults; 0.15 mL/kg per side in children (similar to rectus sheath block)

## Complications
- Femoral nerve block in up to 11%; test for it before discharge
  - Transversalis and iliacus fascia are continuous, so LA injected under or into transversus abdominis can track to the femoral nerve
- Intraperitoneal injection: block failure, bowel perforation

## Flashcards
- Q: Landmark entry point for paediatric ilioinguinal block?
  - A: 1 cm medial and inferior to the ASIS (about one patient fingerbreadth)
- Q: Paediatric landmark dose?
  - A: 0.25% levobupivacaine 0.4 (0.3-0.5) mL/kg
- Q: Incidence of femoral nerve block after ilioinguinal block?
  - A: Up to 11%; test before discharge
- Q: Ultrasound block volume per side?
  - A: Adults 10-15 mL; children 0.15 mL/kg
- Q: Which plane holds the nerves at the ASIS?
  - A: Between internal oblique and transversus abdominis
