# Digital Ring Block and Metacarpal Block

Block of the four digital nerves of a finger or toe, either at the base of the digit (ring block) or between the metacarpals or metatarsals.

## Indications
- Finger and toe surgery, e.g. nail bed repair

## Contraindications
- LA containing adrenaline

## Anatomy
- Finger digital nerves come from the radial and ulnar nerves dorsally and the median and ulnar nerves on the palmar side
- Each finger has dorsal and palmar nerves on each side, at 2, 5, 7, and 10 o'clock around the phalanx

## Digital ring block
- Insert the needle lateral to the extensor tendon
- Advance subcutaneously along the side of the phalanx until the palmar skin tents
- Aspirate, then inject LA while withdrawing
- Reinsert medial to the extensor tendon and repeat
- 0.25% levobupivacaine 1-2 mL/side

## Metacarpal or metatarsal block
- Used when surgery is at the base of the finger
- Needle at 90 degrees to the skin, between the metacarpals, about midway along the bone
- Advance until the palmar aponeurosis resists; aspirate, then inject while withdrawing
- Repeat on the other side of the finger

## Complications
- Digital ischaemia: prevent by avoiding adrenaline and limiting the injected volume

## Flashcards
- Q: Positions of the digital nerves around the phalanx?
  - A: 2, 5, 7 and 10 o'clock
- Q: Dose for a digital ring block?
  - A: 0.25% levobupivacaine 1-2 mL per side
- Q: How is digital ischaemia prevented?
  - A: No adrenaline in the LA and a limited volume
- Q: Where is the needle placed for a metacarpal block?
  - A: Between the metacarpals, about midway along the bone, at 90 degrees, to the palmar aponeurosis
