# Lateral Femoral Cutaneous Nerve Block

Superficial ultrasound-guided block of the LFCN between tensor fasciae latae and sartorius, for the lateral thigh.

## Anatomy
- LFCN splits into branches supplying the lateral and anterior thigh; in 45% of patients its supply reaches the anterior thigh
- Course is very variable, so landmark blocks are unreliable and sensory coverage varies; ultrasound places the needle in the right fascial plane

## Indications
- Analgesia after hip surgery
- Meralgia paraesthetica
- Muscle biopsy of the proximal lateral thigh
- Gives anaesthesia or analgesia of the anterolateral thigh

## Equipment
- Linear probe (18-6 MHz)
- 10 mL of local anaesthetic
- 3–5 cm, 22- to 24-gauge needle

## Technique
- Supine or lateral
- Probe 2 cm inferior and medial to the ASIS, parallel to the inguinal ligament
- Identify tensor fasciae latae (TFLM) and sartorius (SaM); see the lateral edge of sartorius
- LFCN usually lies between TFLM and SaM, 1–2 cm medial and inferior to the ASIS and 0.5–1.0 cm deep
- Needle in plane, lateral to medial, through subcutaneous tissue; a fascial "pop" or "click" may be felt passing through TFLM into the plane between TFLM and SaM
- Inject 1–2 mL to confirm position
- Correct spread: in the TFLM–SaM plane, or around the LFCN superficial to sartorius
- Volume: 5-10 mL in adults
- Optional check: nerve stimulator producing tingling in the lateral thigh

## Flashcards
- Q: Where is the probe placed for an LFCN block?
  - A: 2 cm inferior and medial to the ASIS, parallel to the inguinal ligament
- Q: Between which muscles does the LFCN usually lie?
  - A: Tensor fasciae latae and sartorius, 0.5–1.0 cm deep
- Q: Adult local anaesthetic volume for an LFCN block?
  - A: 5-10 mL
- Q: Name an indication other than hip surgery.
  - A: Meralgia paraesthetica, or proximal lateral thigh muscle biopsy
