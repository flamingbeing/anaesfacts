# Upper Limb Blocks Introduction

Joint innervation of the arm and when to choose selective terminal or cutaneous nerve blocks over a full brachial plexus block.

## Joint innervation
- Shoulder (glenohumeral): axillary and suprascapular nerves
  - Hence an interscalene block, done above where the suprascapular nerve branches off, is the best choice for shoulder anaesthesia
- Elbow: musculocutaneous, radial, median and ulnar nerves
  - Branches of every major brachial plexus nerve that crosses the joint
- Wrist (radiocarpal, ulnocarpal): median, ulnar and radial nerves

## Indications for selective (cutaneous or terminal) nerve blocks
- Most upper limb regional anaesthesia uses the brachial plexus, but single nerves or branches are sometimes blocked on their own
- Three situations:
  - Surgical site partly supplied by nerves outside the plexus, or not reliably covered by plexus blocks
    - Supraclavicular, suprascapular and intercostobrachial nerves
  - A whole plexus block is not needed for the operation
    - Block terminal nerves (radial, median, ulnar) or their branches (lateral and medial antebrachial cutaneous) distally at the elbow
  - Rescue of an incomplete brachial plexus block (controversial)

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerves supply the glenohumeral joint?
  - A: Axillary and suprascapular nerves.
- Q: Why is the interscalene level preferred for shoulder anaesthesia?
  - A: It blocks the plexus before the suprascapular nerve branches off.
- Q: Which nerves supply the elbow joint?
  - A: Musculocutaneous, radial, median and ulnar.
- Q: Name three nerves supplying upper limb surgical sites that plexus blocks may miss.
  - A: Supraclavicular, suprascapular and intercostobrachial.
