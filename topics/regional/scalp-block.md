# Scalp Block

Blocks the trigeminal and cervical plexus branches that supply the scalp, by ring infiltration, band blocks or individual nerve injections; includes the greater occipital nerve block.

## Anatomy
- Scalp layers (SCALP): Skin, Connective tissue (cutaneous vessels and nerves), Aponeurosis, Loose areolar tissue, Pericranium
- Trigeminal nerve (CN V):
  - V1 (ophthalmic): supratrochlear and supraorbital nerves, forehead
  - V2 (maxillary): zygomaticotemporal nerve, temporal scalp
  - V3 (mandibular): auriculotemporal nerve, temporal scalp
- Cervical plexus:
  - Lesser occipital nerve (C2-3): lateral occipital scalp
  - Greater occipital nerve (C2-3): occipital scalp

## The 6 nerves (GLASS Z), plus or minus greater auricular
- Greater occipital: at the occipital protuberance medial to the occipital artery, infiltrating subcutaneously towards the mastoid above the superior nuchal ridge
- Lesser occipital: at the mastoid process
- Auriculotemporal: at the tragus, behind the superficial temporal artery
- Supratrochlear: at the bridge of the nose
- Supraorbital: at the supraorbital notch (upper orbital rim in line with the pupil, eyes forward)
- Zygomaticotemporal: at the zygomaticofacial foramen (lateral orbital border)
- With or without greater auricular nerve

## Drugs
- Lignocaine 0.75% + bupivacaine 0.25% + 1:200,000 adrenaline

## Ring (infiltration) block
- All scalp nerves become superficial and reachable above a line from the **occipital protuberance to the eyebrows** running **along the upper border of the ear**
- Infiltrate **circumferentially** along this line with local anaesthetic plus 1:200,000 adrenaline
- Needs about **30 mL**

## Band blocks (alternative)
- Anterior band: supratrochlear, supraorbital, auriculotemporal, zygomaticotemporal
  - From the supraorbital ridge along the zygomatic arch to the tragus
- Posterior band: greater and lesser occipital
  - From the hollow lateral to the occipital protuberance on the superior nuchal line to behind the ear

## Individual nerve technique (7 injections)
- Anatomical block rather than a ring block
- Supraorbital (V1): forehead, anterior scalp, top of head
  - **Feel the supraorbital notch**, insert just **above** it, perpendicular to the eyebrow
- Supratrochlear (V1): forehead and anterior scalp
  - Just **medial** to the supraorbital injection
- Zygomaticotemporal (V2): small area of forehead and temple
  - Runs through temporalis to reach the fascia, so infiltrate both deep and superficial to the muscle
  - Infiltrate **lateral to the supraorbital margin, continuing behind the distal zygomatic arch**
  - Hardest nerve to block
- Auriculotemporal (V3): temple, lower lip, lower face, auricle and nearby scalp
  - Lies close to the facial nerve, so inject higher
  - 1cm in front of and 1cm above the tragus, feel the **superficial temporal artery; inject behind it, depositing local anaesthetic beneath it**
- Greater auricular (branch of 2nd and 3rd cervical nerves), optional:
  - Skin over parotid, mastoid and auricle
  - Emerges from the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid; splits into anterior and posterior branches
  - Usually **covered by the lesser occipital injection**, 2cm behind the auricle at tragus level
- Lesser occipital (branch of 2nd or 3rd cervical nerve):
  - Lateral scalp behind the auricle; runs up the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid
  - Infiltrate **behind the auricle** from the top of the ear down to the lobule, then **posteriorly along the superior nuchal line**
- Greater occipital (branch of 2nd cervical nerve):
  - Posterior scalp, sometimes the vertex and over the auricle
  - Arises at the level of the 1st and 2nd cervical vertebrae
  - Feel the occipital artery 3-4cm from the occipital protuberance **along the superior nuchal line**; inject **medial to the artery**

## Greater occipital nerve block
- Anatomy:
  - From the C2 root, between atlas and axis
  - Runs up between obliquus capitis inferior and semispinalis capitis, then pierces semispinalis
  - Becomes subcutaneous through the trapezius aponeurosis just below the superior nuchal line, usually just medial to the occipital artery
  - Supplies most of the posterior scalp, from the external occipital protuberance to the vertex
- Indications:
  - Analgesia after posterior craniotomy and ventriculoperitoneal shunt insertion or revision
  - Diagnosis and treatment of headache: primary, cervicogenic, migraine, occipital neuralgia, tension-type
- Landmark technique:
  - About **two-thirds of the way along a line from the mastoid centre to the external occipital protuberance** on the superior nuchal line, **medial to the occipital artery** (easily felt)
  - Pressing here may cause paraesthesia in the nerve's territory
  - 25- or 27-gauge needle depending on patient size
  - Needle at 90° to the occiput; aspirate, inject 1–3 mL
  - Press on the site after withdrawal to spread local anaesthetic and stop bleeding
  - Numbness over the top of the head confirms success
  - Position varies a lot: 1.5–7.5 cm from the midline at the level between the protuberance and mastoid
  - **Lesser occipital nerve lies 1/3rd of the way** along the same line
- Ultrasound technique:
  - Distal approach: probe transverse at the superior nuchal line, centred lateral to the external occipital protuberance
  - Proximal approach at C2 (described by Greher): the nerve lies superficial to obliquus capitis inferior, a constant relationship
    - Slide down from C1 to the C2 spinous process (always bifid), then laterally to find obliquus capitis inferior
    - The nerve crosses the muscle running upwards and medially
- Complications:
  - Few, because the nerve is superficial
  - Intravascular injection possible: aspirate first
  - Vertebral artery (lateral, deep to obliquus capitis inferior and the atlas lamina) and spinal cord (medial, deep to the muscle) are nearby

## Flashcards
- Q: Which 6 nerves are blocked in a scalp block (GLASS Z)?
  - A: Greater occipital, lesser occipital, auriculotemporal, supratrochlear, supraorbital, zygomaticotemporal
- Q: Volume for a ring block of the scalp?
  - A: About 30 mL with 1:200,000 adrenaline
- Q: Where is the auriculotemporal nerve blocked?
  - A: 1cm in front of and 1cm above the tragus, behind the superficial temporal artery
- Q: Landmark for the greater occipital nerve?
  - A: Two-thirds of the way from the mastoid to the external occipital protuberance on the superior nuchal line, medial to the occipital artery
- Q: Volume for a greater occipital nerve block?
  - A: 1–3 mL
