# Penile Nerve Block

Block of the dorsal penile nerves in the subpubic space (or a subcutaneous ring block) for circumcision and distal hypospadias surgery.

## Indications

- Circumcision
- Distal hypospadias repair (discuss with the surgeon)

## Contraindications

- Local anaesthetic containing adrenaline

## Anatomy

- Innervation
  - Dorsal penile nerves (S2-4) supply most of the penis
    - Terminal branch of the pudendal nerve
    - Gives off a ventral branch early in the subpubic space, supplying the ventral surface including the frenulum
  - Ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves supply the base
- Subpubic space
  - Triangular space deep to Scarpa's fascia; symphysis pubis above, corpora cavernosa below; Scarpa's fascia forms the front
  - The deep surface of the fascia forms the suspensory ligament, which splits around the shaft
  - The ligament divides the space into left and right compartments, which usually do not connect (only 6% do)
  - Dorsal nerves pass under the pubic ramus in this space
    - Dorsal arteries and the superficial and deep veins are midline
    - Dorsal nerves lie lateral to the vessels
  - Nerves and vessels sit deep to the suspensory ligament in a closed space, so a large haematoma could compress them
  - Ventral branches arise in the subpubic space
- In the shaft, dorsal nerves lie at the 10 and 2 o'clock positions

## Technique 1: subpubic (dorsal penile nerve) block

- Supine; find the symphysis pubis in the midline
- Pull the penis down so the subpubic skin is taut
- Inject on both sides of the suspensory ligament
  - Safest: two injections, each slightly lateral to the midline, equal volumes; avoids the midline dorsal vessels and haematoma
  - Alternative: single midline puncture, angling the needle to each side below the symphysis
- Needle perpendicular or 10° caudal until it contacts the symphysis (gives depth)
- Withdraw and redirect to pass below the symphysis
- Slight resistance as Scarpa's fascia is crossed into the subpubic space; inject after negative aspiration
  - Small children: fascia is thin, may not be felt
  - Fascia is usually crossed at 8-30 mm; depth does not depend on weight or age
  - An early slight pop may be felt through the superficial fascia
- Alternative depth guide: 3-5 millimetres deeper than the symphysis, depending on size
- Adding a ventral block improves success: subcutaneous injection at the penoscrotal junction

## Dosage

- Children (ATOTW): 1ml + 0.1ml/kg each side; adequate volume should also catch the ventral branch
- Levobupivacaine 0.25%: 0.1 mL/kg each side (0.2ml/kg total, 0.5mg/kg), maximum 5 mL each side
- Bupivacaine 0.25% also used
- Avoid ropivacaine: vasoconstriction, risk of ischaemia

## Technique 2: subcutaneous ring block

- Simpler
- Subcutaneous local anaesthetic around the base of the penis

## Complications

- Superficial haematoma
- Deep haematoma, possibly compromising blood supply and causing ischaemia

## Flashcards

- Q: Which local anaesthetic additive is contraindicated in a penile block?
  - A: Adrenaline
- Q: Levobupivacaine dose for a paediatric penile block?
  - A: 0.25%, 0.1 mL/kg each side (0.5mg/kg total), maximum 5 mL each side
- Q: Why inject either side of the midline rather than in it?
  - A: The dorsal vessels are midline; avoiding them reduces haematoma risk
- Q: Why avoid ropivacaine for penile block?
  - A: Vasoconstriction and risk of ischaemia
- Q: Where do the dorsal penile nerves lie in the shaft?
  - A: At 10 and 2 o'clock, lateral to the midline dorsal vessels
