# Peripheral nerve blocks in children

Children are almost always blocked under GA, which hides the warning signs of intravascular and intraneural injection.

## General points
- Nearly all paediatric blocks are done under general anaesthesia
  - Main worry: signs of intravascular or intraneural injection are masked
- Explain the expected sensation beforehand (e.g. "pins and needles"): children can find paraesthesia distressing
- Catheters are useful for long blocks; tunnelling largely prevents dislodgement
- Spot a failed block during surgery and add local infiltration or IV analgesia
- In recovery, pain and agitation are hard to tell apart: assume pain and treat it promptly

## Contraindications
- Absolute
  - Patient or parent refusal
  - Local infection
  - Local anaesthetic allergy
- Relative
  - Anticoagulation
  - Neuromuscular disease
  - Risk of masking compartment syndrome (discuss with the surgeon)

## Common blocks
- Penile nerve block
- Ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric block

## Flashcards
