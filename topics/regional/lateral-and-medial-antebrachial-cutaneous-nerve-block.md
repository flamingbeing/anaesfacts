# Lateral and Medial Antebrachial Cutaneous Nerve Block

Subcutaneous blocks at the elbow for forearm skin, for superficial forearm surgery or to rescue a patchy brachial plexus block.

## Indications
- Superficial forearm surgery, such as arteriovenous fistula creation
- Supplement to an incomplete brachial plexus block

## Anatomy
- Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LAC)
  - Main cutaneous branch of the musculocutaneous nerve
  - Supplies the lateral (radial) half of the volar forearm
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MAC)
  - Branch of the medial cord
  - Supplies the medial (ulnar) half of the volar forearm; often wrongly thought to be ulnar nerve territory

## Technique
- LAC: two injections along the intercondylar line
  - 5 mL just deep to the lateral edge of the biceps tendon
  - 5 mL subcutaneously, lateral to the first, along the elbow crease
- MAC: subcutaneous half-ring of 5–7 mL, about a quarter of the arm's length above the medial elbow

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerve gives rise to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve?
  - A: Musculocutaneous nerve
- Q: Which nerve supplies the skin of the medial volar forearm?
  - A: Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (from the medial cord), not the ulnar nerve
- Q: How is the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve blocked?
  - A: Two 5 mL injections on the intercondylar line: deep to the lateral biceps tendon edge, then subcutaneously lateral to it
- Q: How is the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve blocked?
  - A: Subcutaneous half-ring of 5–7 mL about a quarter of the arm length above the medial elbow
