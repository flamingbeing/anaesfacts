# Regional Anaesthesia

Blocking the nerves to a body region for surgical anaesthesia or analgesia; avoids many GA complications but has its own contraindications.

## Regional versus local anaesthesia
- Regional: LA around the nerve(s) supplying an area, so a specific part (e.g. arm, foot) is numb to surgery
- Local: LA infiltrated directly around the operative site

## Types
- Central neuraxial blockade
  - Single-shot spinal
  - Epidural, CSE
- Peripheral nerve blockade
  - Single-shot or catheter
  - Perineural or fascial plane
- Intravenous regional anaesthesia

## Drugs used
- Local anaesthetics
  - Differential blockade: sympathetic block first (often with altered temperature sensation), then sensory (pain, light touch), then motor (paralysis)
- Intrathecal and epidural LA, opioids and adjuncts
- Adjuncts: adrenaline, sodium bicarbonate, dexmedetomidine, dexamethasone

## Indications
- Surgical anaesthesia
  - Limb surgery (TKR, hand surgery)
  - Surgery below the umbilicus (urology, gynaecology and obstetrics)
  - When GA is high risk (e.g. severe COPD, difficult airway)
- Analgesia
  - Perioperative (e.g. postoperative pain)
  - Non-perioperative (e.g. paravertebral block for rib fractures)
  - Part of multimodal analgesia
    - When opioid sparing matters: OSA, respiratory disease, chronic pain, substance misuse
    - Multiple analgesic allergies

## Advantages over GA
- Avoids GA complications
  - No airway instrumentation: difficult airway, aspiration risk, loose teeth
  - Fewer pulmonary complications: severe COPD, asthma, myasthenia gravis
  - Better haemodynamic stability (no myocardial depression)
  - Less delirium
- Recovery
  - Better acute pain control (e.g. chronic pain patients)
  - Less PONV (less so for spinal)
  - Faster PACU discharge
- Reduced stress response to surgery (lower cortisol and catecholamines)
- Specific benefits
  - Lower DVT risk after lower limb surgery
  - Less blood loss in orthopaedic surgery
- Controversial: fewer surgical site infections, less cancer recurrence
- Awake neurological monitoring: high stroke risk, TURP, carotid endarterectomy, awake craniotomy
- Less effect on the fetus (obstetrics)

## Contraindications
- Patient refusal
- Local infection at the puncture site
- LA allergy
- Coagulopathy (less strict for single-shot superficial PNB)
- PNB: pre-existing neuropathy, or need for neurological monitoring
- Neuraxial: aortic stenosis, sepsis
- Block-specific, e.g. phrenic nerve palsy or pneumothorax risk with interscalene

## Preoperative assessment
- Look for contraindications
  - Neurological and back examination
  - Neuraxial: coagulopathy (platelets, INR), sepsis (FBC, fever), aortic stenosis (murmur, TTE)
- Medications: check DAPT and anticoagulant cessation
  - Never stop drugs inappropriately just to allow a block (e.g. recent drug-eluting stent)
- Consent: explain risks and complications; respect patient autonomy

## Intraoperative management
- Sedation as needed
- Plan for block failure and conversion to GA
