# Supraclavicular nerve block

Blocks the supraclavicular nerve (from the cervical plexus, not the brachial plexus), which supplies the skin "cape" of the shoulder.

## Indications
- Sensation over the cape of the shoulder
- Usually blocked as part of a cervical plexus block for carotid surgery
- May be needed for shoulder or supraclavicular surgery
- Interscalene block often spreads to the cervical plexus and covers it; supraclavicular brachial plexus block often misses it

## Anatomy
- Single trunk from the anterior rami of C3-C4: separate from the brachial plexus
- Surfaces through the middle of sternocleidomastoid, then splits into 3 branches
  - Medial (suprasternal)
  - Intermediate (supraclavicular)
  - Lateral (supra-acromial)
- Cape area: midline to the deltoid; second rib in front to the top of the scapula behind

## Landmark technique
- Total 5–10 mL of intermediate- or long-acting local anaesthetic, depending on analgesic needs
- 3ml into the mid belly of sternocleidomastoid with a 22- to 25-gauge sharp needle
- Rest injected subcutaneously up and down along the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid

## Complications
- Uncommon
- Avoid the external jugular vein to prevent haematoma

## Flashcards
- Q: Nerve roots of the supraclavicular nerve?
  - A: Anterior rami of C3-C4 (cervical plexus, not brachial plexus)
- Q: Total volume and the first injection for a landmark supraclavicular nerve block?
  - A: 5–10 mL total; 3 ml into the mid belly of sternocleidomastoid
- Q: Which brachial plexus approach often misses the supraclavicular nerve?
  - A: Supraclavicular brachial plexus block (interscalene often covers it)
- Q: Which vessel should be avoided?
  - A: External jugular vein
