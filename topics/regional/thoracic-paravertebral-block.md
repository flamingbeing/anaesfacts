# Thoracic Paravertebral Block

Injection into the paravertebral space for unilateral chest, breast and abdominal analgesia; plus the safer "paravertebral by proxy" blocks.

## Overview
- Established analgesia for thoracic, chest wall and breast surgery, and rib fractures
- High-quality block, enough for major surgery
- One injection covers 4-6 dermatomes
- Can be done at cervical, thoracic or lumbar level
- Ultrasound helps find the paravertebral space (PVS), guide the needle and watch LA spread
  - Keep the needle tip in view throughout to avoid pleural puncture or entering the intervertebral foramen

## Anatomy
- Wedge-shaped space between the heads and necks of the ribs
  - Posterior wall: superior costotransverse ligament
  - Anterolateral wall: parietal pleura with endothoracic fascia
  - Medial wall: side of the vertebral body and intervertebral foramen
- Communications:
  - Medially: epidural space, through the intervertebral foramen and over the rib heads and necks; so unilateral (or bilateral) epidural spread is common
  - Laterally: intercostal space; intercostal spread is part of how it works
  - Cranially and caudally
  - Superiorly: cervical paravertebral space (poorly defined)
  - Inferiorly: limited, as psoas major arises at L1
- Contents:
  - Fat
  - Spinal (intercostal) nerves: anterior and posterior rami
  - Sympathetic chain and rami communicantes

## Contraindications
- Tumour in the paravertebral space
- Empyema
- Infection at the site
- Caution: previous thoracotomy, chest wall deformity (including kyphoscoliosis)

## Level of block
- Sternotomy or breast surgery: T4 (dermatomes needed T2 – T6)
- Thoracotomy: T5-T6 (dermatomes needed T3 – T9)
- Upper abdominal surgery (e.g. hepatectomy): T8
- Midline laparotomy: T10 (dermatomes needed T4 – L1)

## Parasagittal technique
- Scanning:
  - Start 5-10 cm lateral in the parasagittal plane: rounded ribs with pleura below
  - Move medially until the transverse processes appear: squarer and deeper than the ribs (2-3cm from midline)
  - Too medial shows the laminae; move slightly back laterally
  - See the pleura and the area just above the superior costotransverse ligament
- In-plane:
  - Needle from caudal to cranial
  - Advance slowly with close tip control to avoid the pleura
  - Walk off the transverse process until below the superior costotransverse ligament
  - 15-20ml at one level gives caudal and cranial spread
- Out-of-plane:
  - Contact the transverse process, then walk off it 1-1.5 cm deeper and inject
  - Tip not seen, but injection pushes the pleura down
  - Repeat at each level needed
  - Commonly preferred approach:
    - Felt to be safer than in-plane, as the needle is not aimed at the neuraxis
    - Similar to the established surface-landmark technique, with ultrasound locating transverse processes more accurately

## Transverse technique
- Sitting or lateral, operative side up
- Linear high-frequency probe; start with depth about 3 cm
- Scanning:
  - Transverse processes and ribs: bright with acoustic shadow
  - Slide slightly caudal into the intercostal space between ribs to see the PVS and adjoining intercostal space
  - PVS: wedge-shaped hypoechoic layer between bright pleura below and the internal intercostal membrane above
  - Pleura and air artefacts move with breathing
- Injection: needle into the PVS; LA pushes the pleura down, confirming correct spread

## Landmark technique (loss of resistance)
- Lateral position; feel the midline and go 2cm lateral to the spinous process
- Insert perpendicular until bone (transverse process), at most 4cm
- Withdraw a little, angle caudally and walk off the transverse process
- Attach a loss-of-resistance syringe and proceed as for an epidural
- Space is usually found within about 1cm; do not go deeper

## Paravertebral-lite blocks (paravertebral by proxy)
- True PVB places LA in the PVS, anterior to the superior costotransverse ligament (SCTL), between it and the pleura
- Proxy blocks place LA outside this space: safer than PVB
- Options:
  - Erector spinae plane block
  - Retrolaminar block
  - Midpoint transverse process to pleura (MTP) block, now intertransverse process (ITP) block
    - LA just posterior to the SCTL, away from the pleura
    - Blocks the sympathetic chain, unlike other proxy blocks
    - ITP was the umbrella term proposed by the ASRA/ESRA 2020 consensus for MTP and its successors, as it describes the injection site in all of them
  - Intercostal paraspinal block

## Flashcards
- Q: Boundaries of the thoracic paravertebral space?
  - A: Posterior superior costotransverse ligament; anterolateral parietal pleura and endothoracic fascia; medial vertebral body and intervertebral foramen.
- Q: How many dermatomes does one PVB injection cover?
  - A: 4-6.
- Q: Which level for thoracotomy, and which dermatomes are needed?
  - A: T5-T6; T3 – T9.
- Q: Volume for an in-plane single-level PVB?
  - A: 15-20ml.
- Q: Landmark PVB: where and how deep?
  - A: 2cm lateral to the spinous process; hit transverse process within 4cm, walk off caudally, loss of resistance usually within about 1cm.
