# Suprascapular Nerve Block

Block of the suprascapular nerve at the suprascapular notch, an analgesic adjunct for shoulder surgery.

## Indications

- Adjunct for arthroscopic shoulder surgery and total shoulder arthroplasty
- With GA for shoulder arthroscopy: better analgesia, fewer opioid side effects, earlier discharge
- Not better than interscalene block for arthroscopy
- Adds nothing to interscalene block for anterior open shoulder surgery

## Anatomy

- From the superior trunk of the brachial plexus (C4–C5), so usually covered by an interscalene block
- Passes through the suprascapular notch, then laterally along the upper border of the scapular spine
- Sensory: about 70% of the posterior-superior shoulder joint, the acromioclavicular joint, and part of the anterior axilla in up to 10% of patients
- Motor: supraspinatus and infraspinatus
- Little or no skin supply over the scapula or back of the shoulder

## Technique (landmark)

- Draw a line along the upper border of the scapular spine; bisect it with a line parallel to the vertebral spine
- The notch lies about 2–3 cm from the crossing point towards the middle of the upper outer quadrant
- 1.5-in. 22-gauge needle at this point, directed caudad in the sagittal plane until it contacts the scapular spine
- Inject 10 mL long-acting local anaesthetic
- Nerve stimulator: external rotation of the shoulder

## Complications

- Pneumothorax if the needle passes through the notch into the pleura
- Largely avoided by aiming caudad, not anteriorly
