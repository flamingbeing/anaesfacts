# Distal Saphenous Nerve Block

Blocks of the saphenous nerve below the knee or at the ankle, usually added to a sciatic or popliteal block.

## Distribution
- Variable part of the medial leg below the knee, the medial malleolus, sometimes as far as the first metatarsophalangeal joint

## Indications
- With a sciatic or popliteal block, to complete lower leg anaesthesia for vascular, orthopaedic and podiatric surgery

## Anatomy
- Arises in the femoral triangle
- In the adductor canal, under sartorius (which roofs the lower half of the canal), it starts lateral to the femoral artery then crosses in front to lie medial
- Leaves the lower canal through the fascia lata between the sartorius and gracilis tendons, becoming subcutaneous
- Runs down the medial tibia with the long (great) saphenous vein
- Passes with the vein in front of the medial malleolus to the base of the great toe
- Splits into many small branches once subcutaneous, so blocking the whole network is often hard

## Approaches
- Ultrasound subsartorial (adductor canal block)
- Landmark transsartorial
- Landmark below-knee field block
- Landmark perivenous
- Ultrasound perivenous
- Landmark block at the medial malleolus

## Below-knee field block (landmark)
- Supine
- Landmark: tibial tuberosity (a few cm below the patella) round to the medial edge of the upper calf, as a ring
- Needle deep subcutaneous
- 5-10 mL as a ring from the medial tibial tuberosity to the dorsomedial upper calf
- Several passes may be needed; re-enter through the existing wheal

## Perivenous approach (landmark)
- Uses the close link between saphenous vein and nerve for a higher success rate
- Small risk of haematoma if the vein is punctured
- Find the vein with a tourniquet on the dependent leg
- Inject around the vein just below the patella, deep subcutaneous
- 5 mL fanned around the vein

## Perivenous approach (ultrasound)
- Transverse probe 10–15 cm above the medial malleolus, using the great saphenous vein as the landmark
- A proximal calf tourniquet enlarges the vein
- Nerve: small hyperechoic structure beside the vein, often already branched at this level

## Block at the medial malleolus (landmark)
- Best option for foot surgery; same as in an ankle block
- Commonest complication: saphenous vein haematoma
- Insert just above the medial malleolus, circumferentially
- Deep subcutaneous, 6–8 mL as a ring
- Several passes may be needed; re-enter through the wheal

## Flashcards
- Q: What does the saphenous nerve supply?
  - A: Variable medial leg below the knee and the medial malleolus, sometimes to the first metatarsophalangeal joint
- Q: Volume for a below-knee saphenous field block?
  - A: 5-10 mL as a ring from the tibial tuberosity to the upper medial calf
- Q: Where is the probe placed for the ultrasound perivenous saphenous block?
  - A: Transverse, 10–15 cm above the medial malleolus, next to the great saphenous vein
- Q: Volume for the saphenous block at the medial malleolus, and its commonest complication?
  - A: 6–8 mL ring; saphenous vein haematoma
