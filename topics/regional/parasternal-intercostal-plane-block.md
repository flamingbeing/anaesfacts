# Parasternal Intercostal Plane Blocks

Plane blocks next to the sternum that give analgesia to the anterior chest wall; superficial and deep variants.

## Deep parasternal intercostal plane block
- First described as the transversus thoracis muscle plane (TTMP) block in 2015; naming standardised in 2020
- Local anaesthetic goes into the plane between the internal intercostal and transversus thoracis muscles
- Spread depends on volume; can cover T2-T6

## Indications
- Cardiac surgery through a median sternotomy
- Breast surgery
- Sternal fractures

## Technique
- High-frequency linear probe, placed longitudinally 1 cm lateral to the sternal border
- Find the T4-T5 intercostal space: parasternal sagittal view of internal intercostal and transversus thoracis muscles between the 4th and 5th ribs, above the pleura
- 50 to 80-mm needle, in plane
- Needle tip in the plane between internal intercostal and transversus thoracis; a pop may be felt
- Inject 20ml of 0.3% ropivacaine on each side
- Pleura pushed downwards = correct spread in the plane
- Spread above the costal cartilage = injection is too superficial (above the internal intercostal)

## Complications
- Injury to the internal mammary artery (matters because it is harvested for CABG)

## Flashcards
- Q: Between which muscles is local anaesthetic placed in the deep parasternal intercostal plane block?
  - A: Internal intercostal and transversus thoracis
- Q: Where is the probe placed for this block?
  - A: Longitudinally, 1 cm lateral to the sternal border at T4-T5
- Q: Typical dose for this block?
  - A: 20ml of 0.3% ropivacaine on each side
- Q: What sign confirms correct injection?
  - A: Downward movement of the pleura
- Q: Key vessel at risk?
  - A: Internal mammary artery
