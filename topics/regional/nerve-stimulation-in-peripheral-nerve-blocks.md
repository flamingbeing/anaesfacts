# Nerve stimulation in peripheral nerve blocks

A small current through the block needle triggers a motor twitch as the tip nears a nerve; with ultrasound it is mainly a safety monitor against intraneural injection.

## Principle
- External current brings the membrane to threshold, so the nerve depolarises and fires an action potential
- Response depends on fibre type
  - Sensory: tingling or pain
  - Motor: contraction of the supplied muscle
- Stimulus duration needed differs by fibre, giving some selectivity
  - A-alpha (motor): 0.05-0.1ms
  - A-delta (pain and temperature): 0.15ms

## Goal
- Elicit a motor response as the needle approaches the nerve
- Current needed for a twitch is proportional to needle–nerve distance
- High specificity, low sensitivity
  - Twitch at < 0.2 mA: very likely intraneural, reposition the needle (highly specific)
  - Twitch at < 0.5 mA can still be intraneural, so absence is not reassuring (poor sensitivity)

## Settings
- Pulse duration 0.05-1ms; keep it short (50– 100 microseconds; note says ms) for a painless twitch
- Frequency 1-3Hz; no tetanic stimulation (painful); 2-Hz gives more frequent feedback
- Amplitude 0-3mA, adjustable
- Locating a nerve: start at 1-1.5mA
- Excluding intraneural placement: 0.2-0.3mA

## Equipment
- Two leads: one to an ECG skin electrode, one to the needle
- Polarity clearly marked and colour coded; negative lead to the needle
- Needle as cathode (negative) needs less current: it depolarises the nearby membrane and triggers an action potential

## Nerve stimulation with ultrasound
- Main role is safety: detecting intraneural needle placement and intrafascicular injection
- Simple setting: fixed 0.5 mA (0.05-0.1 ms, 1-2 Hz) throughout; confirm no twitch before injecting
- Twitch should fade clearly after a small volume (about 2mL), as the nerve is pushed away from the tip
- Twitch that persists, or pain in an awake patient, after injection suggests intraneural placement
- High currents can stimulate muscle directly
- Adds little to success as a localisation tool with ultrasound
  - Useful where nerves are hard to see, whether targeting or avoiding them

## Arguments for and against
- For
  - Helps prevent intraneural needle tip placement (0.2mA is highly specific); most people use 0.5mA
  - Avoids spearing neighbouring nerves
  - Helps identify nerves (e.g. 1mA)
  - Confirms the right intermuscular fascial plane by direct muscle twitch (e.g. 2mA)
- Against
  - Takes longer, possibly for no benefit
  - 0.5mA does not prevent intraneural needling; touching a nerve does not always cause a twitch
  - Ultrasound already shows intraneural injection
  - The needle need not get that close (e.g. periplexus rather than intraplexus injection)

## Flashcards
- Q: What does a motor twitch at < 0.2 mA mean?
  - A: Very likely intraneural tip; reposition the needle
- Q: Which lead goes to the needle, and why?
  - A: Negative (cathode); less current is needed
- Q: Typical fixed setting when nerve stimulation is used as a safety monitor with ultrasound?
  - A: 0.5 mA, 0.05-0.1 ms, 1-2 Hz; no twitch before injecting
- Q: What should happen to the twitch after about 2 mL of injectate?
  - A: It should fade markedly; persistence suggests intraneural placement
- Q: Current to start nerve localisation?
  - A: 1-1.5 mA
