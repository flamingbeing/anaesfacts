# Peripheral Nerve Block Follow-up

Post-block instructions, expected block duration and daily review of perineural infusions.

## Single-shot blocks: instructions
- Teach patients and carers to protect the numb limb until the block wears off ("care of the insensate limb")
  - Upper limb blocks: arm sling
  - Lower limb blocks: fall precautions
- Advise regular analgesia before sensation returns

## Single-shot blocks: follow-up
- Routine postoperative review
- Check the block has fully resolved; expected duration:
  - Lidocaine (lignocaine): 90-180min
  - Bupivacaine, ropivacaine: 4-18hrs
- Make sure regular analgesia is prescribed
- Screen for complications specific to regional anaesthesia

## Perineural infusions
- Drugs:
  - Bupivacaine 0.1-0.2%
  - Ropivacaine 0.2%
- Common uses:
  - Upper limb: brachial plexus catheters for hand surgery
  - Lower limb: hip fracture awaiting surgery or managed conservatively
- Benefits:
  - Excellent analgesia
  - Avoids opioid side effects
  - Avoids the risks of neuraxial blocks

## Daily ward review (as for epidurals)
- Pain score
- Loss of cold sensation (ice)
- Motor block
- Catheter site:
  - Signs of infection needing removal
  - Catheter marking: check for migration
  - Whether the dressing needs changing
- Top-up: change the infusion bag

## Flashcards
- Q: Expected duration of a single-shot block with lidocaine vs bupivacaine or ropivacaine?
  - A: Lidocaine 90-180min; bupivacaine or ropivacaine 4-18hrs.
- Q: Usual perineural infusion solutions?
  - A: Bupivacaine 0.1-0.2% or ropivacaine 0.2%.
- Q: How do you protect an insensate limb?
  - A: Sling for upper limb blocks; fall precautions for lower limb blocks.
- Q: What do you check daily on a perineural catheter?
  - A: Pain score, cold sensation, motor block, and the site (infection, migration marking, dressing).
