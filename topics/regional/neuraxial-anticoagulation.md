# Neuraxial and Regional Blocks with Anticoagulants

Timing of antiplatelets and anticoagulants around spinals, epidurals, catheters and deep blocks to avoid spinal haematoma.

## Quick summary

- Platelets 80k; INR < 1.2
- Aspirin fine; clopidogrel (Plavix) 7 days
- Enoxaparin (Clexane) 12h prophylactic / 24h treatment
- UFH 4-6hrs, then check aPTT
- Warfarin 5 days and INR normal
- Superficial PNB: usually can proceed
- Catheters: same timings apply to removal
- Prophylactic enoxaparin: first dose 12hrs after placement, catheter removal 12hrs after a dose; may resume 4hrs after removal (ASRA)

## Blood tests

- Platelets > 80 to 100k
  - Consider the platelet trend and platelet function too
- INR < 1.2 (normal)
  - ASRA wants a normal INR; European guidelines accept 1.4 or less
- Thresholds vary between anaesthetists; discuss with your consultant

## Antiplatelets: stop before neuraxial block

- Aspirin: no need to stop
- Clopidogrel: 7 days
- Ticagrelor: 5–7 days
- Prasugrel: 7–10 days
- Ticlopidine: 10-14 days
- Abciximab (GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors): 48 hours

## Anticoagulants: stop before neuraxial block

- Warfarin: 5 days, INR normal
- IV unfractionated heparin: 4–6 hours, then check aPTT
- LMWH (SC)
  - Prophylactic (once nightly): 12hrs
  - Treatment (twice daily): 24hrs
- DOACs
  - Apixaban: 72hrs for high-dose twice daily (longer in renal impairment)
    - Alternative: apixaban level < 30 ng/mL or anti-Xa activity ≤ 0.1 IU/mL (ASRA 2024)
  - Rivaroxaban: 72 hours
  - Dabigatran: 72 hours (longer if CrCl < 50ml/min)
- In general, do not stop essential drugs just to allow a spinal

## Catheters

- While a catheter is in: only short-acting, low-dose agents (UFH, prophylactic enoxaparin)
  - Long-acting agents (antiplatelets, warfarin) contraindicated
  - UFH: wait 1hr after placement
  - Prophylactic enoxaparin: wait 12hrs after placement (longer because the catheter stays in)
- Removing or manipulating a catheter carries the same bleeding risk as the original puncture
  - Use the same timings as for insertion
  - UFH: 4-6hrs after the dose and normal aPTT
  - Prophylactic enoxaparin: 12hrs after the dose

## Peripheral nerve blocks

- Most PNBs do not need neuraxial-level restrictions; careful PNB is possible on anticoagulants
- Exception: deep, non-compressible or high bleeding-risk blocks follow neuraxial rules (e.g. ASRA)
  - Lumbar plexus, paravertebral, coeliac plexus, superior hypogastric plexus, visceral sympathetic blocks
- Catheters: as above

## Reducing haematoma risk

- Before: check coagulation, follow ASRA guidance, avoid deep sites
- During
  - Ultrasound guidance
  - In-plane approach with the needle tip seen throughout
  - Manual pressure after vascular puncture at a compressible site
- After: early neurological checks; inspect the catheter site

## Restarting after single shot or catheter removal

- Shorter wait once the catheter is out (adjust if a loading dose is given)
- Enoxaparin: 4hr
- IV UFH: 2hr (immediately reversible)
- Clopidogrel, warfarin, DOACs: 6hr after removal
- Applies only to clean, atraumatic procedures
  - After a bloody tap or multiple attempts, may need to delay to 6-12hrs postop

## Flashcards
- Q: How long before neuraxial block should clopidogrel be stopped?
  - A: 7 days
- Q: LMWH timing before neuraxial block: prophylactic vs treatment dose?
  - A: 12hrs prophylactic, 24hrs treatment
- Q: How long should apixaban (high-dose) be stopped before neuraxial block?
  - A: 72hrs, longer in renal impairment
- Q: When can prophylactic enoxaparin be resumed after epidural catheter removal?
  - A: 4hr
- Q: Which peripheral blocks should follow neuraxial anticoagulation rules?
  - A: Deep or non-compressible ones: lumbar plexus, paravertebral, coeliac plexus, superior hypogastric, visceral sympathetic
