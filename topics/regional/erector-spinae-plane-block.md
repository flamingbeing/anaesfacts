# Erector Spinae Plane Block

Fascial plane block with local anaesthetic deep to the erector spinae muscles on the transverse process.

## Anatomy
- Erector spinae group: iliocostalis, longissimus and spinalis; core muscles that stabilise the spine
- Run on both sides from skull to pelvis and sacrum, and from spinous to transverse processes, reaching the ribs
- Size and shape change along their length

## Mechanism of action
- Not fully understood
- Possibly anterior spread into the paravertebral space
- Interfascial spread to the posterior rami of spinal nerves is also a main mechanism

## Ultrasound anatomy
- Choose the target transverse process
- Probe paramedian sagittal, about 2cm from the midline (spinous processes)
- Find the transverse process
  - Too medial: laminae appear as flat hyperechoic lines; slide laterally
  - Too lateral: ribs appear as rounded acoustic shadows with the pleural line between them; slide medially
- Above about T5: three muscle layers superficial to the transverse process (trapezius, rhomboid major, erector spinae)
- Mid and lower thoracic: only trapezius and erector spinae

## Technique
1. In-plane, cranial to caudad, until the tip touches the transverse process
2. Inject 1-3 mL to confirm spread deep to erector spinae and superficial to the transverse process
3. Complete with 20-30 mL of local anaesthetic
- Catheters can be placed for continuous ESP block

## Flashcards
- Q: Probe position for an ESP block?
  - A: Paramedian sagittal, about 2cm from the midline, over the transverse process
- Q: Total local anaesthetic volume for an ESP block?
  - A: 20-30 mL (after a 1-3 mL test dose)
- Q: What does the ultrasound show if the probe is too lateral?
  - A: Rounded rib shadows with the pleural line between them
- Q: Which muscles lie over the transverse process above T5?
  - A: Trapezius, rhomboid major and erector spinae
