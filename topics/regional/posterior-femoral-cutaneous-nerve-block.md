# Posterior Cutaneous Nerve of the Thigh Block

Superficial block for skin over the back of the thigh, given at the gluteal fold or above the knee.

## Uses

- Burns patients with skin graft donor sites on the posterior thigh
- With a popliteal block for short saphenous vein stripping

## Distribution

- Skin of the posterior thigh, between the areas of the lateral and anterior femoral cutaneous nerves

## Anatomy

- From the dorsal branches of S1 and S2 and the ventral branches of S2 and S3
- Leaves through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis
- Descends under gluteus maximus with the inferior gluteal vessels, behind or medial to the sciatic nerve
- Runs down the back of the thigh deep to fascia lata
- All branches cutaneous: gluteal region, perineum, flexor surface of thigh and leg

## Positioning

- Prone, lateral, or supine with the leg raised 90 degrees

## Subgluteal approach

- Find the gluteal fold; 10 mL local anaesthetic subcutaneously as a skin wheal
- Plus 5 mL deeper at the midpoint of the gluteal crease, fanned, to reach the nerve before it pierces the deep fascia

## Midthigh approach

- For blocking above the knee, e.g. short saphenous vein stripping with a popliteal block
- 10 mL subcutaneously along a line 5 cm above and parallel to the popliteal crease

## Flashcards

- Q: Volumes for the subgluteal approach?
  - A: 10 mL subcutaneous wheal along the gluteal fold plus 5 mL deep at the midpoint of the crease
- Q: Where is the midthigh injection placed?
  - A: 10 mL subcutaneously along a line 5 cm above and parallel to the popliteal crease
- Q: Nerve roots of the posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh?
  - A: S1 to S3 (dorsal S1–S2, ventral S2–S3)
