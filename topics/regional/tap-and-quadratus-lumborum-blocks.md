# TAP and quadratus lumborum blocks

Fascial plane blocks of the abdominal wall: TAP blocks give somatic (wall and parietal peritoneum) analgesia; QL blocks can also spread to the paravertebral space.

## Anatomy
- TAP = the plane superficial to transversus abdominis, the innermost muscle of the anterolateral abdominal wall
  - Its upper fibrous part sits behind rectus abdominis and reaches the xiphoid
  - Posterior aponeuroses of transversus abdominis and internal oblique fuse and join the thoracolumbar fascia (TLF)
- Intercostal, subcostal and L1 nerves link up in the plane as an upper and a lower TAP plexus
  - Together they supply the anterolateral wall including the parietal peritoneum, so a full TAP block must cover both
  - Upper plexus = subcostal or intercostal plexus; lower plexus lies near the deep circumflex iliac artery
- Coverage by approach:
  - Subcostal: intercostal nerves T6–T9, between rectus sheath and transversus abdominis
  - Lateral (midaxillary line, between costal margin and iliac crest, internal oblique/transversus plane): T10– T11 and subcostal nerve T12
  - Umbilicus = T10
  - L1 is missed by the lateral block; needs an anterior TAP block medial to the ASIS
  - Posterior approach via the triangle of Petit also described
- Quadratus lumborum (QL): posterior abdominal wall, dorsolateral to psoas major
  - From posterior iliac crest and iliolumbar ligament to the 12th rib and transverse processes of L1–L5
  - Laterally flexes the lumbar spine
- TLF has anterior, middle and posterior layers, all continuous with the fused internal oblique/transversus aponeurosis
  - Posterior layer: attaches to the latissimus dorsi aponeurosis; covers erector spinae from the spinous processes to its lateral edge
  - There it fuses with the middle layer as the lateral raphe (iliac crest to 12th rib)
  - Deepest lamina of the posterior layer = paraspinal retinacular sheath (PRS), wrapping erector spinae
  - Lateral interfascial triangle (LIFT): base = lateral edge of erector spinae, sides = PRS with posterior and middle TLF, apex = lateral raphe
  - Middle layer separates QL from erector spinae; anterior layer covers the front of QL
- Transversalis fascia (TF)
  - Lines the deep side of transversus abdominis, QL and psoas major
  - Anterior TLF is fused to it
  - Continuous with the endothoracic fascia behind the diaphragm (thickened as the medial and lateral arcuate ligaments)
  - So injectate in the lumbar QL/psoas planes can spread up to the thoracic paravertebral space
- Iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal and subcostal nerves cross QL between the muscle and TF
- The 4 lumbar arteries each side run behind psoas and QL, pierce the transversus aponeurosis and end in the TAP
- Lower pole of the kidney lies in front of QL and can reach L4 on deep inspiration
  - Separated from QL only by perinephric fat, posterior renal fascia, TF and anterior TLF
  - Always see the kidney during a QL block

## Indications
- QL blocks share the TAP block indications
- Large-bowel resection, open or laparoscopic appendicectomy, cholecystectomy
- Caesarean section, total abdominal hysterectomy
- Open prostatectomy, renal transplant, nephrectomy, abdominoplasty, iliac crest bone graft
- Ileostomy
- Exploratory laparotomy: bilateral blocks for midline incisions

## Dose and equipment
- TAP, TQL and QL1 are tissue plane blocks: they need large volumes
  - Minimum 15 mL for each TAP block; some experts use 25-30ml
  - Check the maximum safe dose for the patient's size, especially with dual bilateral TAP blocks
- Systemic absorption depends mainly on the vascularity of the site
  - QL region is fairly vascular (lumbar arteries behind the muscle) and needs large volumes: calculate the dose carefully to avoid high peak plasma levels
- Single shot: 22-gauge short-bevel needle, 10 cm (note says 10mm)
- Catheters: 18- to 21-gauge, 10-cm Tuohy needle with extension tubing

## Clinical points
- Covers the abdominal wall, not the viscera: use within multimodal analgesia
- Slow onset: up to 60min to full sensory effect
- Complications: failure, intravascular injection, intraperitoneal injection with risk of bowel or other visceral injury

## Lateral TAP block
- Linear high-frequency probe, patient supine
- Probe axial in the midaxillary line between the costal margin and the iliac crest
- Identify external oblique, internal oblique and transversus abdominis
- Target: plane between internal oblique and transversus abdominis
- Needle in at the anterior axillary line, tip to the plane at about the midaxillary line

## Lateral TAP block: landmark technique
- Entry via the lumbar triangle of Petit: external oblique in front, latissimus dorsi behind, iliac crest below
- Blunt short-bevel needle, perpendicular to the skin just above the iliac crest
- First pop: through external oblique into the external/internal oblique plane
- Second pop: through internal oblique fascia into the TAP
- Aspirate, then 20 ml long-acting local anaesthetic in 5 ml aliquots

## Subcostal TAP block
- Linear high-frequency probe, supine
- Probe along the lower costal margin, as medial and cranial as possible
- See rectus abdominis, posterior rectus sheath and transversus abdominis beneath it
- Target: plane between posterior rectus sheath and transversus abdominis
- Needle in above rectus near the midline, medial to lateral (or lateral to medial)

## Oblique subcostal TAP block (OSTAP)
- Variant of the subcostal TAP
- Start at the xiphoid in the plane between rectus sheath and transversus abdominis
- Inject in small increments to open the plane (hydrodissection), advancing the needle towards the lateral-inferior end of the opened space
- Repeat until the probe edge reaches the anterior iliac crest
  - The plane moves under the linea semilunaris aponeurosis, then between internal oblique and transversus abdominis towards the iliac crest
  - About 6- to 7-mL for hydrodissection medial to the semilunaris
  - Remaining 13-14ml for the internal oblique/transversus plane
- With a 10-cm needle (rather than the original 15- to 20-cm one), the needle had to be withdrawn and reinserted along the line 4–5 times
- Bilateral OSTAP used 20 mL of 0.375% ropivacaine

## Anterior TAP block
- Same as the ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric block

## Posterior TAP block
- Linear high-frequency probe
- Probe axial in the midaxillary line, slide back to the most posterior limit of the internal oblique/transversus plane
- Needle in at the midaxillary line, advanced backwards to the posterior end of the TAP

## Quadratus lumborum blocks
- Probe: low-frequency (5- to 2-MHz) curved array, transverse, to see the three lateral wall muscles and QL
- Position: lateral decubitus preferred over supine (better ergonomics and views of neuraxial structures)
- QL1 (type 1, lateral)
  - Probe in the midaxillary line, slide back until the posterior transversus aponeurosis shows as a bright line
  - Target: just deep to the aponeurosis, superficial to TF, at the lateral edge of QL (just lateral to pararenal fat)
  - Same as the transversalis fascia plane block
  - Needle from either end of the probe, tip just through the aponeurosis
  - Mainly blocks lateral cutaneous branches of iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal and subcostal nerves (T12–L1)
- QL2 (type 2, posterior)
  - Slide back as for QL1 until the LIFT appears between latissimus dorsi and QL
  - Target: the PRS within the middle TLF layer, near the LIFT
  - Needle from the lateral end of the probe; inject within the fascia
  - Reported analgesia similar to TQL with faster onset; mechanism unclear
- QL3 (type 3, transmuscular QL / TQL, anterior)
  - Curved probe axial on the flank just above the iliac crest
  - Shamrock sign: L4 transverse process = stem; erector spinae (back), QL (side), psoas major (front) = leaves
  - Target: plane between QL and psoas major
  - In-plane from the posterior end of the probe, through QL
  - Aim: spread to the thoracic paravertebral space for somatic and visceral analgesia T4 to L1

## External oblique intercostal (EOI) block
- Targets both anterior and lateral cutaneous branches of intercostal nerves T6-10
- Probe just medial to the anterior axillary line at the 6th rib
- Advantages: bony backstop, catheter possible, far from the surgical incision

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerves does a subcostal TAP block cover, and which does a lateral TAP block cover?
  - A: Subcostal T6–T9; lateral T10–T11 and subcostal T12 (L1 needs an anterior TAP)
- Q: Minimum volume for each TAP block?
  - A: 15 mL (some use 25-30 ml)
- Q: What is the shamrock sign in the transmuscular QL block?
  - A: L4 transverse process as stem, with erector spinae, QL and psoas major as the three leaves
- Q: Why must the kidney be seen during a QL block?
  - A: Its lower pole lies in front of QL and can reach L4 on deep inspiration
- Q: How long can a TAP block take to reach full effect?
  - A: Up to 60 min
