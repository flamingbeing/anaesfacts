# PENG Block

Motor-sparing fascial plane block between the psoas tendon and pubic ramus for hip fracture and hip surgery analgesia.

## Background
- First described in 2018 (Girón-Arango and colleagues)
- Interfascial plane block: psoas tendon in front, pubic ramus behind
- Developed for postoperative analgesia after total hip arthroplasty (THA), with motor sparing
- Older options (lumbar plexus, femoral, fascia iliaca blocks):
  - Miss articular branches (e.g. from the sciatic nerve), so analgesia is incomplete
  - Quadriceps weakness raises fall risk
- PENG aims to be a more complete (includes accessory obturator), motor-sparing alternative to femoral or fascia iliaca blocks for hip pain

## Anatomy
- Anterior hip capsule: articular branches of femoral, obturator and accessory obturator (when present) nerves
- Posterior capsule: mainly nerve to quadratus femoris; small branches from sciatic and superior gluteal nerves
- Anterior capsule carries most of the joint's sensory supply and most postoperative hip pain, so it is the target

## Coverage
- Articular branches of femoral, obturator and accessory obturator nerves
- Proximal hip fractures down to intertrochanteric level; hip surgery (arthroplasty covered better than fixation)
  - Intertrochanteric cover comes from caudal spread, described with 20 mL
  - Dynamic hip screw and intramedullary nail fall outside its coverage
- Does not block the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN)
  - Posterior and direct lateral approaches cause lateral thigh incision pain in the LFCN territory
  - Add an LFCN block or wound infiltration for better analgesia

## Indications
- Postoperative pain after hip joint surgery
- Pain from proximal femur or femoral head fractures
- Chronic hip pain

## Technique
- Supine
- Linear probe transverse over the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS)
- Rotate about 45 degrees, parallel to the inguinal crease, to line up with the pubic ramus
- Slide medially until the anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS), iliopubic eminence (IPE) and psoas tendon are clear
  - Sequence along the line: ASIS, AIIS, psoas tendon, IPE, pubic ramus
  - Psoas muscle lies over the tendon; femoral vessels and nerve lie more superficially
- Sliding distally or tilting caudally shows the femoral head
- Return to the line with the psoas tendon between AIIS and IPE
- Needle: 100mm, in-plane, lateral to medial, into the plane between psoas tendon and pubic ramus
- Inject 15-20ml long-acting LA (e.g. 0.5% ropivacaine), lifting the psoas tendon
  - Avoid puncturing the tendon
- Make sure the needle passes through the muscle epimysium; spread just under the epimysium can mimic a true lift and is a common error

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerves does the PENG block target?
  - A: Articular branches of the femoral, obturator and accessory obturator nerves to the anterior hip capsule.
- Q: Which plane is injected?
  - A: Between the psoas tendon and the pubic ramus (between AIIS and iliopubic eminence).
- Q: Typical volume and drug?
  - A: 15-20ml long-acting LA, e.g. 0.5% ropivacaine, via a 100mm needle in-plane lateral to medial.
- Q: Which nerve does PENG miss, and how do you cover it?
  - A: Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve; add an LFCN block or wound infiltration.
