# Central Neuraxial Blocks

Summary of neuraxial techniques, contraindications and complications.

## Definition
- Regional technique: local anaesthetic placed around central nervous system nerve roots, blocking transmission

## Techniques
- Spinal: injection into the subarachnoid space; the most common
- Epidural: injection into the epidural space, drug then diffuses into the subarachnoid space
- Variations: combined spinal-epidural (CSE), caudal block

## Contraindications
- Absolute
  - Patient refusal
  - Allergy to the injected drugs
  - Infection at the insertion site
  - Coagulopathy
  - Raised ICP, space-occupying lesions
- Relative
  - Pre-existing neurological disease
  - Fixed cardiac output states such as aortic stenosis
  - Sepsis
  - Hypovolaemia
  - Severe spinal deformity, extensive spinal instrumentation

## Complications
- Common: nausea, shivering, itch
- Uncommon: post-dural puncture headache (PDPH), LNDP, temporary neurological deficits
- Rare: haematoma, abscess, permanent deficits, LAST
- Failure

## Flashcards
- Q: Absolute contraindications to neuraxial block?
  - A: Patient refusal, drug allergy, infection at the site, coagulopathy, raised ICP or space-occupying lesion
- Q: Name three relative contraindications to neuraxial block.
  - A: Fixed cardiac output (aortic stenosis), sepsis, hypovolaemia (also severe spinal deformity or instrumentation, pre-existing neurological disease)
- Q: Rare serious complications of neuraxial block?
  - A: Haematoma, abscess, permanent neurological deficit, LAST
