# Lumbar Epidural Anaesthesia and Analgesia

Catheter technique giving titratable, open-ended neuraxial block; slower and less dense than a spinal.

## Indications
- General surgery
- Labour analgesia
- Caesarean section

## Contraindications
- As for spinal anaesthesia
- Raised ICP
  - Accidental dural puncture with raised ICP and obstructed CSF flow or mass effect (with or without midline shift) can cause herniation or neurological deterioration
  - Epidural injection itself can raise ICP further in patients with high baseline ICP

## Advantages over spinal
- Titratable
  - Duration of analgesia or anaesthesia is not limited
  - Gradual onset of sympathectomy and haemodynamic change

## Disadvantages over spinal
- Slower onset
- Less complete, less dense block
- Sacral sparing
  - Spread can be blocked by anatomical barriers or a poorly placed catheter tip
  - Sacral roots lie far from the catheter tip, are thick and have thick dura, so local anaesthetic penetrates them poorly
  - Epidural infusions spread upward more easily than downward
  - A spinal bathes the cauda equina roots directly, so the block is dense without sacral sparing
- Harder technically, takes longer, fails more often
- Extra risks
  - Dural puncture with a large needle
  - Intravascular or intrathecal injection of a large epidural dose
  - Higher risk of epidural haematoma

## Parturient with uncorrected scoliosis
- Spinal rotation distorts surface landmarks; the epidural space midline shifts towards the convex side of the curve
  - Aim the needle towards the convexity
  - Or use a paramedian approach from the convex side for a direct path
  - Ultrasound helps find the true midline and the right interspace
- Injectate first spreads to the concave side in about 80% of cases
  - Block may be one-sided; larger volumes may be needed

## Flashcards
- Q: Why do epidurals show sacral sparing?
  - A: Sacral roots are far from the catheter, large and thickly sheathed, and spread is mainly upward.
- Q: In scoliosis, which way should the epidural needle be aimed?
  - A: Towards the convexity of the curve.
- Q: In scoliosis, which side does epidural injectate first spread to, and how often?
  - A: The concave side, in about 80% of cases.
- Q: Why is raised ICP a contraindication to epidural?
  - A: Accidental dural puncture risks herniation, and injection can raise ICP further.
