# PECS and Serratus Anterior Plane Blocks

Chest wall fascial plane blocks (now called interpectoral and pectoserratus plane blocks) for analgesia after hemithorax surgery.

## Overview
- PECS I: LA between pectoralis major and minor
  - Blocks the medial and lateral pectoral nerves (supply the pectoral muscles)
- PECS II: PECS I plus a second, more lateral injection between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior
  - Aims to block the lateral branches of the upper intercostal nerves
  - Original PECS II = interpectoral + pectoserratus plane blocks
- Serratus plane block: LA between serratus anterior and latissimus dorsi
  - Blocks the lateral branches of the upper intercostal nerves directly
- Developed as alternatives to thoracic epidural, paravertebral, intercostal and interpleural blocks
- Uses:
  - First designed for breast surgery
  - Case reports for thoracotomy and rib fractures
  - Suggested (letters) for the axilla, proximal medial upper arm and posterior shoulder, areas not supplied by the brachial plexus

## Anatomy
- Muscles of both the pectoral and axillary regions are supplied by the brachial plexus
  - Pectoral region: over pectoralis major; bounded by axillary, mammary and inframammary regions
  - Axillary region: lateral to it, around the axilla
- Pectoral region muscles and nerves:
  - Pectoralis major and minor: lateral and medial pectoral nerves
  - Serratus anterior: long thoracic nerve (C5, C6, and C7)
  - Subclavius: upper trunk of brachial plexus (C5 and C6)
- Axilla: a pyramid with four borders
  1. Apex (inlet): outer border of first rib, upper border of scapula, back of clavicle
  2. Anterior: pectoralis major and minor
  3. Lateral: humerus
  4. Posterior: teres major, latissimus dorsi, subscapularis
- Fasciae:
  - Superficial fascia and deep thoracic fascia
  - Deep thoracic fascia splits into pectoral (superficial), clavipectoral (middle) and exothoracic (deep) layers
  - Clavipectoral fascia runs from clavicle to pectoralis minor and wraps pectoralis minor; its layers fuse between pectoralis minor and subclavius, and rejoin below pectoralis minor as the suspensory ligament of the axilla, attached to the axillary fascia
- Four potential injection compartments:
  1. Between superficial and deep pectoral fascia
  2. Between pectoral and clavipectoral fascia
  3. Between clavipectoral fascia and the surface of serratus anterior
  4. Between serratus anterior and exothoracic fascia
  - 1 and 2 lie in the pectoral region; 3 and 4 connect with the axilla
  - Nerves and vessels cross between compartments
- Nerves of the pectoral region: mainly lateral and medial pectoral; also supraclavicular nerve and lateral and anterior branches of intercostal nerves
- Lateral pectoral nerve (lateral cord, C5–C7):
  - Crosses in front of the axillary artery; pierces clavipectoral fascia with the thoracoacromial artery to reach the deep surface of upper pectoralis major
  - Lies medial to pectoralis minor before entering pectoralis major
  - Joins the medial pectoral nerve across the axillary artery (ansa pectoralis) and so also supplies pectoralis minor
- Medial pectoral nerve (medial cord, C8–T1):
  - Arises behind the axillary artery below the clavicle
  - Pierces pectoralis minor, then enters and supplies pectoralis major
- Both pectoral nerves enter the deep surface of pectoralis major; neither has a cutaneous branch
- Nerves of the axillary region: intercostobrachial, intercostals T3–T9, long thoracic, thoracodorsal
  - Intercostobrachial: lateral cutaneous branch of the second (67%) or third (33%) intercostal nerve; crosses serratus anterior at the midaxillary line to supply the axilla; key for regional anaesthesia of the axilla
  - Intercostal nerves (T3–T9): motor to intercostal muscles; sensory to skin and parietal pleura
    - Posterior, lateral and anterior branches, plus an anterior accessory branch to the sternum
    - Lateral branches supply most of the pectoral and axillary regions and the posterior hemithorax back to the scapula; they pierce external intercostal and emerge between serratus anterior digitations at the midaxillary line
  - Long thoracic nerve: in the axillary compartment near the lateral thoracic branch of the thoracoacromial artery; runs down the lateral surface of serratus anterior, which it supplies
  - Thoracodorsal nerve (C6–C8, nerve to latissimus dorsi): from the posterior cord; runs posteriorly with the thoracodorsal artery; prominent with the arm abducted and externally rotated; large and at risk in lower axillary and reconstructive surgery

## PECS I (interpectoral plane block)
- Hydrodissect the plane between the pectoral muscles to block the lateral and medial pectoral nerves
- Landmarks: pectoralis major, pectoralis minor, pectoral branch of the thoracoacromial artery
- Supine, arm at the side or abducted 90 degrees
- Find the coracoid process with the probe paramedian sagittal
- Rotate the probe slightly (lower end moved laterally, upper end fixed) for an in-plane path from proximal-medial to lateral; this also brings the pectoral branch of the thoracoacromial artery into view
- Confirm the plane by hydrodissection opening the space between the muscles
- Volume: 0.2 mL/kg long-acting LA suggested; no dose-ranging studies, so no evidence-based dose

## PECS II (includes pectoserratus plane block)
- Splits the LA between two compartments:
  - Between the pectoral muscles (pectoral and clavipectoral fascia)
  - Under pectoralis minor (between clavipectoral fascia and serratus anterior)
- Supine, arm abducted 90 degrees or at the side
- First injection as for PECS I
- Second injection at the anterior axillary line, level of the fourth rib
- Depth: usually 1–3 cm for the first, 3–6 cm for the second
- Scanning:
  - Probe at the midclavicular level angled inferolaterally: axillary artery, vein and second rib
  - Move laterally to find pectoralis minor and serratus anterior
  - Move further laterally to find the third and fourth ribs
- Doses: 0.2 mL/kg long-acting LA between pectoralis major and minor, then 0.2 mL/kg between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior

## Serratus anterior plane block
- Superficial vs deep:
  - Superficial: above serratus anterior, between serratus anterior and latissimus dorsi
  - Deep: below serratus anterior, between serratus anterior and the intercostal muscles or ribs
- Site: between the anterior and posterior axillary lines, second to seventh ribs (more lateral, posterior and inferior than PECS I and II)
- Coverage: intercostobrachial nerve, lateral cutaneous branches of T3–T9, long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves (in the compartment between serratus anterior and latissimus dorsi)
- Superficial block landmarks: latissimus dorsi and serratus anterior, with the thoracodorsal artery in the plane between them; ribs, pleura and intercostal muscles also visible
- Position: lateral, or supine with the arm brought forward
- Two ways to find the plane:
  - Count ribs down from the clavicle, moving laterally and distally to the fourth and fifth ribs; probe coronal, tilted posteriorly to find latissimus dorsi (thick, superficial) with serratus anterior (thick, hypoechoic) beneath over the ribs; slide posteriorly to open up the plane
  - Place the probe across the axilla, where latissimus dorsi is more prominent and the thoracodorsal artery easier to find
- In-plane or out-of-plane both acceptable

## Flashcards
- Q: Which plane is injected in PECS I, and which nerves are blocked?
  - A: Between pectoralis major and minor; the medial and lateral pectoral nerves.
- Q: Where is the second PECS II injection made?
  - A: Between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior, at the anterior axillary line at the fourth rib.
- Q: Suggested PECS volume?
  - A: 0.2 mL/kg long-acting LA per injection (not dose-ranged).
- Q: Which intercostal nerve usually gives the intercostobrachial nerve?
  - A: The second (67%); the third in 33%.
- Q: Which nerves does the serratus anterior plane block cover?
  - A: Intercostobrachial, lateral cutaneous branches of T3–T9, long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves.
