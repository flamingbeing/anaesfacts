# Orthopaedic Surgery: Choice of Regional Technique

Almost any orthopaedic operation can be done under a regional technique; the choice depends on where the surgery is.

## Upper limb
- Hand surgery
  - Often local anaesthesia or sedation alone, with a wrist or digital block
  - Axillary or supraclavicular brachial plexus block
  - Other: Bier's block (IV regional anaesthesia)
  - GA plus a wrist or digital block
- Wrist surgery
  - Axillary (preferred) or supraclavicular brachial plexus block
  - Other: Bier's block
- Elbow surgery
  - Axillary (preferred) or supraclavicular brachial plexus block
- Shoulder surgery
  - Supraclavicular or interscalene brachial plexus block

## Lower limb
- Foot and toe surgery
  - Often local anaesthesia or sedation alone
  - Popliteal sciatic plus saphenous block
  - Other: ankle block
- Ankle surgery
  - Popliteal plus saphenous block gives excellent surgical anaesthesia and postoperative analgesia
- Knee surgery
  - Spinal
  - Others: epidural; femoral plus sciatic block (seldom used)
  - GA combined with:
    - Intra-articular local anaesthetic
    - Femoral or adductor canal block
    - Genicular nerve block
    - IPACK block
- Hip surgery
  - Spinal
  - Other: epidural
  - GA combined with:
    - Femoral, fascia iliaca or lumbar plexus block
    - PENG block

## Flashcards
- Q: Preferred block for wrist or elbow surgery?
  - A: Axillary brachial plexus block (supraclavicular is an alternative).
- Q: Block for shoulder surgery?
  - A: Supraclavicular or interscalene brachial plexus block.
- Q: Block combination for ankle surgery?
  - A: Popliteal sciatic plus saphenous block.
- Q: Blocks that can be added to GA for hip surgery?
  - A: Femoral, fascia iliaca, lumbar plexus or PENG block.
