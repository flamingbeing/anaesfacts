# Ankle Block

Block of the five nerves supplying the foot (two deep, three superficial) for foot and toe surgery.

## Nerves
- Five nerves supply the foot; an ankle block anaesthetises all five
- Two deep: tibial nerve and deep peroneal nerve
- Three superficial: superficial peroneal, sural and saphenous nerves
- All are terminal sciatic branches except the saphenous, a sensory branch of the femoral nerve

## Sensory supply
- Posterior tibial: heel
- Deep peroneal: dorsomedial foot and the first web space (between the first two digits)
- Superficial peroneal: dorsum of the foot and all five digits
- Sural: lateral foot
- Saphenous: anteromedial foot

## Indications
- Distal foot and toe surgery; anaesthesia of the whole foot

## Positioning
- Supine
- Footrest under the calf helps access, especially for tibial and sural blocks

## Landmark technique
- Posterior tibial: inject behind the medial malleolus
- Deep peroneal: infiltrate lateral to the dorsalis pedis artery
- Superficial peroneal: subcutaneous wheal from the anterior tibial border to the lateral malleolus
- Sural: subcutaneous injection from just lateral to the Achilles tendon to the lateral malleolus
- Saphenous: subcutaneous infiltration just anterior to the medial malleolus
- About 5–8 mL per nerve

## Ultrasound technique
- Local anaesthetic 3–5 mL per nerve
  - Ultrasound lets you use less volume
  - No need to redirect for circumferential spread: the nerves are small and local anaesthetic soaks in quickly
- Linear high-frequency probe
- In-plane; posterior-to-anterior or anterior-to-posterior depending on ergonomics
- If the small superficial nerves (sural, saphenous, superficial peroneal) cannot be seen, a subcutaneous skin wheal (as in the landmark technique) works

### Posterior tibial nerve
- Probe transverse at or just above the medial malleolus
- Nerve lies just posterior to the posterior tibial artery
  - Colour Doppler helps find the artery
  - Nerve looks hyperechoic with a honeycomb pattern
- "Tom, Dick ANd Harry", anterior to posterior: tibialis posterior tendon, flexor digitorum longus tendon, artery/nerve/vein, flexor hallucis longus tendon
  - Tendons can look like the nerve; trace proximally: tendons become muscle bellies, the nerve stays the same

### Deep peroneal nerve
- Probe transverse at the extensor retinaculum
- Nerve lies just lateral to the anterior tibial artery, on the tibia
  - Near the ankle it crosses the artery from medial to lateral, so in some people it is still medial at this level
  - Small, hypoechoic with a hyperechoic rim, often hard to see; injecting around the artery can help show it

### Superficial peroneal nerve
- Probe transverse on the leg, about 5-10 cm above and in front of the lateral malleolus
- Nerve lies in subcutaneous tissue just superficial to the fascia
  - Pierces the fascia 10–20 cm above the ankle joint on the anterolateral leg, then splits into two or three branches
- To find it before it divides, trace proximally to the groove between extensor digitorum longus and peroneus brevis leading to the fibula; nerve sits in the groove just deep to the fascia
- Then follow it down to the ankle or block it there
- Often too small to see on ultrasound

### Sural nerve
- Supplies the lateral border of the foot and ankle
- Above the lateral malleolus: small hyperechoic structure right beside the small saphenous vein, superficial to the deep fascia
- Traceable up the back of the leg in the midline, superficial to the Achilles tendon and gastrocnemius
- A calf tourniquet enlarges the vein, making it (and the nearby nerve) easier to find

### Saphenous nerve
- Blocked as a separate saphenous nerve block

## Flashcards
- Q: Which ankle block nerve is not a branch of the sciatic nerve?
  - A: The saphenous nerve (from the femoral nerve)
- Q: Local anaesthetic volume per nerve, landmark versus ultrasound?
  - A: About 5–8 mL landmark; 3–5 mL with ultrasound
- Q: Order of structures behind the medial malleolus?
  - A: Tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, artery/nerve/vein, flexor hallucis longus ("Tom, Dick ANd Harry")
- Q: Where does the superficial peroneal nerve pierce the fascia?
  - A: 10–20 cm above the ankle joint on the anterolateral leg
- Q: Which nerve supplies the first web space?
  - A: Deep peroneal nerve
