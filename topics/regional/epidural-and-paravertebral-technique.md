# Epidural and Paravertebral Technique

Practical epidural insertion with ultrasound and waveform confirmation, an intra- and postoperative epidural regimen, and landmark paravertebral block for VATS.

## Hand position for epidural
- Non-dominant hand advances the needle
  - Thumb and index finger hold the needle wings
  - Middle finger gives counterpressure on the back
- Dominant hand: thumb on the LOR syringe
  - Steady gentle pressure to feel LOR; limits saline injected into soft tissue

## Epidural insertion
- Pre-procedure ultrasound: find spinous process, laminae and epidural space; measure depth to lamina; estimate angle
- LA to skin; use a 23G 38mm needle to find the lamina
- Insert the Tuohy at the same point straight onto the lamina; note the depth
- Withdraw and redirect medially (guided by the ultrasound)
- Walk up the lamina; LOR to saline
- Flush and connect a 3-way tap to a transducer: epidural waveform is pulsatile (reflects ICP)
- Once confirmed, flush 3ml 2% lidocaine (60mg); thread catheter 5cm into the space
- Prime filter; give another 2ml 2% lidocaine (40mg) via the catheter; total 100mg lidocaine epidurally

## Intraoperative management
- Bolus 6-7ml of 0.125 to 0.167% bupivacaine (3-4ml diluted to 12ml) every 1.5-2h
- May need vasopressors after each bolus

## Epidural infusion bag
- 200ml 0.2% ropivacaine premixed bag
- Add 4x small fentanyl ampoules (total 8ml, 400mcg)

## Postoperative epidural regimen
- Continuous infusion 3 ml/hr plain 0.2% ropivacaine
- PCEA 3 ml every 20 min; maximum 9ml/hr
- Epidural morphine 2-3 mg every 20-24H
- Regular antiemetic (IV ondansetron) while on the epidural
- Concurrent IV paracetamol and IV parecoxib (limit 3-5 days)

## Hypotension
- Inform the acute pain service or senior on call
- Stop the continuous infusion; keep PCEA
- 250 ml fluid bolus over 15min, and phenylephrine
- Straight leg raise, POCUS; look for hypovolaemia, bleeding, sepsis, cardiac events
- Never leave the patient hypotensive

## Inadequate analgesia
- Inform the acute pain service or senior on call
- Encourage PCEA use
- Check the block height
- If the block does not cover the incision after a PCEA dose and BP is normal: clinician bolus of 4 mls via the pump
- If BP is borderline: IV tramadol, or consider epidural morphine

## Stepping down (ready for the general ward, or by postoperative day 4)
- Omit the night dose of enoxaparin (Clexane) the day before removal
- Epidural morphine 3 mg, then remove the epidural catheter
- Move to abdominal wall catheters
  - Programmed intermittent bolus 30 ml every 4H, PCEA 10 mls, lockout 60 min, maximum 40ml/hr (adjust limits as needed)

## Paravertebral block for VATS
- Landmark technique
  - Non-dominant hand: middle finger on the tip of the spinous process; ring finger (left side) or index finger (right side) beside it
  - LA needle, then Tuohy, perpendicular at the tip of the ring or index finger to hit the transverse process (~3-4cm deep)
  - Walk off the transverse process gently (upwards and lateral) with an LOR syringe to find the space
  - Paravertebral space expected ~1cm past the transverse process
- Ultrasound-guided technique
  - Cover the curvilinear probe with 2x large Tegaderm; the standard sleeve works poorly

## Flashcards
- Q: Epidural test and loading dose of lidocaine in this technique?
  - A: 3ml 2% (60mg) via needle, then 2ml 2% (40mg) via catheter; total 100mg
- Q: Postoperative epidural infusion and PCEA settings?
  - A: 3 ml/hr 0.2% ropivacaine; PCEA 3 ml every 20 min, maximum 9ml/hr
- Q: First steps for epidural-related hypotension?
  - A: Stop the infusion (keep PCEA), 250 ml bolus over 15min, phenylephrine, look for other causes
- Q: Where is the paravertebral space relative to the transverse process?
  - A: About 1cm past it (transverse process at ~3-4cm)
