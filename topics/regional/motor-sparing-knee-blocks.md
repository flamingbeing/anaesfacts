# Motor-Sparing Knee Blocks (Genicular Nerve Block and iPACK)

Sensory-only knee blocks: genicular nerve block for the anterior knee and iPACK for the posterior capsule, both sparing quadriceps strength.

## Knee innervation
- Complex: branches of the femoral, obturator and sciatic nerves
- Wide individual variation, hence inconsistent naming in the literature
- Usual scheme: anterior and posterior compartments, with the anterior compartment split into four quadrants
  - Superolateral (SLGN), superomedial (SMGN), inferolateral (ILGN) and inferomedial (IMGN) genicular nerves, each mainly supplying its own quadrant
- Cadaver studies also show branches from the recurrent peroneal nerve, the nerves to vastus medialis, intermedius and lateralis, and the infrapatellar branch

## Genicular nerve block
- Indications
  - Chronic knee pain
  - Total knee arthroplasty and other operations with moderate to severe knee pain
  - Alternative when femoral or adductor canal blocks are unsuitable or unwanted
- Effect
  - Blocks only the sensory terminal branches to the knee joint, giving anaesthesia of the anterior compartment, roughly by quadrant
  - No skin or motor supply, so quadriceps strength is kept
- Target: next to the genicular arteries if seen, or at the junction of epiphysis and diaphysis of femur and tibia where the bone rises into the condyles
- Volume: 3 to 5 mL per nerve

## Genicular nerve block technique
- Superolateral: probe coronal over the lateral femoral epicondyle, then slide proximally to the metaphysis; superolateral genicular artery may lie between the deep fascia of vastus lateralis and the femur
- Superomedial: probe coronal over the medial femoral epicondyle, slide slightly proximal to the metaphysis just in front of the adductor tubercle; artery between the deep fascia of vastus medialis and the femur
- Inferomedial: probe coronal over the medial tibial condyle, slide distally to the metaphysis; artery lies under the medial collateral ligament
- Common peroneal branches (inferolateral genicular and recurrent peroneal nerves)
  - Can be blocked at Gerdy's tubercle: above, under the iliotibial band; below, under tibialis anterior
  - Often left out because of the risk of blocking the common peroneal nerve itself
  - NYSORA inferolateral: probe coronal on the lateral distal knee; find the lateral tibial epicondyle, slide distally to see the fibular head; artery between the collateral ligament and the lateral tibial condyle
  - NYSORA recurrent peroneal: probe coronal on the anterolateral distal knee at the junction of the lateral tibial epiphysis and diaphysis, in front of the fibula; recurrent tibial artery seen superficial to bone
- Nerve to vastus intermedius
  - Local anaesthetic on the front of the femur a few fingerbreadths above the patella
  - Probe transverse on the femur, needle in-plane

## iPACK block
- Interspace between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the knee
- Purpose: motor-sparing analgesia of the posterior knee capsule
- Targets the articular (sensory) branches of the sciatic and obturator nerves
- Indications: total knee replacement, ACL repair, other knee surgery
- Technique
  - High-frequency linear probe
  - Prone, or supine with the knee flexed about 45° and externally rotated
  - Probe just proximal to the popliteal crease; identify popliteal artery and femoral shaft
  - In-plane, medial to lateral or lateral to medial
  - Target the space between artery and femur
  - Inject 15–20 mL local anaesthetic; watch it spread between artery and capsule

## Flashcards
- Q: Volume per nerve for a genicular nerve block?
  - A: 3 to 5 mL
- Q: Where is the iPACK injection placed and with what volume?
  - A: Between popliteal artery and femur just above the popliteal crease; 15–20 mL
- Q: Which nerves' branches does iPACK target?
  - A: Articular branches of the sciatic and obturator nerves
- Q: Why are genicular nerve blocks motor sparing?
  - A: They hit only sensory terminal branches to the joint; no skin or motor supply
