# Peripheral nerve block catheters

A catheter left near a nerve or plexus after localisation allows repeat boluses or a continuous local anaesthetic infusion.

## Technique
- (1) Place the needle
- (2) Inject 5ml local anaesthetic to confirm tip position and open the space for the catheter
- (3) Advance the catheter
  - About 3cm for blocks close to a nerve
  - Further for plane blocks, e.g. 5-10cm
- (4) Inject through the catheter under ultrasound to confirm a useful position
  - Confirm by seeing the catheter course or the spread of injectate
- (5) Secure it: skin glue (Dermabond), transparent dressing (Tegaderm)

## Practical tips
- Out-of-plane approach helps thread along the perineural sheath, especially popliteal and interscalene
- Skin glue reduces leakage and helps the catheter stick
- Smear ultrasound gel over the dressing if the catheter will lie across it, so it does not stick to the drape
- Reinforce the connector clip; the catheter can also be looped
- Have an assistant thread the catheter
- Watch the catheter go in on ultrasound in real time
- Pump-aspirate the catheter to create motion artefact and find its tip if it needs pulling back
- Prime the catheter with saline to avoid air artefact

## Tunnelling
- Consider in older or obese patients, loose skin over the neck, or when a long infusion is expected
- Downsides: dislodgement while tunnelling, scarring
- One method: insert an orange cannula some distance away, aiming to exit at the catheter site; remove the needle and thread the catheter back through the cannula to the new exit

## Catheter sets
- Contiplex set
  - Side port with valve allows the catheter to be preloaded
  - Open the valve with the introducer before threading
- Catheter-over-needle sets
  - Less leakage: the catheter is the widest thing in the hole

## Flashcards
- Q: How much local anaesthetic is injected before threading a catheter, and why?
  - A: 5 ml, to confirm the tip position and open the space
- Q: How far should a catheter be threaded for a perineural block versus a plane block?
  - A: About 3 cm near a nerve; 5-10 cm for plane blocks
- Q: Why do catheter-over-needle sets leak less?
  - A: The catheter is the widest object in the puncture
- Q: Downsides of tunnelling a catheter?
  - A: Dislodgement during tunnelling and scarring
