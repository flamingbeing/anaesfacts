# Interpleural Block

Local anaesthetic injected between parietal and visceral pleura, giving one-sided somatic block over several thoracic dermatomes.

## Principle
- Injection inside the thoracic cage, between parietal and visceral pleura
- Produces an ipsilateral somatic block of multiple thoracic dermatomes

## Identifying the space
- Relies on detecting negative pressure in the interpleural space
- Methods
  - Air-filled syringe drawn in passively
  - Fluid-filled syringe
  - Balloon deflating
  - Falling column of fluid
  - Hanging drop sucked in from the needle hub
  - Saline infusion or continuous saline flow
  - Electronic negative-pressure detectors

## Flashcards
- Q: Where is local anaesthetic placed in an interpleural block?
  - A: Between the parietal and visceral pleura
- Q: What physical sign identifies the interpleural space?
  - A: Negative pressure (e.g. hanging drop sucked in, falling fluid column, air-filled syringe drawn in)
- Q: What is the distribution of an interpleural block?
  - A: Ipsilateral somatic block of multiple thoracic dermatomes
