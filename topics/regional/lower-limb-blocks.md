# Lower Limb Blocks

Overview of lower limb blocks and the lumbosacral plexus nerve supply to skin, muscles and joints.

  - Obturator nerve block

  - Ankle block

## Plexus anatomy
- Lumbosacral plexus: ventral primary rami of T12–S4; sensory and motor supply to the whole lower limb including hip, knee and ankle
  - Lumbosacral trunk (L4–S1) links lumbar and sacral plexuses, but clinically they are treated as separate
- Lumbar plexus
  - Branches: iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, lateral femoral cutaneous, femoral, obturator
  - Lateral femoral cutaneous, femoral and obturator matter most for the lower limb
  - Usually lies within psoas major, anterior to the L1-L4 transverse processes
- Sacral plexus
  - Gives 12 peripheral nerves; sciatic and posterior femoral cutaneous matter most
  - Lies in the pelvis anterior to piriformis

## Sensory innervation
- Femoral nerve
  - Anterior cutaneous branches: anteromedial thigh (apart from the obturator area)
  - Saphenous nerve: medial leg and foot
  - Articular: hip (nerve to rectus femoris), knee (nerves to the three vasti), medial ankle (saphenous)
  - Vascular: femoral artery and branches
- Obturator nerve
  - Skin of the middle of the medial thigh
  - Articular: hip
- Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve: lateral thigh
- Genitofemoral, ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric: skin over the groin
- Sciatic nerve: all sensation below the knee except the saphenous area
  - Tibial: posterolateral leg and lateral foot (via sural), sole (via medial and lateral plantar nerves and calcaneal branches)
  - Common fibular: lateral leg and dorsum of foot
  - Articular: knee (genicular nerves), ankle, all foot joints
- Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve: posterior thigh and leg

## Motor innervation
- Femoral: anterior thigh compartment except psoas
  - Hip flexors: pectineus, iliacus, sartorius
  - Knee extensors: quadriceps femoris (rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, medialis and intermedius)
- Obturator: medial thigh compartment (hip adductors: adductor longus, brevis, magnus), except the hamstring part of adductor magnus (tibial division of sciatic)
- Sciatic: posterior thigh (hamstrings) and all muscles of the leg and foot
- Hip stabilisers and extensors: superior and inferior gluteal nerves

## Joint innervation
- Hip: mainly femoral, obturator and nerve to quadratus femoris (sacral plexus)
  - Anterior capsule: articular branches of 3 nerves from the lumbar plexus (femoral, obturator, accessory obturator)
    - Holds most of the joint's sensory supply and is the main source of postoperative hip pain; target of the PENG block
  - Posterior capsule: mostly nerve to quadratus femoris; small branches from 3 others (sciatic, superior and inferior gluteal)
- Knee: femoral, obturator and sciatic
  - Anterior: femoral
  - Posterior: tibial and common peroneal divisions of sciatic
  - Medial: may get fibres from the posterior division of obturator
- Ankle: femoral and sciatic
  - Detailed: deep and superficial peroneal, posterior tibial and saphenous nerves
  - Simplified: all sciatic, except skin around the medial malleolus (saphenous, from femoral)

## Flashcards
- Q: Which spinal nerves form the lumbosacral plexus?
  - A: Ventral rami of T12–S4
- Q: Where does the lumbar plexus usually lie?
  - A: Within psoas major, anterior to the L1-L4 transverse processes
- Q: Which nerves supply the anterior hip capsule?
  - A: Femoral, obturator and accessory obturator (the PENG block target)
- Q: Which nerves supply the knee joint?
  - A: Femoral (anterior), sciatic via tibial and common peroneal (posterior), obturator posterior division (medial)
