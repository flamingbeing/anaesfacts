# Forearm and Elbow Level Blocks

Ultrasound-guided blocks of the radial, median and ulnar nerves around the elbow, for hand and wrist surgery or to rescue an incomplete plexus block.

## Overview
- Indications: hand and wrist surgery
- Probe transverse at the elbow
- Aim: local anaesthetic next to each individual nerve (radial, median, ulnar)
- Local anaesthetic: 4–5 mL per nerve
- Useful as a targeted rescue block, keeping total local anaesthetic dose down
- Covers the hand, forearm and wrist territories of each nerve blocked
  - Lateral forearm is supplied by the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (from the musculocutaneous nerve): add a subcutaneous wheal just below the elbow for lateral wrist surgery
  - Same for the medial cutaneous nerve of the forearm
  - An arm or forearm tourniquet usually needs sedation and/or extra analgesia

## Radial nerve
- Best seen above the lateral elbow, in the plane between brachioradialis and brachialis
- Probe transverse on the anterolateral distal arm, 3–4 cm above the elbow crease
- Nerve: hyperechoic triangle or oval with a honeycomb pattern
- Splits just below the elbow crease into superficial (sensory) and deep (motor) branches, which are harder to find in the forearm: one injection above the elbow blocks both
- Slide up and down to optimise the view; proximally the nerve moves posteriorly towards the humerus, deep to triceps in the spiral groove
- In-plane, through brachioradialis, tip beside the nerve
- Nerve stimulation: wrist or finger extension
- Aspirate, then 4–5 mL

## Median nerve
- Superficial at the elbow crease; probe transverse just above the crease in the antecubital fossa
- Find the brachial artery; nerve lies on its medial side, hyperechoic oval about the same size as the artery
  - If not seen, move slightly medially and use colour Doppler for the artery
- Proximally nerve and artery run together to the axilla (relative positions change)
- Distally the nerve leaves the artery and lies deep to pronator teres and flexor digitorum superficialis by mid-forearm
- In-plane from either side; medial to lateral is usually easier as it avoids the artery lateral to the nerve
- Aspirate, then 4–5 mL

## Ulnar nerve
- Posteromedial elbow, a few centimetres above the crease
- Hyperechoic oval honeycomb just under the brachial fascia, superficial to triceps
- Distally it becomes round and hypoechoic, dipping into the ulnar sulcus, then runs under flexor carpi ulnaris into the forearm
- Proximally it can be traced along the medial arm to the axilla
- In-plane from either side (anterior to posterior often more ergonomic)
- Aspirate, then 4–5 mL

## Flashcards
- Q: Local anaesthetic volume per nerve for elbow-level blocks?
  - A: 4–5 mL
- Q: Where is the radial nerve best blocked and why?
  - A: 3–4 cm above the elbow crease between brachioradialis and brachialis, before it splits into superficial and deep branches
- Q: Which side of the brachial artery is the median nerve at the elbow?
  - A: Medial
- Q: Which nerve may need a separate subcutaneous wheal for lateral wrist surgery?
  - A: Lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm (musculocutaneous)
