# Regional Blocks Quick Reference

One-page summary of common ultrasound-guided blocks: level, indications, position, sonoanatomy, needle, volume and twitch, plus general block safety.

## General contraindications
- Patient refusal
- Allergy
- Coagulopathy
- Local infection

## General complications
- Block failure
- Haematoma; inadvertent intra-arterial or intravenous injection
- Infection
- Drug-related: allergy, local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)

## Local anaesthetic
- 0.5% for surgical anaesthesia
- Consider adrenaline 1:400k

## Block performance
- Before the block
  - Check for contraindications; take consent
  - Standard ASA monitoring, IV access, resuscitation drugs and equipment, trained assistant
  - Position the patient; clean and drape, aseptic technique
- Ultrasound
  - Linear probe for most blocks; colour Doppler for vessels in the path
  - Get an image with the relevant structures, target centred, then freeze
- Needle
  - Lidocaine to skin first
  - Echogenic needle (50mm or 80mm), in plane, usually lateral to medial
  - Keep the needle tip in view at all times; feel for the fascial pop
  - Aim for a halo of local anaesthetic around the nerve
- Injection
  - Typical dose: ropivacaine 0.5% 5-10ml
  - Aspirate first (negative for blood), then small aliquots at low injection pressure
  - Ask about pain or paraesthesia; watch for signs of LAST
- Key complications: failure, LAST

## Nerve stimulator technique
- Needle to the black (negative) terminal; skin electrode on the patient (red, positive)
- Frequency 2Hz, pulse width 0.1msec
- Start at 1-1.5mA; advance towards the nerve and look for a twitch
- Reduce to 0.5: twitch should persist
- Reduce to 0.3: there should be no twitch
- Back up to 0.5: twitch present again
- Inject local anaesthetic: twitch should disappear

## Neuraxial
- Thoracic compared with lumbar vertebrae
  - Shallower
  - Kyphotic curve
  - Straighter
- Benefits of epidural
  - Reduces stress and sympathetic response
  - Improves respiratory function
  - Reduces DVT risk
  - Pain control
- Complications include those of catheter insertion
- Monitor motor block with the Bromage scale

## Brachial plexus: four classical approaches
- Interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, axillary

### Interscalene
- Level: roots
- Indications: shoulder and humerus, down to the elbow
- Spares C8 and T1
- Complications: intravascular, intrathecal or epidural injection; Horner's syndrome; phrenic nerve block; recurrent laryngeal nerve block; pneumothorax
- Contraindications: moderate to severe respiratory disease; contralateral phrenic or recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy
- Position: supine, head turned away, pillow under head
- Technique
  - Linear probe transverse on the neck at the cricoid (C6), interscalene groove between anterior and middle scalene
  - See SCM, then anterior and middle scalene with roots C5/6/7 between them as a "traffic light"
  - Carotid and IJV lie medially; colour Doppler for the vertebral artery deep
  - 50mm needle in plane, posterior to anterior, between the roots
  - 10-15ml 0.5% ropivacaine
- Twitch: C5 deltoid, C6 biceps

### Supraclavicular
- Level: trunks
- Indications: mid-humerus and below; may spare the ulnar nerve
- Spares the intercostobrachial nerve
- Complications: higher pneumothorax risk; subclavian artery puncture; otherwise as for interscalene
- Contraindication: contralateral pneumothorax
- Position: supine, head turned away
- Technique
  - Linear probe transverse, above and parallel to the midpoint of the clavicle (base of the interscalene groove), angled into the thorax
  - Subclavian artery on the 1st rib, plexus as a "bunch of grapes" lateral to it, pleural sliding
  - Middle scalene lateral; anterior scalene and SCM medial
  - Colour Doppler for the dorsal scapular artery within the plexus
  - 80mm needle in plane, lateral to medial, to the "corner pocket" first
  - 20-25ml of 0.5% ropivacaine
- Twitch: forearm, wrist, fingers

### Infraclavicular (paracoracoid)
- Level: cords
- Indications: elbow and below
- Spares the intercostobrachial nerve
- Complications: lower pneumothorax risk; axillary artery puncture
- Contraindication: coagulopathy
- Position: supine, arm abducted, elbow flexed 90 deg over the abdomen
- Technique
  - Linear probe parasagittal below the clavicle, 2cm medial and 2cm caudal to the coracoid process
  - Axillary artery deep to pectoralis major and minor, lateral to pleura; axillary vein caudal
  - Lateral cord cephalad, medial cord caudal, posterior cord deep
  - 100mm needle in plane, cranial to caudal, around the artery, deep first
  - 20-25ml of 0.5% ropivacaine
- Twitch: lateral cord elbow flexion (too lateral); medial cord wrist flexion; posterior cord wrist or finger extension

### Axillary
- Level: branches
- Indications: elbow and below; far from lung and neuraxis
- Downsides: spares the intercostobrachial and musculocutaneous nerves; several redirections; very variable anatomy; slower onset
- Complication: axillary artery puncture
- Position: supine, arm abducted and externally rotated, elbow flexed 90 deg above the head
- Technique
  - Linear probe high in the axilla, short axis to the arm
  - Axillary artery with biceps and coracobrachialis anterior, conjoint tendon posterior; axillary vein posterior
  - Median nerve superficial and lateral; ulnar superficial and medial; radial posterior and medial to the artery
  - Musculocutaneous nerve between biceps (superficial) and coracobrachialis (deep); block it separately
  - 50mm needle anterior to posterior, deposit deep first
  - 20ml + 5ml 0.5% ropivacaine
- Twitch: radial wrist or finger extension; ulnar thumb adduction; median wrist flexion; musculocutaneous biceps

### Intercostobrachial (branch of T2)
- Sensation: posteromedial upper arm
- Indications: AV graft surgery, tourniquet
- Linear subcutaneous field block, 5ml, from superior (deltoid prominence) to inferior (medial upper arm)

### Other upper limb blocks
- Distal nerve branches
- Bier's block (IV regional anaesthesia)

## Lower limb

### Fascia iliaca block, infrainguinal
- Indication: hip fracture analgesia
- Landmark: junction of the middle and lateral thirds of the inguinal ligament
- 80mm needle in plane, lateral to medial; two pops (fascia lata, then fascia iliaca)
- Large volume: 30-40ml 0.3-0.4% ropivacaine

### Fascia iliaca block, suprainguinal
- Probe parasagittal just medial to the ASIS along the inguinal ligament
- Slide along the ligament to the AIIS with iliacus on top
- Internal oblique and sartorius form the "bow tie" sign
- Colour Doppler for the deep circumflex iliac artery (DCIA)
- 80mm needle caudal to cranial between internal oblique and iliacus; pop through fascia iliaca and spread below the DCIA

### Femoral nerve block (femoral crease)
- Indications: anterior thigh, knee, medial leg
- Downside: quadriceps weakness
- Position: supine, leg slightly abducted
- Linear probe parallel to the inguinal crease, lateral to the femoral pulse
- See sartorius, iliopsoas and femoral nerve, artery and vein
- 80mm needle in plane, lateral to medial; two pops (fascia lata, fascia iliaca)
- 10-15ml ropivacaine
- Twitch: quadriceps ("patellar dance")

### Adductor canal block
- Indications: knee surgery, medial leg and foot; spares the quadriceps
- Position: supine, leg abducted and externally rotated
- Linear probe at the junction of the middle and distal thirds of the thigh
- Sartorius, vastus medialis and adductor magnus; femoral artery central, femoral vein deep
- 80mm needle in plane, lateral to the artery, between artery and vastus medialis
- 10-15ml ropivacaine

### Sciatic nerve (subgluteal)
- Curvilinear probe along the line from greater trochanter to ischial tuberosity
- Alternative: trace up from the popliteal fossa

### Popliteal block
- Indication: foot surgery
- Position: supine, lateral or prone
- Linear probe in the popliteal fossa; nerve superficial to the artery; scan proximally to the sciatic bifurcation
- Biceps femoris lateral, semimembranosus medial
- Twitch: inversion

### Distal saphenous nerve
- 8ml subcutaneously, lateral to the tibial tuberosity

### Ankle block
- Indication: foot surgery
- 5x5ml
  - Saphenous: superficial ring
  - Superficial peroneal: superficial ring
  - Sural: superficial ring
  - Deep peroneal: medial to the dorsalis pedis artery
  - Posterior tibial: posterior to the posterior tibial artery

### Lumbar plexus
- Curvilinear probe

### PENG block (pericapsular nerve group)
- Indication: hip fracture analgesia
- Probe at the ASIS parallel to the inguinal crease; slide medially to see the AIIS, iliopubic eminence and psoas tendon
- Needle to between the psoas tendon and pubic ramus

## Abdominal wall

### Lateral TAP block
- Indications: lower abdomen, T9-T12; blocks lateral and anterior cutaneous branches
- Position: supine
- Linear probe axial at the triangle of Petit (iliac crest, external oblique, latissimus dorsi), or midaxillary line between costal margin and iliac crest
- See external oblique, internal oblique and transversus abdominis
- 80mm needle anterior to posterior; watch the spread
- 20ml per side, 0.3% ropivacaine

### Other blocks of the TAP plane
- Posterior TAP: move posteriorly to the posterior end of the plane
- Anterior TAP: as for ilioinguinal, at the ASIS
- Quadratus lumborum blocks
  - QLB1 (lateral): move posteriorly; inject between transversus aponeurosis and transversalis fascia
  - QLB2 (posterior): shamrock sign
  - QLB 3 (anterior)
- Oblique subcostal TAP
  - Indications: upper abdomen, T6-T9
  - Linear probe obliquely along the lower costal margin
  - See external oblique, internal oblique, transversus and the lateral edge of rectus abdominis and sheath
  - 80mm needle between transversus and the posterior rectus sheath

### Rectus sheath block
- Indication: midline laparotomy
- Linear probe transverse just lateral to the umbilicus
- 80mm needle between rectus abdominis and the posterior sheath
- 10ml per side 0.5% ropivacaine, 0.1ml/kg

### Ilioinguinal block
- Complications: intraperitoneal injection or bowel perforation; femoral nerve block
- Ultrasound: linear probe beside the ASIS, oblique along the ASIS–umbilicus line; see the 3 muscle layers; colour Doppler for the deep circumflex iliac artery
- 80mm needle between internal oblique and transversus
- 10ml per side 0.5% ropivacaine, 0.15ml/kg
- Landmark: 1cm medial and inferior to the ASIS; two clicks through external then internal oblique

## Spine and paraspinal region

### Paravertebral block (PVB)
- Indications: thoracic surgery, rib fractures, breast surgery
- Complications: pneumothorax, LAST, epidural or intrathecal spread
- Position: sitting or lateral, block side up
- Parasagittal technique
  - Count levels from the angle of the scapula
  - Linear probe parasagittal, parallel to the spinous processes
  - Trapezius, with or without rhomboid, erector spinae
  - Slide laterally: laminae ("horse heads"), articular processes ("camel humps"), transverse processes ("trident"), then ribs
  - 50mm needle caudal to cranial, between superior costotransverse membrane and pleura
  - 5ml ropivacaine 0.5% per level; total at least 20ml
- Transverse technique
  - Linear probe transverse; 50mm needle lateral to medial, between internal intercostal membrane and pleura

### Erector spinae plane block (ESP)
- Indications and complications: as for PVB
- Position: sitting or lateral, block side up
- Linear probe parasagittal: trapezius, rhomboids, erector spinae, transverse process, pleura
- Needle cranial to caudal onto the transverse process
- 30-40ml of 0.35% ropivacaine; watch the spread

### Caudal block
- Landmark: equilateral triangle with the 2 PSIS; sacral hiatus between the 2 sacral cornua
- Pierce the sacrococcygeal ligament

### Intercostal block
- Neurovascular bundle (vein, artery, nerve) in the costal groove
- Posterior axillary line, lateral to the paraspinal muscles
- Walk off the lower border of the rib; pop as the internal intercostal muscle is pierced
- 3-5ml per level
- Issues: high local anaesthetic absorption, pneumothorax

## Other blocks

### Superficial cervical plexus
- Landmark: midpoint of the posterior border of SCM; 10-15ml as a fan
- Intermediate ultrasound technique: linear probe at the same point; 10-15ml between the investing fascia under SCM and the prevertebral fascia over the scalenes
- Branches (OATS): lesser occipital, great auricular, transverse cervical, supraclavicular

### Scalp block (S2ZAGO2)
- Supratrochlear: lateral to the supraorbital notch
- Supraorbital: above the supraorbital notch
- Zygomaticotemporal: lateral corner of the eye along the zygomatic arch
- Auriculotemporal: next to the superficial temporal artery
- Greater auricular: behind the ear
- Lesser occipital: lateral to the greater occipital nerve along the superior nuchal line
- Greater occipital: halfway between occipital protuberance and mastoid process, medial to the occipital artery

### Penile block
- Indication: penile surgery
- Complication: haematoma
- Landmark: walk off the pubic symphysis, direct laterally to the suspensory ligament, pop through Buck's fascia
- No adrenaline

### Airway blocks for fibreoptic intubation
- Superior laryngeal nerve block

### Chest wall
- Options: thoracic epidural, paravertebral, ESP
- PECS: deltopectoral groove, in plane, medial to lateral
  - PECS 1 (between pectoralis major and minor): medial and lateral pectoral nerves
    - Probe parasagittal below the coracoid, tilted towards the thorax
  - PECS 2 (between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior): lateral branches of intercostals (spares anterior branches), long thoracic nerve, intercostobrachial nerve
    - Slide caudally to the 4th rib to see serratus anterior
- Serratus anterior plane block: between anterior and posterior axillary lines
- Transversus thoracis plane block (TTP): between internal intercostal and transversus thoracis muscles

## Emergency cricothyroidotomy (ultrasound)
- Linear probe transverse on the neck, starting at the thyroid cartilage
- TACA sequence: thyroid cartilage, airline, cricoid cartilage, airline

## Practical tips
- Adjust the needle to the probe, not the probe to the needle
- Light sedation: small 2--3ml propofol boluses from a 3ml syringe
- Paravertebral: parasagittal approach is often preferred
- ESP: some aim for the flattest image of the transverse processes; others prefer rounded "tombstone" transverse processes and a caudal to cranial needle
- PECS2: hit the rib, inject, then lift the needle
- Catheters: some place the block through the needle first, without the catheter

## Flashcards
- Q: Nerve stimulator settings for peripheral nerve location?
  - A: 2Hz, 0.1msec, start 1-1.5mA; twitch at 0.5, none at 0.3, back at 0.5, gone after injection.
- Q: Which roots does an interscalene block spare, and what volume is used?
  - A: C8 and T1; 10-15ml 0.5% ropivacaine.
- Q: Volume for supraclavicular and infraclavicular blocks?
  - A: 20-25ml of 0.5% ropivacaine.
- Q: Fascia iliaca volume and concentration?
  - A: 30-40ml of 0.3-0.4% ropivacaine.
- Q: Paravertebral dose?
  - A: 5ml ropivacaine 0.5% per level, at least 20ml total.
