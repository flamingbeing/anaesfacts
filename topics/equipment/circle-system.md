# Circle System and Low Flow Anaesthesia

Breathing system that rebreathes exhaled gas after CO2 removal, adding parts to a Mapleson-type circuit.

## Advantages
- Low fresh gas flow: can be as low as the uptake of oxygen and anaesthetic by the patient and circuit
  - The lower the flow, the longer inspired oxygen and agent take to reflect a dial change; inspired levels can still vary widely
- Easy scavenging and less atmospheric pollution
- Better conservation of heat and humidity

## Disadvantages
- Complex, less portable, more risk of disconnection or malfunction
- Higher circuit resistance from the absorber and unidirectional valves
- Absorbent-related complications
- Organisms can be retained, with risk of respiratory infection; bacterial filters reduce this

## Dead space
- With unidirectional valves, dead space is only the part distal to the Y-connector (where inspired and expired gas mix)

## Optimal configuration (Brown)
- Unidirectional valves near the patient, so a leak does not let gas flow back into the inspiratory limb
  - But not at the Y-connector, where correct orientation and function are hard to check
- Reservoir bag in the expiratory limb, upstream of the absorber
  - Lowers expiratory resistance
  - Squeezing the bag in controlled ventilation vents expired gas through the APL valve, saving absorbent
- APL valve at the reservoir bag, upstream of the absorber
  - Downstream of the absorber it would waste absorbent on gas that is then vented
- Absorber after the APL valve, before the fresh gas inlet
- Fresh gas inlet between the absorber and the inspiratory valve
  - Downstream of the inspiratory valve: fresh gas bypasses the patient into the expiratory limb during expiration
  - Upstream of the absorber: fresh gas is diluted by untreated recirculating gas, and soda lime can take up or release volatile, slowing induction and emergence
- In most machines the common gas outlet joins the circuit just past the exhalation valve, to avoid falsely high exhaled tidal volume readings

## Low flow classification
- Closed: fresh gas flow equals metabolic uptake exactly (oxygen about 3.5 mL/kg/min)
  - APL closed, no waste; sampled gas must be returned to the circuit
- Minimal flow: 0.5 L/min
- Low flow: below alveolar ventilation, typically < 1.5 L/min

## Initial high flow
- Start with 3–5 L/min for denitrogenation (avoids hypoxia from nitrogen build-up) and to prime the circuit and FRC volume
- Duration of high flow: 15 mins before closed circuit, 6 mins before low flow

## Flashcards
- Q: Where is dead space in a circle system?
  - A: Distal to the Y-connector only.
- Q: Where should the fresh gas inlet sit?
  - A: Between the absorber and the inspiratory valve.
- Q: Define minimal flow and low flow.
  - A: Minimal 0.5 L/min; low flow below alveolar ventilation, typically < 1.5 L/min.
- Q: Initial fresh gas flow and duration before low flow?
  - A: 3–5 L/min for 6 mins (15 mins before closed circuit).
- Q: Metabolic oxygen uptake in closed circuit?
  - A: About 3.5 mL/kg/min.
