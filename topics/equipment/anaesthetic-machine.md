# Anaesthetic Machine

Takes gases from the supply, sets their mix and flow, adds volatile agent and delivers fresh gas flow to the breathing circuit.

## Functions
- Receives medical gases and lowers their pressure to a safe level
- Mixes gases to a set composition and flow before adding a known concentration of vapour
- Vaporises volatile anaesthetics into the gas mixture
- Delivers fresh gas flow (FGF) to the breathing circuit through the common gas outlet

## Overview
- Basic parts
  - Gas supply and flowmeters
  - Vaporisers
- Additional parts
  - Mechanical ventilator, usually bag-in-bottle; can be switched out
  - Breathing circuit: usually a circle system; occasionally a Bain circuit
  - Scavenging
- Other: auxiliary oxygen supply

## Key components
- 1. Gas supplies
- 2. Pressure gauges
- 3. Pressure regulators (reducing valves)
- 4. Flowmeters
- 5. Vaporisers
- 6. Common gas outlet
- 7. Other features: high-flow oxygen flush, pressure-relief valve, oxygen supply failure alarm, suction

## High- and low-pressure systems
- High-pressure system: from the wall or cylinder to the flow control valve
  - Pipelines and cylinders
  - Fail-safe valve
  - Pressure regulator
- Flowmeters form the boundary
- Low-pressure system
  - Vaporisers
  - Check valve
  - Common gas outlet
- Oxygen flush valve bypasses both flowmeters and vaporisers

## Flashcards
- Q: Which component marks the boundary between high- and low-pressure systems?
  - A: The flowmeters
- Q: What does the oxygen flush bypass?
  - A: Both the flowmeters and the vaporisers
- Q: Name the high-pressure system components.
  - A: Pipelines and cylinders, fail-safe valve, pressure regulator
