# Common Anaesthesia Machines

Key features of common GE and Dräger workstations: ventilator drive, bellows or piston, vaporisers, circuit volume and battery life.

## GE Healthcare (Madison, Wisconsin)

### GE Aestiva (Aestiva 5)
- Monitoring: volume, pressure, inspired oxygen (VPO)
- Ventilator: older GE 7100, ascending (standing) bellows, dual circuit, pneumatically driven
  - Tidal volume corrected for leaks, compliance and fresh gas flow
  - Variable orifice flow sensors sit near the unidirectional valves, so circuit compliance losses beyond them are not corrected
  - Accurate to small tidal volumes in VCV (20-1500 mL)
  - Single "bag/vent" switch starts the ventilator
- Modes: VCV, PCV, SIMV (optional), PSV, manual/spontaneous
- Checklist: manual; follow the operator's manual
- Flowmeters: mechanical needle valves and glass flowtubes; no electronic capture of fresh gas flow; minimum oxygen flow 50 mL/min
- Vaporisers: variable-bypass (Tec 4, 5, 6, 7), removable without tools
- Breathing circuit: large (5.5 L including two canisters of 1.35 kg absorbent each); loose or prepacked absorbent; works with non-rebreathing circuits
- Scavenging: closed interface
- Power failure: 30 minute battery with fresh gas, vaporisers and ventilator working
- MRI-compatible version available
- Weight: 136-154 kg
- Overall: traditional design with an easier-to-clean absorber head; strong ventilator, first gas machine with pressure support; flow sensors have been moisture-sensitive; gas and patient monitors bought separately

### GE Aespire
- Same GE 7100 ventilator as the Aestiva
- Conventional vaporisers, like the Avance

### GE Aisys (Aisys CS2)
- Monitoring: volume, pressure, paramagnetic oxygen (VPO), spirometry, agent analysis, capnography; optional integrated patient monitoring
- Ventilator: newer GE 7900, ascending (standing) bellows, dual circuit, pneumatically driven
  - Tidal volume corrected for leaks, compliance and fresh gas flow (fresh gas compensation)
  - Flow sensors near the unidirectional valves correct for compliance losses and leaks
  - Accurate to small tidal volumes in volume control (20-1500 mL)
  - Single "bag/vent" switch
- Modes: manual/spontaneous, volume control, pressure control, PCV with volume guarantee, PSVPro (pressure support with apnoea backup), SIMV (volume or pressure), electronic PEEP
- Checklist: automated with some manual steps
- Flowmeters: electronic control and capture of fresh gas flow; no minimum oxygen flow
- Vaporisers: electronically controlled Aladin cassettes
- Breathing circuit: 2.7 L on the ventilator, 1.2 L in bag mode (800 gm absorbent); loose or prepacked absorbent
- Scavenging: closed, open or passive interface
- Power failure: 30 minute battery; a safety oxygen flow control still delivers oxygen without power
- Weight: 168 kg
- Overall: advanced modes and monitoring, small circuit, electronic gas and vapour control

### GE Avance (Avance S5)
- Same GE 7900 ventilator and breathing system as the Aisys
- Difference: conventional pneumatic/mechanical vaporisers

## Dräger Medical (Telford, Pennsylvania)

### Dräger Apollo
- Piston ventilator, electrically driven, electronically controlled, with fresh gas decoupling

### Dräger Perseus A500
- Monitoring: volume, pressure, inspired oxygen (VPO), gas analysis, CO2, spirometry
- Ventilator: turbine
  - Tidal volume corrected for leaks, patient and circuit compliance, and fresh gas flow (fresh gas compensation)
  - First turbine anaesthesia ventilator in the US
  - No "bag/vent" switch: mode changes are electronic
  - Accurate to small tidal volumes (20-1400 mL); use paediatric circle hoses for small VT and repeat the leak/compliance test after changing circuits
- Modes: wide range, first anaesthesia ventilator with APRV and CPAP
  - VC-CMV (with Autoflow, SIMV, PEEP, PSV), PC-CMV (with SIMV, PSV, PEEP), PC-BiPAP, APRV, CPAP, manual/spontaneous
- Checklist: electronic self-test, partly manual
- Flowmeters: mechanical needle valves with electronic display, total fresh gas flow glass flowtube; electronic capture; no minimum oxygen flow; S-ORC (sensitive oxygen ratio controller) as hypoxic guard
- Vaporisers: variable-bypass Vapor 3000, removable without tools
- Breathing circuit: small (1.5 L absorbent, 2.1 L total); loose or prepacked absorbent
- Scavenging: open interface
- Power failure: at least 30 minute battery with fresh gas, vaporisers, integrated monitors and ventilator working; patient monitors may stop
  - After the battery runs out, pneumatic parts still work: vaporisers, S-ORC, APL valve, flowmeters, cylinder and pipeline gauges, total flowmeter
- Reservoir bag moves with each ventilator breath, acting as an extra disconnect alarm (empties during inspiration, the opposite of the Apollo)
- Weight: 150 kg without cylinders and vaporisers
- Overall: modern top-end machine with a novel ventilator

### Dräger Fabius GS
- Monitoring: volume, pressure, inspired oxygen (VPO)
  - Galvanic cell oxygen sensor; new cells need a 15 minute warm-up before calibration
  - Hot-wire (thermal anemometry) flow sensor in the circuit, shared with Apollo, Perseus and a few others; can be upset by humidity or diathermy interference; shows VT, minute volume, rate and pressure waveform
- Ventilator: piston, electrically driven and electronically controlled
  - Tidal volume corrected for leaks, patient and circuit compliance, and fresh gas flow (fresh gas decoupling)
  - Vertical piston visible in a window left of the flowmeters
  - "Fresh gas low" alarm warns the piston is drawing in room air (negative pressure threshold -2 to -5 cm of water)
  - No "bag/vent" switch
  - Accurate to small tidal volumes (20-1400 mL); paediatric hoses for small VT, repeat leak/compliance test after circuit change
  - Shows either measured values or settings, not both at once
- Modes: VCV, PCV, PSV, SIMV-volume with PSV, manual/spontaneous
- Checklist: mostly manual FDA-style, with electronic self-tests (system, leaks, flow sensor, oxygen sensor)
- Flowmeters: vertical needle valves, digital display, total flow glass tubes, graphic flow display; electronic capture; no minimum oxygen flow; S-ORC hypoxic guard
- Vaporisers: variable-bypass (Vapor 2000, Vapor 19 or Tec 6), removable without tools
- Breathing circuit: absorber not warmed; small (2.8 L, of which 1.5 L absorbent); loose or prepacked absorbent
  - Change absorbent if idle for 48 hours, or weekly on Monday
  - Not usable with non-rebreathing circuits
- Scavenging: open interface
- Power failure: 45 minute battery with fresh gas, vaporisers, integrated monitors and ventilator; separate patient monitors stop
  - Pneumatic parts keep working after the battery is flat (vaporisers, S-ORC, APL valve, flowmeters, breathing pressure gauge, cylinder and pipeline gauges, total flowmeter)
- Desflurane compensation must be entered when using desflurane, or flow readings are wrong
- Fresh gas decoupling makes the reservoir bag move with each breath, acting as a disconnect alarm
- Weight: 101 kg without cylinders and vaporisers
- Overall: good ventilator, economical if keeping existing monitors; buy own monitors and gas analysis
  - Versions: MRI, Tiro (small spaces), Tiro M (military field use)

### Dräger Julian
- Monitoring: volume, pressure, inspired oxygen (VPO)
  - Galvanic cell oxygen sensor
  - Hot-wire flow sensor (shared with Fabius GS, Apollo, Perseus); sensitive to diathermy interference; shows VT, minute volume, rate, pressure waveform
  - Infrared gas analysis and capnography
  - Optional integrated pulse oximetry
- Ventilator: descending (hanging) bellows, pneumatically driven, dual circuit
  - Compensates for leaks, circuit compliance and fresh gas flow
  - Modes: VCV, PCV, manual/spontaneous
  - No "bag/vent" switch
  - Accurate to small tidal volumes (50-1400 mL); paediatric hoses for VT less than 200 mL; repeat leak/compliance test after circuit change
- Checklist: mostly manual FDA-style with electronic self-tests (system, leaks/compliance, oxygen sensor)
- Flowmeters (its most distinctive feature): set total fresh gas flow, carrier gas (nitrous oxide or air) and target inspired oxygen
  - Fully electronic; no needle valves or glass tubes
  - Digital display, optional graphic display; electronic capture
  - No minimum oxygen flow; electronic hypoxic guard; mechanical "Safety O2" backup flowmeter
- Vaporisers: variable-bypass (Vapor 2000 or Vapor 19), removable without tools
- Breathing circuit: absorber warmed; large (4.5 L without hoses, of which 1.5 L absorbent); loose granules only; not usable with non-rebreathing circuits
- Scavenging: open interface
- Power failure: 30 minute battery with fresh gas, vaporisers, integrated monitors and ventilator; separate patient monitors stop
- Compact breathing system, easy to strip and clean
- Weight: 90 kg without cylinders and vaporisers
- Overall: modern compact machine, unfamiliar to many US-trained anaesthetists; buy own patient monitors

## Flashcards
- Q: What ventilator drive and bellows does the GE Aisys use?
  - A: GE 7900, pneumatically driven, ascending (standing) bellows.
- Q: How long is the battery reserve on the Dräger Fabius GS?
  - A: 45 minutes.
- Q: What does the Fabius "fresh gas low" alarm mean?
  - A: The piston is drawing in room air (negative pressure -2 to -5 cm of water).
- Q: What is the minimum oxygen flow on the GE Aestiva?
  - A: 50 mL/min.
- Q: Which Dräger machine uses a turbine ventilator?
  - A: Perseus A500.
