# Ideal Anaesthetic Agent

No single drug has every ideal property, so anaesthesia combines several drugs ("balanced anaesthesia").

## Ideal anaesthetic drug

- Easy to give, even to uncooperative patients
- Fast onset
- Short duration
- Few side effects
- IV and inhaled drugs fit this better than oral, IM or SC drugs

## Ideal drug for induction and maintenance

- Plasma levels predictable from a known pharmacokinetic model
- Predictable link between pharmacokinetics and effect
- No active metabolites
- Rapid onset and rapid offset
- Stable in a plastic syringe

## Aims of general anaesthesia

- Ease airway management, including intubation
- Unconsciousness and amnesia throughout
- Good surgical conditions
- Haemodynamic stability
- No harm to the intraoperative course or recovery

## Balanced anaesthesia

- Several drugs used together, since none achieves all aims in every patient
- Maximises the benefits of each drug and minimises side effects

## Flashcards

- Q: What is balanced anaesthesia?
  - A: Combining several drugs to get their benefits while limiting each one's side effects
- Q: Name pharmacokinetic properties of an ideal induction and maintenance drug.
  - A: Predictable plasma levels from a known model, predictable PK-PD link, no active metabolites, rapid onset and offset
- Q: Which routes best suit the ideal anaesthetic profile?
  - A: IV and inhalational
