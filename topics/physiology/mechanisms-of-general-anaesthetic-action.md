# Mechanisms of general anaesthetic action

General anaesthetics act mainly on ligand-gated ion channels (especially GABAA) in the brain and spinal cord, not through a single structural mechanism.

## Requirements of any theory
- Explain loss of awareness, loss of response to noxious stimuli, and above all reversibility
- Anaesthetic molecules are simple and varied, with no clear structure-activity relationship

## Anatomical sites
- Brain and spinal cord: together explain antinociception, unconsciousness and loss of explicit memory
- Thalamus: likely main target (evoked potential evidence)
- Secondary: limbic system (memory) and some cortical areas
- Volatiles act more on the spinal cord than IV agents

## Lipid theories
- Meyer-Overton: inhaled agent potency correlates with lipid solubility, suggesting action at hydrophobic sites
- Once thought to be the membrane lipid bilayer; now thought to be lipid sites next to ion channels

## Protein sites
- Ligand-gated channels are more sensitive than voltage-gated channels
- Studied targets: inhibitory (GABAA, glycine) and excitatory (neuronal nicotinic, NMDA)

## GABAA receptor
- Pentameric ligand-gated ion channel, same family as the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor
- At least 30 subtypes with different subunit make-up
- Binding sites
  - GABA: associated with α subunits
  - Benzodiazepines: α/γ interface
  - Etomidate, barbiturates, propofol, volatiles: β subunit, separate from the benzodiazepine site
  - Etomidate acts more on β2 and β3 than β1
- Anaesthetics prolong channel opening, so more chloride enters and the cell hyperpolarises
  - Seen with etomidate, propofol, barbiturates and volatiles
- Etomidate is selective for GABAA; propofol also prolongs glycine channel opening and inhibits neuronal nicotinic and 5HT3 receptors
- Stereospecificity supports a protein site: etomidate is the R(+) isomer; S(−) is inactive, with a 30-fold activity difference at GABAA

## Glycine receptor
- Main inhibitory transmitter in spinal cord and brainstem; chloride channel similar to GABAA
- Volatiles strongly potentiate glycine, without stereoselectivity
- Spinal cord may be a key site for volatiles rather than IV agents; effect here relates to immobility more than awareness

## NMDA receptor
- Excitatory; involved in long-term potentiation (learning and memory)
- Activated by glutamate, modulated by magnesium
- Non-competitively inhibited by ketamine, nitrous oxide and xenon: a further anaesthetic pathway
- Barbiturates also reduce glutamate effect, less potently than their GABAA action

## Flashcards
- Q: Which GABAA subunit carries the propofol and etomidate site?
  - A: The β subunit (separate from the benzodiazepine α/γ site)
- Q: What does the Meyer-Overton correlation state?
  - A: Inhaled anaesthetic potency correlates with lipid solubility
- Q: How does etomidate support a protein site of action?
  - A: Only the R(+) isomer is active; 30-fold activity difference at GABAA
- Q: Which agents inhibit the NMDA receptor non-competitively?
  - A: Ketamine, nitrous oxide and xenon
