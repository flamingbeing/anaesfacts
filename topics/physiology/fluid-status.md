# Fluid status

Bedside assessment of volume status: recognising fluid overload and dehydration.

## Overview of assessment

- History: shortness of breath, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea (PND)
- Examination: ability to lie flat, crepitations, pedal oedema
- Investigations: CXR, POCUS, NT-proBNP

## Principles

- Euvolaemia: normal body fluid volume giving optimal cardiac filling, so cardiac output meets global O2 demand
  - Corresponds to point O ("optimised") on the Frank–Starling curve
  - Some patients are kept deliberately "dry" (hypovolaemic): raised pulmonary artery pressure, COPD, after some operations, especially thoracic surgery
  - A few are best kept "full" (hypervolaemic), but this is less common
  - For most patients the aim is to find and keep euvolaemia
- Volaemia is a static measure of volume status (contrast with fluid responsiveness)

## Fluid overload

### Signs
- Raised JVP: assess further with CVP, PCWP, IVC POCUS
- Bibasal crepitations: lung POCUS, CXR
- Orthopnoea, PND
- Pedal, sacral or other peripheral oedema
- Hypertension

### Investigations
- CXR
- POCUS (fluid responsiveness)
- Sodium and haematocrit trend
- NT-proBNP
- Daily weights; strict input/output charting

## Dehydration

### History
- Children: crying without tears, number of wet nappies

### Signs
- Dry tongue and mucous membranes; reduced skin turgor (skin tenting); sunken eyes or fontanelle
- Poor perfusion: postural hypotension, cool peripheries, prolonged capillary refill time
- Vital signs

### Other ways of tracking
- Poor urine output (input/output charting); urine osmolality
- Lactic acidosis as an indirect sign of hypovolaemia
- Urea:creatinine ratio
- Sodium and haematocrit trend

### Severity
- Severe if there is decompensation:
  - Drowsiness
  - Prolonged capillary refill
  - Hypotension

## Flashcards
- Q: What is euvolaemia on the Frank–Starling curve?
  - A: Point O, the optimised point of cardiac filling
- Q: Which patients may be kept deliberately dry?
  - A: Raised pulmonary artery pressure, COPD, after some surgery especially thoracic
- Q: Signs of fluid overload?
  - A: Raised JVP, bibasal crepitations, orthopnoea or PND, peripheral oedema, hypertension
- Q: Features of severe (decompensated) dehydration?
  - A: Drowsiness, prolonged capillary refill, hypotension
