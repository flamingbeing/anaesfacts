# Anaesthesia and Immunity

Anaesthesia and surgery cause a reversible fall in immune function; drug allergy is a key anaesthetic risk.

## Effects on immune function
- Reversible immune depression is well documented
- Barrier defences may be impaired during and after anaesthesia
  - Less tracheal ciliary activity
  - Phagocytosis depressed, roughly in proportion to surgical stress
- Specific immunity falls after surgery
  - Driven by stress hormones (raised cortisol) depressing lymphocyte function
  - T-cell numbers and activity fall
- NK cells: biphasic change
  - Early brief rise as cells are recruited from extravascular space, lymph nodes and spleen
  - Later depression after surgery, via suppressor monocytes

## Allergic drug reactions
- Drug or metabolite reacts with immune effector cells
- Most drugs bind host proteins; the drug-protein complex triggers the immune response
- Anaphylaxis: 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 25,000 anaesthetics; mortality 3.4%

## Induction agents
- Reported rates vary widely
- Propanidid 1 in 540 to 1 in 1,700; althesin 1 in 900 to 1 in 1,100
- Thiopental less often: 1 in 14,000

## Muscle relaxants
- Non-depolarising relaxants: most common cause of life-threatening reactions under anaesthesia, up to 1 in 1,200 exposures
- Mostly IgE-mediated; suxamethonium is the most common single agent
- Alcuronium and atracurium react more often than pancuronium and vecuronium

## Local anaesthetics
- True allergy is rare; IgE-mediated when it occurs
- IgE allergy to methylparaben (preservative in LA solutions) reported

## Protamine
- Reactions via IgE, IgG or complement
- Either classic anaphylaxis or acute pulmonary hypertension with right ventricular failure

## Plasma volume expanders
- Gelatins: about 1 in 1,000
- Plasma protein preparations: about 1 in 10,000
- Haemaccel releases histamine directly from mast cells, without complement or IgE

## Antibiotics
- Penicillin: most common antibiotic cause; 1 in 2,500 to 1 in 10,000
- Fatality about 10% of those affected
- Beta-lactam ring opens to form the penicilloyl group, which binds plasma proteins and triggers IgE production

## Flashcards
- Q: How common is anaphylaxis under anaesthesia, and its mortality?
  - A: 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 25,000 anaesthetics; mortality 3.4%.
- Q: Which drug group is the most common cause of life-threatening reactions under anaesthesia?
  - A: Muscle relaxants (up to 1 in 1,200 exposures), mostly IgE-mediated.
- Q: Which preservative in LA solutions can cause IgE allergy?
  - A: Methylparaben.
- Q: What two reaction patterns can protamine cause?
  - A: Classic anaphylaxis, or acute pulmonary hypertension with RV failure.
- Q: How does Haemaccel cause reactions?
  - A: Direct mast cell histamine release, without complement or IgE.
