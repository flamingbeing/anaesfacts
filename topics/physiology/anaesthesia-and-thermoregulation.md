# Effect of Anaesthesia and Surgery on Thermoregulation

Anaesthesia widens the interthreshold range and abolishes behavioural responses, so patients lose heat in a predictable three-phase pattern.

## Overview
- General anaesthesia severely challenges thermoregulation
  - Behavioural responses are abolished
  - Interthreshold range widens from about 0.4 to 4.0°C; thresholds for vasoconstriction and shivering fall even further in the elderly
- So the body cannot respond well to the many sources of heat loss during surgery

## Effects of anaesthesia on thermoregulation
### All anaesthesia
- Behavioural responses abolished

### General anaesthesia
- Widens the interthreshold range
  - Cold response threshold falls by about 2.5°C
  - Warm response threshold rises by about 1.3°C
- Lowers the threshold for vasoconstriction and other defences
- Within this range the patient is poikilothermic: temperature drifts passively with the balance of metabolic heat production and heat loss
- Vasodilation moves heat from core to periphery
- Paralysis prevents shivering
- Metabolic heat production may fall

### Regional anaesthesia
- Lowers shivering and vasoconstriction thresholds below the block level (probably less afferent thermal input from the periphery)
- Early hypothermia from redistribution: vasodilation lets cool peripheral blood reach the core
- Vasoconstriction above the block partly compensates
- Combined regional and general anaesthesia carries the greatest risk of heat loss

## Three-phase pattern of hypothermia
- Phase 1: redistribution
  - Rapid initial fall in core temperature as vasodilation sends warm blood to the periphery and cool peripheral blood to the core
  - Vasodilation is both a direct drug effect and the result of a lower vasoconstriction threshold
- Phase 2: linear fall
  - Heat loss exceeds metabolic heat production
  - Metabolic rate falls by 15-40% under GA, giving a negative heat balance
  - Mechanisms of loss
    - Radiation (40%): infrared energy travelling in straight lines, can be reflected; loss proportional to the temperature difference to the power 4
    - Convection (30%): warmed air molecules move away from the body; loss rises with air speed around the body, worsened by theatre air currents, laminar flow and exposed skin
    - Evaporation (25%): latent heat of vaporisation; sweating, open body cavities and mucosa, skin preparation fluids, humidifying dry inspired gases
    - Conduction (5%): direct contact with cooler objects, e.g. unwarmed IV fluids, cold table or mattress
- Phase 3: plateau
  - Maximal vasoconstriction, so ongoing heat loss is matched by metabolic production

## Other causes
- Unwarmed fluids or blood products: warm with an approved device
- Massive blood loss: hard to keep patients warm once >1 blood volume is lost

## Flashcards
- Q: How much does GA widen the interthreshold range?
  - A: From about 0.4 to 4.0°C (cold threshold falls about 2.5°C, warm threshold rises about 1.3°C)
- Q: Relative contributions of heat loss mechanisms under anaesthesia?
  - A: Radiation 40%, convection 30%, evaporation 25%, conduction 5%
- Q: By how much does GA reduce metabolic rate?
  - A: 15-40%
- Q: What causes the phase 3 plateau?
  - A: Maximal vasoconstriction balancing heat loss with metabolic heat
