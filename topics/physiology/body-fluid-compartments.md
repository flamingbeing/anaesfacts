# Body fluid compartments

How total body water and blood volume are divided, what separates the compartments, and how they are measured.

## Blood volume (quick reference)

- Infants: 80ml/kg
- Males: 70ml/kg
- Females: 65ml/kg

## Overview

- Water distribution depends on the osmolality (protein content) inside and outside cells

## Total body water

- About 60% of body weight in an average adult male: 42 litres for a 70kg male
- Range 50-80% of body weight; varies with age, size, sex and fat content
- By age
  - Neonate: 75-85%
  - Infant: 70%
  - Adult: 60%
  - Elderly: 50%
- By sex
  - Adult male: 60%
  - Adult female: 50%

## Compartments

- Intracellular fluid (ICF): inside cell membranes
  - 2/3 of TBW, 40% of body weight, 28 litres
  - Exact ICF:ECF ratio 23 : 11.6 (66%)
  - "We are mainly cells"
- Extracellular fluid (ECF): outside cell membranes
  - 1/3 of TBW, 20% of body weight, 14 litres
  - Interstitial fluid: in the spaces between cells
    - 3/4 of ECF, 15% of body weight, 9.5L
    - Exact interstitial:intravascular ratio 8.4:3.2 (72%)
    - "So many cells, hence so much fluid between them"
  - Intravascular fluid (plasma)
    - 1/4 of ECF, 5% of body weight, 3.5L
    - Excludes red cell volume (haematocrit)
    - Total blood volume (plasma plus red cells) 5-6L, or 7% of body weight (8-9% in children)
  - Transcellular fluid
    - Fluid in epithelium-lined spaces, separate from plasma: pleural, peritoneal, gastrointestinal, CSF, synovial
    - 5% of ECF, <1% of TBW, about 1L
    - Often treated as a separate compartment, not in dynamic equilibrium with the others
    - "Third spacing": loss of ECF from the vessels into other compartments
- Fat
  - Adult: 20-25%
  - Neonate: 5-15%

## Neonates

- TBW: 75-85%
- ECF: 30-45%; in premature babies ECF is larger than ICF
- ICF: <40%
- Plasma: 5%

## Barriers between compartments

- Capillary endothelium (around the intravascular compartment)
  - Water and solutes cross freely, mostly passively
    - Filtration (bulk flow) driven by Starling forces carries water and dissolved solutes
    - Diffusion moves solutes down their concentration gradient
    - Routes: gaps between cells or directly through cell membranes
  - Large molecules do not cross
- Cell membrane (around the intracellular compartment)
  - Water crosses freely
    - Iso-osmotic at steady state
    - Tonicity differences move water by osmosis, from hypo-osmolar to hyper-osmolar
    - So ECF sodium concentration controls water distribution between ECF and ICF
  - Ions and molecules cross via transporters and channels (transmembrane proteins, ion channels), giving selective permeability

## Fluid shifts

- Movement of fluid between compartments, driven by hydrostatic and osmotic pressure gradients (Starling forces)
- Oncotic plus osmotic gradients tend to draw water back into vessels
- Hydrostatic pressure and the intact semipermeable capillary wall push the other way
- Most shifts are within the ECF, intravascular to interstitial (oedema, sepsis, effusions)
- Shifts between ICF and ECF cause more severe illness: cell lysis, cerebral oedema

## Blood volume

- Normal values
  - Males: 70ml/kg
  - Females: 65ml/kg
  - Neonates: 85ml/kg
- About 5 liters in an average male; 3.6 liters in an average female
- Made of two compartments
  - Red cell volume (intracellular): 2L
  - Plasma (extracellular, intravascular): 3L
  - Total 2 + 3 = 5L
- The whole blood volume circulates in about one minute (60 s)
  - The circulation is closed and blood is incompressible, so total flow round the circuit equals flow through the heart
  - So average cardiac output is 5L per minute

## Measurement

- Total body water: dilution of a tracer that spreads through all body water
  - Isotopically labelled water: deuterium (2H), tritium (3H) or oxygen-18
- ECF: tracer that crosses capillaries but not cell membranes
  - Inulin, mannitol, radiosodium, radiochloride, thiosulphate (most used)
- Plasma volume: tracer that stays in vessels
  - Radiolabelled albumin, or Evans blue dye (binds plasma albumin)
- Interstitial volume: not measured directly; ECF minus plasma volume

## Flashcards
- Q: Blood volume in males, females and neonates?
  - A: Males 70 ml/kg; females 65 ml/kg; neonates 85 ml/kg
- Q: Total body water in a 70 kg adult male?
  - A: About 60% of body weight, 42 litres
- Q: ICF and ECF fractions of TBW?
  - A: ICF 2/3 (28 L); ECF 1/3 (14 L)
- Q: Plasma volume as a share of ECF and body weight?
  - A: 1/4 of ECF, 5% of body weight (3.5 L)
- Q: Tracer used to measure plasma volume?
  - A: Radiolabelled albumin or Evans blue dye
