# High Spinal and Total Spinal Anaesthesia

Excessive spread of neuraxial local anaesthetic: support airway, breathing and circulation until the block wears off.

## Summary
- Support ABCs until the spinal wears off
- Treat seizures, hypotension and bradycardia
- In obstetrics, manage the baby

## Definitions
- High spinal: no strict definition
  - Block above the T1 dermatome involving cervical segments, or an excessively high block
  - Quoted risk varies with the definition used
- Total spinal: excessive sensory and motor block with loss of consciousness, from intracranial spread of local anaesthetic
  - Unconsciousness is thought to come from medullary respiratory centre ischaemia due to profound hypotension
  - Rare: less than 1/100 000

## Risk factors
- Anything that raises block height
- Patient
  - Short stature, high BMI
  - Spinal canal abnormalities
- Technique
  - Large local anaesthetic dose, positioning
  - Epidural dose given through an unrecognised intrathecal catheter
  - Spinal after a failed epidural or epidural top-up

## Presentation
- A spectrum; starts within minutes of the spinal and evolves over the next several minutes
- Early
  - Sensory block above T1 with arm weakness after spinal or epidural, spreading to the neck
  - Rapidly rising block if checked early
  - Nausea, vomiting, anxiety
- Later
  - Dyspnoea, difficulty speaking, slurred speech and voice change
  - Hypotension, bradycardia
- Total spinal
  - Apnoea (phrenic nerve paralysis), cardiorespiratory collapse
  - Loss of consciousness (brainstem anaesthesia)

## Differential diagnosis
- Vasovagal (Bezold-Jarisch reflex) causing bradycardia and hypotension
- Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)
- Other causes of hypotension

## Management
- Call for help
- Supportive care of ABCs; no specific treatment
  - Airway: secure it; intubate if consciousness is lost
  - Breathing: supplemental oxygen
  - Circulation
    - Treat bradycardia aggressively: atropine, ephedrine, adrenaline (bradycardia usually comes before cardiac arrest)
    - Treat hypotension: IV fluids plus vasopressors such as ephedrine or phenylephrine; adrenaline may be needed
- Obstetrics: monitor fetal heart rate; prepare for emergency caesarean section
- Continue support until the block wears off: usually 2-4 hours, depending on the local anaesthetic
- Afterwards: talk with the patient; explain what happened, answer questions and look for psychological distress

## Flashcards
- Q: What is thought to cause loss of consciousness in total spinal?
  - A: Medullary respiratory centre ischaemia from profound hypotension.
- Q: How rare is total spinal anaesthesia?
  - A: Less than 1/100 000.
- Q: Why must bradycardia be treated aggressively in a high spinal?
  - A: It usually precedes cardiac arrest.
- Q: How long is support usually needed in a total spinal?
  - A: Until the block wears off, usually 2-4 hours.
- Q: What causes apnoea in a total spinal?
  - A: Phrenic nerve paralysis.
