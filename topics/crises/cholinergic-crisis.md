# Cholinergic Crisis

Acetylcholine excess from cholinesterase inhibition (e.g. organophosphate poisoning): SLUDGE, bradycardia, bronchospasm; treat with atropine and an oxime.

## Clinical features

- SLUDGE
  - Salivation
  - Lacrimation
  - Urination
  - Diaphoresis
  - Gastrointestinal upset
  - Emesis
- Bradycardia, asystole, hypotension
- Bronchospasm
- Central excitation at first, then depression, coma and apnoea
- Compare with anticholinergic toxicity

## Supportive treatment

- Atropine
  - Organophosphate (OP) pesticide poisoning often needs far bigger doses than other uses
  - Double the dose to atropinise quickly: 1 mg, then 2 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, 16 mg and so on
  - A severe case can use up a hospital's whole atropine stock
  - If atropine is short: IV glycopyrrolate or diphenhydramine can treat muscarinic effects
    - Glycopyrrolate does not cross the blood-brain barrier, so it does not treat central effects
- Anticonvulsant: diazepam
- Ventilation may be needed in OP poisoning

## Oxime reactivators

- Nucleophilic agents, e.g. pralidoxime (2-PAM), obidoxime
- Antidotes for OP poisoning; treat the cause rather than the symptoms
- Mechanism
  - The oxime group attacks the OP group bound to the AChE serine active site
  - The OP-enzyme bond breaks, a phosphorylated oxime forms and the enzyme is freed
- Ageing
  - The phosphorylated enzyme can be dealkylated ("aged"); aged enzyme cannot be reactivated
  - So give oximes early: current advice is within 48h of poisoning
