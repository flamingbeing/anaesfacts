# Bone Cement Implantation Syndrome

Hypoxia, hypotension and loss of consciousness within seconds to minutes of inserting pressurised bone cement, from a sudden rise in PVR.

## Key points
- Triad: hypoxia, hypotension, loss of consciousness
- Onset immediate

## Cement
- Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA): acrylic polymer used in orthopaedics for 30yr
- Expands as it sets, sharply raising pressure in the bone cavity; cement, marrow and fat are forced into the veins as microemboli

## Who gets it
- Potentially fatal; occurs during insertion of pressurised cement
- Most common with femoral cementing, especially hip fractures
- In very old, frail patients, cement is probably best avoided

## Pathophysiology
- Several theories
- Embolic: high medullary pressure drives fat, marrow, air and bone into veins, mechanically blocking the pulmonary circulation
- Monomer: PMMA monomer triggers a hypersensitivity or inflammatory reaction
- Result: sudden severe rise in PVR, RV failure, reduced LV filling, cardiovascular collapse

## Clinical features
- Onset within seconds to minutes of cementing
- Hypoxia and sudden fall in EtCO2
- Hypotension, vasodilation, tachycardia, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest
- Loss of consciousness if awake or sedated

## Grading (Donaldson 2009)
- Grade 1: moderate hypotension (systolic BP falls >20%) or hypoxia (SpO2 <94%)
- Grade 2: severe hypotension (systolic BP falls >40%) or hypoxia (SpO2 <88%), or loss of consciousness
- Grade 3: cardiovascular collapse needing CPR

## Risk factors
- Patient: elderly, frail, ASA 3–4, pulmonary hypertension, significant cardiac or respiratory disease, osteoporosis, male sex
- Anaesthetic: existing hypovolaemia, hypoxia or hypotension
- Surgical: intertrochanteric or pathological fracture, long-stem prosthesis

## Prevention
- Anaesthetist ("optimise before the smell")
  - Surgeon announces cementing
  - Optimise volume and perfusion before cementing
  - Raise FiO2 (hypoxia common); stop N2O
  - Frequent BP, watch SpO2; loss of EtCO2 may herald collapse
- Surgeon
  - Discuss cemented versus uncemented prosthesis beforehand
  - Pulsatile lavage of the femoral canal before cementing
  - Vent hole to depressurise the canal
  - Bone-vacuum cementing technique
  - Retrograde cement insertion
  - Low-viscosity cement

## Management
- Supportive ABC
- Inotropes; pulmonary vasodilators if available
- ICU or HDU after surgery for survivors; PVR usually normal within 24 hours
