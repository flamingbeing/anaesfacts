# Pulmonary Hypertension and Right Ventricular Dysfunction

Mean PA pressure > 20 mmHg; high perioperative risk. Keep PVR from rising, protect RV perfusion with MAP, and be ready for a pulmonary hypertensive crisis.

## Definition

- mPAP > 20mmHg
  - Moderate to severe if PASP > 50mmHg
- Pre-capillary PH (includes pulmonary arterial hypertension, PAH)
  - PVR > 2 Wood Units
  - Pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP) ≤ 15 mm Hg
- High perioperative morbidity and mortality
- PVR is not always raised, but management centres on avoiding sudden rises in PVR that worsen RV dysfunction

## Preoperative assessment

### History
- Low cardiac output: dyspnoea, fatigue, syncope
- Exertional chest pain from RV ischaemia
- Arrhythmias
- Causes: smoking, COPD, VTE, illicit drugs

### Examination
- Right heart failure: raised JVP, hepatomegaly, peripheral oedema
- TR murmur with large v waves
- Parasternal heave (RV enlargement)
- Loud P2
- Hypoxaemia

### Investigations
- TTE (essential)
  - RV size and function
  - PASP / RVSP from the TR jet (modified Bernoulli): RVSP = 4 x peak TR velocity squared; PASP = 4v2 + RAP
  - Severity by PASP
    - Mild: 40 mmHg ≤ PASP < 50 mmHg
    - Moderate: 50 mmHg ≤ PASP < 60 mmHg
    - Severe: PASP ≥ 60 mmHg
  - ESC uses TR Vmax > 2.8 m/s as the PH cut-off (with RA pressure 5 mmHg this equals a peak PA pressure of 36 mmHg)
  - Pericardial effusion: high right-sided pressures impair drainage of pericardial fluid (via veins and lymphatics into the RA)
- ECG: right atrial enlargement, RVH, RV strain
- CXR: right atrial enlargement, RVH
- BNP / NT-proBNP: raised with RV or LV dilatation
- Right heart catheter (diagnostic gold standard)
  - mPAP > 20
  - Plus PVR > 2.0 WU (pre-capillary) or PAWP > 15mmHg (post-capillary)
- Functional capacity: 6-minute walk test (6MWT), CPET
- Cause: CXR, lung function, ABG, HRCT

### Medical treatment
- Pulmonary vasodilators (mainly Group 1 PH)
  - Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, nifedipine, diltiazem): only if acute vasoreactivity test positive
  - Endothelin receptor antagonists (ambrisentan, bosentan): block endothelin-1 (ET-1), an endothelial vasoconstrictor peptide
  - Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil): stop cGMP breakdown, boosting the nitric oxide pathway
  - Guanylate cyclase stimulators (riociguat): raise cGMP directly via the nitric oxide pathway
  - Prostacyclin pathway agonists (epoprostenol, iloprost, selexipag): act on the prostacyclin (PGI2) receptor, a G-protein-coupled receptor causing vasodilation
- Others: diuretics, supplemental O2
- Treat the cause: bronchodilators for lung disease, anticoagulation for CTEPH

## Risk assessment

- Surgery carries significant morbidity and mortality
- Patient factors
  - Right heart failure, syncope, chest pain, arrhythmias, worsening symptoms
  - WHO functional class (adapted from NYHA)
  - Exercise capacity: 6MWT distance, CPET (VO2)
  - NT-proBNP
  - TTE: PASP (e.g. RVSP/SBP ratio > 0.6), RV dilatation, RV function (e.g. TAPSE), RA area, pericardial effusion
  - Cardiac MRI: RVEF
  - Right heart catheter: mPAP, PVR, RAP, CI, ScO2
  - Other cardiac disease and comorbidities
- Procedure factors: emergency, major surgery, operating time > 3 hours
- 2022 ESC/ERS risk models
  - Three-strata model (from the SPAHR registry)
  - Four-strata model (from the COMPERA 2.0 registry)
  - Functional class with 6-minute walk distance, and NT-proBNP, carry the most prognostic weight

## Preoperative optimisation

- MDT with cardiology
- Continue all PH therapy up to surgery
  - Newly diagnosed, emergency surgery: sildenafil 50-100mg orally before surgery
- Supplemental O2

## Intraoperative management

### Goals
- Large haemodynamic swings likely at induction and with positive pressure ventilation
- Avoid acute rises in PVR (raise RV afterload, cut RV output)
  - Treat hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis, pain and hypothermia promptly; all raise PVR
- Avoid RV ischaemia and failure: keep MAP up to perfuse the RV
- Maintain RV contractility (avoid myocardial depression) and optimise RV preload

### Technique
- Sticking to the goals matters more than the choice of technique
- Peripheral nerve blocks: cardiostable, with good opioid-free analgesia during and after surgery
- Neuraxial blocks: sympathectomy lowers BP and RV perfusion; blocking cardioaccelerator fibres can further impair RV contractility
  - Top up epidurals slowly
- Sedation for regional can cause hypoxia and hypercarbia; may be contraindicated

### Monitoring
- Arterial line before induction (rapid changes)
- CVP: for vasopressors and as an index of RV preload
- With or without PA catheter or TOE
- Temperature

### Induction
- Cardiostable, avoid myocardial depression
- Give vasoconstrictors alongside to hold SVR and MAP (RV perfusion)
- Ketamine and N2O with caution (raise PVR)

### Maintenance
- Controlled ventilation; check ABGs
  - RV-protective: avoid both over- and under-distension (Pplat < 26, driving pressure < 17)
- Prevent or treat arrhythmias aggressively; atrial kick is needed for preload and output
  - Tachyarrhythmias common
- Vasopressors
  - Noradrenaline first line in most centres: beta-1 support of the RV and beta-2 lowering of PVR (theoretical)
  - Pure alpha agonists (e.g. phenylephrine) may raise PVR
  - Vasopressin: selective systemic vasoconstrictor that raises SVR without affecting PVR; infusion or bolus in a PH crisis
  - None of these benefits proven in outcome trials
- Short-acting opioids (e.g. remifentanil) to avoid postoperative respiratory depression
- Procedure-specific
  - Laparoscopy is high risk: lower lung compliance and CO2 load; control pulmonary pressures and CO2 closely
  - Trendelenburg: lower compliance, higher RV preload
  - Long bone surgery: fat embolism can raise PVR

### Emergence
- Extubate only when awake, fully reversed and ventilating well (avoid hypoxia and hypercapnia in recovery)
- Multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia

## Pulmonary hypertensive crisis

- Sudden rise in PVR overwhelms the RV: RV failure and systemic hypotension; mortality 50%
- Lower PVR with inhaled and IV pulmonary vasodilators
  - Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO) 20-40ppm: fast and potent in ventilated patients; no systemic hypotension (inactivated by Hb); useful in bypass
  - Inhaled prostacyclin (iloprost; raises cAMP): 50mcg in saline nebulised hourly, or 50ng/kg/min into the inspiratory limb
  - Other nebulised options: prostacyclin (needs suitable nebuliser), milrinone
  - IV prostacyclin (if inhaled not available): 4-10ng/kg/min
  - IV sildenafil
- Support the RV
  - Optimise preload (CVP 8–12 mm Hg) with careful fluid challenges or diuresis
  - Afterload reduction relies on lowering PVR
  - Treat hypotension aggressively to prevent RV ischaemia; do not stop pulmonary vasodilators
    - Noradrenaline up to 0.5 mcg/kg/min
    - Low-dose vasopressin up to 0.04 units/min
  - Inotropes for RV contractility: dobutamine, dopamine, milrinone
    - Milrinone (50mcg/kg bolus then 0.25 - 0.5 mcg/kg/min) also lowers PVR
- ECMO for the RV as a bridge to recovery or transplant

## Postoperative management

- ICU; a PH crisis can still happen after surgery
- Keep interruptions to PH therapy to a minimum
- Aim for early spontaneous ventilation and extubation (less intrathoracic pressure and RV stress)
- Good analgesia

## Flashcards
- Q: Haemodynamic definition of pre-capillary PH?
  - A: mPAP > 20 mmHg, PVR > 2 Wood Units, PAWP ≤ 15 mmHg
- Q: Echo severity bands for PASP?
  - A: Mild 40-50, moderate 50-60, severe ≥ 60 mmHg
- Q: Dose of inhaled nitric oxide in a PH crisis?
  - A: 20-40ppm
- Q: Which vasopressor raises SVR without raising PVR?
  - A: Vasopressin (up to 0.04 units/min)
- Q: Mortality of a pulmonary hypertensive crisis?
  - A: 50%
