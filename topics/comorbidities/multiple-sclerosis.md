# Multiple sclerosis

Anaesthetic aim: avoid triggering a relapse and avoid neurological complications.

## Key principles
- Prevent hyperthermia
- Avoid suxamethonium and spinal anaesthesia
- Monitor respiratory function
- Avoid pain and stress

## Preoperative assessment
- Document baseline motor, sensory and autonomic deficits
  - Lets new postoperative deficits be sorted into surgical complication or MS relapse
- Disease-modifying therapies: usually continue; plan with the patient's neurologist
- Recent steroids for a relapse: may need perioperative stress-dose steroids (adrenal suppression)

## Intraoperative management
- Temperature
  - Monitor core temperature closely
  - Heat worsens symptoms by blocking conduction in demyelinated nerves; treat fever early
- Neuromuscular blockade
  - Avoid suxamethonium: risk of severe, possibly fatal hyperkalaemia from extrajunctional acetylcholine receptor upregulation
  - Non-depolarising agents: response may be increased or reduced; titrate dose by dose with a nerve stimulator
- Spinal anaesthesia
  - Traditionally a relative contraindication: demyelinated nerves may be more vulnerable to concentrated local anaesthetic, possibly triggering relapse
  - Epidural (e.g. in labour) is safe
- Peripheral nerve blocks
  - Generally considered safer; MS seldom involves peripheral nerves

## Postoperative care
- Respiratory function: medullary or cervical lesions can weaken respiratory drive and diaphragm, raising the risk of hypoventilation
- Pain: give good multimodal analgesia, since physical and emotional stress can trigger relapse

## Flashcards
- Q: Why avoid suxamethonium in MS?
  - A: Upregulated extrajunctional acetylcholine receptors can cause severe, potentially fatal hyperkalaemia.
- Q: Why is temperature control important in MS?
  - A: Hyperthermia worsens symptoms by causing conduction block in demyelinated nerves.
- Q: Is epidural analgesia acceptable in a pregnant patient with MS?
  - A: Yes, epidural is considered safe; spinal is traditionally a relative contraindication.
- Q: How do patients with MS respond to non-depolarising relaxants?
  - A: Unpredictably (increased resistance or sensitivity); titrate with a nerve stimulator.
