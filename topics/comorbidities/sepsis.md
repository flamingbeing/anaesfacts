# Sepsis

Resuscitate before surgery, expect collapse on induction and plan for ICU afterwards.

## Preoperative assessment
- Haemodynamic stability and volume status
- Sepsis bundle: IV antibiotics plus cultures, fluids then vasopressors, lactate
  - Resuscitate before surgery
- Look for complications and multi-organ failure:
  - ARDS
  - Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIVC)
  - AKI, ischaemic hepatitis
  - Lactic acidosis

## Intraoperative management
- Technique
  - GA: haemodynamic instability
  - Spinal may be contraindicated because of:
    - Severe hypotension
    - Bacteraemia and risk of seeding
    - Common coagulopathy
  - Peripheral nerve blocks are a good option
- Infection: continue antibiotics
- Cardiovascular
  - Cardiovascular collapse may occur on induction
    - Arterial line and CVP before induction, with or without FloTrac
    - Start noradrenaline during induction
    - Consider midazolam and ketamine
  - Optimise intravascular volume
  - Noradrenaline, vasopressin
  - Trend lactate
  - Septic showers may occur
- Respiratory: lung protective ventilation
- Renal: hourly urine output; may need dialysis after surgery
- Haematology: ROTEM only if clinically bleeding (DIVC is prothrombotic)

## Postoperative care
- Critical care monitoring or ICU

## Flashcards
- Q: What invasive monitoring should be in place before induction in a septic patient?
  - A: Arterial line and CVP, with or without FloTrac
- Q: When should noradrenaline be started in a septic patient for GA?
  - A: During induction
- Q: Give three reasons spinal may be contraindicated in sepsis.
  - A: Severe hypotension, bacteraemia with risk of seeding, and coagulopathy
- Q: When is ROTEM indicated in septic DIVC?
  - A: Only if clinically bleeding, as DIVC is prothrombotic
