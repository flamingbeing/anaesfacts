# Restrictive Lung Disease

Reduced lung volumes from disease of the lung itself or of the chest wall, pleura or neuromuscular system.

## Condition
- TLC, VC and tidal volume all fall
- Caused by disease of lung parenchyma, pleura, chest wall or neuromuscular apparatus

## Causes
- Intrinsic: pulmonary fibrosis
  - Idiopathic: sarcoidosis, idiopathic interstitial pneumonias, cryptogenic organising pneumonia
  - Autoimmune: rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma
  - Inhaled dusts: asbestosis, coal, copper
  - Allergens: farmer's lung, bird fancier's lung
  - Drugs: amiodarone, chemotherapy, radiotherapy
  - ARDS
- Extrinsic: mechanical causes outside the lung
  - Kyphoscoliosis
  - Ankylosing spondylitis
  - Severe obesity
  - Abdominal problems splinting the diaphragm

## Clinical features
- Work of breathing minimised with rapid, small breaths
- Often prefer sitting upright

## Medical management
- Intrinsic: oral steroids, immunosuppressants, lung transplant
- Extrinsic: treat the cause if possible

## Preoperative assessment
- Exercise tolerance is important
- Investigations guided by the cause
  - ABG: raised CO2 means very late-stage disease
  - PFTs and DLCO
    - Repeat if not done in the last 8wks
    - Lung volumes reduced
    - VC below 15ml/kg suggests severe dysfunction
  - CXR
- Optimise with the patient's respiratory physician

## Intraoperative management
- Regional anaesthesia if possible
- Minimise IPPV and airway instrumentation
- If ventilated: high rate, small volumes, keep airway pressures low
- Patients on steroids may need a higher dose
- Watch closely for pneumothorax

## Postoperative management
- ICU or HDU
- NIV
- Extubate sitting up
- Titrated oxygen
- Physiotherapy
- Analgesia
- Early mobilisation
- Treat chest infection early

## Flashcards
- Q: Which lung volumes fall in restrictive lung disease?
  - A: TLC, VC and tidal volume
- Q: What VC suggests severe restrictive dysfunction?
  - A: Below 15ml/kg
- Q: What does a raised CO2 on ABG mean?
  - A: Very late-stage disease
- Q: Ventilation strategy under GA?
  - A: High rate, small volumes, low airway pressures; watch for pneumothorax
