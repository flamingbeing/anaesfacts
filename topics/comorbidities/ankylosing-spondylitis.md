# Ankylosing Spondylitis

Inflammatory arthropathy of the sacroiliac joints and spine leading to fusion: expect a difficult airway, difficult neuraxial block and positioning, and cardiorespiratory involvement.

## Background
- Male to female 4:1; peak onset in the 30's
- History: back pain worse in the morning and better with movement, fatigue, sweats, weight loss
- Investigations: spine X-rays, HLA B27, raised ESR, CRP and white cell count, renal function (amyloid)
- Treatment: NSAIDs, DMARDs

## Airway
- Difficult airway: may need awake fibreoptic intubation, videolaryngoscopy or an intubating LMA
- Cervical disease: stiff neck, limited movement, fixed flexion deformity
  - Neck extension can dislocate the cervical spine, compress the cord or the vertebral arteries
  - Support the neck
  - Awake fibreoptic intubation is safest and allows ongoing neurological checks
  - An intubating LMA allows intubation without neck movement
- Atlantoaxial instability: minimise neck manipulation
- TMJ disease: limited mouth opening
- Cricoarytenoid arthritis: narrow glottis, cords easily injured
- Preoperative cervical spine X-ray

## Neuraxial block
- Often difficult: paramedian approach or ultrasound may help
  - Poor positioning
  - Spinal fusion
  - Obliterated epidural space: give a test dose to avoid total spinal
  - Higher epidural haematoma risk (ASRA): difficult or traumatic insertion, NSAID bleeding risk
- May already have neurological deficits, spondylolisthesis, or cauda equina syndrome in long-standing disease

## Positioning
- Back is tender and stiff; fracture risk: position carefully and protect pressure areas

## Extra-articular disease
- Cardiovascular: aortic regurgitation (1%), conduction defects
  - Chest compressions may be ineffective with a rigid chest wall
  - Preoperative TTE and ECG
- Respiratory: lung fibrosis adds to restriction
  - Progressive kyphosis limits chest expansion: restrictive defect, harder ventilation
  - Preoperative CXR and lung function tests
  - Multimodal analgesia, chest physiotherapy, early mobilisation

## Drug-related problems
- NSAIDs and DMARDs: renal impairment, platelet dysfunction, marrow suppression, immunosuppression (wound infection risk)

## Flashcards
- Q: Safest intubation technique in severe ankylosing spondylitis?
  - A: Awake fibreoptic intubation, which also allows neurological monitoring
- Q: Why is neck extension dangerous?
  - A: Risk of cervical dislocation, cord compression and vertebral artery compression
- Q: Which valve lesion is associated and how often?
  - A: Aortic regurgitation, about 1%
- Q: Tip for neuraxial block?
  - A: Paramedian approach or ultrasound; test dose because the epidural space may be obliterated
