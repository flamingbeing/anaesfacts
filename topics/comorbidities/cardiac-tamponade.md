# Cardiac Tamponade

Fixed pericardial space limits filling: keep the patient fast, full and tight, avoid positive pressure, and drain under LA if possible.

## Key points
- Signs: Beck's triad, pulsus paradoxus, electrical alternans, effusion with RV or RA collapse on POCUS
- Local anaesthetic drainage is ideal
- Fast, full and tight: preserve every compensatory mechanism
- Minimise intrathoracic pressure: keep spontaneous ventilation (as for anterior mediastinal mass)
- Surgeons scrubbed before induction (as for ruptured AAA)

## Physiology
- 1) Fixed pericardial volume impairs diastolic filling
  - Rising pericardial fluid and pressure leave less room for the ventricles to fill
  - Stroke volume becomes fixed, so cardiac output depends on heart rate
- 2) Ventricular interdependence
  - Right and left ventricles fill at each other's expense
- Acute versus chronic
  - Acute effusions (trauma, iatrogenic) cause tamponade at small volumes, e.g. 100–200 ml
  - Chronic effusions stretch the pericardium and can hold litres before collapse

## Preoperative assessment
- Key questions
  - Localised (e.g. around the RA) or global?
  - Acute or chronic?
- Symptoms and signs
  - Beck's triad: hypotension, distended JVP, muffled heart sounds
  - Kussmaul's sign: JVP rises on inspiration
  - Pulsus paradoxus: SBP falls by more than 10 mmHg on inspiration, from ventricular interdependence
- Investigations
  - ECG: low voltages, electrical alternans
  - CXR: large globular heart shadow
  - POCUS (gold standard): confirms effusion and shows tamponade physiology (diastolic chamber collapse, plethoric IVC)

## Intraoperative management
- Ask the surgeon to drain under LA before GA, especially if haemodynamically compromised
- Fast, full and tight
  - Keep compensatory tachycardia and raised SVR
- Minimise intrathoracic pressure
  - Avoid positive pressure ventilation if possible: it further cuts venous return to the compressed heart
  - If needed: small tidal volumes, low PEEP, high rate, to keep mean airway pressure low
- Prepare for collapse
  - Patient prepped and draped, surgeon scrubbed and ready to cut before induction

## Preparation
- Large-bore IV access
- Arterial line essential
- Central line useful, but may wait until after drainage if unstable
  - Run vasopressors peripherally at first
- Vasopressors and emergency drugs ready before induction

## Induction
- Ketamine is useful: keeps sympathetic tone and spontaneous breathing

## After drainage
- Rebound hypertension: cardiac output rises sharply once the constraint is released
- Be ready to deepen anaesthesia or give vasodilators

## Tamponade after cardiac surgery
- Early (first 24 hours) or late (5-7 days after surgery)
- Early causes: surgical bleeding or coagulopathy
- Can follow removal of epicardial pacing wires
- Diagnosis: POCUS
- Management
  - Treat as tamponade physiology
  - Immediate sternotomy and surgical re-exploration, possibly chest opening at the ICU bedside
  - Correct coagulopathy

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the components of Beck's triad?
  - A: Hypotension, raised JVP and muffled heart sounds.
- Q: Define pulsus paradoxus.
  - A: A fall in SBP of more than 10 mmHg on inspiration.
- Q: What volume of acute effusion can cause tamponade?
  - A: As little as 100–200 ml.
- Q: What are the haemodynamic goals in tamponade?
  - A: Fast, full and tight, with minimal intrathoracic pressure.
- Q: When does tamponade occur after cardiac surgery?
  - A: Early (within 24 hours) or late (5-7 days).
