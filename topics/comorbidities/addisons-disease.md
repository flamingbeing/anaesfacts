# Addison's Disease and Adrenocortical Insufficiency

Loss of adrenal cortex function; give steroid cover throughout the perioperative period and recognise adrenal crisis early.

## Adrenal cortex zones
- Glomerulosa: mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)
- Fasciculata: glucocorticoids (cortisol)
- Reticularis: androgens (DHEA, testosterone)

## Classification
- Primary (Addison's disease): the cortex itself is destroyed
  - Causes: autoimmune (80%), infection (TB), septicaemia, AIDS, haemorrhage, metastases, surgery
  - Both glucocorticoid **and** mineralocorticoid deficiency
- Secondary: too little ACTH to stimulate the cortex
  - Pituitary suppression by exogenous steroids, or generalised hypopituitarism (tumour)
  - Glucocorticoid deficiency **only**

## Causes of acute crisis
- Chronic insufficiency without enough steroid cover
- Acute adrenal haemorrhage
- Pituitary apoplexy: sudden neurological impairment, usually from bleeding or infarction

## Preoperative assessment
- Symptoms of chronic insufficiency:
  - Fatigue (100%)
  - Weakness
  - Skin hyperpigmentation, primary only (90%)
  - Postural symptoms, more marked in primary (90%)
  - Diarrhoea and vomiting
  - Weight loss (60%)
  - Myalgia and joint pain
  - Salt craving, primary only
  - Pale skin, secondary only
- Examination: postural hypotension; pallor (secondary); pigmentation (primary)
- Investigations:
  - Low glucose
  - Low Na+ (90%)
  - High K+ (70%)
  - High urea and creatinine (primary only)
  - High Ca2+ (primary only)
  - Low early morning cortisol: <165nmol/L (primary) or <100nmol/L (secondary)
  - Short Synacthen test: peak cortisol <500nmol/L 30mins after 250mcg Synacthen
- Usual treatment:
  - Hydrocortisone 20mg in the morning and 10mg at night, oral
  - Fludrocortisone 0.1mg oral (primary only)
  - Give all usual medication on the morning of surgery

## Intraoperative management
- Hydrocortisone 25mg IV at induction, then 5mg/hr infusion
  - Or 100mg IM
- Follow perioperative steroid guidelines

## Postoperative management
- Glucose every 4 hours
- Urea and electrolytes daily
- Involve endocrinology; rough steroid plan:
  - Repeat the induction steroid regimen every 6 hours until eating and drinking
  - Then double the usual oral dose: 48hr after major surgery, 24hr after minor surgery
  - Then back to the usual regimen
  - If problems arise, return to high-dose oral steroids
- Mineralocorticoid replacement:
  - 20mg hydrocortisone is equivalent to 0.05mg fludrocortisone
  - So once more than 50mg hydrocortisone is being given, extra fludrocortisone is not needed

## Acute adrenal (Addisonian) crisis
- Classic features: hypotension, low Na+, high K+, low glucose, abdominal pain
- Resembles hypovolaemic shock, but can mimic septic shock (fever, vasodilation, high CO)
- In type 1 diabetes may show as worsening glucose control with recurrent hypoglycaemia
- Treatment:
  - Supportive care and IV fluid resuscitation
  - Take baseline cortisol and ACTH, then hydrocortisone 200mg IV, then 100mg four times a day
  - If samples cannot be taken first, dexamethasone 4mg IV can be given instead, as it does not interfere with cortisol testing
  - Treat the precipitant

## Flashcards
- Q: Which deficiency is absent in secondary adrenal insufficiency?
  - A: Mineralocorticoid; only glucocorticoid is deficient
- Q: Short Synacthen test cut-off?
  - A: Peak cortisol <500nmol/L 30mins after 250mcg Synacthen
- Q: Intraoperative hydrocortisone regimen in this note?
  - A: 25mg IV at induction then 5mg/hr infusion (or 100mg IM)
- Q: Above what hydrocortisone dose is extra fludrocortisone unnecessary?
  - A: More than 50mg
- Q: Initial hydrocortisone dose for adrenal crisis?
  - A: 200mg IV, then 100mg four times a day
