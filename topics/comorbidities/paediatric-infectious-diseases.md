# Paediatric infectious diseases and immunisation

Protect staff, other patients and the child; know incubation and infectious periods; elective surgery is often deferred 1-2 weeks after immunisation.

## Key concepts
- Management protects staff and other patients as well as the child
- Decide when to delay surgery (if possible) and how to manage the child if it cannot be delayed
- Incubation period: from contact with an infected person to onset of disease
- Infectious period: time during which the patient can infect others

## Effects of the illness
- Anaesthetic agents affect the immune system and may blunt the immune response, possibly increasing the risk of severe complications
- Unwell children have higher oxygen and fluid needs from raised metabolism
- Secondary infection or pneumonia can complicate measles, varicella and pertussis; lung pathology may last months
- Measles: may be complicated by staphylococcal pneumonia with bullous lung disease
- Myocarditis can occur in severe forms of many diseases (e.g. mumps)
- Septicaemia and multi-organ failure (including thrombocytopenia or encephalopathy) can occur

## Immunisation
- Protects the individual and the population
- Two vaccine types:
  - Live attenuated: stronger immune response, but higher risk of a mild form of the disease or of infecting non-immune people
  - Inactivated or detoxified agents
- Post-vaccine symptoms are common and mostly mild (local redness, swelling, irritability)
  - Inactivated vaccines: reactions usually within 5 days
  - Live vaccines: reaction may be delayed and look like a mild form of the illness; timing depends on the disease's incubation period

## Anaesthesia and immunisation
- Anaesthesia and surgery suppress immunity somewhat in vitro
  - Theoretical concern: reduced vaccine effectiveness when given close together; not shown in practice
- Fever after surgery in a recently immunised child can cause diagnostic confusion
- Little research and wide variation in practice; many anaesthetists ignore immunisation history
- Some centres:
  - Avoid elective, non-urgent anaesthesia for 1-2 weeks after immunisation
  - Discourage opportunistic immunisation under anaesthesia; delay immunisation until 1 week after anaesthesia
