# Perioperative Steroids and Stress Dosing

Decide on steroid cover from the patient's steroid dose and duration and the size of the surgical stress.

## Key points
- UK guidelines: hydrocortisone stress dose 100 mg for more than 5 mg/day or more than 4 wk of prednisolone
- Equivalent doses: prednisolone 5 mg = hydrocortisone 20 mg = dexamethasone 0.8 mg = methylprednisolone 4 mg
- Dexamethasone 8 mg (for PONV)
  - Equals about 200 mg hydrocortisone as a long-acting glucocorticoid
  - Enough to cover most situations for 24 h
  - No mineralocorticoid activity: not adequate cover in primary adrenal insufficiency

## Main considerations
- Perioperative stress dosing
  - Primary adrenal insufficiency: always needs stress dose steroids to prevent adrenal crisis
  - Chronic long-term steroids: depends on dose
- Etomidate blocks conversion of 11β-deoxycortisol to cortisol, lowering cortisol synthesis for up to 48 hours after a single intubating dose

## Preoperative assessment
- Steroid use over the last 12 months
  - Always ask about traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and steroids
  - Ask about "health supplements" too (some patients see illicit TCM as a supplement)
- Indication (acute or chronic)
- Current steroid type and dose
- Duration of therapy, current or past
- Disease flare when steroids were stopped
- Adrenal insufficiency when steroids were stopped

## Physiology of cortisol secretion during surgery
- Normal basal secretion: about 8 to 10 mg/day
- Minor surgery or illness: rises to about 50 mg/day, back to baseline within 24 hours after uncomplicated surgery
- Greater stress (e.g. subtotal colectomy): 75 to 100 mg/day, normal by postoperative day 5
- Severe stress (e.g. major trauma): can reach 200 to 500 mg/day
- Over 200 mg/day in the 24 hours after surgery is rare

## Physiology of HPA axis suppression
- Suppression usually starts more than 3 weeks into therapy, but can start as early as 1 week
- Usually needs 5-20 mg/day of prednisone equivalent or more
- Steroids stopped within the last 3 months: treat as if still on steroids
- Severe suppression can take up to a year to recover
- Choose the regimen from the preoperative steroid dose and the size of the surgical stimulus

## Assessing the HPA axis
- Uncertain steroid history: either test the HPA axis or give a short perioperative steroid course
- Not suppressed (not significant)
  - Any dose for under 3 weeks
  - Morning doses under 5 mg/day prednisone equivalent ("physiological replacement")
  - Under 10 mg every other day (EOD) prednisone equivalent
  - No testing needed and no perioperative supplementation; continue usual daily dose
- Suppressed (assume functional suppression)
  - More than 5-20 mg/day prednisone equivalent for more than 3 weeks
  - Any patient on steroids with clinical Cushing's syndrome
  - Give supplemental steroids matched to the surgical stress
- Indeterminate
  - 5 to 20 mg prednisone equivalent for more than 3 weeks: HPA suppression varies widely
    - Likely due to differing steroid metabolism; poorly related to age, sex, dose or duration
  - Under 5 mg prednisone daily taken in the evening may disturb the diurnal rhythm and the stress response
  - Test the HPA axis before surgery
    - Screening: 8am cortisol
      - Under 5 mcg/dL (under 138 nmol/L): likely suppressed
      - Over 10 mcg/dL (over 276 nmol/L): likely not suppressed
    - Short Synacthen test if the 8am cortisol is indeterminate
      - Withhold the morning steroid dose
      - Cortisol at 30 and 60 min after 250 mcg cosyntropin
      - Over 18 mcg/dL (over 497 nmol/L): not suppressed
  - Urgent or emergency surgery with unknown suppression: give empirical perioperative steroid cover
- Other routes that can suppress
  - Inhaled: e.g. 1500 mcg daily or more (750 mcg daily of fluticasone)
  - Topical: e.g. 2 g/day or more of high or super high potency steroid
  - Significant intermittent use

## Perioperative steroid supplementation (UpToDate)
- Minor surgery or local anaesthesia (e.g. inguinal hernia repair)
  - Usual morning dose
  - No extra cover
- Moderate stress (e.g. lower limb revascularisation, joint replacement)
  - Usual morning dose
  - Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV just before the procedure, then 25 mg every eight hours for 24 hours
  - Then resume usual dose
- Major stress (e.g. oesophagogastrectomy, total proctocolectomy, open heart surgery)
  - Usual morning dose
  - Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV before induction, then 50 mg every eight hours for 24 hours
  - Halve the dose each day down to maintenance, usually over 2-3 days
- Short Synacthen test: adrenal insufficiency traditionally defined as peak cortisol under 18 mcg/dL (497 nmol/L)

## UK guideline (2020)
- Any surgery under anaesthesia, including regional and minor procedures such as oocyte retrieval: give hydrocortisone 100 mg
- Adrenosuppressive dose: prednisolone equivalent of 5 mg or more for 4 weeks or longer

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the hydrocortisone equivalent of prednisolone 5 mg, and of dexamethasone 8 mg?
  - A: 20 mg; about 200 mg
- Q: Why is dexamethasone inadequate cover in primary adrenal insufficiency?
  - A: It has no mineralocorticoid activity
- Q: What is the UpToDate regimen for major surgical stress?
  - A: Usual morning dose, hydrocortisone 100 mg IV before induction, then 50 mg every eight hours for 24 hours, then halve daily
- Q: What 8am cortisol suggests HPA suppression?
  - A: Under 5 mcg/dL (under 138 nmol/L)
- Q: How does the 2020 UK guideline define an adrenosuppressive dose?
  - A: Prednisolone equivalent of 5 mg or more for 4 weeks or longer
