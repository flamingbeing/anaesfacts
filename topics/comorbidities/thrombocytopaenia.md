# Thrombocytopaenia

Platelets <150 x 10^9/L; know the procedure thresholds (>50k for lines and surgery, >80k for neuraxial and critical surgery).

## Definition and causes
- Platelets <150 x 10^9/L
- Causes
  - Reduced production: hereditary, drugs, alcohol, viral infection, marrow failure
  - Dilution: massive transfusion
  - Increased consumption: ITP, drugs, viral infection, SLE, lymphoproliferative disease, DIC, bypass, TTP, hypersplenism
- Spontaneous bleeding is very uncommon until <10-20

## History
- Bleeding
- Bruising

## Examination
- Splenomegaly

## Investigations
- FBC, with a repeat sample
- Blood film
- Bone marrow

## Management
- Treat the cause
- ITP
  - Platelet transfusion only for major haemorrhage, as transfused platelets are destroyed quickly
  - Better to prepare for surgery with steroids or high-dose immunoglobulin

## Perioperative management
- Platelet targets
  - <20k: prophylactic transfusion
  - >50k for invasive lines, transbronchial biopsy, liver biopsy, or before surgery
  - >80k for LP, epidural, or critical surgery (neurosurgery, eye surgery)
  - >100k for active bleeding
- Manage bleeding
- Ongoing bleeding despite platelets >50: suspect DIC; give FFP and cryoprecipitate
- Renal failure, haemophilia and von Willebrand disease: DDAVP 0.3 mcg/kg IV over 30min

## Flashcards
- Q: Platelet count needed for an epidural or LP in the note?
  - A: >80k (same as neurosurgery or eye surgery)
- Q: Platelet count needed for invasive lines or before surgery?
  - A: >50k
- Q: Below what count is spontaneous bleeding usually seen?
  - A: <10-20
- Q: DDAVP dose for uraemic or von Willebrand bleeding?
  - A: 0.3 mcg/kg IV over 30min
- Q: Best preparation for surgery in ITP?
  - A: Steroids or high-dose immunoglobulin; transfuse only for major haemorrhage
