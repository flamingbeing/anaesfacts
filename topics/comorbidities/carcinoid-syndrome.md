# Carcinoid Syndrome

Prevent carcinoid crisis with octreotide, smooth anaesthesia and avoidance of trigger drugs; similar in approach to phaeochromocytoma.

## Summary
- Before surgery: start octreotide, correct dehydration, TTE
- Arterial line, CVP and FloTrac before induction
- Avoid crisis triggers (crisis = hypotension, bronchospasm)
  - Sympathetic stimulation: smooth induction and emergence
  - Indirect sympathomimetics: ephedrine
  - Histamine-releasing drugs: atracurium, morphine, pethidine
- Preferred: vasopressin, milrinone, ipratropium
- Octreotide infusion with 100mcg bolus ready

## Background
- Asymptomatic carcinoid tumour: no specific anaesthetic issues
- Carcinoid syndrome: major problems
  - Haemodynamic instability, tachycardia
  - Carcinoid heart disease with right heart failure (endocardial fibrosis of pulmonary and tricuspid valves)
- Carcinoid crisis
  - Mediated by serotonin, bradykinins and histamine
  - Hypotension, bronchospasm, flushing
  - Triggered by surgery and by drugs releasing catecholamines or histamine
- Sites
  - Gastrointestinal tract (55%), mainly small intestine
  - Bronchopulmonary (30%): lobectomy; left-sided valve fibrosis

## Preoperative assessment
- Symptoms: flushing; diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting
- Investigations
  - 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA (5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid, a serotonin breakdown product)
    - Alternatives: plasma 5-HIAA, chromogranin A
  - TTE focusing on the right heart
  - Spirometry

## Optimisation
- Symptomatic patients: optimise bronchodilators, hydration and electrolytes
- Octreotide 100mcg SC three times daily for 2 weeks before surgery to block mediator release
  - Perioperative octreotide is the most effective way to prevent intraoperative crisis and the main treatment of crisis
  - Emergency surgery: IV octreotide 50 to 100 mcg/h started at least 2 hours before surgery, continued for 48 hours after
- Antihistamines

## Intraoperative management
- Octreotide infusion 50-500mcg/hr, with boluses as needed
- Avoid trigger drugs
  - Indirect sympathomimetics: ephedrine
  - Histamine releasers: atracurium, morphine, pethidine
  - Possibly suxamethonium: fasciculations raise intra-abdominal pressure and may squeeze the tumour into releasing mediators
- Arterial line and CVP before induction
- Vasoactive drugs: vasopressin and milrinone preferred over noradrenaline or dobutamine, avoiding beta stimulation of the tumour
- Smooth induction with remifentanil to blunt the sympathetic response
- Adequate depth and analgesia to block surgical stimulation; avoid hypothermia and hypercarbia
- Treat carcinoid crisis
  - Hypotension: octreotide 100mcg boluses; phenylephrine
    - Crisis can be hard to tell from hypotension due to myocardial dysfunction
  - Hypertension: labetalol, esmolol
  - Bronchospasm: avoid beta-agonists (salbutamol); give ipratropium or increase octreotide
- Metabolic: ABGs for electrolytes and glucose; watch fluid shifts
- Consider an epidural
  - Reduces surgical stress response and so crisis risk
  - Top up local anaesthetic carefully: hypotension can trigger a compensatory sympathetic surge and crisis
- Smooth emergence; may be delayed by serotonin-induced sedation

## Postoperative management
- Stay alert for crisis or hypotension: IV octreotide 10-20mcg
- If on an IV octreotide infusion, continue for 48hrs after surgery
  - May then need SC octreotide, depending on prior somatostatin analogue needs and symptom control
  - Wean IV octreotide over 7 days after resection

## Flashcards
- Q: Preoperative octreotide regimen for carcinoid syndrome?
  - A: 100mcg SC three times daily for 2 weeks before surgery.
- Q: Intraoperative octreotide infusion rate and bolus for crisis?
  - A: 50-500mcg/hr infusion; 100mcg boluses for crisis.
- Q: Drugs to avoid in carcinoid syndrome?
  - A: Ephedrine, and histamine releasers such as atracurium, morphine and pethidine.
- Q: Treatment of bronchospasm in carcinoid crisis?
  - A: Ipratropium or more octreotide; avoid beta-agonists such as salbutamol.
- Q: Main urinary marker for carcinoid?
  - A: 24-hour urinary 5-HIAA.
