# Valvular Heart Disease and Murmurs

Assess the murmur, get a TTE for moderate or worse disease, fix severe disease first if it meets intervention criteria; stenosis "slow and tight", regurgitation "fast and forward".

## Undiagnosed murmur before surgery
1. Examination
   - Murmur character, including manoeuvres
   - Signs of AF, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, infective endocarditis (IE)
2. Age group
   - Child: innocent or pathological murmur
   - Elderly: aortic sclerosis or aortic stenosis
3. Symptoms
   - Exercise tolerance
   - Symptoms of AF, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, IE
4. Investigations
   - ECG: right or left axis deviation, RVH or LVH, arrhythmias, ischaemia
   - CXR: cardiomegaly
5. Optimisation
   - Proceed, or refer to cardiology for an echo (2DE)?
     - Proceed: emergency or minor surgery, good effort tolerance, no pathological features, normal ECG and CXR
     - Refer: major surgery, poor effort tolerance, pathological features, abnormal ECG or CXR
   - Need for IE prophylaxis?

## Known valve disease before surgery
1. Define the lesions, severity and complications: TTE
2. Set haemodynamic goals; with mixed lesions, treat the predominant one
   - E.g. AS with MR: treating AS can worsen MR
     - Syncope or angina: manage as AS
     - Dyspnoea or pulmonary oedema: manage as MR or heart failure
3. IE prophylaxis: antibiotics before dental procedures in high-risk patients only; not recommended for non-dental procedures (2020 AHA VHD)
4. Anticoagulation plan

## Evaluation for non-cardiac surgery
- Clinical review for symptoms
- ECG
- TTE if known or suspected disease of moderate or greater severity
  - If clinically unchanged, an echo within the past 12 months is acceptable (2020 AHA VHD)
  - Assess severity, LVEF and PASP

## Risk assessment
- AS and MS are high risk
- Left-sided regurgitant lesions also raise cardiac risk, but are tolerated better than stenoses

## Management
- If the patient meets standard criteria for valve intervention, defer elective non-cardiac surgery and treat the valve first
  - Generally: severe symptomatic disease, or asymptomatic with other risk factors such as reduced LVEF (except secondary MR)
- In emergencies, non-cardiac surgery may have to go ahead with severe uncorrected valve disease

## Haemodynamic goals
- Stenotic lesions: "slow and tight"
  - HR low normal, 60-70bpm, for filling time
  - SVR high normal: stroke volume is fixed, so a fall in SVR drops BP (no compensatory rise in output without tachycardia) and coronary perfusion pressure
  - Normal preload
- Regurgitant lesions: "fast and forward"
  - HR high normal, 80-90bpm, less time for regurgitation
  - SVR low normal; a rise worsens the regurgitant fraction
  - Preload high normal ("full")
- Sinus rhythm preferred; especially important in mitral stenosis and HOCM, which rely on atrial kick

## Severe disease criteria (AHA 2020)
- AS: AVA <1cm2, mean gradient >40, Vmax >4; also LVH, diastolic dysfunction, pulmonary hypertension
- MS: MVA <1.5cm2, pressure half-time >150ms; also PASP >50, severe LA enlargement
  - ASE: MVA <1cm2, mean gradient >10, PASP >50
- AR: regurgitant volume >60, regurgitant fraction > 50%, ERO >30mm2; also severe LV dilation
- MR: regurgitant volume >60, regurgitant fraction > 50%, ERO >40mm2; also severe LA and LV enlargement, pulmonary hypertension

## Flashcards
- Q: Severe AS criteria (AHA 2020)?
  - A: AVA <1cm2, mean gradient >40, Vmax >4
- Q: Severe MS criteria (AHA 2020)?
  - A: MVA <1.5cm2, pressure half-time >150ms
- Q: HR targets for stenotic vs regurgitant lesions?
  - A: Stenotic 60-70bpm; regurgitant 80-90bpm
- Q: How recent must an echo be if the patient is clinically unchanged?
  - A: Within the past 12 months (2020 AHA VHD)
- Q: ERO thresholds for severe AR and MR?
  - A: AR >30mm2; MR >40mm2
