# Anaesthesia after Lung Transplant

Denervated, lymphatic-poor lungs with a fragile bronchial anastomosis in an immunosuppressed patient.

## Denervated lung
- Cough reflex lost and mucosal sensitivity reduced: risk of silent aspiration
- Ciliary clearance impaired: sputum retention and infection
- Still intact: airway reactivity and hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction

## Lymphatic drainage
- Lymphatics are cut at transplant
- Very prone to acute pulmonary oedema early; drainage returns after about 4 weeks

## Single lung transplant
- Native and graft lungs often differ greatly in compliance
  - Obstructive native lung: dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP) of the native lung, mediastinal shift, haemodynamic collapse
  - Restrictive native lung: ventilation goes mainly to the more compliant graft, which may over-distend
- Double-lumen tube and one-lung ventilation may help; place under fibreoptic guidance to avoid injuring the bronchial anastomosis

## Other issues
- Acute rejection can occur at any time and is very hard to tell from infection
- May have significant pulmonary hypertension and RV failure

## Anaesthetic management
- Protect the lungs: careful fluids, lung-protective ventilation, opioid-sparing analgesia (as for high respiratory risk patients)
- Protect the anastomosis: avoid placing the tube too deep; bronchoscopy
- Immunosuppression
  - Infection risk
  - Stress-dose steroids
  - End-organ effects (e.g. renal impairment)

## Flashcards
- Q: When is lymphatic drainage re-established after lung transplant?
  - A: After about 4 weeks; before that the lungs are very prone to pulmonary oedema.
- Q: Which reflexes stay intact in a transplanted lung?
  - A: Airway reactivity and hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction; the cough reflex is lost.
- Q: Risk when ventilating a single lung transplant with an obstructive native lung?
  - A: Dynamic hyperinflation of the native lung, mediastinal shift and haemodynamic collapse.
- Q: How should a double-lumen tube be placed after lung transplant?
  - A: Under fibreoptic guidance to avoid trauma to the bronchial anastomosis.
