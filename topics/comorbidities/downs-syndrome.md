# Down's Syndrome

Commonest chromosomal abnormality; key issues are behaviour, airway and C-spine, congenital heart disease and endocrine disease.

## Summary
- Intellectual disability: premed, parental presence, inhalational induction, difficult IV access
- Airway: macroglossia, atlanto-axial instability (AAI), subglottic stenosis, OSA, GORD
- Congenital heart disease (AVSD)
- Endocrine: obesity, hypothyroidism, diabetes

## Epidemiology and genetics
- About 1:700 live births; similar across populations, varies mainly with maternal age
  - Maternal age 25 years: 1:1400; age 45 years: 1:46
- 95% are trisomy 21 from meiotic non-disjunction; translocation 2-3%, mosaic 2-3%
- Survival has improved with active treatment of life-threatening anomalies
  - About 20% die in the first year
  - 45% expected to reach 60 years (86% of the general population)
  - A generation ago about two-thirds died in early childhood

## Clinical features
- Typical craniofacial appearance, often diagnosed at birth; intellectual impairment; other organ anomalies common
- Small head, brachycephaly (wide head), often a third fontanelle, short thick neck
- Upslanting palpebral fissures, epicanthic folds, Brushfield's spots (white spots at iris edge), flat nasal bridge
- Large protruding tongue, small mouth and ears
- Single transverse palmar crease in both hands (50%)
- Low birth weight, but tend to obesity in childhood; often short

## Airway and cervical spine
- Craniofacial: macroglossia, micrognathia, midface hypoplasia, short neck
- Atlanto-axial instability common (30%), not always symptomatic
  - Lax transverse ligament and/or odontoid peg malformation
  - Subluxation or dislocation can compress the cord
  - Manual in-line stabilisation, or awake fibreoptic intubation
  - Avoid excess neck movement at laryngoscopy and in surgical positioning
- Also OSA, subglottic stenosis, GORD; congenital heart disease affects induction choice

## Respiratory
- OSA common (50%): obstruction during anaesthesia or in recovery
  - Avoid premeds, inhalational induction, opioid-sparing technique, extubate awake, paediatric high dependency care
- Subglottic stenosis (2-6%): ETT 0.5-1.0 mm smaller than predicted for age
  - Make sure there is an audible leak with positive pressure, to avoid post-extubation oedema, stridor and distress
- Chronic lower respiratory infections common (reduced immunity, reflux)

## Gastrointestinal
- GORD risk: consider RSI
- GI anomalies in 7%
- One-third of patients with duodenal atresia have Down's syndrome
- Reflux, oesophageal atresia, Hirschsprung's disease and coeliac disease more common

## Neurological
- Mean adult IQ about 50; IQ falls with age in adults from early-onset Alzheimer's dementia
  - Manage as in children: premed, caregiver presence, inhalational induction, plan emergence
- Up to 10% have epilepsy
- Neonates may be hypotonic

## Cardiovascular
- Congenital heart disease in 40-60%: review echo, cardiology follow-up
  - Commonest: atrioventricular canal defects and VSD; also ASD, PDA, tetralogy of Fallot
  - Pulmonary hypertension earlier and more severe with left-to-right shunts; without early repair can progress to Eisenmenger's (shunt reverses to right-to-left)
  - May be on heart failure drugs or drugs to reduce tetralogy spells
  - Antibiotic prophylaxis often needed
  - Plan by lesion: manage shunt dynamics, avoid pulmonary hypertension; bubble precautions with shunts
- Risk of bradycardia at induction, especially with sevoflurane

## Haematological and immune
- Leukaemia 10-18-fold more common
  - First year of life: acute non-lymphoblastic leukaemia predominates
  - Older children: acute lymphoblastic leukaemia predominates
  - Neonates may have a transient leukaemoid reaction
- More infections: defects in cell-mediated immunity and granulocytes

## Endocrine
- Obesity common
- Hypothyroidism very common (up to 40%): make euthyroid to avoid perioperative bradycardia, hypothermia and delayed emergence
- Higher risk of type 1 diabetes
- Adrenal insufficiency

## Musculoskeletal
- Hypotonia: exaggerated response to muscle relaxants
- Ligamentous laxity: joint dislocations; position carefully to avoid nerve palsies and hip or shoulder injury

## Flashcards
- Q: How common is atlanto-axial instability in Down's syndrome?
  - A: About 30%; often asymptomatic.
- Q: ETT sizing in Down's syndrome?
  - A: 0.5-1.0 mm smaller than predicted for age (subglottic stenosis 2-6%), with an audible leak.
- Q: Incidence of congenital heart disease and commonest lesions?
  - A: 40-60%; atrioventricular canal defects and VSD.
- Q: What induction problem occurs with sevoflurane in Down's syndrome?
  - A: Bradycardia.
- Q: How common is hypothyroidism?
  - A: Up to 40%.
