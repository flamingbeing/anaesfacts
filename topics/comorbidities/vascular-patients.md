# Vascular Patients

Vascular patients nearly always have coronary disease: assess risk, keep supply and demand balanced, and block where possible.

## Key points
- Risk assessment: risk calculator, functional status, biomarkers
- Ischaemic heart disease: optimise myocardial oxygen supply and demand
- Heart failure: haemodynamically stable induction, careful fluids, afterload reduction
- Cardiovascular complications cause 25% to 50% of deaths after noncardiac surgery
  - Mostly in patients with existing cardiovascular disease

## Sources of cardiovascular stress during surgery
- Adrenergic response to surgery
- Circulatory effects of anaesthetic drugs, intubation and IPPV
- Blood loss and fluid shifts
- Temperature changes

## Patient factors
- Often elderly and frail
- Coronary artery disease almost always present
  - Myocardial ischaemia is common and often silent
- More diabetes and hypertension
- Many smoke; COPD is common
- Renal impairment is common

## Drugs
- Common: aspirin, beta-blockers, diuretics, heart failure drugs, insulin or oral hypoglycaemics
  - Continue beta-blockers and statins perioperatively
  - Plan insulin management
- Some are anticoagulated; others will be given anticoagulants perioperatively

## Vascular surgery
- Suprainguinal vascular surgery is a risk factor in Lee's RCRI
- 30% to 40% of vascular operations happen out of hours
- Serial operations are common, so look for previous anaesthetic records
- Many cases are semi-urgent, such as wound debridement for sepsis control

## Preoperative assessment
- Quantify cardiorespiratory disease
- Look for major cardiac risk factors
  - Unstable coronary syndromes
  - Decompensated heart failure
  - Significant arrhythmias
  - Significant valve disease
- Measure NIBP in both arms, as arteriopathy can cause a difference
  - Use the higher reading, or site the arterial line on that side

## Intraoperative management
- Regional techniques can reduce morbidity and mortality
  - Block wherever possible, for example all lower limb wound debridements
- Procedure-specific benefits
  - Haemodynamic stability
  - Carotid endarterectomy: awake regional is the gold standard for neurological monitoring
  - Open AAA: epidural gives better analgesia and helps respiratory weaning
  - Amputation: analgesia, possibly less phantom limb pain
  - AV fistula: better vasodilation
- Many take clopidogrel (Plavix) or anticoagulants, which preclude neuraxial and epidural techniques
  - Enoxaparin (Clexane) is usually at prophylactic dose

## Flashcards
- Q: What share of deaths after noncardiac surgery are cardiovascular?
  - A: 25% to 50%
- Q: What proportion of vascular operations happen out of hours?
  - A: 30% to 40%
- Q: Why measure NIBP in both arms in vascular patients?
  - A: Arteriopathy can cause a difference; use the higher arm or site the arterial line there
- Q: Which drugs should be continued perioperatively in vascular patients?
  - A: Beta-blockers and statins
