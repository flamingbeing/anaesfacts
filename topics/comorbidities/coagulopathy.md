# Coagulopathy

Deranged clotting raises bleeding risk: correct to target, avoid neuraxial and deep blocks, avoid nasal airways, involve haematology.

## Key issues
- Bleeding risk
- **Neuraxial and deep peripheral nerve blocks contraindicated**; nasal airways contraindicated

## Investigations
- PT and APTT
- Mixing studies
- DIC screen
- Refer to haematology

## Causes
- Congenital: may first present in adulthood with a trigger
- Acquired:
  - Reduced factor synthesis
  - Increased consumption
  - Substances that interfere with factor function
- Common causes:
  - **Liver disease**
    - Standard clotting screen misleading (pro- and anticoagulants both low); **ROTEM** is better
    - Give **vitamin K 10mg** daily, with or without FFP 15ml/kg
  - **DIC**: sepsis, malignancy
  - Autoimmune disease (SLE, RA)
  - Drugs
  - Malabsorption causing vitamin K deficiency

## Targets
- **INR <1.5** before surgery or line insertion
  - Low-risk procedures: <2.0 (e.g. compressible peripheral lines, ultrasound-guided femoral access if low risk)
  - High-risk procedures: **normal INR, i.e. <1.2/1.3** (e.g. **neurosurgery, neuraxial**)
- **PT <19.8s (<1.5 times normal)**
- **APTT <53.4s (<1.5 times normal)**
  - Low-risk procedures: <1.8 times normal, e.g. 64s

## Intraoperative management
- Surgical haemostasis is paramount
- Correct coagulopathy: **prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC)**
- Blood conservation and preparing for bleeding: **tranexamic acid (TXA)**; avoid the lethal diamond
- Technique: **neuraxial and deep peripheral nerve blocks contraindicated**
- Airway: **avoid nasal intubation and oropharyngeal airways** in severe coagulopathy (risk of airway bleeding that cannot be compressed)

## Haemophilia
- Types:
  - Haemophilia A: X-linked recessive defect in factor VIII activity
    - Severe (spontaneous bleeding): factor VIII <2%
    - Moderate: 2-5%
    - Mild (bleeds only after trauma): 5-30%
  - Haemophilia B (Christmas disease): X-linked recessive defect in factor IX activity
- Affects males; females are carriers but can have mild disease
- Female homozygotes die in utero
- History: bleeding into muscles, joints and internal organs
- Examination: signs of bleeding or bruising
- Investigations:
  - Bleeding time normal
  - APTT prolonged
  - INR normal
  - Fibrinogen normal
  - Specific factor levels reduced
  - von Willebrand factor normal

### Preoperative management
- Discuss with haematologist
- Give recombinant factor as needed:
  - Aim for factor levels of 50-100% before surgery
  - Maintain levels for 2-7 days after the procedure

### Intraoperative management
- Minimise blood loss and transfusion
- Avoid antiplatelets and anticoagulants
- Avoid IM injections
- Mild bleeding risk:
  - DDAVP 0.3mcg/kg over 30min
  - Tranexamic acid 20mg/kg IV over 5 min, three times daily
- Moderate to severe bleeding risk:
  - Replace with recombinant factors, maintained for 2-7 days after surgery
  - Cryoprecipitate (factor VIII) and FFP (factor XI) only in emergencies

### Postoperative management
- Monitor for bleeding
- Close joint care with haematology

## Von Willebrand disease
- von Willebrand factor: protein for platelet adhesion that also carries factor VIII
- Deficiency causes:
  - Factor VIII deficiency
  - Abnormal platelet adhesion
  - Abnormal vascular endothelium
- Autosomal dominant inheritance
- Types:
  - Type 1 (90% of cases): reduced amount of vWF; mild or no symptoms; heterozygous
  - Type 2 (9%): abnormal vWF function; 4 subtypes (A, B, N, M)
    - DDAVP contraindicated in 2B (gain-of-function defect: risk of low platelets and thrombosis)
  - Type 3 (1%): like type 1 but severe symptoms; homozygous; autosomal recessive
- History: epistaxis, bruising, haemarthrosis, haematoma, menorrhagia
- Investigations:
  - Platelet count normal
  - INR normal
  - APTT increased
  - Fibrinogen normal
  - Factor VIII level decreased
  - vWF level decreased
  - Bleeding time increased
  - Platelet function assay time increased

### Perioperative management
- Consult haematologist
- Test DDAVP response (0.3mcg/kg IV over 30min), measuring vWF before and after
  - Responders: **DDAVP** for prophylaxis and bleeding
  - Non-responders: **factor VIII concentrate or cryoprecipitate**
  - **Tranexamic acid 20mg/kg IV three times daily** can also be used

## Flashcards
- Q: What INR is the usual target before surgery or line insertion, and for neurosurgery or neuraxial?
  - A: INR <1.5; normal INR (<1.2/1.3) for high-risk procedures
- Q: What dose of DDAVP is used in mild haemophilia A or von Willebrand disease?
  - A: 0.3mcg/kg IV over 30min
- Q: In which von Willebrand subtype is DDAVP contraindicated?
  - A: Type 2B (risk of low platelets and thrombosis)
- Q: What factor VIII level defines severe haemophilia A?
  - A: <2%
- Q: Why is a standard clotting screen misleading in liver disease?
  - A: Pro- and anticoagulants are both low; ROTEM is better
