# Cardiomyopathy (HOCM, DCM, Restrictive)

Most patients have little reserve; HOCM needs a full, slow, well-constricted heart, DCM needs support of a failing pump.

## Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HOCM): definition
- Primary hypertrophy of the left ventricle with no other cause (e.g. aortic stenosis, hypertension)
- Grouped by left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO): obstructive at rest, labile, or non-obstructive
  - One-third obstructed at rest: peak LVOT gradient over 30 mm Hg
  - One-third labile: gradient over 30 mm Hg only on provocation (Valsalva, inhaled amyl nitrite, treadmill exercise)
  - One-third never obstructed: peak gradient under 30 mm Hg at rest or on provocation
- LVOT gradient over 50mmHg is the usual threshold for surgical or percutaneous intervention, mainly if symptomatic

## HOCM: aetiology
- Cause often unknown
- Often inherited: autosomal dominant with variable penetrance
  - Mostly sarcomere protein mutations, causing hypertrophy and myocardial fibre disarray
  - Up to 40% have no genetic explanation

## HOCM: pathophysiology
- LV hypertrophy, most often of the interventricular septum (asymmetric septal hypertrophy)
- Dynamic LVOT obstruction
  - Basal septal bulge narrows the outflow tract
  - Abnormal mitral apparatus (long leaflets, anteriorly placed papillary muscles) leads to systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve
  - Venturi effect: low pressure in the narrowed tract pulls the anterior leaflet further forward
  - Result: pressure overload and low cardiac output, causing syncope
  - Worse with more contractility, less ventricular volume, or lower afterload
- Secondary mitral regurgitation: SAM distorts the leaflets so they do not coapt; MR lowers cardiac output and worsens symptoms
- Diastolic dysfunction: fibre disarray stiffens the LV and slows relaxation, raising LV, LA and pulmonary venous pressures and lowering cardiac output
- Arrhythmias: electrical remodelling leads to VT and sudden cardiac death
- Myocardial ischaemia (angina)
  - Greater muscle mass raises oxygen demand
  - Systolic compression of intramyocardial vessels
  - High diastolic pressures reduce coronary flow
  - Coronary microvascular dysfunction
- Autonomic dysfunction: inappropriate firing of LV stretch receptors, causing syncope

## HOCM: clinical features and investigations
- Heart failure, ischaemia and angina, sudden cardiac death
- Ejection systolic murmur (ESM): louder on standing or Valsalva, softer on squatting or handgrip
- ECG: LVH with T-wave inversion (strain), dagger Q waves (narrow lateral or inferior Q waves from septal depolarisation), ischaemia
  - ESM plus these ECG changes suggests HOCM
- CXR: pulmonary oedema, cardiomegaly
- TTE confirms: septal thickness, SAM, LVOTO, MR, diastolic dysfunction, heart failure, RV function; stress echo
- Exercise capacity (e.g. 6-minute walk test, 6MWT)
- Cardiac MRI
- Holter for ventricular arrhythmias

## HOCM: treatment
- Medical
  - Avoid situations with sudden changes in preload or afterload
  - Beta-blockers first line, then calcium channel blockers (rate control, negative inotropy)
  - ICD to prevent sudden cardiac death
- Septal reduction if medical therapy fails: surgical myomectomy or percutaneous alcohol septal ablation

## HOCM: haemodynamic goals
- Avoid worsening LVOT obstruction: keep the ventricle full and slow
- Limit contractility; avoid sympathetic stimulation (key difference from aortic stenosis)
  - With significant obstruction, some myocardial depression is usually desirable
  - Beta-blockers counter sympathetic drive and reduce obstruction
- Low-normal heart rate, 60-80bpm, for diastolic filling time
  - Blunt the laryngoscopy response: esmolol, lidocaine, alfentanil
  - Adequate depth and analgesia
  - Ventilate to EtCO2 35-40 mmHg; avoid hypercarbia
  - Smooth extubation (on remifentanil)
- High-normal preload: avoid hypovolaemia; give IV fluid before induction
- High-normal afterload: falls in SVR worsen the gradient, cause tachycardia and reduce coronary perfusion
  - Direct alpha-agonist: phenylephrine
  - Avoid histamine-releasing drugs: rocuronium over atracurium, oxycodone over morphine
- Keep sinus rhythm (filling depends on atrial kick); be ready for malignant arrhythmias
  - Correct electrolytes and acidosis
  - Normothermia: temperature probe and active warming
  - Manage the ICD; defibrillator pads on, resuscitation drugs drawn up, crash cart ready
  - Treat tachyarrhythmias aggressively, including cardioversion
- Avoid ischaemia
  - Keep diastolic pressure, diastolic time (slow rate) and filling
  - Titrate FiO2 and PEEP to SpO2 over 95%
  - Treat anaemia
  - Avoid sympathetic surges

## HOCM: preoperative assessment
- History: heart failure (breathlessness, dizziness, orthopnoea, ankle oedema), angina, exercise capacity, palpitations, syncope
- Examination: ESM behaviour (as above), crepitations
- Investigations: ECG, CXR, TTE, exercise capacity (6MWT) as above
- Continue beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers
- ICD: interrogate and suspend antitachycardia function
- Multidisciplinary discussion with surgeon and cardiologist: urgency versus optimisation
- Counselling and consent
- Premedicate as needed to prevent anxiety-related tachycardia

## HOCM: intraoperative management
- Avoid spinal anaesthesia: large falls in preload and SVR
- Monitoring
  - Low threshold for an arterial line
  - Central line for major surgery or symptomatic patients
  - Consider cardiac output monitoring (e.g. FloTrac)
  - Consider TOE to assess obstruction
  - BIS to avoid overdose and falls in SVR
  - Temperature monitoring and warming (hypothermia leads to tachycardia)
- Induction
  - Cardiostable: avoid large propofol doses; slow titrated boluses or co-induction with volatile; phenylephrine ready
  - Blunt laryngoscopy: high-dose opioid, adequate depth, esmolol ready
  - Avoid pancuronium
- Maintenance
  - Balanced technique with opioids to avoid overdose and low SVR
  - Adequate depth and analgesia
  - Avoid desflurane (sympathetic stimulation)
  - Avoid hypercarbia
  - Watch blood loss closely; replace losses, avoid dehydration, give maintenance fluid
  - Avoid inotropes (e.g. ephedrine); use vasopressors (e.g. phenylephrine) for SVR
  - Avoid large rises in BP; maintain BP, especially diastolic
  - SpO2 over 95; Hb over 8
- Acute LVOT obstruction
  - Slow rate and reduce contractility: esmolol or diltiazem
  - Raise SVR: phenylephrine
  - Fill: IV fluid
- Treat arrhythmias aggressively (esmolol, defibrillation)
- Emergence: smooth; consider extubation on remifentanil

## HOCM: postoperative management
- Multimodal analgesia to prevent pain-related tachycardia; no adrenaline in local anaesthetic
- Aggressive antiemetic strategy to prevent nausea-related tachycardia
- Re-enable ICD antitachycardia therapy and interrogate
- High dependency care for cardiovascular monitoring

## Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM)
- Causes: idiopathic, alcohol, haemochromatosis, post-viral, peripartum, IV drug use, myotonic dystrophies
- Heart failure with an enlarged, poorly contracting heart
- Progressive dilatation increases LV end-diastolic volume, causing functional MR and TR
- History: heart failure, palpitations (arrhythmias), stroke (embolic)
- Examination: heart failure signs, neurological examination, murmurs
- Investigations
  - FBC: anaemia or polycythaemia
  - Urea and electrolytes: electrolytes, renal function
  - ECG: arrhythmia, conduction defects, ischaemia
  - Echo: LV function, valves
  - CXR: left ventricular failure
- Treatment: diuretics, ACE inhibitors, nitrates, amiodarone (arrhythmias), digoxin (arrhythmias and positive inotropy), beta-blockers (carvedilol), anticoagulation, dual-chamber pacing with or without ICD
- Intraoperative management
  - Invasive monitoring including cardiac output
  - Keep sinus rhythm, adequate volume, normal SVR
  - Avoid myocardial depression
  - Inotropes often needed (dobutamine or milrinone)
- Postoperative: HDU or ICU

## Restrictive cardiomyopathy
- Rare; right heart failure often dominant
- Amyloid infiltration: stiff ventricle and diastolic dysfunction
- History: breathlessness, fatigue; examination: heart failure, especially right-sided
- Echo: diastolic dysfunction
- Intraoperative management
  - High-risk anaesthetic
  - Keep sinus rhythm, load volume, high-normal SVR
  - Avoid myocardial depression
  - Vasodilation, myocardial depression and reduced venous return together cause cardiovascular collapse
  - Spontaneous ventilation if possible
  - Ketamine is a good choice (raises contractility and peripheral resistance)

## Flashcards
- Q: Peak LVOT gradient defining obstruction in HOCM, and the intervention threshold?
  - A: Over 30 mm Hg defines obstruction; over 50 mmHg is the threshold for intervention, mainly if symptomatic
- Q: Three things that worsen dynamic LVOT obstruction?
  - A: More contractility, less ventricular volume, lower afterload
- Q: Target heart rate in HOCM?
  - A: Low-normal, 60-80 bpm
- Q: First-line treatment of acute intraoperative LVOT obstruction?
  - A: Esmolol (or diltiazem), phenylephrine and IV fluid
- Q: Inotropes often needed in dilated cardiomyopathy?
  - A: Dobutamine or milrinone
