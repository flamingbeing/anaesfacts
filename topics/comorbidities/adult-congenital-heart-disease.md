# Adults with Congenital Heart Disease

Risk depends on whether the lesion is uncorrected, corrected or palliated; watch shunt dynamics, pulmonary hypertension and Fontan physiology.

## Uncorrected disease
- Small VSDs and ASDs cause few problems apart from paradoxical emboli
- A large left-to-right shunt leads to pulmonary hypertension and then shunt reversal (Eisenmenger syndrome): very high risk

## Corrected disease
- Manage as normal
- Exclude associated congenital abnormalities

## Palliated disease
- Normal anatomy has not been restored
- Examples: neonatal switch for transposition of the great vessels; Fontan for hypoplastic left heart syndrome or pulmonary atresia
- Transfer to a specialist centre

## Fontan circulation
- Palliative procedure for patients with **functional univentricular physiology**
- High CVP increases bleeding risk, e.g. from tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy or nasal intubation
- Avoid anything that raises pulmonary artery pressure

## Management
- Depends on the underlying lesion; always consider **Eisenmenger syndrome**
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis for dental procedures** (see infective endocarditis)
- Shunts: manage **shunt dynamics** (avoid pulmonary hypertension) and take **bubble precautions** on IV lines
- Eisenmenger syndrome: manage as for pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular dysfunction

## Flashcards
- Q: What is Eisenmenger syndrome?
  - A: Reversal of a large left-to-right shunt after it has caused pulmonary hypertension
- Q: Why are Fontan patients at higher bleeding risk with nasal intubation?
  - A: Their CVP is high
- Q: What is the main concern with small uncorrected ASDs or VSDs?
  - A: Paradoxical emboli
- Q: Two key intraoperative precautions for a patient with a shunt?
  - A: Avoid pulmonary hypertension and take bubble precautions
