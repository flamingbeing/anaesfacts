# Acute Liver Failure

New hepatocellular dysfunction with coagulopathy and encephalopathy in a patient with no prior liver disease.

## Definition
- Hepatocellular dysfunction with coagulopathy and encephalopathy, without pre-existing liver disease
- High mortality (10-100%)

## Types (mortality)
- Hyperacute: within 7 days (30%)
- Acute: 7-28 days (33%)
- Subacute: 28 days – 6 months (14%)

## Causes
- Viral hepatitis (A to G, CMV, HSV, EBV)
- Drugs (paracetamol, halothane, idiosyncratic)
- Toxins
- Acute fatty liver of pregnancy
- HELLP
- Wilson's disease
- Reye's syndrome
- NASH

## Clinical features
- Encephalopathy
- Cerebral oedema
- Severe coagulopathy with active fibrinolysis
- Metabolic: low glucose, low potassium, low sodium, metabolic acidosis
- High cardiac output with low SVR
- Multiorgan failure: raised ICP, ARDS, acute renal failure

## Examination
- Jaundice
- Vasodilated
- High cardiac output
- Signs of raised ICP
- Ascites
- Hepatomegaly
- Bruising

## Investigations
- Bloods:
  - LFTs raised, albumin low
  - PT/PTT
  - Low glucose: depleted glycogen stores and reduced hepatic gluconeogenesis
  - Ammonia
  - Electrolytes: low urea (inadequate hepatic production), low potassium
  - Virology: hepatitis serology
  - Immunology:
    - Antinuclear antibody (chronic active hepatitis)
    - Smooth muscle antibody (primary biliary cirrhosis)
    - Alpha-fetoprotein (hepatoma)
- Imaging: ultrasound, CT/MRCP
- ERCP

## Preoperative management
- Neuro-focused intensive care
- Active hepatitis: delay all elective surgery for 30 days, until LFTs normalise
- Universal precautions
- Support failing organs
- Intubate if encephalopathy is severe
- Correct electrolytes and hypoglycaemia
- Noradrenaline for circulatory support if needed
- Bicarbonate (NaHCO3)-buffered haemofiltration
- ICP monitoring
- N-acetylcysteine (NAC) for paracetamol overdose
- Do not correct coagulopathy routinely: PT tracks liver function and response to treatment
  - Correct if actively bleeding or before invasive procedures
- Liver transplant may be needed

## Intraoperative management
- PPI or H2 antagonist
- Rapid sequence induction
- Drugs:
  - Expect exaggerated CNS effects
  - Sevoflurane and desflurane preferred (little metabolism); TIVA may accumulate
  - Induction: propofol, thiopentone, etomidate
  - Relaxant: atracurium; avoid rocuronium
  - Opioid: remifentanil
  - Avoid paracetamol
- Ascites may need draining
- Treat bleeding aggressively

## Flashcards

  - A: Hepatocellular dysfunction with coagulopathy and encephalopathy in a patient without prior liver disease.

  - A: Within 7 days; 7-28 days; 28 days – 6 months.
