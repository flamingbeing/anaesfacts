# Arthrogryposis Multiplex Congenita

Syndrome of multiple congenital joint contractures; watch for a difficult airway, difficult IV access and myopathy-related risks.

## Background
- Descriptive label for multiple joint contractures present at birth, not one diagnosis
- Many causes; thought to stem from reduced fetal movement (fetal akinesia) early in development
- Cause groups:
  - External mechanical: oligohydramnios, twins, amniotic bands, fibroids
  - Neuropathic
  - Myopathic: central core disease, myotonic dystrophy, congenital myasthenia
  - Abnormal connective tissue
- Commonest subgroup is amyoplasia congenita (hypoplastic muscle)
  - Likely a defect of fetal muscle development
  - Sporadic, no inheritance pattern
  - Limb muscle replaced by fat and fibrous tissue
- Neuropathic forms: denervation atrophy, fewer and smaller anterior horn cells; demyelination of pyramidal tracts and motor roots may occur
- Contractures also occur with other syndromes affecting other systems, e.g. Pierre Robin, Potter, cerebral palsy, Turner syndrome, spina bifida
- May have other congenital anomalies: cardiac, gastrointestinal, genitourinary
- Incidence 1:3000 to 1:10000 live births

## Clinical features
- Often all four limbs involved
- Upper limbs: internally rotated shoulders, extended elbows, flexed wrists and fingers
- Lower limbs more variable; usually severe equinovarus feet
- Hips usually flexed and dislocated; knees hyperextended and dislocated
- Contractures do not progress
- Small, wasted distal muscles with absent reflexes; possible soft tissue webbing
- Thick, tense skin with dimples over joints and absent normal skin creases
- Intelligence usually normal
- Possible associations: micrognathia, congenital heart disease, scoliosis, cleft palate, hypoplastic lungs
- Typical surgery: orthopaedic release of contractures or deformity correction, ENT, oral surgery, diagnostic muscle biopsy

## Anaesthetic considerations
- Airway: possible difficulty from micrognathia, TMJ involvement, trismus, cleft palate, cervical spine disease (scoliosis, Klippel-Feil)
- Respiratory: chronic lung disease from recurrent aspiration (due to dysphagia) and restrictive effect of scoliosis
  - Risk of postoperative atelectasis and pneumonia
- IV access often very difficult (contractures, little subcutaneous tissue)
- Positioning: pad pressure points carefully
- Regional anaesthesia may be hard: positioning and abnormal bony landmarks
- Muscle relaxants:
  - Avoid suxamethonium if a myopathic cause is known (exaggerated hyperkalaemic response)
  - Slightly increased sensitivity to non-depolarising relaxants
- Hypermetabolic responses reported, most likely from an underlying primary neuromuscular disorder
  - Associated myopathy may carry malignant hyperthermia risk
  - Link between AMC and MH is unproven; volatiles are routinely used without MH-type reactions
