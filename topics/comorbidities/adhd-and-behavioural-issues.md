# ADHD and behavioural issues

Plan early, first on the list, low-stimulus setting and premedication; stimulants may raise MAC and interact with sympathomimetics and pro-convulsant drugs.

## Preoperative assessment
- Refer early to anaesthesia
- Identify specific triggers (e.g. sensory overload)

## Management
- First case on a morning list
  - Long waits are poorly tolerated and behaviour on the ward may become disruptive; minimise waiting
- Low-stimulus environment: a quiet waiting room reduces anxiety and difficult behaviour
- Sedative premedication is common; effect is less predictable in children on stimulants
- Distraction while waiting and at induction

### If oral premedication is refused
- Other routes: intranasal, IM
- Change list order: time to calm down or for more preparation with play therapists
- Low threshold to cancel or reschedule elective cases if risk of psychological trauma or physical injury is high

## ADHD
- Most common neurobehavioural disorder of childhood: incidence 5% in schoolchildren
- Inattention, poor impulse control, motor overactivity, restlessness
- More common in boys and lower socio-economic groups
- Multifactorial; about 50% have other behavioural, emotional, learning or language disorders
- Thought to involve too little dopamine and noradrenaline in fronto-subcortical-cerebellar circuits, so inhibitory pathways are understimulated (supported by the response to stimulants)
- Anaesthetic problems resemble those in autism spectrum disorder
- Two main issues: behaviour, and drug interactions with anaesthetic agents

## Medications
### Stimulants
- Methylphenidate: by far the most used drug in ADHD and autism spectrum disorder; blocks dopamine transport, so striatal extracellular dopamine increases
- Dexamphetamine: blocks dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake and inhibits monoamine oxidase
- May increase MAC
- Seizure risk and cardiovascular effects can be worsened by tramadol, pethidine and ephedrine

### Non-stimulants
- Atomoxetine: highly selective presynaptic noradrenaline transporter inhibitor (especially prefrontal cortex)
  - Use sympathomimetics with caution; enhances cardiovascular effects of salbutamol
- Bupropion: selective inhibitor of neuronal catecholamine uptake; same cautions as stimulants (seizure threshold, cardiovascular effects)
- Clonidine: used alone or with stimulants

## Flashcards
- Q: Incidence of ADHD in schoolchildren?
  - A: 5%
- Q: What proportion of children with ADHD have other behavioural, learning or language disorders?
  - A: About 50%
- Q: Which drugs can worsen seizure and cardiovascular effects of stimulants?
  - A: Tramadol, pethidine, ephedrine
