# Acromegaly

Rare syndrome of growth hormone (GH) excess from the anterior pituitary; key concerns are the airway, heart and glucose.

## Preoperative assessment
- History:
  - Raised ICP: morning headache, vomiting
  - Neck stiffness
  - Nausea and vomiting
  - Visual disturbance
  - Growth changes
  - Changed facial appearance
  - Associated conditions: hypertension (30%), ischaemic heart disease, cardiomyopathy, heart failure, valve disease (aortic regurgitation), OSA, diabetes (25%)
- Drugs:
  - Somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide): diarrhoea and vomiting
  - Bromocriptine: long-acting dopamine agonist; can lower GH but may cause severe postural hypotension
- Examination:
  - Airway: large head, jaw, tongue and lips; laryngeal and tracheal hypertrophy leading to vocal cord thickening and laryngeal chondrocalcinosis; OSA; enlarged thyroid with tracheal deviation
  - Cardiovascular: murmurs, blood pressure, heart failure
- Investigations:
  - ECG if indicated
  - Chest X-ray if indicated
  - Echocardiogram for murmurs
  - Blood glucose: 25% have diabetes
  - U&E: electrolyte regulation altered
  - Endocrine axis: GH, TFT, short Synacthen test, LH, FSH
- Planning:
  - Endocrinology input: optimisation and perioperative hormone therapy
  - Neurosurgical input: approach, position, blood loss
  - Glucose monitoring
  - Prehydration

## Intraoperative management
- Large facemasks
- Large laryngoscope blades
- Awake fibreoptic intubation if indicated
- Careful pressure area care
- Protect nerves at particular risk: ulnar at the elbow, median at the wrist, common peroneal at the fibular head
- OSA precautions

## Postoperative care
- ICU or HDU if indicated

## Flashcards
- Q: Airway changes in acromegaly?
  - A: Large jaw, tongue and lips; laryngeal and tracheal hypertrophy with vocal cord thickening and chondrocalcinosis; OSA; goitre with tracheal deviation.
- Q: How common are hypertension and diabetes?
  - A: Hypertension 30%; diabetes 25%.
- Q: Which nerves are at particular risk intraoperatively?
  - A: Ulnar at the elbow, median at the wrist, common peroneal at the fibular head.
- Q: Main side effect of bromocriptine to watch for?
  - A: Severe postural hypotension.
