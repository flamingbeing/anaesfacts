# Miscellaneous Haematological Conditions

Polycythaemia, polycythaemia vera and essential thrombocythaemia (thrombosis and bleeding risk), plus a reminder on porphyria.

## Polycythaemia
- Hb >175 in males, >155 in females, with raised red cell count and haematocrit
- Primary: polycythaemia vera (PV)
- Secondary
  - Appropriate EPO rise
    - Altitude
    - Cardiorespiratory disease: cyanotic heart disease, OSA, methaemoglobinaemia, heavy smoking
  - Inappropriate EPO rise
    - Renal disease: hydronephrosis, cysts, carcinoma
    - Large uterine fibroids
    - Hepatocellular carcinoma
    - Cerebellar haemangioblastoma
- Relative
  - Stress
  - Dehydration or hypovolaemia
  - Burns
  - Enteropathy

## Polycythaemia vera
- History: headache, breathlessness, chest pain, vertigo, pruritus, epigastric pain, hypertension, gout, thrombosis (e.g. retinal)
- Examination: splenomegaly
- Investigations
  - High platelets
  - FBC as above
  - Arterial blood gas
  - Bone marrow aspirate
  - EPO level
  - Genetics: JAK2 mutation in 90-95% of PV
- Treatment
  - Venesection
  - Myelosuppressive drugs: 10% develop myelofibrosis, rarely acute leukaemia
  - Monitor for transformation to myelofibrosis or leukaemia
- Perioperative
  - Venesect to a normal Hb before surgery
  - VTE prophylaxis: compression devices and LMWH

## Essential thrombocythaemia
- Megakaryocyte proliferation with platelets >450
- Closely related to PV
- Recurrent bleeding and thrombosis
- Investigations
  - Film: large abnormal platelets, megakaryocyte fragments
  - Abnormal platelet function tests
- Differential diagnosis of a high platelet count: haemorrhage, chronic infection, malignancy, PV, myelosclerosis, chronic granulocytic leukaemia
- Treatment: hydroxycarbamide

## Porphyria
- Avoid trigger drugs

## Flashcards
- Q: What Hb defines polycythaemia?
  - A: >175 in males, >155 in females
- Q: What proportion of PV patients carry the JAK2 mutation?
  - A: 90-95%
- Q: How should PV be prepared for surgery?
  - A: Venesect to a normal Hb; VTE prophylaxis with compression devices and LMWH
- Q: What platelet count is seen in essential thrombocythaemia?
  - A: >450
