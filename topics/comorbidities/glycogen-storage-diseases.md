# Glycogen Storage Diseases

Inherited enzyme defects causing abnormal glycogen build-up in tissues; key risks are fasting hypoglycaemia, cardiomyopathy, macroglossia and muscle weakness.

## Overview
- Over twelve types, each from a specific enzyme defect
- Only four types cause myopathy
- Often present for liver or muscle biopsy for diagnosis

## Type I (von Gierke disease)
- Severe hypoglycaemia with ketosis and lactic acidosis
- Hyperlipidaemia
- Massive hepatomegaly
- Hyperuricaemia

## Type II (Pompe disease)
- Mainly affects heart (cardiomegaly) and skeletal muscle (hypotonia, respiratory distress)
- Associated with macroglossia
- Infants: hypotonia, failure to thrive, cardiomyopathy
- Death usually by age 1-2 from cardiac or respiratory failure
- Later-onset form: progressive myopathy, death around puberty
- Diagnosis: muscle biopsy or enzyme defect found in leucocytes

## Type III (Forbes disease)
- Like type I but milder
- Milder hypoglycaemia; lactate normal
- Hepatomegaly
- Cardiomyopathy and myopathy later in life

## Type IV (Andersen disease)
- Hepatomegaly with progressive cirrhosis
  - Death from liver failure, usually by age 2
- Muscle weakness
- Cardiomegaly

## Type V (McArdle disease)
- Myophosphorylase deficiency: glycogen cannot be broken down
- Presents in adolescence with muscle cramps and pain after exercise
- Myoglobinuria possible after heavy exercise
- Otherwise normal, well-developed patient
- Diagnosis: muscle biopsy and enzyme assay

## Types VI and VII
- Type VI resembles type I
- Type VII resembles type V

## Anaesthetic management
- Fasting hypoglycaemia in types I, III and IV: give glucose-containing IV fluids while fasted
- Check for cardiac involvement: ECG and echocardiogram
- Macroglossia may make laryngoscopy difficult
- Probably best to avoid suxamethonium (theoretical hyperkalaemia risk)
- Often sensitive to non-depolarising muscle relaxants: monitor neuromuscular function
- McArdle disease: avoid limb tourniquets (risk of myoglobinuria)
- Pompe disease: poor respiratory reserve; may need postoperative ventilation

## Flashcards
- Q: Which glycogen storage disease types cause fasting hypoglycaemia, and how is it managed?
  - A: Types I, III and IV; give glucose-containing IV fluids while fasting.
- Q: Why avoid limb tourniquets in McArdle disease?
  - A: Risk of myoglobinuria.
- Q: Which glycogen storage disease features macroglossia, cardiomegaly and poor respiratory reserve?
  - A: Type II (Pompe disease).
