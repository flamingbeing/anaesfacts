# Cushing's Syndrome

Syndrome caused by excess plasma cortisol.

## Causes
- Iatrogenic steroid administration (commonest)
- Of the remainder:
  - Pituitary adenoma (80%)
  - Ectopic ACTH, e.g. small cell carcinoma (15%)
  - Adrenal adenoma (4%) or carcinoma (rare)

## Clinical features
- Appearance: moon face, truncal obesity, proximal myopathy
- Tissues: osteoporosis, easy bruising, thin skin
- Comorbidities: diabetes, OSA, GORD, peptic ulcer disease
- Cardiovascular: hypertension (80%), LVH, IHD

## Investigations
- Diagnosis:
  - Dexamethasone suppression test (8 mg)
  - ACTH level:
    - Normal or high suggests pituitary cause
    - Low suggests adrenal cause or exogenous cortisol
    - Very high suggests ectopic ACTH
  - Plasma cortisol high, with loss of diurnal variation
  - Urinary 17-OH-steroids raised
- Associated findings:
  - Bloods: sodium raised, potassium raised, calcium low
  - Bicarbonate (HCO3-) raised
- ECG: high-voltage QRS, inverted T waves
  - Hard to tell apart from IHD but directly due to Cushing's

## Preoperative management
- Treat the cause
- Diabetes care
- Perioperative steroid supplementation if indicated

## Intraoperative management
- Difficult venous access
- Careful airway assessment
- Peptic ulcer prophylaxis
- Monitor electrolytes
- Careful pressure area care

## Flashcards
- Q: Commonest cause of Cushing's syndrome?
  - A: Iatrogenic steroid administration.
- Q: What does a very high ACTH level suggest?
  - A: Ectopic ACTH secretion (e.g. small cell carcinoma).
- Q: What proportion of patients with Cushing's are hypertensive?
  - A: About 80%.
- Q: Name three intraoperative concerns in Cushing's.
  - A: Difficult veins, airway assessment, pressure areas (also peptic ulcer prophylaxis and electrolytes).
