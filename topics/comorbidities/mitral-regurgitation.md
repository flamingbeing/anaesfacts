# Mitral Regurgitation and Mitral Valve Prolapse

Haemodynamic goal for MR: "fast, full, forward": high-normal heart rate, normal preload, low afterload.

## Severity
- Graded by the 2020 AHA valvular heart disease criteria

## Preoperative assessment
- Asymptomatic with functional capacity < 4 METs
  - Mitral valve replacement before elective surgery
- Asymptomatic with good effort tolerance
  - Proceed with perioperative care guided by haemodynamic goals
- Mixed mitral disease (MR with mitral stenosis): manage the dominant lesion
- Symptomatic severe MR meeting criteria for valve intervention: intervene before elective non-cardiac surgery (2024 AHA)

## Preoperative management
- Continue antiarrhythmics
- Switch warfarin to heparin bridging
- Titrate premedication carefully
  - Opioid-induced hypoventilation and hypercarbia can raise PVR
  - Venodilation can reduce preload
  - Avoid stress-induced rises in SVR

## Intraoperative monitoring
- 5-lead ECG with standard ASA monitoring (NIBP, SpO2)
- Arterial line before induction
- CVP line to guide fluids
- PA catheter to guide fluids

## Anaesthetic technique
- Central neuraxial blocks are well tolerated

## Haemodynamic goals
- Heart rate high-normal, 80-90 bpm
- Keep sinus rhythm
- Normal preload
- Low SVR and low PVR
  - Treat hypotension with fluid rather than vasoconstrictors
- Normal contractility

## Cardiopulmonary bypass
- Consider afterload reduction, with drugs or an IABP

## Mitral valve prolapse
- Perioperative management is similar to MR

## Flashcards
- Q: Target heart rate in MR?
  - A: High-normal, 80-90 bpm, in sinus rhythm.
- Q: Preferred first treatment of hypotension in MR?
  - A: Fluids rather than vasoconstrictors.
- Q: Asymptomatic MR with functional capacity < 4 METs before elective surgery?
  - A: Mitral valve replacement first.
- Q: Why titrate opioid premedication carefully in MR?
  - A: Hypoventilation and hypercarbia raise PVR; venodilation reduces preload.
