# Breastfeeding Women

Breastfeeding can continue after anaesthesia ("sleep and keep"): drug transfer to milk is minimal, so use opioid-sparing multimodal or regional analgesia and support feeding.

## Key points
- Pumping and discarding milk for 24hrs is not needed (ASA); "sleep and keep" is now advised
- Simple analgesics are safe; regional techniques preferred
  - Fentanyl and morphine safe
  - Avoid aspirin; codeine, tramadol and oxycodone best avoided (see cautions below)
- Support breastfeeding

## Risks of continuing: drug transfer
- All anaesthetic and analgesic drugs reach breast milk, but in tiny, clinically insignificant amounts
- Concerns
  - Just after delivery, gaps between milk gland cells are wide open
  - Immature infant organs may clear drugs poorly and accumulate them
- Relative infant dose (RID): infant dose via milk (mg/kg/day) divided by maternal dose (mg/kg/day)
  - < 10% is considered safe
  - Almost all anaesthetic drugs are well under 10%; morphine is about 9% but has an excellent safety record
- Opioids and their metabolites pass into milk a little more: use an opioid-sparing technique

## Risks of stopping
- Mastitis
- Infant dehydration
- Formula exposure

## Infant factors
- Infants at high risk of apnoea (e.g. premature): caution with opioids
- Duct-dependent cardiac lesion: avoid NSAIDs

## Supporting breastfeeding
- Pain hinders breastfeeding: patients should not avoid analgesia after surgery
- Avoid dehydration
- Day surgery where possible

## Preoperative assessment
- Ask every patient with a child < 2 years about breastfeeding
- Assess the infant's apnoea risk (e.g. prematurity)
- First on the list to shorten fasting and separation from the baby
- Keep fasting short: dehydration reduces milk supply

## Intraoperative management
- LactMed is a useful drug reference
- Most anaesthetic drugs are compatible
- Multimodal opioid-sparing and locoregional analgesia preferred
  - Morphine is the preferred long-acting opioid
  - Use with caution
    - Tramadol: watch the baby for unusual drowsiness
    - Oxycodone: more drowsiness at doses > 30 mg/day
    - Codeine: watch the baby for unusual drowsiness
    - Pethidine: watch the baby for unusual drowsiness
  - Contraindicated: aspirin (Reye's syndrome in the infant)
- Sugammadex reduces hormonal contraceptive efficacy: advise barrier methods for 7 days

## Postoperative care
- Breastfeed: resume as soon as alert and physically able; the old 24hrs pump-and-dump advice no longer applies
- Opioids are safe; watch the baby for
  - Unusual sleepiness, hard to wake for feeds
  - Weaker sucking
  - Constipation
- Use an effective antiemetic strategy, including prophylaxis if needed

## Flashcards
- Q: Is pump-and-dump needed after anaesthesia?
  - A: No; "sleep and keep": resume feeding once alert and able
- Q: What relative infant dose is considered safe?
  - A: < 10%; morphine is about 9% with an excellent safety record
- Q: Which analgesic is contraindicated while breastfeeding and why?
  - A: Aspirin: risk of Reye's syndrome in the infant
- Q: Contraception advice after sugammadex?
  - A: Barrier methods for 7 days
- Q: Oxycodone dose above which infant drowsiness is more likely?
  - A: > 30 mg/day
