# Hypophosphataemia

Low serum phosphate from intracellular shift, poor intake, bone uptake or renal loss; treat the cause and replace orally where possible.

## Definition
- Serum phosphate below 2.4 mg/dL (0.85 mmol/L)

## Causes
- Shift into cells
  - Recovery from metabolic acidosis
  - Respiratory alkalosis
  - Refeeding after starvation (insulin driven)
- Inadequate intake
  - Starvation
  - Malabsorption (diarrhoea, steatorrhoea)
  - Antacid use
  - Alcoholism
- Excess skeletal uptake
  - Mineralisation
  - Osteoblastic metastases
  - After parathyroidectomy ("hungry bone syndrome")
- Renal losses
  - Hyperparathyroidism
  - Diuretics
  - X-linked or autosomal dominant hypophosphataemic rickets
  - Fanconi syndrome
  - Multiple myeloma

## Clinical features
- Non-specific: heart failure, coma, hypotension, weakness, defective clotting

## Treatment
- Treat the underlying cause
- Oral phosphate (PO43-): 2-4 g/day in 2 to 4 divided doses (bid-qid); start at 1 g/day to limit diarrhoea
- IV phosphate: only if severely symptomatic or oral therapy not tolerated
