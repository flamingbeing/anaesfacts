# Nonobstetric Surgery in Pregnancy

Two patients: protect the mother (airway, aspiration, aortocaval compression) and the fetus (teratogenicity, uteroplacental flow, preterm labour).

## Summary
- Mother
  - Difficult airway: difficult intubation, aspiration, desaturation
  - Aortocaval compression
- Fetus (second patient)
  - Teratogenicity: drugs, radiation
  - Keep uteroplacental blood flow; avoid fetal acidosis and hypoxaemia; monitor fetal heart rate
  - Preterm labour
- Regional preferred
- Multidisciplinary: involve obstetricians and neonatologists
- Timing: 6 weeks postpartum, or 2nd trimester

## Common procedures
- Appendicectomy, laparoscopic cholecystectomy, breast biopsy, trauma surgery

## Risks
- Fetus
  - Pregnancy loss in early pregnancy
  - Preterm labour in late pregnancy
    - Highest with abdominal and pelvic surgery
    - Preoperative progesterone to prevent procedure-related preterm labour has not been studied
  - Teratogenicity, neurotoxicity
  - Ionising radiation
- Mother: usual pregnancy issues
  - Difficult airway and aspiration
  - Aortocaval compression
  - Reduced lung compliance and FRC

## Preoperative management
- Defer elective surgery
  - Elective: wait until 6 weeks postpartum (physiology returns to non-pregnant in 2–6 weeks)
  - Semi-elective: delay to the second trimester
    - Teratogenic risk is highest with first-trimester drugs
    - Preterm labour risk is highest in the third trimester
- Counsel on risks
- Multidisciplinary team with obstetrics and neonatology (possible emergency delivery or fetal compromise)
  - Arrange perioperative CTG
- Premedication: sodium citrate

## Choice of technique
- Regional anaesthesia preferred
  - Less fetal drug exposure, less maternal airway handling, some postoperative analgesia

## Airway
- Expect a difficult airway
- Aspiration risk under GA: RSI or awake intubation
  - Many anaesthetists routinely do RSI beyond 16 weeks (15–18 weeks) gestation
  - Ask about reflux symptoms

## Circulation
- Aortocaval compression and supine hypotensive syndrome may start in the second trimester
- Left lateral tilt for anyone beyond 18-20 weeks gestation

## Ventilation
- Increase minute ventilation to match the normal respiratory alkalosis of pregnancy
- Reduced lung compliance and FRC

## Fetal considerations
- Keep uteroplacental blood flow
  - Maintain MAP; avoid aortocaval compression
  - Avoid vasoconstriction from sympathetic stimulation, pain, alkalosis, hypothermia
- Avoid hypoxia
- Teratogenicity: highest risk in first trimester, but no current anaesthetic is a proven human teratogen at clinical doses
- Minimise image intensifier use; cover the uterus with a lead gown
- Monitor fetal heart rate and uterine activity
  - CTG or Doppler, before and after surgery
  - Consider continuous intraoperative CTG (discuss with obstetrics)
  - CTG may look flat straight after GA because of anaesthetic drugs

## Drugs
- Induction
  - Propofol and thiopental: safe
  - Ketamine: avoid in 1st/2nd trimester (raises uterine tone); safe in 3rd (e.g. haemorrhage)
  - Etomidate: avoid (adrenal suppression)
  - Midazolam: single dose for anxiety is safe
  - High-dose maternal diazepam has some link to IUGR and cleft palate
- Volatile agents: good (tocolytic)
- N2O: avoid in 1st and 2nd trimester (effect on DNA synthesis)
- Muscle relaxants: safe
  - Suxamethonium duration varies: larger Vd but lower pseudocholinesterase activity
- Reversal: give atropine with neostigmine
  - Atropine crosses the placenta and counters neostigmine-induced fetal bradycardia; glycopyrrolate does not cross
- Analgesia: multimodal, regional best
  - Paracetamol safest
  - Opioids cross the placenta, but short courses are fine
  - Avoid NSAIDs

## Other medications
- Antibiotics
  - Safe: penicillins and other beta-lactams; clindamycin or vancomycin if penicillin allergic
  - Avoid: tetracyclines (tooth staining, bone growth), quinolones (arthropathy), aminoglycosides (ototoxicity)
- Antihypertensives
  - Safe: labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine
  - Avoid: ACE inhibitors and ARBs (renal dysgenesis), atenolol (IUGR)
- Anticoagulants
  - Heparin and LMWH: safe (do not cross placenta)
  - Warfarin: teratogenic (fetal warfarin syndrome)
- Cardiac: avoid amiodarone (fetal goitre and hypothyroidism)
- Calf pumps for DVT risk

## FDA pregnancy categories: examples
- A: multivitamins
- B: paracetamol; lidocaine; NSAIDs in first trimester
- C
  - Opioids: codeine, butorphanol, fentanyl, hydrocodone, levorphanol, methadone, morphine, oxycodone, oxymorphone
  - Antidepressants: fluoxetine; tricyclics amitriptyline, imipramine
  - Anticonvulsant: gabapentin
  - Antimigraine: metoprolol, propranolol, sumatriptan, nifedipine, verapamil
- D
  - NSAIDs in 3rd trimester
  - Steroids: cortisone
  - Anticonvulsants: diazepam, phenobarbital, phenytoin
- E
  - Antimigraine: ergotamine
  - Antidepressants: paroxetine
  - Anticonvulsants: valproic acid

## ADEC (Australian) pregnancy categories
- A: taken by a limited number of pregnant women without more malformations or fetal harm
- B1: animal studies show no increase in damage
- B2: animal studies inadequate or lacking, no evidence of damage
- B3: animal studies show damage, human significance uncertain
- C: harmful (or suspected harmful) fetal or neonatal effects without malformation; may be reversible
- D: have caused, are suspected of causing, or may be expected to cause more fetal malformations or irreversible damage
- X: high risk of permanent fetal damage; do not use in pregnancy or possible pregnancy

## Flashcards
- Q: Best timing for surgery during pregnancy?
  - A: Elective: 6 weeks postpartum; semi-elective: 2nd trimester
- Q: From what gestation is left lateral tilt needed?
  - A: Beyond 18-20 weeks
- Q: From what gestation do many anaesthetists perform RSI?
  - A: Beyond 16 weeks (15–18 weeks)
- Q: Which anticholinergic should be paired with neostigmine in pregnancy, and why?
  - A: Atropine: it crosses the placenta to counter fetal bradycardia; glycopyrrolate does not
- Q: When should ketamine be avoided in pregnancy?
  - A: 1st and 2nd trimester (raises uterine tone)
