# Anaesthesia After Renal Transplant

Protect the graft, keep immunosuppression going and avoid pressure on the kidney.

## Graft function
- Creatinine may be normal, yet renal function and creatinine clearance are not
- A transplanted kidney never works fully and has only half the nephrons
- Immunosuppression (e.g. ciclosporin) reduces function further

## Immunosuppression and steroids
- Continue immunosuppression
- Strict asepsis
- Watch for complications of immunosuppression
- Stress-dose steroids
- Leukoreduced, with or without irradiated, red cells

## Protecting the graft
- Avoid hypovolaemia and hypotension
- Avoid nephrotoxic drugs; no NSAIDs

## Positioning
- Graft sits superficially in the abdomen
- Avoid pressure from positioning (e.g. prone) or supports

## Flashcards
- Q: Does a normal creatinine mean normal graft function after renal transplant?
  - A: No; the graft has only half the nephrons and clearance is reduced.
- Q: Which analgesic class should be avoided after renal transplant?
  - A: NSAIDs (and other nephrotoxic drugs).
- Q: What blood product modification is used for renal transplant recipients?
  - A: Leukoreduced red cells, with or without irradiation.
- Q: Why take care with prone positioning?
  - A: The graft lies superficially in the abdomen and can be compressed.
