# Cancer and chemotherapy

Think of the 4 Ms in cancer patients (mass, metastases, metabolic, medications) and know the organ toxicity of each chemotherapy class.

## The 4 Ms
- Mass: airway, SVC, stomach
- Metastases: brain (raised ICP), lung (effusion)
- Metabolic: SIADH, hypercalcaemia
- Medications: chemotherapy toxicity, steroids

## Preoperative assessment
- Assess the effects of the disease itself and the harms of its treatment
- Do not overlook other comorbidities
- Correct malnutrition, electrolyte problems, anaemia and coagulopathy as needed
- DNR orders may exist: reasonable to modify or suspend them perioperatively (goals of care discussion)

## Intraoperative management
- Airway: head, neck and anterior mediastinal tumours can compromise the airway; previous radiotherapy can cause airway problems not obvious on examination
- Technique: opioids and volatiles may suppress immunity; some evidence that regional anaesthesia and TIVA may lower recurrence
- Paraneoplastic syndromes (e.g. myasthenia gravis, Eaton-Lambert syndrome) can alter responses to muscle relaxants
- Avoid dexamethasone as an antiemetic: may be cytotoxic and can trigger tumour lysis
- Difficult IV access: veins damaged by chemotherapy, lymphoedema

## Postoperative management
- Pain: many are already on opioids for cancer pain, so adjust doses for tolerance
- VTE prophylaxis: VTE affects at least 15% of cancer patients

## Chemotherapy: general issues
- Myelosuppression with pancytopenia (most agents)
- GI: nausea, diarrhoea, mucositis (oral mucositis may bleed with intubation)
- Poor wound healing
- Friable veins for cannulation
- Timing: neoadjuvant (before resection), adjuvant (after resection), palliative
- Regimens: usually drug combinations; cycles every 2-3 weeks for 3-6 months
- Assessment: know the drugs and ask about side effects; FBC, U&E, LFT; ECG, echo, CXR, lung function tests and ABG as needed

## Key toxicities
- Bleomycin: pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial oedema
- Anthracyclines (doxorubicin): cardiotoxic
- Platinum agents (cisplatin): nephrotoxic
- Vincristine: peripheral neuropathy
- Steroids: adrenal suppression
- Methotrexate: nitrous oxide may worsen its toxicity, so avoid

## Drug classes
1. Anthracyclines
2. Alkylating agents
3. Antimetabolites
4. Antibodies
5. Tumour antibiotics
6. Topoisomerase inhibitors
7. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors
8. Mitotic inhibitors
9. Mitotic stabilisers
10. Hormone agents

### Anthracyclines (doxorubicin, daunorubicin)
- Intercalate into and cleave double-stranded DNA
- Cardiomyopathy: cumulative and dose dependent
  - Get a preoperative TTE
  - Even with normal function after chemotherapy, more sensitive to myocardial depression from anaesthetic agents
  - Risk factors: extremes of age, mediastinal radiotherapy, existing heart disease, concurrent cyclophosphamide or mitomycin

### Alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide, cisplatin)
- Alkylate DNA and disrupt mitosis
- Platinum drugs (cisplatin, carboplatin, oxaliplatin)
  - Nephrotoxic: avoid other nephrotoxins (e.g. NSAIDs), maintain urine output
  - Ototoxicity, peripheral neuropathy
- Cyclophosphamide: metabolite acrolein causes haemorrhagic cystitis and bladder cancer; lung toxicity
- Ifosfamide: Fanconi syndrome; metabolite chloroacetaldehyde is nephrotoxic

### Antibodies
- Trastuzumab (Herceptin): breast cancer; can cause cardiomyopathy
- Rituximab: non-Hodgkin lymphoma and RA; flu-like effects

### Antimetabolites (methotrexate, 5-FU)
- Corrupt DNA synthesis
- Mainly myelosuppression and GI effects
- High-dose methotrexate: AKI from crystals precipitating in the distal tubule
- Avoid nitrous oxide: it inhibits methionine synthase and adds to methotrexate toxicity

### Tumour antibiotics (bleomycin)
- Pneumonitis (hypersensitivity), worse with high FiO2, leading to pulmonary fibrosis
  - Mechanism unclear: possibly superoxide generation and neutrophil recruitment overwhelming lung antioxidant defences
- Interstitial pulmonary oedema, probably from impaired lung lymphatic drainage: titrate fluids carefully, consider ABG
- Worsened by high oxygen: keep FiO2 less than 30%
- Risk factors: older age, renal impairment, lung radiotherapy, IV route, oxygen exposure, high cumulative dose
- Treat with high-dose steroids

### Topoisomerase inhibitors (etoposide)
- Block DNA unwinding during replication
- Myelosuppression, anorexia, nausea

### Tyrosine kinase inhibitors
- Newer agents; switch off intracellular signalling
- Hypertension, rash, GI upset, myelosuppression, liver dysfunction

### Mitotic inhibitors (vincristine)
- Stop microtubule formation, arresting cell growth
- Peripheral neuropathy, constipation
- Vincristine-induced peripheral neuropathy affects sensory, motor and autonomic nerves; a common cause of neuropathic pain referral

### Mitotic stabilisers (docetaxel, paclitaxel)
- Lock formed microtubules so mitosis cannot proceed
- Myelosuppression, peripheral neuropathy

### Hormone agents
- Change the hormonal environment; side effects vary
- Corticosteroids: tumour lysis
- Tamoxifen: breast cancer
- LHRH analogues: prostate cancer

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the 4 Ms of cancer for anaesthesia?
  - A: Mass, metastases, metabolic, medications
- Q: What FiO2 limit is advised after bleomycin?
  - A: Keep FiO2 less than 30%
- Q: Why avoid nitrous oxide with methotrexate?
  - A: It inhibits methionine synthase and worsens methotrexate toxicity
- Q: What proportion of cancer patients develop VTE?
  - A: At least 15%
- Q: Which preoperative test after anthracyclines?
  - A: TTE (cumulative, dose-dependent cardiomyopathy)
