# Hypoparathyroidism

Underactive parathyroid glands, causing low calcium; correct calcium before theatre and watch it after surgery.

## Causes
- After parathyroidectomy
- Radiotherapy to the neck
- Idiopathic

## Preoperative assessment
- History
  - Carpopedal spasm
  - Tetany
- Examination
  - Chvostek and Trousseau signs positive
  - Hypotension
- Investigations
  - Dysrhythmias
  - Low corrected calcium (below 2.2 mmol/l)
    - After parathyroidectomy, calcium reaches its lowest at about 20 hours and normalises by day 2-3
  - Low PTH
  - ECG: prolonged PR and QT intervals

## Preoperative management
- Calcium gluconate 10% 10 ml IV over 10 min
- Replace magnesium
- Vitamin D
- Oral calcium
- Correct before theatre

## Intraoperative management
- Monitor neuromuscular blockade

## Postoperative management
- Monitor calcium

## Flashcards
- Q: When does calcium reach its lowest after parathyroidectomy?
  - A: About 20 hours; normalises by day 2-3
- Q: What IV calcium dose is used to treat symptomatic hypocalcaemia?
  - A: Calcium gluconate 10% 10 ml over 10 min
- Q: What ECG changes occur in hypoparathyroidism?
  - A: Prolonged PR and QT intervals
- Q: What two clinical signs suggest hypocalcaemia?
  - A: Chvostek and Trousseau signs
