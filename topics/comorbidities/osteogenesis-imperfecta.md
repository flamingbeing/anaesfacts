# Osteogenesis Imperfecta

Inherited type I collagen disorder with fragile bones: handle, position and manage the airway gently, monitor temperature and watch for platelet dysfunction.

## Background

- Abnormal type I collagen from mutations in the pro-collagen genes on chromosomes 7 and 17
- Hallmark: bone fragility
- Also affects skin, fasciae, ligaments, tendons, sclera and inner ear
- Four main types classified (other variants probably exist)
  - Types I and IV: autosomal dominant, milder
  - Types II and III: autosomal recessive, fatal or progressive

## Clinical features

### Type I
- Commonest (80%)
- Pathological fractures in toddlers or older children, mostly lower limb; can be mistaken for non-accidental injury
- Spinal deformity from osteoporotic vertebral collapse; fractures may become less frequent after puberty
- Blue sclera
- Hypotonia, hypermobile joints that may dislocate, hernias, thin atrophic skin
- Early deafness from otosclerosis is common (50% of adults)
- Aortic regurgitation and mitral valve prolapse may occur
- Dentinogenesis imperfecta: teeth bluish, yellow or translucent
- Height often normal

### Type II
- Severe skeletal abnormality; death in utero or as a neonate

### Type III
- Progressive, severe skeletal abnormality; fractures from birth and through infancy
- Chest deformity (kyphoscoliosis, prominent sternum) predisposes to recurrent bronchopneumonia
- Sclera often normal; short stature; macrocephaly
- Untreated, death from cardiorespiratory failure usually in the third or fourth decade
- Pamidronate in severely affected children (under 3 years) increases bone mineral density, eases pain and immobility and cuts fracture rate

### Type IV
- Like type I
- Fractures at birth in about 25%; frequent in childhood, fewer after puberty
- Fractures heal with bowing; long bone deformity and kyphoscoliosis worse than in type 1
- Short stature, pointed mandible, midface hypoplasia
- Sclera normal; no deafness
- Skull deformity may cause basilar compression

## Anaesthetic considerations

- Common operations: fracture reduction, scoliosis surgery, neurosurgery, middle ear surgery, dental surgery
- Positioning
  - Handle carefully and pad well to avoid new fractures
  - Large misshapen heads often need a pillow under the chest
- Airway
  - Be gentle: risk of mandible fracture, vertebral fracture from neck extension, and fragile teeth
  - Midface hypoplasia and micrognathia make face mask choice important
- Respiratory: severe kyphoscoliosis gives a restrictive defect and awkward positioning
- Cardiac: valve lesions may need antibiotic prophylaxis
- Temperature
  - Hypermetabolic state: mild hyperthermia, sweating and acidosis under anaesthesia
  - Delay surgery if pyrexial
  - Intraoperative pyrexia, acidosis and hypercarbia reported; thought not to be malignant hyperthermia; usually settles with active cooling
  - Monitor temperature
- Suxamethonium fasciculations can cause fractures
- Haematology
  - Platelet dysfunction possible despite a normal count
  - Easy bruising: investigate before major surgery or central neuraxial block

- Q: What is the molecular defect in osteogenesis imperfecta?
  - A: Abnormal type I collagen from mutations in pro-collagen genes on chromosomes 7 and 17
