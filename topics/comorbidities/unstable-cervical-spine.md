# Unstable Cervical Spine and Cervical Myelopathy

Airway management is the main concern: limit neck movement and protect the cord through intubation and positioning.

## Quick summary
- Cervical spondylosis: limit neck extension (LMA or videolaryngoscope)
- Unstable cervical spine: videolaryngoscopy with manual in-line stabilisation (MILS), or awake fibreoptic intubation and self-positioning

## Airway management
- Airway is the first perioperative concern
- Plan from history, examination, imaging and discussion with the surgeon
- Direct laryngoscopy is reasonable if neck movement (especially extension) does not worsen neurology, the spine is stable and the airway is otherwise normal
- Consider a videolaryngoscope, or an assistant holding the neck neutral
- If laryngoscopy could threaten the cord: awake fibreoptic intubation, then a quick neurological check once the tube is in

## Positioning
- If positioning could worsen an unstable injury, check evoked potentials (especially motor) before and after positioning
- If so, choose an anaesthetic that allows monitoring

## Investigations
- Cervical spine flexion and extension X-rays

## Flashcards
- Q: Airway options for an unstable cervical spine?
  - A: Videolaryngoscopy with MILS, or awake fibreoptic intubation (with self-positioning)
- Q: What check follows awake fibreoptic intubation in cervical spine disease?
  - A: A brief neurological examination once the tube is placed
- Q: How can cord injury during positioning be detected?
  - A: Evoked potentials (especially motor) before and after positioning
