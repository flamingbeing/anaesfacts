# Perioperative Hypertension and Hypotensive Anaesthesia

High BP under anaesthesia: check the reading, depth, analgesia and IV line first, then treat the cause before reaching for antihypertensives.

## Quick checks
- Depth, analgesia, IV cannula site
- Surgical: tourniquet pain, adrenaline, vasopressin
- Hypervolaemia

## Intraoperative causes
- **Light anaesthesia with surgical stimulus**: deepen with volatile or a propofol bolus
  - Check **MAC** and for an empty vaporiser
  - Check **IV cannula site** and infusion pumps, especially with TIVA
- **Pain**: give an opioid such as fentanyl or remifentanil
  - **Watch what the surgeons are doing**; check when analgesia was last given
  - **Tourniquet pain**: "cuff hypertension", hard and resistant to treatment
- Other **sympathetic** stimuli: **hypercarbia**, **hypoxia**, bladder distension
  - **Drugs**: **adrenaline** in local anaesthetic, vasopressin
- **Hypervolaemia**
  - Fluid overload from IV fluid or blood products
  - Surgical: **TURP syndrome** and similar
  - Can cause pulmonary oedema and heart failure in cardiac patients
- Raised intracranial pressure
- Thyroid storm
- Pre-existing essential or secondary hypertension: may stay markedly hypertensive despite adequate anaesthesia
- **Measurement error**: arterial transducer at wrong level, wrong cuff size

## Intraoperative management
- **Confirm the BP is real**: check cuff placement
- **Deepen anaesthesia or give opioid while finding the cause**
  - Consider midazolam if awareness is suspected
- If depth and pain are unlikely causes, or deepening is not enough or not practical, give **antihypertensives**:
  - Esmolol 10–20 mg IV
  - Labetalol 5–10 mg IV
  - Hydralazine 5–10 mg IV
  - Occasionally, in severe cases: GTN 50 mcg IV or nitroprusside 50 mcg IV, as boluses or infusions

## Hypotensive anaesthesia (deliberate hypotension)
- Definition: MAP **55-65 mmHg** in normotensive patients, or a 20-30% fall from baseline
- Benefits
  - **Better surgical field** from less bleeding
  - **Less blood loss and transfusion**
- Indications
  - **Spine surgery**
  - **FESS, middle ear surgery**
- Harms
  - Lower cerebral and spinal cord perfusion pressure
  - Lower diastolic pressure, so lower coronary perfusion pressure
  - Lower renal and other end-organ perfusion
- Contraindications
  - **Ischaemic heart disease, stroke**
  - **Uncontrolled hypertension** (autoregulation curve shifted right)
- Methods
  - Deeper anaesthesia
  - **Remifentanil**
  - Alpha-2 agonists: clonidine, **dexmedetomidine**
  - Magnesium
  - Beta blockers: esmolol, labetalol
  - Vasodilators: GTN, sodium nitroprusside
- Monitoring
  - **Urine output, ECG**
  - **Arterial line** for close BP monitoring and lactate as a perfusion marker
  - NIRS

## Hypertension in recovery (PACU)
### Causes
- Pre-existing essential or secondary hypertension
- Noxious stimuli
  - **Pain**
  - **Nausea and vomiting**
  - **Acute urinary retention**
  - Delirium or agitation
- **Hypercarbia**
- Hypothermia with **shivering**
- **Fluid overload**

### Assessment
- Exclude hypertensive emergency and other urgent reasons to lower BP (e.g. neurosurgery, pre-eclampsia)
  - Neurological: **GCS, limb power, headache**
  - Cardiac: **chest pain, breathlessness**
- **Pain**
- **Full bladder**
- Intraoperative and **preoperative BP**
- Adequacy of breathing

### Management
- Analgesia, antiemetics
- Bladder catheterisation
- Antihypertensives
  - **IV labetalol 5-10mg** boluses, 10-15 min between doses
  - **IV hydralazine 4-8mg** boluses, 20 min between doses
  - Choose dose by the BP fall wanted and how fragile the patient is
  - Oral nifedipine or amlodipine
  - If the usual **morning antihypertensive** was missed, consider giving it

## Hypertension on the ward or ICU
### First steps
- Confirm BP: compare non-invasive and arterial readings
- Look for triggers, e.g. pain
- Exclude end-organ damage: headache, neurological signs, chest pain
- Is it safe to start antihypertensives (recent hypotension)?
- **Check the BP target**

### Hypertensive urgency (BP >180/120 without target organ damage)
- Do not lower BP too fast: aim over hours to days
- **Give usual antihypertensives early** if near the due time
- Oral or nasogastric options
  - **Amlodipine 2.5-5mg every morning, up to 10mg every morning**
  - Enalapril **5mg every morning to 20mg twice daily**
  - Bisoprolol
  - Carvedilol
  - Hydralazine
  - Prazosin
- IV options
  - Labetalol
  - Hydralazine 4-8mg boluses
- Continuous IV: labetalol infusion
- Surgical ICU patients
  - Propofol bolus
  - Fentanyl bolus
  - Start dexmedetomidine

### Hypertensive emergency
- Target: initial MAP reduction of **~25% over an hour**
- End-organ damage
  - Neurological: infarct, bleed, encephalopathy
  - Cardiovascular: acute MI, acute pulmonary oedema
  - Aortic dissection: pulse delay
  - Renal: AKI
  - Malignant hypertension: papilloedema
  - Pre-eclampsia or eclampsia
- History: **chest pain, breathlessness**, **blurred vision, headache**
- Examination
  - ABC, vital signs, **manual BP** on both arms, GCS
  - **Neurological**: altered mental state, focal deficits
  - **Cardiovascular**: JVP, lung crackles, pedal oedema, pulse delay
  - Fundoscopy
- Orders
  - **Inform a senior**
  - **ECG, cardiac enzymes**
  - **Urea, electrolytes, creatinine**
  - CT brain if GCS falls
  - CT aortogram if new aortic regurgitation
  - **Monitor** hourly vital signs and GCS; treat complications
    - Arrange **high dependency or ICU transfer**
- Drugs (depending on the condition)
  - **IV GTN infusion** 5mcg/min up to 100mcg/min
  - **IV hydralazine** 10mg bolus (up to 20mg), then oral 50mg three times daily
  - IV labetalol 20mg bolus, then 20 to 80mg every 10 min, or 0.5 to 2 mg/min infusion
- Later: consider a young-hypertension work-up (plasma renin and aldosterone, renal ultrasound, 24hr urinary catecholamines or metanephrines)
