# Hypothyroidism

Hypodynamic circulation, hypoventilation, drug sensitivity, hypothermia and hypoglycaemia; continue levothyroxine and postpone elective surgery if overt or severe.

## Key problems
- Myocardial depression, cardiac failure
- Hypoventilation
- Increased drug sensitivity, delayed emergence
- Hypoglycaemia, hypothermia

## Severity
- Severe
  - Myxoedema coma, or severe features of chronic hypothyroidism (altered mentation, pericardial effusion, heart failure)
  - Or very low total T4 (e.g. below 1.0 mcg/dL) or free T4 (e.g. below 0.5 ng/dL)
- Moderate
  - Any other overt hypothyroidism (raised TSH, low free T4) without severe features
- Mild
  - Subclinical: normal free T4 with raised TSH

## Clinical features relevant to anaesthesia
- CNS
  - Lethargy, so less premedication needed
  - Myxoedema coma, reduced consciousness
- Cardiovascular
  - Hypodynamic circulation: low cardiac output and hypotension (slow heart rate, weak contractility)
  - Sensitive to myocardial depression
  - Expect haemodynamic instability; consider an arterial line
- Ventilation
  - Tends to hypoventilate (weak respiratory muscles, blunted responses to hypoxia and hypercapnia); controlled ventilation recommended
- Airway
  - Delayed gastric emptying, so higher regurgitation risk
- GI
  - Constipation and ileus (reduced gut motility)
- Metabolic
  - Anaemia
  - Hyponatraemia (less free water clearance)
  - Hypoglycaemia
  - Hypothermia
  - Adrenal insufficiency may coexist
- Drugs
  - More sensitive to anaesthetic agents (including volatiles), though MAC is unchanged
    - Likely causes: low cardiac output, low blood volume, abnormal baroreceptor function, slower drug metabolism and excretion
  - Slow drug metabolism: reduce relaxant and opioid doses

## Preoperative assessment
- Look for tracheal compression or deviation from a goitre
  - If present, CT or MRI to define site and degree of narrowing
- Check for other autoimmune disease

### Investigations
- Subclinical hypothyroidism: raised TSH, normal free T4, no symptoms
  - No evidence of increased perioperative risk
- Do not measure T3 routinely
  - T3 stays normal until hypothyroidism is severe, because more T4 is converted to T3

## Preoperative management
### Established hypothyroidism on levothyroxine
- Continue T4 on the morning of surgery
- Missing a day's dose is acceptable: half-life about 7 days
  - If oral intake is not possible after surgery, doses may be missed for several days
  - If still no enteral route after 5 days, give IV levothyroxine at 60% to 80% of the oral dose

### Subclinical hypothyroidism
- Do not postpone surgery

### Moderate (overt) hypothyroidism
- Start replacement as soon as diagnosed
  - Young patients: near full replacement, levothyroxine (T4) 1.6 mcg/kg daily
  - Older patients or cardiopulmonary disease: 25 to 50 mcg daily, increased every two to six weeks
- Elective surgery: postpone until euthyroid
- Urgent surgery: proceed without delay, accepting possible minor perioperative complications

### Severe hypothyroidism
- High perioperative risk
- Elective surgery: postpone until euthyroid; start replacement
- Urgent surgery
  - Start IV thyroid replacement as soon as possible; give both T3 and T4 for rapid correction if myxoedema coma is a concern
    - IV T4: loading dose 200-300 mcg, then 50 mcg daily
      - Peak basal metabolic rate takes 10–12 days
    - Consider IV T3: loading dose 5-20 mcg, then 2.5-10 mcg every eight hours, depending on age and cardiac risk
      - Works within 6 hours; peak basal metabolic rate at 36–72 hours
  - Consider steroid cover (hydrocortisone or dexamethasone), as adrenal insufficiency often coexists
  - Postoperatively, follow fluids and electrolytes closely, especially sodium
  - Keep a high index of suspicion for ileus, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and infection without fever

### Myxoedema coma
- Medical emergency

## Intraoperative management
- Monitoring
  - Consider an arterial line if cardiopulmonary disease
  - Temperature monitoring is essential: low metabolic rate predisposes to hypothermia
  - Actively warm
- Drugs
  - Reduce anaesthetic doses
  - Treat hypotension with ephedrine, dopamine or adrenaline, not a pure alpha agonist (phenylephrine)
- Controlled ventilation if hypoventilating

## Flashcards
- Q: Should levothyroxine be given on the morning of surgery, and does a missed dose matter?
  - A: Continue it; a missed dose is fine as the half-life is about 7 days
- Q: When and how much IV levothyroxine if enteral route is unavailable?
  - A: After 5 days, at 60% to 80% of the oral dose
- Q: IV T4 regimen for urgent surgery in severe hypothyroidism?
  - A: Loading 200-300 mcg IV, then 50 mcg daily
- Q: Which vasopressor should be avoided for hypotension in hypothyroid patients?
  - A: Pure alpha agonists such as phenylephrine; use ephedrine, dopamine or adrenaline
- Q: Should surgery be postponed for subclinical hypothyroidism?
  - A: No
