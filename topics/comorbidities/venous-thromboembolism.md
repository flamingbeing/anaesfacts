# Venous Thromboembolism and Prophylaxis

Thrombotic conditions, antiphospholipid syndrome and perioperative VTE prophylaxis by Caprini risk.

## Perioperative drug management
- May need bridging with unfractionated heparin (UFH)
- Prosthetic valves, AF and anticoagulant cessation are covered in their own topics

## Antiphospholipid syndrome
- Clinical features
  - Thrombosis, arterial or venous: effects range from subacute migraine to heart failure or stroke
  - Recurrent miscarriage
  - Arterial thrombosis should prompt thought of antiphospholipid syndrome
- Associations: autoimmune disease such as SLE
- Investigations
  - Positive antiphospholipid antibody
  - With or without lupus anticoagulant, which prolongs clotting tests such as APTT
- Treatment
  - Aspirin
  - After a confirmed thrombotic event: lifelong warfarin
- Perioperative: may need bridging

## VTE risk assessment
- Caprini Risk Assessment Model (Caprini score)
  - Points-based tool for VTE within 30 days of surgery or admission
  - Score >5 is high risk

## Prophylaxis by risk
- Very low risk (<0.5% VTE risk; Caprini 0)
  - Minor ambulatory surgery
  - Early ambulation alone
- Low risk (about 1.5%; Caprini 1–2)
  - Chemosurgery, minor general surgery
  - Mechanical prophylaxis preferred: intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC)
- Moderate risk (about 3.0%; Caprini 3–4)
  - Non-major orthopaedic, major general, open urological or gynaecological, cardiothoracic, craniotomy, spinal surgery
  - LMWH or low-dose UFH, or mechanical prophylaxis (IPC preferred over graduated compression stockings)
  - High bleeding risk: IPC alone
- High risk (about 6.0%; Caprini ≥5)
  - Major general or pelvic cancer surgery, hip or knee arthroplasty, hip fracture surgery, major trauma, spinal cord injury
  - Pharmacological (LMWH or low-dose UFH) plus mechanical (IPC) combined
  - High bleeding risk: IPC until bleeding risk settles, then add pharmacological

## Orthopaedic (hip and knee arthroplasty, hip fracture)
- LMWH preferred over low-dose UFH, fondaparinux, low-dose aspirin or DOACs

## Obstetric
- LMWH is first line
- UFH reserved for peri-delivery timing in high-risk patients, severe renal failure (CrCl <30 ml/min), or when rapid reversal may be needed (reversible with protamine)
- No warfarin: it causes complications in pregnancy

## Flashcards
- Q: What Caprini score is high risk?
  - A: 5 or more (about 6.0% VTE risk).
- Q: What prophylaxis is used for high-risk patients?
  - A: LMWH or low-dose UFH plus IPC.
- Q: What is first-line thromboprophylaxis in pregnancy?
  - A: LMWH.
- Q: When is UFH preferred in obstetric patients?
  - A: Around delivery in high-risk patients, CrCl <30 ml/min, or when rapid reversal is needed.
- Q: What is the long-term treatment of antiphospholipid syndrome after a thrombotic event?
  - A: Lifelong warfarin.
