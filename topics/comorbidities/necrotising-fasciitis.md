# Necrotising fasciitis

Rapidly spreading deep soft tissue infection needing broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and emergency surgical debridement.

## Risk factors
- Diabetes
- Alcohol
- Immunocompromise

## Presentation
- Pain out of proportion to the signs

## Examination
- Rapidly spreading erythema
- Tenderness proximal to the lesion (e.g. calf tenderness with a foot ulcer)
- Patient becoming more septic
- Later: mottling, dusky discolouration, with or without haemorrhagic bullae and loss of sensation

## Findings at incision
- Finger test
  - Small incision down to deep fascia in the affected area, then gentle finger dissection along the deep fascial plane
  - Positive if tissue separates with little resistance
- No bleeding
- Necrotic tissue
- Murky grey "dishwater" fluid

## LRINEC score
- Components: white cell count, CRP, Hb, sodium, creatinine, glucose
- A score of at least 6 should prompt serious consideration of NF
- Not externally validated

## Management
- Early aggressive broad-spectrum IV antibiotics
- Emergency surgical debridement

## Anaesthetic considerations
- Preoperative
  - Assess severity of sepsis
  - Resuscitate
- Intraoperative
  - Invasive monitoring
  - Central line and noradrenaline
- Postoperative
  - ICU
  - Adequate analgesia

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the classic presenting symptom of necrotising fasciitis?
  - A: Pain out of proportion to the clinical signs.
- Q: What LRINEC score should make you seriously consider NF?
  - A: At least 6 (but the score is not externally validated).
- Q: What makes up the LRINEC score?
  - A: White cell count, CRP, Hb, sodium, creatinine and glucose.
- Q: What is a positive finger test?
  - A: Minimal resistance to finger dissection along the deep fascia through a small incision.
