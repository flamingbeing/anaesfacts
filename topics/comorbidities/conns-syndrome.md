# Conn's Syndrome

Primary hyperaldosteronism: hypertension, hypokalaemia and alkalosis; optimise with spironolactone and potassium before adrenalectomy.

## Key points
- Triad: hypertension, hypokalaemia, alkalosis
- Spironolactone before surgery
- Correct electrolytes
- Arterial line: blood pressure swings during gland handling (less than in phaeochromocytoma)
- Steroids for bilateral adrenalectomy

## Definition
- **Conn's syndrome**: primary hyperaldosteronism from a unilateral aldosterone-secreting adrenal adenoma

## Causes of hyperaldosteronism
- **Primary**: low ACTH and renin, high aldosterone, raised aldosterone/renin ratio (ARR)
  - **Adrenal adenoma (Conn's syndrome)**: 60%
  - **Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia**: 30%
  - Adrenal carcinoma (rare)
  - Glucocorticoid-remediable aldosteronism (GRA)
- **Secondary**: high ACTH and renin, high aldosterone, normal ARR
  - Congestive cardiac failure
  - Accelerated hypertension
  - Liver cirrhosis
  - Nephrotic syndrome
  - Renal artery stenosis
  - Diuretics

## Clinical features
- Triad of **hypertension, hypokalaemia and alkalosis** in a patient not on diuretics
- **Hypervolaemia causing hypertension**
  - Primary hyperaldosteronism is the commonest cause of secondary hypertension
  - Aldosterone acts on Na-K ATPase in the distal tubule and cortical collecting duct, increasing sodium and water reabsorption and expanding ECF volume
- **Hypokalaemia**
  - Headache, fatigue, muscle weakness, cramps, palpitations
  - Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus from tubular damage (polyuria, polydipsia)
  - Suspect if spontaneous hypokalaemia (< 3.5mmol/L), or moderately severe hypokalaemia (< 3mmol/L) on diuretics despite oral KCl
  - Urinary potassium loss because:
    - Aldosterone drives tubular potassium secretion via Na-K ATPase in the distal tubule and cortical collecting duct
    - Adequate intravascular volume delivers water to the distal nephron
    - Adequate dietary sodium increases distal sodium delivery and potassium exchange
  - Worsened by diuretic treatment of hypertension
- **Metabolic alkalosis**: aldosterone increases tubular hydrogen ion secretion
- **Impaired glucose tolerance (20%)**:
  - Hypokalaemia inhibits insulin secretion and action
  - Hypokalaemia stimulates H-K ATPase in the collecting duct, increasing hydrogen ion secretion

## Investigations
- **Confirm the diagnosis**
  - **Aldosterone/renin ratio (ARR)** > 400 (pg/mL)/(ng/ml/h)
  - Postural test (renin, aldosterone, cortisol at 9am lying and 12 noon standing):
    - ACTH-responsive (cortisol and aldosterone fall on standing): Conn's adenoma or GRA
    - Renin-angiotensin responsive (cortisol falls, aldosterone rises on standing): bilateral adrenal hyperplasia
  - **CT or MRI of the adrenals**
  - **Adrenal vein sampling**: usually an outpatient interventional radiology procedure
    - Catheter samples both adrenal veins and compares aldosterone levels on each side
    - If no side is identified, bilateral adrenal hyperplasia is presumed
  - Adrenal scintigraphy with 131I- or 75Se-labelled aldosterone precursors may separate adenoma from hyperplasia
- **Assess complications**
  - Urea, electrolytes and creatinine: hypernatraemia, hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia
  - ABG: metabolic alkalosis

## Medical management
- **ACE inhibitors, ARBs**
- **Spironolactone** PO 300mg/day (aldosterone antagonist)
  - Start 4wks before surgery
  - Anti-androgenic effects: gynaecomastia, menstrual irregularity
  - Alternative: **eplerenone**, similar without anti-androgenic effects
- **Amiloride** PO 10-20mg/day (ENaC blocker)
- **Glucocorticoids** for GRA

## Anaesthetic overview for adrenalectomy
- Patient:
  - Diagnosis: hypertension, hypokalaemia, hypernatraemia, metabolic alkalosis, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, diabetes; ARR, adrenal imaging, adrenal vein sampling, scintigraphy
  - Comorbidities: hypertension, diabetes and their complications
  - Optimise blood pressure and glucose, correct electrolytes and alkalosis, steroid cover
- Surgery: open or laparoscopic, positioning, long operation, blood loss, haemodynamic instability during adrenal handling
- Anaesthesia:
  - Hypokalaemia can blunt baroreceptors, prolong neuromuscular block and inhibit insulin secretion
  - Arterial line and TOF monitoring
  - GA
  - Keep haemodynamics stable: blunt sympathetic responses, treat hypotension and hypovolaemia, alpha blockers during adrenal handling
  - Analgesia
  - Hormone replacement: fludrocortisone, hydrocortisone
  - Postoperative blood pressure control

## Preoperative assessment
- **Primary disease**: diagnostic tests; hypertensive end-organ damage (LVH, IHD, PVD, nephropathy)
- **Comorbidities**

## Preoperative optimisation
- **Control hypertension**: spironolactone, ACE inhibitors
- **Correct hypokalaemia and metabolic alkalosis**:
  - Hypokalaemia blunts baroreceptor tone: treat hypovolaemia aggressively
  - Hypokalaemia prolongs neuromuscular block: TOF monitoring and reversal
  - Hypokalaemia inhibits insulin secretion: monitor blood glucose, perioperative glycaemic control

## Intraoperative management
- **Monitoring**
  - Standard ASA monitoring
  - Invasive: arterial line, CVP (risk of haemodynamic instability)
  - TOF and blood glucose monitoring
- **Technique**
  - GA for unilateral or bilateral adrenalectomy
  - Blunt hypertensive responses
  - Treat hypotension aggressively
- **Haemodynamic stability during adrenal handling**
  - Instability less severe than with phaeochromocytoma
  - Short-acting alpha blockade: phentolamine
- **Hormone replacement**
  - Hydrocortisone for perioperative steroid cover
  - Fludrocortisone
- **Analgesia**: systemic analgesia, regional techniques

## Postoperative management
- Disposition
- **Blood pressure control**: hypertension may persist after adenoma removal from permanent changes in vascular resistance
- **Hormone replacement**: continue IV hydrocortisone until oral hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone are tolerated
- Analgesia
