# Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

Chronic multisystem autoimmune disease; anaesthetic risk comes from the organs involved and from antiphospholipid-related hypercoagulability.

## Overview

- Chronic multisystem disease, most common in young women
- Widespread autoantibodies cause tissue damage
- No specific bony erosion or airway problems
- Main risks come from the organs affected
- Antiphospholipid syndrome: hypercoagulable

## Clinical features

- Skin and joints: commonly involved; oral and pharyngeal ulcers
- Cardiovascular
  - Pericarditis 15%; myocarditis; endocarditis (less common)
  - Raynaud's phenomenon 30%
  - IHD; arteritis causing ischaemia
- Respiratory: infections, PE, pleuritis, pleural effusion, pulmonary fibrosis
- Neurological
  - Cranial and peripheral nerve lesions
  - Transverse myelitis causing weakness and paraplegia
  - Depression, psychosis
  - Seizures
  - Stroke (with antiphospholipid antibodies)
- Renal: glomerulonephritis leading to renal failure
- Haematological
  - Immune thrombocytopenia or antibodies to factor VIII
  - Antiphospholipid syndrome 33%
    - Hypercoagulable, yet the lupus anticoagulant paradoxically prolongs the APTT
    - Get haematology advice if coagulation tests are abnormal

## Investigations

- Platelet count (thrombocytopenia)
- Coagulation screen
- U+E
- CXR
- Echo
- CT or MRI as indicated

## Management

- Medication: steroids, immunosuppressants

## Perioperative management

- Regional anaesthesia
  - Check coagulation carefully
  - Peripheral nerve lesions may favour another technique
- Keep normothermic (less Raynaud's)
- Avoid airway trauma: laryngeal oedema is common
- Invasive monitoring
- Steroid supplementation
- Strict asepsis

## Flashcards

- Q: How common is antiphospholipid syndrome in SLE?
  - A: 33%
- Q: Why can the APTT be prolonged in SLE despite a hypercoagulable state?
  - A: The lupus anticoagulant prolongs it paradoxically
- Q: Airway concern in SLE?
  - A: Laryngeal oedema is common; avoid airway trauma
- Q: How common are Raynaud's and pericarditis in SLE?
  - A: Raynaud's 30%; pericarditis 15%
