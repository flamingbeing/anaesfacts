# Alcoholism

Acute intoxication lowers anaesthetic requirements and raises aspiration risk; chronic use raises MAC and brings withdrawal, nutritional, liver and cardiac problems.

## Acute intoxication
- Often uncooperative, so consent and assessment are difficult
- MAC and induction doses are reduced
- Airway: high aspiration risk
- Haemodynamic instability: alcohol is a myocardial depressant and causes peripheral vasodilation

## Chronic use
- MAC increased; opioid tolerance common
- Withdrawal risk, including delirium tremens: plan high dependency care after surgery
- Give **thiamine prophylaxis** before glucose (prevents Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome)
- **Malnutrition and refeeding risk**
- Cirrhosis
- Alcoholic cardiomyopathy and arrhythmias
- Peripheral neuropathy

## Flashcards
- Q: How does MAC change with acute intoxication versus chronic alcohol use?
  - A: Reduced when acutely intoxicated; increased with chronic use
- Q: Why give thiamine before glucose in chronic alcohol users?
  - A: To prevent Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome
- Q: Name the main airway concern in an acutely intoxicated patient.
  - A: High aspiration risk
- Q: What postoperative care should be planned for withdrawal risk?
  - A: High dependency care because of the risk of delirium tremens
