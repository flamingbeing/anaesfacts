# Opioid-Dependent Patients

Patients on long-term opioids or with opioid use disorder (OUD) need their opioid continued, extra analgesia and opioid-sparing techniques.

## Background
- Long-term morphine causes adrenal hypertrophy and impairs corticosteroid secretion

## Management
- Premedicate adequately with opioids
- Give extra opioid intraoperatively and postoperatively
- Add non-opioid analgesics and nerve blocks
- No ideal agent or technique, in OUD or in acute overdose; an opioid antagonist may be used judiciously where indicated
- Combined techniques work well: regional anaesthesia with low-dose ketamine and α2-agonists
- Support the circulation with fluids; monitor arterial blood gases and respiratory function

## Flashcards
- Q: What should opioid-dependent patients receive perioperatively?
  - A: Their usual opioid as premedication, plus supplemental intra- and postoperative opioid, non-opioid analgesics and nerve blocks
- Q: Which adjuncts help in opioid-dependent patients?
  - A: Low-dose ketamine and α2-agonists, combined with regional anaesthesia
- Q: What endocrine effect does long-term morphine have?
  - A: Adrenal hypertrophy with impaired corticosteroid secretion
