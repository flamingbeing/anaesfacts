# Wolff-Parkinson-White Syndrome

Accessory atrioventricular pathway (Bundle of Kent) causing re-entrant tachycardia; avoid triggers and be ready to cardiovert.

## Main considerations
- Risk of perioperative SVT or atrial fibrillation
- Avoid triggering SVT
  - Avoid sympathetic stimulation
  - Avoid drugs that can enhance anterograde conduction down the accessory pathway: adenosine, beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin
  - Reduce anxiety, which can precipitate tachycardia
- Stay vigilant for intraoperative arrhythmias
- Have equipment ready for rapid pharmacological or electrical cardioversion

## Preoperative assessment
- Suspect WPW if the ECG shows pre-excitation delta waves
- Ask about arrhythmia symptoms: asymptomatic patients are lower risk
- Cardiology or electrophysiologist review
  - Consider treadmill exercise testing and electrophysiological study (EPS)
  - Consider preoperative ablation
- Check electrolytes

## Preparation
- Crash cart in theatre; defibrillator pads on before induction
- Emergency drugs ready: procainamide, amiodarone
- Avoid sympathetic stimulation from pain and anxiety
- Give adequate IV fluids if fasting is prolonged (avoid hypovolaemia)

## Intraoperative management
- Monitoring
  - 5-lead ECG
  - Arterial line
- Temperature probe and forced-air warmer to avoid shivering
- Airway and ventilation
  - Ensure adequate depth before airway instrumentation
  - LMA may be preferred to avoid the sympathetic response to laryngoscopy
  - Keep normocarbia
- Drugs and fluids
  - Ensure normovolaemia before induction
  - Avoid ketamine for induction
- Maintenance
  - Watch for arrhythmias; decide whether narrow complex (orthodromic), wide complex (antidromic) or irregular
  - Adequate analgesia
  - Watch blood loss, give enough fluid, avoid hypovolaemia
  - Use AV nodal blockers selectively (adenosine, calcium channel blockers, beta blockers, digoxin)
- Emergence
  - Reverse with sugammadex to avoid the neostigmine plus anticholinergic combination
  - Ensure good analgesia before emergence
  - Aim for a smooth emergence

## Postoperative management
- Adequate analgesia
- Avoid PONV

## Treatment of orthodromic AVRT
- Same approach as ordinary SVT
- First line
  - Vagal manoeuvres
  - Verapamil 5 mg IV every 3 min, up to 15 mg
  - Adenosine 6-12 mg IV rapid bolus with flush
- 2nd line: procainamide, beta blockers, amiodarone (prolongs refractoriness of all cardiac tissue)

## Treatment of antidromic AVRT
- Unstable: cardiovert
- Stable: procainamide is the IV drug of choice
  - Infuse 20 to 50 mg/min, checking blood pressure every 5 to 10 min
  - Stop when the arrhythmia ends, hypotension develops, QRS widens by more than 50%, or 17 mg/kg total is given (1.2 g for a 70 kg patient)
- Avoid AV nodal blocking drugs

## Treatment of pre-excited atrial fibrillation
- Avoid AV nodal blockers (adenosine, verapamil, beta blockers, digoxin)
  - Blocking the AV node pushes conduction down the accessory pathway and may speed it up
- Goals: rapid control of ventricular rate, ideally termination of AF
- Unstable: cardiovert
- Stable: careful trial of IV drugs (no clear first-line agent)
  - Procainamide (class IA): 20-50 mg/min until the arrhythmia is suppressed, hypotension develops, QRS widens by 50% of baseline, or 17 mg/kg total is given
  - Amiodarone (class III): 150 mg over 10 min, then 1 mg/min for 6 hr, then 0.5 mg/min for 18 hr
  - Ibutilide (class III): under 60 kg, 0.01 mg/kg over 10 min; over 60 kg, 1 mg over 10 min

## Flashcards
- Q: Which ECG finding suggests WPW?
  - A: Pre-excitation delta waves.
- Q: Which drugs should be avoided in pre-excited AF?
  - A: AV nodal blockers: adenosine, verapamil, beta blockers, digoxin.
- Q: What is the IV drug of choice for stable antidromic AVRT, and its rate?
  - A: Procainamide 20-50 mg/min, to a maximum of 17 mg/kg.
- Q: What reversal agent is preferred at emergence in WPW and why?
  - A: Sugammadex, to avoid neostigmine plus anticholinergic.
- Q: Which induction agent should be avoided in WPW?
  - A: Ketamine.
