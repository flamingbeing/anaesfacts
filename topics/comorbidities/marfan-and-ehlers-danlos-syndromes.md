# Marfan and Ehlers-Danlos Syndromes

Inherited connective tissue disorders: guard the aorta and lungs, and position and intubate gently.

## Marfan syndrome
- Autosomal dominant disorder of connective tissue metabolism
- Most patients take long-term beta blockers
- Common operations: cardiovascular surgery (valve replacement) and spinal surgery for severe scoliosis
- Tall, with long thin fingers

### Cardiovascular
- Aortic dissection: control blood pressure and aortic shear stress (dP/dt)
  - Perioperative beta blockade if not already on it
  - Minimise the sympathetic response throughout
- Aortic root dilation: TTE
- Aortic and mitral regurgitation: listen carefully, TTE
  - May have had a Bentall procedure
- Bundle branch blocks and coronary thrombosis: ECG

### Respiratory
- Spontaneous pneumothorax: avoid high ventilation pressures
- Emphysema, pectus excavatum and scoliosis can make ventilation harder

### Airway
- High-arched palate: laryngoscopy may be harder
- OSA
- Tracheomalacia

### Musculoskeletal
- Hypermobile joints that dislocate easily: position carefully
- Kyphoscoliosis: neuraxial blocks may be harder

## Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
- A group of disorders of defective collagen cross-linking

### History
- Recurrent dislocations
- Prolonged spontaneous bleeding
- Rupture of cerebral or other vessels
- Bowel perforation
- Spontaneous pneumothorax

### Examination
- Stretchy, fragile skin
- Joint laxity and hypermobility
- Eye abnormalities
- Kyphoscoliosis

### Anaesthetic management
- Careful positioning and padding
- Lung-protective ventilation
- Gentle intubation: severe tracheal bruising can occur

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the main perioperative cardiovascular goal in Marfan syndrome?
  - A: Control blood pressure and aortic shear stress (dP/dt) with beta blockade and a blunted sympathetic response
- Q: Why avoid high ventilation pressures in Marfan and Ehlers-Danlos syndromes?
  - A: Risk of spontaneous pneumothorax
- Q: Which preoperative test assesses the aortic root and valves in Marfan syndrome?
  - A: TTE
- Q: What airway risk is specific to Ehlers-Danlos syndrome?
  - A: Severe tracheal bruising, so intubate gently
