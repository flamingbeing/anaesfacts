# Mitral Stenosis

Fixed-output valve lesion: keep the heart slow and in sinus rhythm, keep preload and SVR up, and avoid anything that raises PVR.

## Key points
- Severe: valve area 1.5 cm², pressure half-time (PHT) 150ms
- Slow and tight, ideally sinus (AF is common)
- Watch for pulmonary hypertension and RV failure

## Severity
- Severe: area <1.5 cm², PHT >150ms (changed in 2014 from 1 cm² and 220ms)
- PHT is used because the pressure gradient depends heavily on heart rate, while the rate of pressure equalisation stays fairly constant
- Severity tables: AHA 2020

## Preoperative assessment
- Often well tolerated, with subtle signs: look for dyspnoea, fatigue, recurrent chest infections
- Persistently raised PA pressure strains and eventually fails the RV: look for raised JVP and peripheral oedema
- TTE
  - Severity, pulmonary hypertension and RV function
  - LV function usually preserved
  - Asymptomatic with systolic PA pressure <50 mm Hg: non-cardiac surgery considered safe
- Intervention
  - Severe MS: valve intervention first, delay elective surgery (surgical valve replacement or percutaneous balloon commissurotomy)
  - Algorithm: AHA 2024
- Premedication with caution
  - Opioid hypoventilation and hypercarbia raise PVR
  - Venodilation reduces preload
- Dilated left atrium makes AF common; warfarin may need bridging with heparin

## Intraoperative management

### Monitoring
- 5-lead ECG
- CVP or PA catheter to guide fluids

### Technique
- Local or peripheral nerve block where possible
- Spinal is relatively contraindicated in significant MS
  - Falls in preload (venodilation) and afterload are poorly tolerated
  - High filling pressure is needed to fill the LV across the valve; lower arterial tone cannot be offset by raising stroke volume or heart rate
  - Can spiral into poor myocardial perfusion and worsening function
  - Patient may also be anticoagulated
- A slowly titrated epidural has been used successfully

### Haemodynamic goals
- Low-normal heart rate (time to fill)
  - Avoid shivering and stimulants such as ketamine
  - Good analgesia and antiemetic prophylaxis
  - Treat tachycardia with beta-blockers
  - Treat hypotension with direct alpha-agonists (metaraminol, phenylephrine) rather than ephedrine
- Avoid falls in SVR (fixed cardiac output)
  - Stroke volume cannot rise much when arterial tone falls, and a reflex tachycardia shortens filling time
  - Avoid potent vasodilators and drugs causing tachycardia
    - Induction: may avoid propofol and ketamine; thiopental or etomidate reasonable
    - Maintenance: may avoid desflurane; sevoflurane and short-acting opioids reasonable
    - Avoid pancuronium and anticholinergics (tachycardia)
- Maintain preload
  - Watch for and promptly replace blood loss, but avoid unnecessary fluid and overload
  - Position changes (legs into or out of lithotomy) can destabilise
  - Fluid boluses can worsen pulmonary oedema
- Maintain sinus rhythm (atrial kick gives 40% of ventricular filling)
  - Many are already in AF from LA enlargement: rate control is vital
  - If in sinus, avoid AF triggers (sympathetic surges, atrial stretch); have drugs and a defibrillator ready
  - Be ready to cardiovert new AF
- Prevent rises in PVR
  - Avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia and acidosis (acute RV failure)
  - Nitrous oxide is controversial (raises PA pressure)

### Cardiopulmonary bypass
- Manage pulmonary hypertension from raised PVR with RV failure
- LA stasis: check carefully on echo for atrial thrombus
- Avoid handling the heart before cross-clamp
- Inotropes often needed after bypass for RV failure; after MVR the chronically underfilled LV may not cope with the new volume load
- Consider afterload reduction and better systemic perfusion with an IABP

## Postoperative care
- Same haemodynamic principles; usually in a high-dependency unit unless surgery is very minor
- Tell recovery staff to avoid fluid boluses
- Any hypotension needs anaesthetic review and prompt treatment

## Flashcards
- Q: What defines severe mitral stenosis?
  - A: Valve area <1.5 cm² and PHT >150ms
- Q: What proportion of ventricular filling does the atrial kick provide in MS (per the notes)?
  - A: 40%
- Q: Below what systolic PA pressure is non-cardiac surgery considered safe in asymptomatic MS?
  - A: <50 mm Hg
- Q: Which vasopressor type is preferred for hypotension in MS?
  - A: Direct alpha-agonists (metaraminol, phenylephrine) rather than ephedrine
