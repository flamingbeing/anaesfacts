# Airborne Precautions

Isolation theatre with everything prepared, full PPE, and fewer aerosols: avoid GA where possible, RSI with first-pass intubation, smooth emergence.

## Goals
- Prevent patient-to-staff transmission
- Prevent patient-to-patient transmission

## Minimise aerosol-generating procedures (AGP)
- Delay elective surgery, if possible, until the patient is no longer infectious or airborne infection is excluded
- Regional anaesthesia: avoid GA if possible
  - Avoids airway management and aerosolised airway secretions
- AGPs include intubation, extubation, mask ventilation, supraglottic airways, bronchoscopy and suctioning

## Planning and location
- Discuss the plan with the team
- Negative pressure theatre with anteroom
  - Dedicated route to and from theatre
  - Bypass holding area and PACU; induct and recover in theatre
  - Prominent signage
  - Remote sites: may need to intubate in a positive pressure theatre, then transfer

## Equipment and drugs
- Prepare in advance
- Disposable equipment
- Protect non-disposable equipment
- Decontaminate theatre, equipment and staff afterwards

## Staff and PPE
- Gloves, gown, cap, goggles, N95 mask; consider PAPR (powered air-purifying respirator)
- Minimum staff in theatre
- Immunised staff only
- Avoid high-level PPE when not needed (outside airway management or other AGPs)
  - It hampers communication, breathing, vision and dexterity and causes overheating, especially a PAPR

## Intubation
- Have a plan; prepare all equipment beforehand
  - Disposable airway equipment where possible (mask, laryngoscope blades)
  - HME filter
- Full PAPR precautions; vaccinated staff
- Keep non-essential staff out during intubation
- Reduce aerosolised secretions and coughing
  - Minimise mask ventilation; consider RSI if the airway looks easy
  - Adequate depth of anaesthesia and neuromuscular blockade to stop coughing
  - Most experienced operator for first-pass success; optimise it, e.g. videolaryngoscope
  - Inflate the ETT cuff before ventilating
  - Smooth extubation without coughing
  - Adequate antiemetics to reduce postoperative nausea and retching

## Surgery
- Electrocautery and both open and laparoscopic surgery can aerosolise body fluids
- Transmission from procedures not involving the respiratory tract has not been documented

## Cardiopulmonary resuscitation
- Chest compressions, positive pressure ventilation and intubation can aerosolise virus
- Keep the number of resuscitators to a minimum
- Intubate early

## Flashcards
- Q: What theatre setup is needed for a patient on airborne precautions?
  - A: Negative pressure theatre with anteroom, dedicated route, bypassing holding area and PACU, prominent signage.
- Q: What mask is part of airborne PPE?
  - A: N95 mask, with a PAPR considered.
- Q: How do you reduce aerosols at induction?
  - A: Minimise mask ventilation, RSI if the airway looks easy, deep anaesthesia and full paralysis, experienced operator, cuff up before ventilating.
- Q: Why avoid high-level PPE outside AGPs?
  - A: It impairs communication, breathing, vision and dexterity and causes overheating.
