# Infective Endocarditis

Infection of the endocardium and valves; prophylaxis only for high-risk patients having high-risk (dental) procedures.

## Prophylaxis in brief
- Needs both a high-risk patient and a high-risk procedure
- Dental procedures only
- 4 high-risk groups
  - Prosthetic valves or cardiac material
  - Certain congenital heart disease: unrepaired cyanotic, partially repaired, or fully repaired within 6 months
  - Heart transplant recipients who develop valvulopathy
  - Previous IE

## Overview
- Infective inflammation of the endocardium and valves
- Most often affects the aortic valve (previously the mitral valve)

## Pathophysiology
- Starts at a site of endothelial damage
- Sterile thrombotic vegetation lets bacteria stick during transient bacteraemia
- Platelet and fibrin deposition sets up a positive feedback cycle
- Destructive effects follow

## Classification
- Duration
  - Acute: fulminant over days to weeks; more often S. aureus
  - Subacute: low-virulence streptococci; mild to moderate, progresses over weeks to months
- Culture
  - Culture-positive
  - Culture-negative: often from prior antibiotics
- Side of heart
  - Right: 10%, usually IV drug use; 98% tricuspid valve
  - Left: patients without IV drug exposure
- Valve type
  - Native valve
  - Prosthetic valve: early (<60 days), intermediate (60 days to 1 year), late (>1 year)

## Diagnosis
- Duke criteria: 2 major, or 1 major plus 3 minor, or 5 minor
  - Low sensitivity; not usable if culture-negative, foreign implant IE or right-sided IE
- Major
  - Positive blood culture with typical organisms
  - Endocardial involvement on echocardiography
- Minor
  - Predisposition: known cardiac lesion, injecting drug use
  - Fever >38
  - Embolic phenomena
  - Immunological: glomerulonephritis, Osler's nodes
  - Positive blood culture with atypical organism

## Clinical features
- Ranges from nothing, to malaise, night sweats, anaemia, weight loss, to cardiogenic shock and sepsis
- Haematuria; stroke
- Risk factors
  - Rheumatic heart disease
  - Prosthetic valves
  - Congenital heart disease (trileaflet MV)
  - IV drug use
  - Previous IE
  - High-risk procedures (dental, respiratory, infected sites)
  - But 50% occur on normal valves
- Examination: murmurs, heart failure, valve lesions, stroke, splenomegaly
- Peripheral stigmata: clubbing, splinter haemorrhages, Osler's nodes, vasculitic rash, retinal changes

## Complications
- Emboli: brain, lungs, limbs, organs
- Sepsis, abscess
- Valve incompetence
- Arrhythmias
- Death

## Investigations
- Blood cultures: positive in 80%
  - Ideally 3 sets from different sites; volume also matters
  - Mostly gram-positive: Strep viridans (about 25%), Staph aureus (about 50), Strep pneumoniae; also fungi
- Serology
- ECG: PR prolongation, P mitrale, T wave inversion
- Echo: vegetations and valve incompetence
  - TTE sensitivity 45-60%
  - TOE sensitivity 90-100%; must do if paravalvular complications suspected, e.g. shocked patient
  - Suggestive findings: vegetation >10mm (higher embolic risk), peri-annular abscess (TTE especially poor), new prosthetic valve dehiscence

## Management
- IV antibiotics for 4-6 weeks
  - Ask microbiology; common empirical choice flucloxacillin and gentamicin
  - Curative in 50%
- Valve replacement
- Prophylaxis

## Prophylaxis guidance
- Older AHA advice: prophylaxis for valvular disease (including rheumatic) having dental, GI or GU procedures
- Now more conservative: antibiotic harms outweigh the IE risk from most procedures
- Current guideline from 2007, reaffirmed in the 2021 statement
- High-risk patients: the 4 groups above
- High-risk procedures
  - Dental work manipulating gingiva or the periapical region, or perforating oral mucosa
  - Check-ups and simple fillings not involving gingiva need no prophylaxis
  - Routine GI and GU procedures no longer qualify on endocarditis risk alone

## Antibiotic choice
- Single dose before the procedure (or within 2 hours of it)
  - Amoxicillin 50mg/kg PO, or ampicillin
  - Cefazolin 50mg/kg IV or IM
  - Cephalexin 50mg/kg PO (penicillin allergy)
- Active infection at the operative site with an organism likely to cause IE: give perioperative treatment (ask infectious diseases)

## Flashcards
- Q: Which 4 patient groups are high risk for IE prophylaxis?
  - A: Prosthetic valves or material, certain congenital heart disease, heart transplant with valvulopathy, previous IE.
- Q: Which procedures qualify for IE prophylaxis?
  - A: Dental procedures manipulating gingiva or periapical region or perforating oral mucosa; not routine GI or GU.
- Q: What are the Duke criteria thresholds?
  - A: 2 major, or 1 major plus 3 minor, or 5 minor.
- Q: Sensitivity of TTE versus TOE for IE?
  - A: TTE 45-60%; TOE 90-100%.
- Q: Prophylactic amoxicillin or cefazolin dose?
  - A: 50mg/kg as a single dose before the procedure.
