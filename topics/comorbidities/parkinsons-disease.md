# Parkinson's Disease

Keep antiparkinson drugs going, avoid dopamine antagonists, and expect aspiration risk, autonomic swings and a possibly difficult airway.

## Pathophysiology
- Progressive loss of melanin-containing dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta
- Low dopamine removes normal inhibition of the striatum, upsetting the basal ganglia direct and indirect pathways; the net result is motor inhibition
- Lewy bodies: intracellular alpha-synuclein clumps that spread in a set order through brainstem, limbic system and neocortex (Braak staging)
- Other systems degenerate too: locus coeruleus (noradrenaline), raphe nuclei (serotonin), nucleus basalis of Meynert (acetylcholine); these drive non-motor symptoms

## Clinical features
- Motor (parkinsonism)
  - Bradykinesia: slow movement that shrinks in size and speed with repetition; needed for diagnosis
  - Rest tremor: pill-rolling, 4–6 Hz, one-sided at first, goes with purposeful movement
  - Rigidity: lead-pipe, or cogwheel when tremor is added
  - Postural instability: late loss of postural reflexes and balance
- Non-motor
  - Cardiovascular: autonomic dysfunction with orthostatic and postprandial hypotension and reduced heart rate variability
  - Gastrointestinal: drooling from poor swallowing, dysphagia (aspiration risk), gastroparesis, severe constipation
  - Respiratory: upper airway obstruction from vocal cord dystonia, restrictive pattern from chest wall rigidity, weak cough with more postoperative pneumonia
  - Neuropsychiatric: cognitive impairment and dementia, visual hallucinations, depression, anxiety, sleep disorders (REM sleep behaviour disorder)
  - Urogenital: urgency, frequency, nocturia, erectile dysfunction

## Medications
- Madopar (levodopa + benserazide)
  - Levodopa: dopamine precursor; benserazide: peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor
  - Side effects: orthostatic hypotension, hallucinations
- Benzhexol (trihexyphenidyl): central antimuscarinic
  - Rebalances dopamine and acetylcholine in the striatum, where cholinergic activity is relatively high
  - Mainly helps tremor
  - Side effects: delirium, urinary retention, ileus
- Dopamine agonists: stimulate striatal dopamine receptors directly
  - Non-ergot (preferred): pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine (transdermal patch)
  - Ergot (rarely used): bromocriptine, cabergoline
- MAO-B inhibitors: selegiline, rasagiline, safinamide
  - Reduce central dopamine breakdown
  - Risk of serotonin syndrome; hypertensive crisis with ephedrine
- COMT inhibitors: entacapone, opicapone, tolcapone; lengthen levodopa half-life
- NMDA antagonist: amantadine

## Key considerations
- Usually elderly with other comorbidities
- Possible difficult airway: stiff temporomandibular joint and cervical spine
- Aspiration risk: bulbar and oesophageal dysfunction, gastroparesis
  - Ask about reflux
  - Aspiration prophylaxis, modified RSI
  - Balance aspiration risk against autonomic instability and a difficult airway
- Restrictive lung disease from chest wall rigidity
  - Fentanyl and morphine may increase chest wall and muscle rigidity
- Autonomic instability: arrhythmias, orthostatic hypotension, marked hypotension at induction, rapid swings during surgery
- Worsening of symptoms and postoperative delirium
  - Continue antiparkinson drugs through the perioperative period; make sure Madopar doses are given
  - Avoid dopamine antagonists, which provoke parkinsonism: metoclopramide, butyrophenones (droperidol, haloperidol), phenothiazines (chlorpromazine, methotrimeprazine), i.e. antipsychotics
  - Alfentanil and fentanyl have been linked to dystonic reactions
- Avoid dopamine and acetylcholine antagonists: droperidol, promethazine, prochlorperazine, metoclopramide, scopolamine, high-dose glycopyrrolate
- PONV: avoid metoclopramide and droperidol
- Anticholinergic: avoid atropine (central anticholinergic syndrome); use glycopyrrolate
- Patients on MAO inhibitors such as selegiline
  - Avoid serotonergic drugs: tramadol, pethidine (meperidine)
  - Use only direct-acting sympathomimetics (phenylephrine) if needed
  - Selegiline is MAO-B, not MAO-A, so usually safe; case reports of hyperthermia, agitation, rigidity and seizures with pethidine or tramadol

## Intraoperative management
- Regional anaesthesia in a patient with tremor: weigh against the risks of GA

## Flashcards
- Q: Which antiemetics should be avoided in Parkinson's disease?
  - A: Dopamine antagonists such as metoclopramide, droperidol and prochlorperazine
- Q: Which opioids to avoid in a patient taking selegiline?
  - A: Pethidine (meperidine) and tramadol: serotonergic
- Q: Which anticholinergic is preferred and why?
  - A: Glycopyrrolate; atropine can cause central anticholinergic syndrome
- Q: Which vasopressor type is preferred in patients on MAO inhibitors?
  - A: Direct-acting such as phenylephrine; ephedrine risks hypertensive crisis
