# Previous Stroke and TIA

Delay elective surgery at least 3 months after a stroke, keep antiplatelets and statins, and hold blood pressure within 20% of baseline.

## Summary
- Neurology input
- Delay elective surgery 3 months
- Continue antiplatelets
- Tight BP control, within 20% of baseline
- Neuroprotection
- NIRS
- Early neurological assessment after surgery

## Definitions
- Stroke (CVA): deficit from a cerebrovascular event (infarct or haemorrhage) lasting beyond 24hrs
- TIA: focal deficit that resolves completely within 24 hours
  - High stroke risk afterwards, about 5% per year, with mortality of 30%
  - Stratify all with ABCD2 (gives the 2 day stroke risk)

## Preoperative assessment
- Neurology and multidisciplinary input for patients at high risk of recurrence
- Risk factors: ischaemic heart disease, hypertension, previous stroke, diabetes, smoking, cholesterol, age
- Document existing neurological deficits carefully
- Ask about vertebrobasilar insufficiency brought on by posture or neck position
- Carotid duplex ultrasound if there is a bruit or significant peripheral vascular disease

## Optimisation
- Interrupt stroke drugs as little as possible
  - Continue aspirin unless bleeding risk is prohibitive; clopidogrel (Plavix) ideally continued as well
  - Continue statins
- Optimise blood pressure
- Stop smoking
- Carotid endarterectomy if stenosis > 70%, or >50% with symptoms (TIA or stroke)

## Timing of surgery
- After stroke or TIA, risk of recurrent stroke or worse deficits is higher; it falls over time as inflammation settles, haemorrhage risk drops and autoregulation recovers
- 2024 AHA/ASA: delay elective surgery 3 months
  - Medicare data: little further fall in risk between 61-90 days and 6-12 months (OR 5.0 vs 4.76), so waiting ≥6 months adds little (JAMA Surgery 2022)
- 2021 AHA/ASA: postoperative ischaemic stroke risk is high in the first 3 months, then falls and plateaus at 9 months
- Practical windows
  - < 1 month: emergency or life-saving surgery only; recurrence risk extremely high
  - 3 months: minimum for time-sensitive surgery (e.g. cancer, where a 9-month delay would worsen outcome)
  - 6–9 months: preferred for purely elective surgery (e.g. joint replacement, stable hernia) so risk is near baseline

## Intraoperative management
- Similar to neuroanaesthesia principles
- Prefer locoregional anaesthesia over GA
- Tight BP control: MAP within 20% of baseline, maintain CPP; arterial line; vasopressors, remifentanil
- Neuroprotection: avoid hypoxaemia, hypocarbia, hypotension, hyperthermia, hyperglycaemia, anaemia
- Airway
  - Avoid suxamethonium if hemiplegia persists
  - Gentle neck handling if vertebrobasilar insufficiency
- NIRS monitoring
- BIS to avoid burst suppression
- Avoid ketamine and midazolam (delirium and cognitive dysfunction risk)
- Tranexamic acid is contraindicated

## Postoperative care
- Early neurological assessment
- Restart antiplatelets early

## Flashcards

  - A: 3 months; 6–9 months preferred for purely elective surgery
- Q: When is surgery acceptable within 1 month of a stroke?
  - A: Only for emergency or life-saving procedures
- Q: Blood pressure target during surgery after previous stroke?
  - A: MAP within 20% of baseline, maintaining CPP
- Q: Carotid stenosis thresholds for endarterectomy?
