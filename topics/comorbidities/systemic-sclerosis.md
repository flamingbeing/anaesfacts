# Systemic Sclerosis

Autoimmune collagen deposition: difficult airway, Raynaud's (keep warm) and interstitial lung disease are the key issues.

## Key issues
- Difficult airway
- Raynaud's: needs warming
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD)

## Pathophysiology
- Autoimmune, widespread collagen deposition
- Abnormal cellular and immune function
  - Microvascular injury leads to T cell build-up in skin, fibroblast overgrowth and excess collagen
  - Genetic and environmental causes
- Severity varies widely
- 2 major types:
  - Limited cutaneous form
    - Commoner (60%) and milder
    - Skin changes only on the face and distal to the elbow
    - Can progress to CREST: calcinosis, Raynaud's, oesophageal dysfunction, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia
  - Diffuse cutaneous form
    - More aggressive
    - Widespread skin hardening and internal organ involvement, often lung (fibrosis leading to pulmonary hypertension)
    - High mortality

## History
- Features above
- Fatigue
- Reduced joint range of movement
- Tough skin

## Examination
- Airway: narrowed mouth opening and tight skin around the neck
- Cardiovascular: Raynaud's, pericarditis, myocardial fibrosis, conduction defects, pulmonary hypertension
- Respiratory: fibrosing alveolitis (40% in the diffuse form)
- Renal: renal failure
- GI: GORD

## Investigations
- FBC
- U&E
- PFTs
- ECG

## Medical management
- Immunosuppression (methotrexate)
- Steroids

## Anaesthetic considerations
- General: higher risk of infection
- Skin: difficult cannulation; use ultrasound
- Raynaud's: warm theatre and warm fluids
- TMJ dysfunction and reduced cervical spine movement: awake fibreoptic intubation, videolaryngoscopy or asleep fibreoptic intubation
- GI: antacids
- Respiratory: ABG, high FiO2, avoid hypercarbia and hypoxia (IPPV)
- Cardiovascular: invasive monitoring; consider echo if concerned
- Avoid nephrotoxic drugs

## Intraoperative management
- Awake fibreoptic intubation
- No consensus on GA versus regional anaesthesia
- Difficult IV access
- Lung protective ventilation
- Strict asepsis

## Flashcards
- Q: What does CREST stand for?
  - A: Calcinosis, Raynaud's, oesophageal dysfunction, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia
- Q: Which form of systemic sclerosis is commoner and how common?
  - A: Limited cutaneous form, 60%
- Q: How often does fibrosing alveolitis occur in diffuse systemic sclerosis?
  - A: 40%
- Q: Why is the airway difficult in systemic sclerosis?
  - A: Narrow mouth, tight neck skin, TMJ dysfunction and reduced cervical spine movement
