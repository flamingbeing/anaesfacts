# Obstetric Complications and Anaesthesia

Anaesthetic points for fetal distress, tocolysis, preterm labour, multiple pregnancy, fibroids and previous caesarean section.

## Fetal distress and fetal bradycardia

### Clinical findings
- Abnormal CTG: 99% accurate but not specific
- Fetal scalp blood pH <7.2
- Meconium passed in utero

### Causes
- Maternal hypotension or hypoxia
- Uterine hyperstimulation
- Placental abruption
- Cord compression
- Growth-restricted (IUGR) fetus

### Complications
- Hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy, cerebral palsy

### Intrauterine resuscitation (SPOILT)
- May be needed before an emergency caesarean section
- S: Syntocinon off
- P: Phenylephrine or other vasopressors
- O: Oxygen
- I: IV fluids
- L: Left lateral position
- T: Terbutaline (250mcg SC/IV) and other tocolysis (GTN 2 puffs SL)
- If distress persists, emergency LSCS may be needed; a crash (category 1) section needs GA

### Anaesthetic management
- Document times
- Check the fetal heart rate in theatre before starting the LSCS
- Oxygen by face mask; avoid hypoxia
- Keep MAP adequate to maintain placental perfusion
- Neonatal team standing by for resuscitation

## Tocolysis in anaesthesia
- Expect PPH if tocolysis has been given
- Terbutaline:
  - 250mcg SC/IV, given slowly
  - Side effects: tachycardia and arrhythmias, vasodilation and hypotension, tremor
- GTN:
  - IV bolus 50-200mcg
  - IV infusion
  - 2 puffs SL

## Preterm labour
- Preterm babies have less plasma protein to bind drugs, more bilirubin competing for binding sites, an immature blood-brain barrier and reduced drug metabolism
- How this changes the fetal response to maternal anaesthesia is unclear

## Twin and multiple pregnancy

### Maternal
- Physiological changes of pregnancy are exaggerated
- More supine hypotension, pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes
- Optimise with a multidisciplinary team

### Anaesthetic
- Regional anaesthesia is technically harder with the larger uterus, so fails more often
- GA carries higher risks of difficult airway and aspiration

### Surgical
- Surgery can be prolonged and complicated: the regional block must last long enough
- Higher PPH risk from the larger uterine incision and uterine atony: valid group and crossmatch, blood products available

### Neonatal
- 2 incubators and 2 neonatal resuscitation teams needed, especially for premature babies

## Uterine fibroids in pregnancy
- Pregnancy enlarges fibroids in 20–30% of cases
- Usually asymptomatic; may be painful from degeneration
- Degeneration and torsion are the commonest complications
- Perioperative complications come from mechanical effects and are generally uncommon:
  - Antepartum haemorrhage (placenta praevia, placental abruption)
  - Intrapartum problems
  - Postpartum haemorrhage (uterine atony, retained placenta)
  - More caesarean sections: dysfunctional labour, malpresentation, obstructed labour
  - Others: preterm labour, uterine inversion, puerperal sepsis

## Previous caesarean section and VBAC
- VBAC (vaginal birth after caesarean) can be attempted
- Complications: uterine rupture, placenta accreta spectrum
- Epidural analgesia may be a risk factor for unrecognised uterine rupture by masking pain
  - Epidurals are still encouraged: the benefits outweigh the risks
- Suspect uterine rupture if a woman needs frequent epidural top-ups during a trial of VBAC

## Flashcards
- Q: What does SPOILT stand for in intrauterine resuscitation?
  - A: Syntocinon off, Phenylephrine or vasopressors, Oxygen, IV fluids, Left lateral, Terbutaline or other tocolysis
- Q: What is the dose of terbutaline for acute tocolysis?
  - A: 250mcg SC/IV, given slowly
- Q: What GTN doses are used for acute tocolysis?
  - A: IV bolus 50-200mcg or 2 puffs SL
- Q: What fetal scalp blood pH suggests fetal distress?
  - A: Below 7.2
- Q: What should raise suspicion of uterine rupture during a trial of VBAC with an epidural?
  - A: Needing frequent epidural top-ups
