# Rheumatoid Arthritis

Multisystem inflammatory arthritis: the key risks are an unstable cervical spine (atlanto-axial instability), a difficult airway, and cardiorespiratory disease including ILD.

## Main concerns

- Difficult airway and unstable cervical spine (similar to ankylosing spondylitis)
  - Atlanto-axial instability: risk of neurological injury
    - Consider awake fibreoptic intubation
    - Consider flexion-extension and odontoid imaging
  - Cervical arthritis: limited neck movement
  - TMJ disease: small mouth opening; assess range before surgery
  - Cricoarytenoid arthritis: narrow glottis, cords easily injured
- Multisystem disease
  - Cardiovascular: pericarditis, pericardial effusion or tamponade; conduction defects; LV dysfunction; accelerated coronary disease; aortitis with dilated aortic root and aortic regurgitation
  - Respiratory: interstitial fibrosis, restrictive defect (including chest wall restriction); pulmonary hypertension; pleuritis and effusions
  - Neurological: peripheral neuropathy from nerve compression; carpal and tarsal tunnel syndrome common
  - Renal: chronic renal failure possible (long-term NSAIDs, amyloidosis, vasculitis)
  - Gastrointestinal: peptic ulcer (NSAIDs, steroids)
  - Haematological: chronic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia (Felty syndrome)
  - Infection: higher risk on DMARDs or immunosuppressants

## Preoperative assessment

- Airway
  - Neck movement and pain: ask the awake patient to show the head positions they tolerate without discomfort or symptoms
  - Document existing neurological symptoms and deficits carefully
- Screen for systemic disease, especially heart and lungs

## Investigations

- Cervical spine X-ray with flexion-extension views (especially if awkward head positioning is expected)
  - Screens for instability; tailor management to the findings to reduce neurological and vascular injury
  - Instability: atlanto-dens interval (ADI) >3 mm
  - Suggested indications
    - Clear symptoms: pain radiating to the occiput (earliest and commonest), shoulder and arm paraesthesia on head movement, painless sensory loss in the hands
    - Severe disease needing steroids, methotrexate or immunosuppressants
    - Disease for >10 years
- MRI cervical spine
  - When there are neurological signs, severe pain or a significant X-ray abnormality
  - Better than X-ray for nerves, soft tissue, cord compression and canal involvement; shows the odontoid peg relative to medulla and brainstem
  - Often unavailable, especially for urgent surgery

## DMARDs

- Methotrexate: continue (fewer flares, no rise in surgical site infection); check FBC and LFT
- Less evidence for other DMARDs

## Intraoperative management

- Use regional or local anaesthesia whenever suitable: avoids neck and airway handling and systemic GA effects
  - Upper limb: brachial plexus block
  - Lower limb: spinal, epidural, or plexus or individual nerve blocks
- Airway
  - LMA may be hard to place if the oral-pharyngeal angle at the tongue base is under 90°; a flexible LMA may be better
  - Intubate without further harm to a possibly unstable cervical spine
    - Avoid the sniffing position (head extended on flexed neck): can worsen anterior atlanto-axial subluxation, causing neurological injury, tetraplegia or sudden death
    - In all RA patients, even without known instability: minimal neck extension, videolaryngoscopy, manual in-line stabilisation
  - Awake fibreoptic intubation strongly advised when difficulty is expected (cervical instability, TMJ disease, reduced neck movement)
    - Much less successful in urgent or emergency settings
  - Awake surgical tracheostomy under local is an option with cricoarytenoid disease
    - Difficulties: severe fixed flexion deformity with little tracheal access, tracheal deviation, patient distress
- Drugs
  - May need stress-dose steroids
  - Avoid NSAIDs if peptic ulcer disease

## Flashcards

- Q: What atlanto-dens interval suggests atlanto-axial instability?
  - A: More than 3 mm
- Q: Indications for preoperative cervical spine imaging in RA?
  - A: Neck symptoms (occipital pain, arm paraesthesia, painless hand sensory loss), severe disease on steroids or immunosuppressants, disease >10 years
- Q: Why avoid the sniffing position in RA?
  - A: It can worsen anterior atlanto-axial subluxation, causing cord injury, tetraplegia or death
- Q: Perioperative plan for methotrexate?
  - A: Continue it; check FBC and LFT
- Q: Laryngeal problem that narrows the glottis in RA?
  - A: Cricoarytenoid arthritis
