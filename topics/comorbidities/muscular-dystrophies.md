# Muscular Dystrophies in Children

Inherited dystrophin-related muscle disorders with cardiac and respiratory involvement; avoid suxamethonium and volatiles (hyperkalaemia).

## Key points
- Avoid GA where possible
- Avoid suxamethonium and volatiles
- Avoid paralysis
- Opioid-sparing analgesia

## Introduction
- Inherited muscle disorders from abnormal or absent dystrophin
- Muscle fibres degenerate and are replaced by fat or connective tissue
- Progressive weakness of affected muscle groups; the severe Duchenne form is ultimately fatal

## Duchenne muscular dystrophy
- X-linked recessive; commonest and most severe: 1:3000 live male births
- Presents at 1-4 years: falls, waddling (Trendelenburg) gait
  - Gowers' sign: climbs up own legs with hands to stand (proximal limb and pelvic girdle weakness)
- Calf pseudohypertrophy (fat and connective tissue)
- Flexion contractures; 95% wheelchair bound by 12 years
- Once in a wheelchair, scoliosis develops fast and worsens respiratory muscle weakness
- Distorted diaphragm leads to oesophageal reflux; with poor cough, aspiration risk rises
- Cardiac involvement
  - ECG: tall R waves, deep Q waves in V3-6, prolonged P-R interval, sinus tachycardia
  - Persistent tachycardia, mitral valve prolapse (25%), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (over age 10 years), wall motion abnormalities, sudden death
- Death usually from cardiorespiratory failure in the third decade
- 30% have intellectual disability (non-progressive)
- Diagnosis
  - Clinical features
  - CPK 10 times normal in children not walking by 18 months or with worsening gait
  - Genetic testing (chromosome Xp21.3)
  - Muscle biopsy

## Becker muscular dystrophy
- X-linked recessive, rarer (1:60000) and milder than Duchenne; same dystrophin gene at Xp21
- Later onset, slower progression; up to 50% survive beyond 40 years
- Intellectual disability less common
- Heart may be involved
- CPK, EMG and biopsy may look like Duchenne

## Emery-Dreifuss muscular dystrophy
- X-linked recessive; humeral, peroneal and cardiac muscle
- Heart block and sudden death
- Achilles tendon and elbow contractures
- CPK may be normal
- Gene abnormality at Xp28

## Congenital muscular dystrophy
- Floppy baby, or myopathic arthrogryposis
- Autosomal recessive
- Severe form may be fatal; milder form presents with contractures and scoliosis

## Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy
- Autosomal dominant; severity varies widely within families
- Relatively benign, slowly progressive, later onset
- Upper limb abductor weakness, facial weakness, scapular winging, sensorineural deafness, retinopathy

## Autosomal recessive limb girdle dystrophy
- Wasting weakness, variable progression; often wheelchair bound by 30 years
- Both sexes
- May have calf hypertrophy; CPK high

## Preoperative assessment
- Airway: possible macroglossia, limited cervical spine movement
- Functional status (walks with help or wheelchair bound)
- Cardiac: ECG and TTE
- Respiratory: PFTs and ABG to grade risk and need for postoperative ventilation
  - Poor cough, kyphoscoliosis and respiratory weakness lead to recurrent chest infections
- Aspiration risk (oropharyngeal weakness)
- Contractures, skin condition
- Optimise lung function

## Intraoperative management
- Regional or local anaesthesia preferred
  - Spinal, epidural and caudal have been used without problems
- Thorough preoxygenation
- Consider invasive monitoring if cardiac function is poor
- Contraindicated (hyperkalaemia risk)
  - Suxamethonium
  - Volatiles: use TIVA
- Non-depolarising relaxants: variable response but safe; use with caution
  - Reduced doses
  - Neuromuscular monitoring
- Opioid-sparing analgesia

## Postoperative management
- Assess fitness for extubation: awake, sitting up, fully reversed
- Disposition: HDU or ICU

## Flashcards
- Q: Incidence of Duchenne muscular dystrophy?
  - A: 1:3000 live male births
- Q: Why are suxamethonium and volatiles contraindicated in muscular dystrophy?
  - A: Risk of hyperkalaemia; use TIVA
- Q: What proportion of Duchenne patients are wheelchair bound by 12 years?
  - A: 95%
- Q: CPK level in Duchenne?
  - A: About 10 times normal
- Q: Extubation conditions after GA in muscular dystrophy?
  - A: Awake, sitting up, fully reversed; HDU or ICU afterwards
