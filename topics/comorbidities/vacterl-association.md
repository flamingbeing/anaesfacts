# VACTERL Association

Non-random cluster of birth defects; screen for all components and beware occult spinal dysraphism before caudals.

## Components
- V: vertebral anomalies (70%): missing or malformed vertebrae, missing or extra ribs, scoliosis, sacral agenesis
- A: anorectal malformation (80%): anal atresia, imperforate anus
- C: cardiac defects (53%): most often VSD, also ASD and tetralogy of Fallot
- TE: tracheo-oesophageal fistula or oesophageal atresia (70%)
- R: renal anomalies (53%): renal dysplasia, vesicoureteric reflux, hypospadias
- L: limb anomalies (65%): radial agenesis, polydactyly, syndactyly, club foot

## Background
- Intelligence usually normal
- Cause unknown in most; probably multifactorial
- Not considered hereditary

## Screening for associated defects
- Vertebral: X-ray, ultrasound and/or CT or MRI of the spine
- Anal atresia: examination, abdominal ultrasound
- Cardiac: ECG (arrhythmias), echocardiogram
- Tracheo-oesophageal: examination
- Renal: renal ultrasound
- Limb: examination, X-rays

## Anaesthetic considerations
- Caudal and neuraxial blocks need care (e.g. spinal ultrasound) or should be avoided: occult spinal dysraphism is common
