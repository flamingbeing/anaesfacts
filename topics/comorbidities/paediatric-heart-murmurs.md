# Paediatric Heart Murmurs

A murmur found for the first time at preoperative assessment: decide whether it is innocent (proceed) or pathological (defer for cardiology).

## Background
- Most pathological murmurs are found in the neonatal period; rarely, one first appears at preoperative assessment
- Parents and the family doctor usually know about innocent and abnormal murmurs, and they are documented
- Children with known, often treated, congenital heart disease are common; the notes hold what is needed and no extra tests are needed on the day
- The difficulty is a murmur heard for the first time at preoperative assessment

## New murmur
- Common: functional or flow murmurs occur at some time in up to 50% of normal children; most are innocent
- Innocent murmur: child has no symptoms, normal growth and normal exercise tolerance
- Symptoms suggesting pathology: failure to thrive, poor exercise tolerance, cyanotic episodes

## Innocent versus pathological features
- Innocent (the 7 S's, e.g. Still's murmur): soft, sweet, short, systolic, small, single (normal heart sounds), louder supine
- Pathological features:
  - Loud (grade 4-6) or with a thrill (versus soft)
  - Harsh (versus sweet)
  - Pansystolic or late systolic (versus short, mid-systolic)
  - Diastolic (versus systolic)
  - Heard widely over the precordium, with radiation (versus small area)
  - Abnormal heart sounds, e.g. fixed split S2 in ASD (versus normal sounds)
  - No change with posture (versus louder supine)
  - Others: praecordial heave, weak femoral pulses

## Management
- Innocent-sounding murmur: either proceed (antibiotic prophylaxis if surgery will cause bacteraemia) or defer for investigation
  - Depends on anaesthetist preference or local policy
  - ECG for ventricular hypertrophy may help
  - CXR is usually unhelpful
  - If surgery goes ahead, refer for investigation afterwards so the murmur is classified and documented
- Any pathological feature: defer surgery and arrange paediatric cardiology review first
  - Also seek cardiology opinion if the child is younger than 1 year
  - Urgent surgery with an uncertain murmur and no cardiology available: decide on clinical status, and give antibiotic prophylaxis

## Flashcards
- Q: How common are flow murmurs in normal children?
  - A: Up to 50% at some time; most are innocent.
- Q: Name the 7 S's of an innocent murmur.
  - A: Soft, sweet, short, systolic, small, single (normal sounds), louder supine.
- Q: Below what age should a new murmur prompt cardiology review?
  - A: Younger than 1 year.
- Q: Urgent surgery, uncertain murmur, no cardiologist: what to give?
  - A: Antibiotic prophylaxis; proceed based on clinical status.
- Q: Murmur grade that suggests pathology?
  - A: Loud, grade 4-6, or a thrill.
