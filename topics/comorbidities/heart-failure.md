# Heart Failure

Assess compensation, keep euvolaemic, reduce afterload, induce slowly and support contractility.

## Summary
- Grade with NYHA class and NT-proBNP
- Neuraxial techniques help by reducing afterload
- Careful, goal-directed fluids with cardiac output monitoring
- Slow, titrated induction; consider arterial line, CVP, inotropes
- Optimise myocardial oxygen supply and demand
- Drugs: withhold SGLT2 inhibitors for 72hrs
- Patient may be "wet" (congested) or "cold" (poorly perfused)

## Preoperative assessment
- Elective non-cardiac surgery is not advised in new or decompensated heart failure
  - Delay elective surgery by 1-3 months
- Symptoms
  - Effort tolerance
  - Orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
  - Pedal oedema
- NYHA classification
  - Class I: no symptoms with physical activity
  - Class II: symptoms with ordinary activity; slight limitation
  - Class III: symptoms with less than ordinary activity; marked limitation
  - Class IV: symptoms with any activity or at rest
- Killip classification
  - Class I: no clinical signs of heart failure
  - Class II: crackles or wheeze in less than the lower half of the lung fields, or S3
  - Class III: crackles over more than half the lung fields
  - Class IV: cardiogenic shock
- Investigations
  - ECG: arrhythmias
  - TTE: LVEF, regional wall motion, functional mitral regurgitation, RV function, PASP
  - MIBI stress EF
  - CT coronary angiogram or coronary angiography if revascularisation is being considered
  - NT-proBNP: ESC 2022 advises it for prognosis as well as screening

## Optimisation
- Aim for euvolaemia; watch fluid balance closely
  - Titrate diuretics
- Plan management of any CIED

## Intraoperative management
- Monitoring
  - 5-lead ECG with ST analysis
  - Arterial line before induction; consider CVP
  - Consider FloTrac or TOE to guide stroke volume and fluid responsiveness
  - Urinary catheter
- Technique
  - Neuraxial helps through afterload reduction
- Induction
  - Slow and titrated
    - Vasopressors (noradrenaline) ready
    - Inotrope (e.g. adrenaline) may be needed if cardiac output is inadequate
  - Poor tolerance of apnoea if pulmonary oedema present
- Ventilation
  - PEEP reduces pulmonary congestion and improves oxygenation
- Fluids: careful and goal directed; avoid overload
- Haemodynamic goals
  - Preload: maintain adequate filling, avoid overload
  - Afterload: reduce
  - Contractility: maintain, support with inotropes
  - Normal heart rate, sinus rhythm
- Analgesia: good pain control prevents sympathetic surges
- Extubation: supplemental oxygen; consider extubating onto NIV

## Postoperative management
- Strict fluid input and output monitoring
- Treat pain well: pain and its haemodynamic effects can worsen heart failure

## Flashcards
- Q: How long should SGLT2 inhibitors be withheld before surgery?
  - A: 72hrs.
- Q: How long should elective surgery be delayed after new or decompensated heart failure?
  - A: 1-3 months.
- Q: Haemodynamic goals in heart failure?
  - A: Maintain preload, reduce afterload, support contractility, normal heart rate, sinus rhythm.
- Q: What does ESC 2022 recommend NT-proBNP for?
  - A: Prognosis as well as screening.
