# Anorexia Nervosa

Psychologically driven weight loss with multisystem effects: bradycardia and arrhythmia, fragile myocardium, electrolyte loss, slow gastric emptying, hypothermia and altered drug handling.

## Background
- Strict, psychologically driven weight loss by restricting food, sometimes with exercise and purging
  - BMI <17.5, or 15% below expected weight
  - Prevalence 0.3-1%; 10% are male
  - Depression, anxiety and OCD each in about 60%

## Cardiovascular
- Sinus bradycardia and hypotension; the slow rate reflects the adaptive fall in basal metabolic rate in starvation
- More arrhythmias
  - Usually sinus rhythm, but ECG changes in >80% of strict dieters (electrolytes, drugs or starvation)
  - AV block, ST depression, T wave inversion, QT prolongation
- Starvation impairs the myocardium: give fluids carefully (risk of heart failure); consider echo and a CVC to avoid overload
- More mitral valve prolapse, possibly from loss of LV volume and mass

## Respiratory
- Metabolic alkalosis from vomiting can slow breathing
- Low lung compliance from starvation

## Renal
- Proteinuria and reduced GFR common
- Dehydration can lead to renal stones

## Electrolytes
- Vomiting: low Na, K, Cl and Mg; metabolic alkalosis
- Low Ca from poor intake
- Low PO4, K and Mg in refeeding syndrome
- Laxative abuse
- Diuretic abuse: severe hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia

## Gastrointestinal
- Low albumin very common
- Slow gastric emptying, which recovers with adequate nutrition
- Dental caries, periodontitis
- Fatty liver, hepatomegaly, occasionally cirrhosis
- Raised amylase from pancreatic hypersecretion
- Salivary gland hyperplasia
- Mallory-Weiss tear, oesophagitis, gastritis

## Musculoskeletal and skin
- Myopathy and myalgia, usually from electrolytes (muscle weakness is the commonest sign of hypophosphataemia); can cause respiratory failure
- Osteoporosis, osteopenia, stress fractures
- Poor wound healing
- Lanugo hair (heat conservation)
- Russell's sign

## Endocrine
- Amenorrhoea with low FSH, LH and GnRH; periods usually stop below BMI 15
- Raised cortisol and GH (stress)
- Low glucose and insulin; hypoglycaemia
- Low T3 and T4 from reduced thyroid activity

## Haematology, immune and neurology
- Thrombocytopenia, leucopenia, immune dysfunction
- Reduced cognition; grey and white matter changes
- Mild neuropathies and general weakness common
- Seizures from electrolyte disturbance

## Temperature
- Often hypothermic, commonly <36.3C: low metabolic rate, little insulating fat, impaired thermoregulation and shivering

## Other
- Possible illicit drug use for weight loss (e.g. amphetamines)

## Preoperative assessment
- History including laxatives, drugs and alcohol; collateral history helps
- Investigations
  - FBC
  - U&E, Mg, Ca, PO4
  - Creatinine, urine dipstick
  - LFT, glucose
  - ECG in all
  - Echo if history or examination suggests myocardial dysfunction

## Anaesthetic management
- Consider RSI, antacid and a gastric tube (slow gastric emptying)
- Position carefully: nerves, skin and bone are vulnerable
- Monitor temperature throughout; active warming
- Drug handling
  - Weigh every patient and reduce doses accordingly
  - Low albumin means less protein binding and more free drug
  - Metabolism and clearance may be slower (low metabolic rate, reduced renal function)
  - Hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia can prolong non-depolarising block: consider neuromuscular monitoring

## Flashcards
- Q: BMI threshold for anorexia nervosa?
  - A: BMI <17.5, or 15% below expected weight
- Q: How common are ECG abnormalities in strict dieters?
  - A: Over 80%: AV block, ST depression, T inversion, QT prolongation
- Q: Electrolyte pattern from vomiting?
  - A: Low Na, K, Cl and Mg with metabolic alkalosis
- Q: Why consider RSI in anorexia?
  - A: Slow gastric emptying
- Q: Why care with IV fluids?
  - A: Starved myocardium is prone to heart failure
