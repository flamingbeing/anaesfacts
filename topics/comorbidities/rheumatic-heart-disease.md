# Rheumatic Fever and Rheumatic Heart Disease

Autoimmune reaction to group A streptococcus that damages the mitral and aortic valves; anaesthesia is planned around the valve lesion.

## Overview

- Autoimmune disease triggered by group A streptococcus
- Age 5-15 years
- 1-3 weeks after a sore throat
- Leads to mitral and aortic valve disease

## Diagnosis: Jones criteria

- Major
  - Carditis
  - Migratory polyarthritis
  - Subcutaneous nodules
  - Erythema marginatum
  - Chorea
- Minor
  - Arthralgia
  - Fever
  - Previous rheumatic fever
  - Raised ESR or CRP
  - Prolonged PR interval
  - Evidence of recent group A streptococcal infection (throat swab or titres)

## Investigations

- ASO titres
- Echo
- CXR

## Management

- Antibiotics
- Aspirin and steroids for pain and inflammation
- Heart failure treatment: digoxin, ACE inhibitor, diuretics, beta-blockers, O2
- Endocarditis prophylaxis if indicated
- Chorea: phenobarbitone, haloperidol

## Intraoperative management

- Manage according to the specific valve lesion
- Consider antibiotic prophylaxis (newer AHA/ACC guidelines are more restrictive)

## Flashcards

- Q: Typical age and timing of rheumatic fever?
  - A: 5-15 years, 1-3 weeks after a streptococcal sore throat
- Q: Major Jones criteria?
  - A: Carditis, migratory polyarthritis, subcutaneous nodules, erythema marginatum, chorea
- Q: Valves affected by rheumatic heart disease?
  - A: Mitral and aortic
