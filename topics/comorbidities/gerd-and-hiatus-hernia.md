# GERD and Hiatus Hernia

Reflux is an aspiration risk: give acid prophylaxis (PPI) and secure the airway with RSI and an ETT.

## Anaesthetic implications
- Main concern is aspiration
- Mucosal complications (oesophagitis, stricture) can dilate the oesophagus and add to the risk
- Three steps are needed for pulmonary aspiration:
  - Gastric contents reflux into the oesophagus
  - Contents reach the pharynx
  - Laryngeal reflexes are blunted (sedation or general anaesthesia)
- Aspiration pneumonitis is thought to need both:
  - Aspirate volume of at least 0.4 ml/kg (about 30 ml in a 70 kg adult)
  - Gastric pH below 2.5
- Extra-oesophageal and respiratory complications also matter
  - Laryngitis, bronchitis, bronchospasm, recurrent pneumonia, progressive pulmonary fibrosis

## Preoperative management
- Reflux medication may be given before surgery, depending on the operation and anaesthetic
- H2 antagonists reduce acid secretion and raise gastric pH
  - Cimetidine: onset 1-1.5 hours, lasts about 3 hours
  - Ranitidine: 4-6 times as potent as cimetidine, fewer side effects
    - Example regimen: ranitidine 150 mg at 10 pm and 6 am
  - Famotidine and nizatidine: can be given IV; similar to ranitidine but longer acting
- Proton pump inhibitors: usually oral, the night before and the morning of surgery
- Sodium citrate: oral non-particulate antacid that raises gastric pH
  - Can be combined with a prokinetic (such as metoclopramide) shortly before induction
  - Commonly used in diabetic, morbidly obese and pregnant patients

## Intraoperative management
- Rapid sequence induction with prompt tracheal intubation is usual
  - Cricoid pressure applied to help reduce aspiration risk
- Suxamethonium raises both lower oesophageal sphincter pressure and intragastric pressure
  - Barrier pressure (LES pressure minus intragastric pressure) is unchanged
- Tracheal intubation is essential when aspiration is a risk
  - The ETT protects against aspiration better than any other airway device

## Flashcards
- Q: What aspirate volume and pH are thought necessary for aspiration pneumonitis?
  - A: At least 0.4 ml/kg (about 30 ml in a 70 kg adult) with pH below 2.5
- Q: How does suxamethonium affect barrier pressure?
  - A: No change: LES and intragastric pressures both rise
- Q: Onset and duration of cimetidine?
  - A: Onset 1-1.5 hours; lasts about 3 hours
- Q: How much more potent is ranitidine than cimetidine?
  - A: 4-6 times
- Q: Which airway device best protects against aspiration?
  - A: A cuffed endotracheal tube
