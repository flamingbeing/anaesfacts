# Dacrocystorhinostomy

Tear duct surgery: the duct is probed, a drainage tube inserted and a stoma made between the tear duct and nasopharynx.

## Preoperative assessment
- Standard assessment
- Can be done under local anaesthesia alone if the surgeon is experienced

## Intraoperative management
- Higher risk of bacteraemia: patients with cardiac defects should get prophylactic antibiotics
- Supine, head up
- Airway: RAE ETT or LMA
- Spontaneous ventilation or IPPV
- Blood runs into the nasopharynx
  - Topical vasoconstrictors
  - Throat pack
- Moderate hypotension with slight head-up tilt
- Before extubation, suction and inspect the airway under direct vision

## Postoperative care
- Simple analgesia
- Topical local anaesthetic at the end of surgery

## Flashcards
- Q: Which patients need antibiotic prophylaxis for dacrocystorhinostomy?
  - A: Those with cardiac defects, as the procedure carries a higher risk of bacteraemia.
- Q: How is blood in the nasopharynx managed during DCR?
  - A: Topical vasoconstrictors, a throat pack, moderate hypotension with slight head up, and suction and direct inspection before extubation.
- Q: Can DCR be done without GA?
  - A: Yes, under local anaesthesia alone with an experienced surgeon.
