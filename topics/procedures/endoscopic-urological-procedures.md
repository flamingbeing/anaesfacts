# Endoscopic Urological Procedures

Endoscopic surgery on the lower urinary tract: brief, lithotomy, often day case, little postoperative pain.

## Overview
- An endoscope is passed to view and treat the lower urinary tract
- Operations include:
  1. Cystoscopy
  2. TURP (often elderly patients)
  3. Bladder neck incision
  4. TURBT (common in smokers)
  5. Ureteroscopy
  6. Stone removal
  7. DJ stent insertion
- Indications: investigating haematuria, biopsy, stone extraction, stricture treatment, resection of bladder tumours, ureteric stenting for obstruction

## Surgical factors
- Usually in **lithotomy**
- Irrigation fluid is needed to see clearly and wash out debris
- Usually **brief, under 1 h**

## Anaesthetic management
- Little postoperative analgesia needed; **short-acting opioids** are enough
- Often **day case**, home a few hours after surgery
  - GA usually preferred for day cases
  - A short-acting spinal is an alternative
- Spinal or epidural for lower genitourinary tract surgery needs a block to **T10 or higher**

## Flashcards
- Q: What block height is needed for neuraxial anaesthesia in lower genitourinary tract surgery?
  - A: T10 or higher
- Q: How long do endoscopic urological procedures usually take?
  - A: Usually under 1 h
- Q: Which anaesthetic is usually preferred for day-case endoscopic urology?
  - A: GA, though a short-acting spinal may suit
