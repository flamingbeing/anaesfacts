# Airway stent insertion

Shared, often tubeless airway (rigid bronchoscopy) in patients whose airway may close completely during induction or stenting.

## Key points
- Shared airway
- Rigid bronchoscopy: no tube
- Airway can occlude during stenting

## Stents
- Used for
  - Intrinsic airway lesions
  - Extrinsic compression
  - Tracheomalacia
- Mostly to palliate malignancy
- Expanding metal mesh that becomes epithelialised
- Usually placed via rigid bronchoscopy
- Flexible bronchoscope sometimes used
  - Diagnostic
  - Reaches further distally
  - Can also deploy a metal stent
  - Use the largest ETT and hold it while they scope

## Preoperative assessment
- Look for airway obstruction: stridor, tracheal tumour, foreign body
- Look for vascular compression
- Decide safe or unsafe
  - Unsafe: ECMO
  - Safe: GA and paralysis, step by step
- Risk: obstruction may worsen after induction or paralysis

## Intraoperative management
- Supine, head and neck extended
- Technique
  - GA usually needed for rigid bronchoscopy or high-grade tracheal obstruction
  - Flexible bronchoscopy usually manageable with good airway topicalisation
- Anaesthetic options
  - TIVA (propofol, midazolam, opioid)
  - Intermittent suxamethonium, atracurium or mivacurium (very stimulating procedure)
  - Spontaneous ventilation possible (e.g. tracheo-oesophageal fistula patch repair)
- Topicalise larynx and trachea well with 4-5ml lidocaine 4% before induction
- Ventilation options
  - IPPV through the side port of a Storz bronchoscope
  - Jet ventilation
  - Apnoeic oxygenation: watch for falling oxygen, rising CO2, and awareness if relying on volatiles
  - Spontaneous ventilation with volatile: watch and feel the abdomen
- Coordinate ventilation with the surgeon
- Very stimulating: marked hypertensive response
  - Short-acting opioids and muscle relaxants
  - Suxamethonium works well but repeat doses cause bradycardia
  - Mivacurium often gives too little block
- At the end: insert an LMA, then reverse or wait for waking

## Postoperative management
- After biopsy: lie the patient biopsied side down (keeps blood out of the good lung)
- Sit up once awake
- Watch for bleeding and airway obstruction: can deteriorate fast
- Local anaesthetic on the cords weakens the cough

## Flashcards
- Q: How should the airway be topicalised before induction for rigid bronchoscopy and stenting?
  - A: 4-5 ml lidocaine 4% to larynx and trachea
- Q: What is the plan if the airway is judged unsafe for GA?
  - A: ECMO before induction
- Q: How can you ventilate through a rigid bronchoscope?
  - A: IPPV via the Storz side port, jet ventilation, apnoeic oxygenation, or spontaneous ventilation
- Q: Which way should the patient lie after a lung biopsy?
  - A: Biopsied side down
