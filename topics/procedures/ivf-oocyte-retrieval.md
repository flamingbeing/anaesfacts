# IVF Oocyte Retrieval and Embryo Transfer

Short, high-turnover day cases: keep pain and PONV low and limit drug exposure to the oocytes.

## IVF procedures
- Ovarian stimulation and monitoring
- Ultrasound-guided oocyte retrieval (transvaginal follicle aspiration)
- Embryo transfer back into the uterus

## Patient population
- Usually young and well
- May be on antidepressants or anxiolytics
- Cancer patients: oocyte retrieval is usually done before chemotherapy or radiotherapy

## Goals
- Minimise pain and anxiety
  - Patient comfort
  - Keeps the patient still, lowering the risk of puncturing vessels
- Improve pregnancy rate
- Avoid teratogenicity

## Anaesthetic technique options
- Sedation
- GA, TIVA
- Regional: spinal, epidural, paracervical block
- PCA
- Alternative: acupuncture

## Choice of drugs
- Main concerns
  - Entry into follicular fluid and any harm to fertilisation, cleavage and pregnancy rates: ideally minimal
  - Length of drug exposure: ideally short
- Propofol
  - Widely used in assisted reproduction
  - Effects on fertilisation, cleavage and pregnancy rates well studied
- Opioids
  - Fentanyl or alfentanil work well combined with propofol
  - Fentanyl barely enters follicular fluid
  - Remifentanil
- Midazolam
  - Small amounts reach follicular fluid; no proven harm so far
- Other options: ketamine, thiopental
- Avoid
  - Nitrous oxide: inactivates methionine synthase
  - Volatile agents: lower cleavage rates and more miscarriages

## Postoperative care
- PONV is common; its frequency tracks peak plasma oestradiol
- Usually a day case

## Oocyte retrieval
- Once laparoscopic; now transvaginal
- Pain comes from the needle puncturing vaginal skin and ovarian capsule, and from manipulation inside the ovary throughout
- Pain can resemble menstrual pain
- General anaesthesia
  - Airway: LMA; or hold a face mask; pressure support ventilation (short case)
  - Aim for a smooth anaesthetic
    - Propofol plus fentanyl, keeping spontaneous ventilation (e.g. propofol 120 mg, 1.5 mg/kg; fentanyl 50 mcg, 1 mcg/kg)
    - Lidocaine
    - Two antiemetics
    - Analgesia: paracetamol, parecoxib
    - Co-amoxiclav (Augmentin)
  - Monitoring and access: standard; a 22G cannula is enough
  - Equipment: nothing special
  - Maintenance: sevoflurane is still typically used (short case)
  - Duration: 15 min
  - Blood loss: minimal

## Embryo transfer
- Sedation or monitored anaesthesia care
- Airway: none needed
- Drugs
  - TCI propofol sedation, effect-site target 1.5-2.5, plus fentanyl 0.5 mcg/kg
  - Ondansetron
  - IV analgesia not needed
  - Antibiotics not needed
- Monitoring and access: standard; a 22G cannula is enough
- Equipment: nothing special
- Often done without an anaesthetist

## Flashcards
- Q: Why is nitrous oxide avoided for oocyte retrieval?
  - A: It inactivates methionine synthase.
- Q: What is a typical induction for oocyte retrieval?
  - A: Propofol 1.5 mg/kg plus fentanyl 1 mcg/kg, keeping spontaneous ventilation.
- Q: Which opioid barely enters follicular fluid?
  - A: Fentanyl.
- Q: What is PONV frequency after IVF procedures linked to?
  - A: Peak plasma oestradiol level.
- Q: What sedation suits embryo transfer?
  - A: TCI propofol effect-site 1.5-2.5 plus fentanyl 0.5 mcg/kg.
