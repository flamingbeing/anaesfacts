# Liver Transplantation

Recipient anaesthesia for liver transplant: careful work-up, then three surgical phases with major bleeding, IVC clamping and reperfusion instability.

## Indications
- Acute liver failure (ALF): rapid-onset encephalopathy and coagulopathy (INR ≥ 1.5) without prior liver disease
- End-stage chronic liver disease (cirrhosis):
  - Refractory complications: ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, portopulmonary hypertension, hepatopulmonary syndrome
  - HCC
- Liver-based metabolic disease: alpha1-antitrypsin deficiency, Wilson's disease, haemochromatosis, glycogen storage disease

## Scoring
- MELD-Na: bilirubin, INR, creatinine, sodium; predicts 3-month waiting-list mortality
- Child-Pugh: albumin, bilirubin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy; class C = severe dysfunction
- King's College criteria for ALF

## Contraindications
- MELD < 15
- Fulminant hepatic failure with sustained ICP > 50 mmHg or CPP < 40 mmHg
- Severe systemic disease: severe cardiac or lung disease, irreversible brain damage, uncontrolled sepsis
- Extrahepatic malignancy, HCC with metastases, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma
- Anatomical abnormality that makes transplant technically impossible
- Social: ongoing alcohol or drug misuse, persistent non-adherence, no social support
- Relative: BMI < 18 or BMI > 40

## Effects of liver failure
- Cardiovascular:
  - Hyperdynamic circulation (high CO, low SVR)
  - Total body fluid overloaded but intravascularly dry
  - Portopulmonary hypertension (POPH): not a contraindication but raises mortality; work up with right heart catheter; manage with PA catheter and nitric oxide
  - Cirrhotic cardiomyopathy
- Respiratory:
  - Hepatopulmonary syndrome (HPS); effusions and ascites give a restrictive pattern
  - HPS: trial of oxygen to confirm saturation improves before considering transplant
  - Hepatic hydrothorax is usually not drained before surgery
- Neurological: hepatic encephalopathy; raised ICP in fulminant liver failure
- GI: ascites increases aspiration risk
- Renal: hepatorenal syndrome (HRS) type 1 (acute), type 2 (chronic)
- Hyponatraemia reflects severity
- Haematological:
  - Thrombocytopenia
  - Rebalanced haemostasis: both pro- and anticoagulants are low, so PT/PTT poorly predict bleeding
  - Usually not corrected before surgery unless severe
- Refer early; speed up evaluation in rapid decompensation or ALF

## Preoperative assessment

### Cardiac
- 12-lead ECG, TTE, stress MIBI
- Right heart catheter if pulmonary hypertension or POPH suspected (PASP >40):
  - mPAP < 35 mmHg: safe
  - mPAP 35–45 mmHg: high risk; co-manage with cardiology (vasodilators)
  - mPAP > 45 mmHg: absolute contraindication
- CT coronary angiogram, then coronary angiography, if at risk of CAD
- CPET for high-risk or marginal cases

### Respiratory
- CXR
- PFTs (restrictive pattern common with ascites)
- If HPS suspected: contrast echo and lung perfusion scan to detect intrapulmonary vascular dilatation (IPVD)

### Other
- Dental review

## Donor factors
- Graft quality depends on:
  - Living donor vs brain-dead donor vs donation after cardiac death (DCD)
  - Living donor grafts are usually better, but vascular anastomoses can be difficult (especially in children); watch early graft function
  - Donor age, degree of fatty liver, cold ischaemia time, retrieval damage, split liver
- These affect reperfusion haemodynamics, bleeding and early graft function

### Ischaemia times
- Warm ischaemia time (WIT): ideally <30min, cut-off 45min
- Cold ischaemia time (CIT): ideally <8hr, cut-off 12h
  - In living donation the right lobe graft is not delivered until the recipient is almost ready, to shorten CIT
- Liver sinusoidal endothelial cells are most sensitive to cold ischaemia; hepatocytes and cholangiocytes to warm ischaemia

## Optimisation in acute liver failure
- Neurological:
  - Target Na 145–155 mmol/L
  - Mannitol (0.5–1 g/kg) for suspected ICP surges
  - EEG for seizures
  - Consider MARS for hepatic encephalopathy
- Cardiovascular: target MAP 65–70 mmHg; albumin for volume
- Respiratory: intubate for HE grade 2; lung-protective ventilation
- GI: lactulose aiming for bowels open ≥ 3 times a day; stress ulcer prophylaxis
- Renal: early CRRT for metabolic acidosis or fluid overload; terlipressin for HRS
- Coagulation: vitamin K 10mg IV for 3 days (x3); correct coagulopathy (FFP, cryo, platelets) only for active bleeding or before a procedure
- Metabolic: target glucose 4–6 mmol/L; reduce protein if ICP raised or ammonia > 150umol; consider high-volume plasma exchange (HVPE)
- Specific treatment:
  - N-acetylcysteine for paracetamol overdose (may also be given generally)
  - Entecavir or tenofovir for hepatitis B
  - Steroids for autoimmune hepatitis
  - Anticoagulation for Budd-Chiari

## Surgical stages (deceased donor)
- Donor retrieval: midline laparotomy, aortic and portal flush with cold HTK or UW solution; cold ischaemia starts
- Bench work: graft inspection and vessel preparation on ice
- Recipient explant: total hepatectomy (dissection and anhepatic phases)
- Recipient implant (neohepatic phase)

## Monitoring and lines
- CVP and CO monitoring (FloTrac); PA catheter if pulmonary hypertension
- Consider TEE (varices may not be an absolute contraindication)
- 2x arterial lines (ideally radial): one for sampling, one for monitoring; frequent ABGs
- Large-bore IV access (PA catheter sheath, dialysis catheter, rapid infusion catheter) and rapid infuser
- Point-of-care coagulation testing (TEG or ROTEM) in theatre

## Intraoperative phases (3 phases)

### Dissection phase
- Liver mobilisation and skeletonisation of the porta hepatis
- Haemorrhage from varices and adhesions, worse with coagulopathy
  - Keep CVP low; too much fluid or blood product causes splanchnic congestion and more bleeding
- Haemodynamic instability from ascitic drainage and low SVR

### Anhepatic phase
- Starts when the hepatic arteries are divided; no liver metabolic function
- Final mobilisation, IVC clamping (classical or piggyback), explant, new liver in (WIT starts), vascular anastomoses
- Partial or full IVC clamping reduces venous return and preload
  - Full (classical) clamping gives more instability and renal injury; not usually used with living donor grafts; do a test clamp
  - Venovenous bypass is rarely needed
- Coagulopathy and bleeding: no clotting factor production, low fibrinogen
- Hypocalcaemia and metabolic acidosis (no citrate or lactate metabolism)
- Hypoglycaemia

### Neohepatic (reperfusion) phase
- Starts with graft reperfusion: IVC clamp released, then portal vein clamp, then hepatic artery anastomosis and biliary reconstruction
- Sudden rise in preload when the IVC clamp is released
- Post-reperfusion syndrome (PRS): >30% fall in MAP within 5 min of reperfusion, with or without bradycardia
  - Causes: hyperkalaemia and acidosis; vasoactive and inflammatory mediators (SVR falls, PVR rises); cold graft depressing the myocardium
  - Treatment and prevention: calcium chloride, small prophylactic doses of adrenaline, vasopressors
  - Methylprednisolone 10mg/kg protects against reperfusion injury
- Fibrinolysis after reperfusion
- Signs of good graft function: bile production, falling lactate, calcium normalising, haemodynamics settling

## Maintenance
- Haemodynamics: MAP >60; vasopressors
- Bleeding and coagulopathy:
  - Avoid the lethal diamond (hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy, hypocalcaemia): calcium, active warming
  - TXA
  - Fine balance between bleeding and thrombosis: guide with ROTEM
  - Antithrombin III
- Judicious fluids and blood products
- ABG hourly, then q30mins after the anhepatic phase: Na, K+, Ca2+, lactate, acid-base, glucose
- Renal protection:
  - Aim urine output >0.5ml/kg/hr
  - Mannitol, furosemide
  - Continue a preoperative terlipressin infusion for HRS until the native liver is out
- Octreotide reduces splanchnic flow and portal pressure

## Postoperative care
- Most go to ICU intubated; some centres extubate on table

## Patients with a previous liver transplant
- Check graft function (LFTs)
- Immunosuppression complications such as renal impairment:
  - Strict asepsis
  - Stress-dose steroids
  - Leukodepleted, with or without irradiated, red cells
- Protect the graft: maintain MAP

## Flashcards
- Q: How is post-reperfusion syndrome defined?
  - A: >30% fall in MAP within 5 min of reperfusion, with or without bradycardia
- Q: What mPAP is an absolute contraindication to liver transplant?
  - A: > 45 mmHg (35–45 mmHg is high risk)
- Q: What are the ideal and cut-off warm and cold ischaemia times?
  - A: WIT ideally <30min (cut-off 45min); CIT ideally <8hr (cut-off 12h)
- Q: What sodium target is used for ICP control in acute liver failure?
  - A: Na 145–155 mmol/L
- Q: What dose of methylprednisolone is given at reperfusion?
  - A: 10mg/kg
