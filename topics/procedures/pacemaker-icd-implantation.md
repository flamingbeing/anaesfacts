# Pacemaker and ICD Implantation

Anaesthesia or sedation for permanent pacemaker (PPM), implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD) and CRT insertion, often in a remote location with VF testing.

## General considerations
- Patients range from fit young adults with normal contractility to elderly patients with poor cardiac function
- Procedure ranges from simple (transvenous sensing and shocking leads) to complex (PPM replacement; coronary sinus cannulation to reach the LV in CRT)
- Access is usually via the left cephalic vein, with leads positioned under fluoroscopy
- Strict asepsis to avoid infecting the device
- Track the total local anaesthetic dose given by the cardiologist

## Key considerations
- Patient: indication for the device, cardiac function, comorbidities
- Surgery: remote location, sterility, local anaesthetic dose, VF testing, complications, day surgery
- Anaesthesia: monitoring, resuscitation drugs and equipment, defibrillation pads, sedation vs GA, disposition

## Preoperative assessment
- Assess cardiac disease and functional reserve
  - Compromised patient: local anaesthetic plus sedation
- Establish the indication for PPM or ICD
  - Check for anticoagulants
- Existing pacemaker plus surgical diathermy may lead to loss of pacemaker function
- Explain the procedure and sedation to the patient

## Intraoperative management
- Monitoring
  - Arterial line if contractility is impaired
- Remote location
  - Limited space and equipment, staff not trained in anaesthesia
- Sterility
  - IV antibiotics are essential
  - A large plastic drape (as for awake carotid surgery) keeps airway access without breaking sterility
- VF testing
  - VF is induced repeatedly to test the device; sedate the patient for this phase
  - Place external defibrillator pads before starting
  - If the device fails, do not leave the heart arrested for long; repeated shocks may be needed
  - BP can stay low briefly after VF and defibrillation; vasopressors may be needed
- Pacemaker function
  - During a generator change, diathermy can cause loss of pacemaker function

## Equipment, drugs and staff
- Resuscitation drugs ready: vasopressors and vagolytics (e.g. ephedrine, metaraminol, glycopyrrolate)
- Temporary pacing and defibrillation equipment available
- External defibrillator pads placed before starting
- Skilled anaesthetist and trained assistant present
- Airway trolley and GA drugs available
- Supplemental O2 throughout

## Anaesthetic technique
- Sedation
  - Low-dose short-acting sedative (e.g. midazolam) plus opioid (e.g. fentanyl) until comfortable, cooperative and sleepy
  - Deepen sedation before defibrillator testing (TCI propofol is an option)
  - Deepen sedation if the device goes under muscle; full regional block is hard to achieve there
- General anaesthesia
  - Light GA with careful induction, as many have limited cardiac reserve
  - Use a trolley that allows head tilt for intubation

## Postoperative care
- Continuous ECG monitoring
- Day surgery: young patients, normal cardiac reserve, sedation only, uncomplicated procedure
- Overnight monitoring: elderly, limited cardiac reserve, GA, complicated procedure (e.g. refractory VF)

## Flashcards
- Q: When must external defibrillator pads be placed for ICD insertion?
  - A: Before the procedure starts, as VF is induced repeatedly to test the device.
- Q: What can happen to an existing pacemaker when the cardiologist uses diathermy during a generator change?
  - A: Loss of pacemaker function.
- Q: When should sedation be deepened during device implantation?
  - A: Before defibrillator testing, and when the device is placed under muscle.
- Q: Which patients suit day surgery after device implantation?
  - A: Young, normal cardiac reserve, sedation only, uncomplicated procedure.
