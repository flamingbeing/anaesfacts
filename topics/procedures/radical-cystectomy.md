# Radical Cystectomy

Bladder excision with urinary diversion (ileal conduit or bladder reconstruction): long, painful, bloody pelvic surgery with bowel work.

## Indications
- Muscle-invasive bladder cancer
- Less often: neurogenic bladder, chronic urinary obstruction, pelvic malignancy

## Surgery
- Men: bladder, prostate, seminal vesicles and urethra removed
- Women: bladder, urethra, anterior vaginal wall, uterus, both ovaries and fallopian tubes removed
- Urinary diversion to colon or ileum at the end
- Anaesthesia and positioning similar to radical prostatectomy
- Bowel component adds longer operating time and bacteraemia risk
- Prior chemotherapy toxicity
  - Doxorubicin: cardiotoxic
  - Methotrexate: hepatotoxic
  - Cisplatin and methotrexate: neurotoxic and nephrotoxic

## Preoperative management
- Assess IHD, COPD, renal function
- FBC, crossmatch
- Book HDU bed
- VTE prophylaxis
- Consider preoperative IV fluids to offset bowel prep losses

## Intraoperative management
- Very painful: epidural, used cautiously until haemostasis
- Lithotomy, with or without head down
- GA with invasive monitoring
- Cell salvage (stop once bowel is opened)
- Antibiotics
- Large-bore IV access, blood warmer, blood available in theatre
- Nasogastric tube
- Active warming against hypothermia
- Air embolism is a possible complication

## Postoperative management
- Commonest complication: ileus
- Enhanced recovery reduces the risk
  - Avoid bowel prep
  - Late preoperative carbohydrate drink (1hr before) and early feeding
  - Restrictive fluids, limiting sodium load
  - Early mobilisation
  - Regional analgesia
    - Wound catheters placed after first incision: can run up to 5days
    - Visceral pain usually lasts only 24-36hrs: cover with IV or neuraxial opioids
    - Spinal or epidural analgesia
- NSAIDs: some evidence of increased anastomotic leak
- PCA or epidural for 2 days (2/7)
- Careful fluid management: urine output from the conduit is positional and unreliable
- Ureteric anastomotic leak shows as urine in the surgical drain

## Flashcards
- Q: Commonest postoperative complication after radical cystectomy?
  - A: Ileus
- Q: When should cell salvage stop during cystectomy?
  - A: Once the bowel is opened
- Q: How long does visceral pain usually last after cystectomy?
  - A: 24-36hrs
- Q: Why is conduit urine output unreliable?
  - A: It is positional
