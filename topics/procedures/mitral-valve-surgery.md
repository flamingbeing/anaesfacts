# Mitral Valve Surgery

Replacement or repair for stenosis or regurgitation: control rate and PVR, protect the RV, and watch for SAM after repair.

## Mitral stenosis: valve replacement
### Issues
- Fixed cardiac output
- With or without pulmonary hypertension
- AF on warfarin

### Haemodynamic goals
- Heart rate 60–80 bpm
  - Tachycardia is the main threat: shorter diastole reduces LV filling and raises LA pressure, leading to pulmonary oedema
- Rhythm: keep sinus rhythm if possible
  - Loss of atrial kick can cut CO by 20–30%
- Preload: full but not overloaded
  - High filling drives flow across the narrow valve; excess fluid risks RV failure and pulmonary congestion
- Afterload: maintain SVR
  - MAP depends on SVR when CO is fixed
  - Avoid sudden vasodilation (neuraxial block, rapid induction); the heart cannot raise CO to compensate
- PVR: keep low
  - Avoid hypoxia, hypercapnia and acidosis
  - High PVR precipitates acute RV failure

### Special points
- AF on warfarin: plan bridging, check INR
- RV failure is the commonest cause of failure to wean from bypass in MS
  - Aggressive PVR reduction (inhaled nitric oxide, milrinone)
  - RV inotropic support if needed (dobutamine, adrenaline)
  - Pulmonary artery pressures take days to weeks to fall; continue pulmonary vasodilator care

## Mitral regurgitation: replacement or repair
### Issues
- Comorbidities
  - Cardiomyopathy, IHD
  - Acute regurgitation leads to pulmonary oedema
  - AF in 75%
  - Right heart failure: MR raises PAP, leading to RV failure
- Transthoracic echo
  - Preoperative LV function is often overestimated
  - Chronic significant MR raises pulmonary artery pressures
- Haemodynamic goal: "fast, full, forward"

### Special points
- LVEF may fall after replacement because the LV has to work harder
  - Risk of LV failure; inotropes or inodilators may be needed
  - IABP can help a failing LV in the short term
- Systolic anterior motion (SAM) after repair causes dynamic LVOT obstruction
  - Trigger: aggressive inotropy with underfilling after repair
  - Signs: hypotension, new systolic murmur, LVOT gradient on TOE
  - Treat as for HOCM: fluid, raise afterload (noradrenaline, phenylephrine), stop inotropes, slow the heart rate (beta-blockers)
- Repair is replacing replacement more often; anaesthetic management is the same

## Monitoring
- TOE
  - Before bypass: define the lesion (Carpentier classification) and severity, so especially important for repairs; exclude left atrial appendage thrombus
  - After bypass: residual MR, SAM (especially after repair), LV and RV function
- PA catheter if needed: key for PAP and PVR, especially MS with secondary pulmonary hypertension

## Surgical access and cannulation
- Bicaval cannulation needed for a bloodless field in the atria
- Minimally invasive approach: double-lumen tube and femoral cannulation

## Complications
- Conduction block: the AV node lies close to the mitral annulus, so epicardial pacing is mandatory
- AV groove disruption: surgical catastrophe with massive haemorrhage on weaning
