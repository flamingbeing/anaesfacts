# Intestinal Obstruction and Laparotomy

Hypovolaemic, electrolyte-deranged, aspiration-prone patients: optimise first, RSI, avoid N2O, HDU afterwards.

## Causes
- Paralytic ileus
- Concentric narrowing, e.g. Crohn's disease, neoplasm
- Kinking: adhesions
- Twisting: volvulus
- Intussusception: spontaneous, or secondary to a polyp
- Foreign body: ingested, or gallstone
- Pseudo-obstruction
- Differential diagnosis: mesenteric ischaemia

## Clinical features
- 4 main features: pain, vomiting, distension, constipation
- **Closed loop obstruction is a surgical emergency**

## Preoperative assessment
- Complications to look for:
  - Hypovolaemia and electrolyte imbalance
    - Proximal small bowel obstruction: early, heavy vomiting with loss of fluid, chloride, sodium and potassium
    - Large bowel obstruction: slower, more insidious onset
    - Hypo- or hypernatraemia, hypokalaemia, hypochloraemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypomagnesaemia, hypophosphataemia
  - Sepsis
  - Diaphragmatic splinting and reduced FRC
  - Aspiration
  - Perforation
  - Large bowel obstruction may be associated with vitamin K deficiency, leading to coagulopathy
- Full assessment of current clinical state: examination, bloods, X-rays, vital signs, past history, hydration
- Discuss urgency carefully with surgeons:
  - 90% of obstructions secondary to adhesions resolve spontaneously
  - Most cases can wait for the next in-hours list
- Optimise adequately before surgery with **inotropes and fluid**
  - 4 hours of optimisation in HDU reduced mortality from 17 to 3%

## Induction
- Nasogastric tube: controversial whether to leave in place or remove before intubation; aspirate it
- RSI with a double suction set-up
- Epidural: weigh benefits against risks
- Avoid N2O

## Postoperative care
- HDU is the ideal place
- Establish early:
  - Cardiovascular and respiratory monitoring
  - Fluid balance
  - Wound care and antibiotics
  - Early enteral feeding
  - VTE prophylaxis
  - Physiotherapy

## Flashcards

  - A: Pain, vomiting, distension and constipation
- Q: What proportion of adhesional obstructions resolve spontaneously?
  - A: 90%
- Q: What mortality benefit was seen with 4 hours of preoperative optimisation in HDU?
  - A: Mortality fell from 17 to 3%
- Q: Which gas should be avoided in bowel obstruction?
  - A: N2O
- Q: What acid-base disturbance follows heavy vomiting in proximal obstruction?
  - A: Metabolic alkalosis (with hypochloraemia and hypokalaemia)
