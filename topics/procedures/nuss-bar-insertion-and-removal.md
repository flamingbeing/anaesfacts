# Nuss Bar Insertion and Removal

Minimally invasive repair of pectus excavatum using a curved bar placed behind the sternum.

## Indication and assessment
- Done for pectus excavatum
- Haller index (HI) grades severity of the deformity
  - Transverse chest diameter divided by the anteroposterior diameter at the deepest point of the depression
  - Above 3.25 suggests surgical correction is needed
- Severe pectus excavatum may cause cardiac or pulmonary problems
  - Review the CT for cardiac compression
  - Consider pulmonary function tests

## Nuss bar insertion: anaesthetic management
- Lung isolation and bilateral VATS
- Arterial line
- Large bore IV in case mediastinal structures are damaged
- Postoperative pain is difficult to manage

## Nuss bar insertion: procedure
- A tunnel is created just behind the sternum from one side to the other and widened as needed
- Umbilical tape is pulled through the tunnel and used to draw the bar into place
- The bar sits across the mediastinum just behind the sternum with its curve facing backwards
- It is then turned 180°, so the curve faces forwards and pushes the sternum out
- The bar is fixed to the lateral chest wall on both sides
- A second bar is sometimes needed above or below
- Thoracoscopic viewing of the bar as it crosses the mediastinum lowers the risk of vascular, pericardial and cardiac injury

## Nuss bar removal
- Single lumen ETT; an LMA has also been used
- Lung injury may occur during removal

## Ravitch procedure
- Open and more invasive
- Was the standard of care before the minimally invasive Nuss procedure

## Flashcards
- Q: How is the Haller index calculated?
  - A: Transverse chest diameter divided by the anteroposterior diameter at the deepest point of the depression.
- Q: What Haller index suggests surgical correction is needed?
  - A: Above 3.25.
- Q: What airway and access are needed for Nuss bar insertion?
  - A: Lung isolation for bilateral VATS, arterial line and large bore IV in case of mediastinal injury.
- Q: By how much is the Nuss bar rotated once in place?
  - A: 180°, so its curve faces forwards and pushes the sternum out.
