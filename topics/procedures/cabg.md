# Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG)

Unrevascularised ischaemic heart disease plus the general cardiac issues (TOE, bypass and organ protection, bleeding) and graft-specific points.

## Grafts
- Left internal mammary artery (LIMA): usually to the LAD; best long-term patency
  - Low tidal volumes help access
  - May be avoided in subclavian stenosis (risk of steal)
- Radial artery: better than saphenous vein but prone to spasm
  - No arterial line in that radial artery (usually the left)
- Great saphenous vein (GSV): the remaining grafts; the leg can bleed
- Specific risk: graft failure

## Preoperative assessment
- Cardiac history
  - Angina (stable or unstable), breathlessness (systolic or diastolic dysfunction)
  - Recent MI, especially since the decision to operate
  - Episodes of heart failure or pulmonary oedema
  - Exercise tolerance (cardiorespiratory reserve)
- Comorbidities
  - Diabetes: will probably need an insulin infusion
  - Previous stroke or TIA: may need cerebral oximetry (NIRS)
  - Renal impairment
  - Bleeding or clotting disorders: investigate, as full heparinisation is needed for bypass
  - TOE risk factors: reflux, hiatus hernia, dysphagia, oesophageal diverticulum (e.g. Zenker), varices, strictures, previous gastric or oesophageal surgery, chest wall radiotherapy
- Medications: antihypertensives, antianginals, antiarrhythmics
  - Specific plans for diabetes and asthma; insulin-treated diabetics first on the list if practicable
  - Heparin infusions usually run until arrival in theatre; check serial platelet counts (HIT)
  - Review all recent antiplatelet drugs
    - Aspirin may need continuing after recent ACS
    - Clopidogrel (Plavix, Iscover): stop 5-7 days before surgery
- Examination
  - BP in both arms (subclavian stenosis)
  - Arterial line in the arm with the higher BP, or femoral
  - Lower left arm BP: tell the surgeons, as the LIMA may be a poor conduit
- Investigations
  - PT and APTT; chase every abnormal clotting result, especially platelets on heparin (HIT)
  - Group and crossmatch; blood available in the fridge
  - ECG, CXR, TTE, myocardial perfusion imaging, catheter data
  - TTE: LV ejection fraction, LVEDP, pulmonary artery pressures; RV function (PASP); valve areas and gradients
  - Recent peak CK, CK-MB, troponin
  - Carotid duplex, especially after cerebral symptoms; consider transcranial Doppler, vasomotor reactivity and neurology referral; if plaques, consider cerebral oximetry
  - CT thorax for aortic plaque (aortic cannulation site)

## Reading the coronary angiogram
- Dominance: which vessel supplies the AV node and LV inferior wall
- Site and severity of stenoses
  - High-grade left main stem disease (or equivalent): very high risk of catastrophic ischaemia at induction ("widowmaker")
  - TIMI flow grade (speed and completeness of distal filling)
    - Grade 0: no flow (occluded)
    - Grade 1: contrast passes the lesion but does not fill the distal bed
    - Grade 2: distal bed fills, but slowly
    - Grade 3: normal, prompt filling
    - Grade 0/1 territories rely entirely on collaterals
- Length and diffuseness
  - Diffuse disease (common in diabetic and elderly patients) may leave no landing zone for a graft
  - Very diffuse disease may need coronary endarterectomy
- Haemodynamic significance, for borderline lesions (e.g. 50–70% stenosis)
  - FFR: distal coronary pressure over aortic pressure at maximal hyperaemia (usually adenosine); under 0.80 is significant
  - iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio): under 0.89 is significant

## Counselling
- Specific consent for TOE risks
- Renal impairment: higher chance of temporary dialysis postoperatively

## Premedication
- Anxiety can trigger ischaemia; many patients still have ischaemia despite good premedication
- Oral options
  - Lorazepam 2mg or temazepam at night if needed, the night before
  - Lorazepam 2mg at 0600 on the morning of surgery
  - Morphine (5-10mg) at 0630 for a 0800 start; on call for later cases
- Give supplemental oxygen with the premed (hypoxaemia is likely otherwise)
- Top up with IV midazolam or fentanyl once a cannula is in, for line insertion

## Theatre set-up
- Induction and emergency drugs; vasopressor/inotrope ("uppers") and vasodilator ("downers") infusions ready
- TOE machine ready
- Oxygen by nasal prongs
- Access and monitoring
  - Large-bore 16G IV on the right; consider PA sheath or Mahurkar
  - Arterial line before induction on the left (unless the left radial is to be harvested), after midazolam or fentanyl
  - Right internal jugular central line before or after induction, depending on severity, after sedation
  - Very high-risk cases: consider a PA catheter before induction; passing one in a recent RV infarct can cause VF
  - 5 lead ECG, urinary catheter, orogastric tube (decompress stomach for TOE), forced-air warmer, temperature probe
  - BIS; consider cerebral oximetry
  - TOE
  - Cell salvage
  - External defibrillator pads for very high risk (left main over 70 %, ischaemic emergencies from the cath lab) and redo cases
  - Intra-aortic balloon pump or ECMO if needed
- Drugs
  - Heparin for bypass; protamine kept in the drawer
  - Emergency drugs including calcium chloride
  - Pressors and inotropes: noradrenaline, adrenaline
  - Vasodilators: GTN infusion, sodium nitroprusside
  - RV support: milrinone, dobutamine
  - Insulin infusion if diabetic
  - With or without propofol infusion
- Position: supine with shoulder roll; pad well with gel rolls
- Airway: Hi-Lo ETT unless extubation is planned

## Induction
- Be ready for arrest at induction; never induce without
  - A surgeon able to start bypass
  - A perfusionist and pump available
- Cardiostable high-dose opioid, with or without benzodiazepine
  - Fentanyl 5–10 mcg/kg
  - Midazolam 3mg
- Rocuronium; atracurium may be avoided (histamine release)
- Inotropes may be needed from induction
- Choice of anaesthetic
  - No technique is superior: sevoflurane, propofol TIVA, and high- or low-dose opioid techniques are equivalent if haemodynamics are controlled
  - Avoid desflurane and ketamine induction: linked to pulmonary hypertension and myocardial ischaemia; the only agents not recommended in known coronary disease
  - Avoid nitrous oxide: expands gas emboli in open-heart surgery

## Maintenance
- Insert TOE probe after intubation
- Post-induction ABG and baseline ACT
- Analgesia
  - Fentanyl ranges: high over 100 mcg/kg; medium 20-60 mcg/kg; low 5-10 mcg/kg
  - Remifentanil is an alternative
  - Long-acting opioid towards the end; paracetamol
- Fluids: judicious and goal-directed
  - Too much intraoperative fluid often delays extubation; the pump prime adds more
  - Exception: pre-bypass plateletpheresis needs at least 2 litres of crystalloid
- Hypotension: think of surgical handling of the heart (chest open) or tamponade (chest closed)
  - Handling the heart can drop BP suddenly or cause ectopics: look at the field before treating
  - Call the number aloud (e.g. "Pressure is 60/30") so the surgeons stop lifting the heart
  - Head-down tilt raises BP briefly

## Blood pressure targets
- Before bypass: systolic 90-120mmHg, heart rate between 50 and 80 depending on the situation
  - Tachycardia is linked to ischaemia and infarction
  - Systolic over 120 mmHg increases bleeding
- Aortic cannulation and purse-string: systolic about 100 to prevent dissection
- On bypass: MAP 40-50mmHg while cold (cross-clamp on), 50-60mmHg while warm (cross-clamp off)
  - Carotid disease or chronic renal impairment may need 60-80 mmHg throughout
- After bypass: systolic 90-120mmHg, heart rate 50-80
  - Cardiac index over 2.0 is acceptable
  - PA diastolic under 20 mmHg and CVP under 15 mmHg (if normal before)
  - CVP above PA diastolic means poor calibration or RV failure

## Bleeding and coagulation
- Platelets are often low
  - Order 1 CSP (platelet product) on rewarming if on antiplatelets
  - Check ROTEM or TEG
- Haematocrit falls with pump haemodilution
- Cell salvage often used
- Large-bore access, possibly Mahurkar; fluid warmer (e.g. Hotline); rapid infuser (e.g. Level 1) may be needed

## Electrolytes, metabolic and other
- Aim K over 4
- Calcium supplementation often needed coming off bypass
- Glucose often rises on bypass: monitor; consider insulin infusion
- Cefazolin 2g before skin incision, at start of bypass, then 2g every 4H
- With or without esomeprazole (Nexium) 40mg

## Sternotomy and graft harvest
- Give fentanyl at skin incision; sternotomy and sternal retraction are stimulating and may need more
- Stop ventilating during sternotomy
- LIMA and saphenous vein usually harvested at the same time
- IMA dissection: table tilted left and raised; surgeons may want smaller tidal volumes with a higher rate, or hand ventilation

## Heparinisation and cannulation
- Baseline ACT already done
- Heparin 300-400 U/kg via the central line
- Check ACT 3mins after the dose
- Aortic cannulation (ascending aorta): ACT over 300; systolic under 100; avoid tachycardia
- Venous cannulation (single right atrial cannula): let BP rise a little
- Going on bypass: ACT over 400-480
- At full flow: alarms and ventilator off

## On bypass
- Cooled to 32-24 degrees
- Aortic cross-clamp and cardioplegia arrest the heart
- Distal anastomoses and LIMA to LAD first
- Cross-clamp off, then rewarming
- Proximal anastomoses to the ascending aorta with a side-biting clamp

## Rewarming and weaning
- Start adrenaline
- Consider GTN for even rewarming
- Top up relaxant; midazolam
- Epicardial pacing wires
- Draw up protamine (keep it out of the way)
- Do not over-inflate the lungs: can stretch and tear the IMA graft
- Remove cannulae (aortic last), give protamine, close chest, remove TOE probe

## Emergency CABG (failed PCI)
- May be peri-arrest; ischaemia needs correcting
- Arterial (femoral) access from the cath lab should stay
- Inotropes and vasopressors for stability
- IABP raises diastolic pressure (more coronary flow) and helps LV ejection (better LV function)
- Multiple antiplatelets likely: will need platelets after bypass
- Cardiostable induction; adrenaline as needed
- Postoperatively: continue IABP, no rush to extubate, high risk of renal dysfunction

## Flashcards
- Q: Heparin dose for bypass, and ACT targets for cannulation and bypass?
  - A: 300-400 U/kg; ACT over 300 for aortic cannulation, over 400-480 for bypass
- Q: Systolic target during aortic cannulation and why?
  - A: About 100 mmHg, to prevent aortic dissection
- Q: MAP targets on bypass?
  - A: 40-50 mmHg cold (clamp on), 50-60 mmHg warm (clamp off); 60-80 mmHg in carotid or renal disease
- Q: When to stop clopidogrel before CABG?
  - A: 5-7 days before
- Q: FFR value indicating a significant stenosis?
  - A: Under 0.80 (iFR under 0.89)
