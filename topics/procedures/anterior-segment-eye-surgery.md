# Anterior Segment Eye Surgery

Open-eye front-of-eye surgery where intraocular pressure control is critical, as sudden decompression can cause prolapse or haemorrhage.

## Procedures
- Penetrating keratoplasty (corneal transplant)
- Glaucoma drainage surgery
- Intracapsular cataract extraction

## Preoperative assessment
- Check glaucoma drugs: beta-blocker eye drops can cause bradycardia

## Induction
- IOP control is crucial: sudden decompression of a tense eye causes iris or lens prolapse and choroidal haemorrhage
  - IPPV with end-tidal CO2 control
  - Consider mannitol or acetazolamide
- Oculocardiac reflex: IV atropine is not contraindicated, as little of it reaches the eye

## Maintenance
- Sub-Tenon's block very useful
- Less pain than vitreoretinal surgery

## Flashcards
- Q: Why is IOP control crucial in anterior segment surgery?
  - A: Sudden decompression of a tense eye causes iris or lens prolapse and choroidal haemorrhage
- Q: Drugs to lower IOP at induction?
  - A: Mannitol or acetazolamide, plus IPPV with CO2 control
- Q: Is IV atropine safe in glaucoma patients?
  - A: Yes, little reaches the eye
