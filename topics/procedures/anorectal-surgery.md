# Ambulatory Anorectal Surgery

Short, highly stimulating day-case surgery (haemorrhoidectomy, perianal fistula): deep anaesthesia, watch for laryngospasm and vagal reflexes.

## Summary

- Procedures: haemorrhoidectomy, perianal fistula
- Anaesthesia: GA with LMA, or spinal/saddle block, caudal, or pudendal block
- Position: lithotomy head down; sometimes prone
- Antibiotics: ceftriaxone and metronidazole (not always needed)
- Duration: about 20min
- Blood loss: minimal
- Pain: moderate to severe; surgeon infiltrates local anaesthetic but morphine usually needed
- PONV: single antiemetic
- Complications: laryngospasm (anal stretch is very stimulating; consider alfentanil), vagal reflexes
- Needs deep anaesthesia: highly reflexogenic
- Day surgery; high risk of urinary retention

## Preoperative assessment

- Check suitability for LMA, lithotomy and head-down position

## Positioning

- Lithotomy, head down; alternative: jackknife (prone)
- Lithotomy: pressure on the common peroneal nerve can cause foot drop; pad well
- Prone or jackknife: support the chest so ventilation and venous return are not impaired
- Position limbs and genitals carefully; keep pressure off eyes and ears

## Anaesthetic technique

- GA with spontaneous ventilation and LMA
  - Consider ETT if obese or reflux history
- Spinal or saddle block
  - Sacral-only spinal with heavy bupivacaine: little cardiovascular effect
  - Do not tilt head down straight after the spinal
- Caudal
- Pudendal nerve block

## Intraoperative management under GA

- Short but intensely painful stimulus: fentanyl or alfentanil
- Anal stretch
  - Too light anaesthesia risks laryngospasm and coughing
  - Deepen beforehand: increase volatile, bolus short-acting opioid (e.g. alfentanil 500 micrograms)
  - Can increase vagal tone: bradycardia or asystole as surgery starts; have vagolytics ready

## Postoperative management

- Surgeon's local anaesthetic infiltration usually helps, but morphine often needed
- No rectal (PR) drugs
- Watch for urinary retention

## Other anorectal procedures

- Delorme's procedure for rectal prolapse (same day admission)

## Flashcards

- Q: Two reflex complications of anal stretch under light anaesthesia?
  - A: Laryngospasm (and coughing) and vagal bradycardia or asystole
- Q: Suggested opioid bolus before anal stretch?
  - A: Alfentanil 500 micrograms
- Q: Nerve at risk in lithotomy, and the result?
  - A: Common peroneal nerve; foot drop
- Q: What should you avoid right after a saddle spinal for anorectal surgery?
  - A: Immediate head-down tilt
