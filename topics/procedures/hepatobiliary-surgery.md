# Hepatobiliary Surgery

Liver surgery risks major bleeding (keep CVP low); biliary surgery ranges from routine laparoscopic cholecystectomy to major tumour resections.

## Liver surgery
- Indications: primary or metastatic tumours, bile duct surgery
- Major resections are usually done by laparotomy
- Tumours involving vessels can bleed heavily during surgery; plan for:
  - Adequate IV access
  - Monitoring
  - Rapid infusion device
  - Blood products available
- If the extent of surgery is unclear at the start, consider placing arterial and central lines before incision
- Low CVP (2–5 mmHg) reduces distension of hepatic veins and sinusoids and so reduces blood loss
- The liver makes every clotting factor except factor VIII; hepatic impairment can cause coagulopathy
  - Coagulopathy or low platelets often rule out an epidural

## Biliary tract surgery
- Spectrum: simple laparoscopic cholecystectomy to complex bile duct tumour surgery in very sick patients
- Tailor the anaesthetic to disease severity and surgical extent
- Gallbladder surgery: usually laparoscopic, standard monitoring, often elective day case
- Positioning: usually left arm out, right arm tucked

## Flashcards
- Q: What CVP target reduces blood loss in liver resection?
  - A: Low CVP, 2–5 mmHg
- Q: Which clotting factor is not made by the liver?
  - A: Factor VIII
- Q: Why are epidurals often avoided in liver surgery patients?
  - A: Coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia from hepatic insufficiency
- Q: When should invasive lines be placed before incision in liver surgery?
  - A: When the extent of surgery is not known at the start
