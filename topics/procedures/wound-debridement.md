# Wound Debridement

Plan depends on the wound's site, size and cause.

## Site of wound
- Lower limb
  - Often suitable for a nerve block
  - May include toe amputation; below-knee and above-knee amputations are covered separately
- Upper limb
  - Often general anaesthesia, but a block may be possible
- Back, neck or spine
  - May be prone and may need intubation
- Abdomen
  - If deep or intraperitoneal, consider intubation
- Chest

## Size of wound
- Very large wounds can be very painful and can bleed a lot

## Intraoperative points
- The area may be scrubbed straight after induction: painful stimulus can trigger laryngospasm

## Indication
- Open fracture
  - May take longer; see the limb fractures topic

## Flashcards
- Q: What airway risk arises when the wound is scrubbed right after induction?
  - A: Pain can trigger laryngospasm.
- Q: When should intubation be considered for abdominal wound debridement?
  - A: If the wound is deep or intraperitoneal.
- Q: What two problems do very large wounds bring?
  - A: Severe pain and heavy bleeding.
