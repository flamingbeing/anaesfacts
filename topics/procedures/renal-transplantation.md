# Renal Transplantation (Recipient)

Patients with end-stage renal failure: protect the graft by keeping renal blood flow and volume up, avoid vasoconstrictors, and give immunosuppression on time.

## Contraindications to transplant
- Absolute: active cancer, infection, severe vascular disease, recent MI, other end-stage systemic disease
- Relative: morbid obesity, old age, poorly controlled systemic disease, primary renal disease that often recurs

## Key considerations
- End-stage renal failure complications and dialysis
- Graft protection
  - Keep renal blood flow up for good reperfusion; avoid vasoconstrictors
  - Keep intravascular volume up
- Immunosuppression

## Surgical overview
- Supine
- Antibiotics before surgery as advised by infectious diseases
- Lower quadrant curvilinear incision
- Anastomosis order: renal vein to iliac vein, then renal artery to external iliac artery, then ureter to bladder
- Diuretic cocktail (saline, mannitol, albumin) given during the arterial anastomosis
- Avoid phenylephrine

## Preoperative assessment
- Reviewed by nephrologist, surgeon and infectious diseases; agree immunosuppression at induction
- Renal failure: volume status, timing of last dialysis, electrolyte and acid-base problems, anaemia
  - Dialysis usually the day before
  - Consider EPO for anaemia (target Hb 9–10 g/dL)
- Common comorbidities: diabetes, hypertension, coronary disease, autoimmune disease
- Cardiovascular
  - TTE or MIBI if at risk of poor ventricular function, valve disease or pulmonary hypertension (e.g. several cardiovascular risk factors)
  - Cardiology review if PASP >45 mmHg
- Stop smoking 4-8 weeks before; nicotine replacement
- Valid group and crossmatch

## Intraoperative management
- Immunosuppression
  - Hydrocortisone 100mg (usually on the ward)
  - Moderate risk: basiliximab (Simulect) 20mg IV on transfer to theatre
  - High risk: anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) the evening before via central line; watch for cytokine release syndrome (fever, gut upset, cytopenias, deranged clotting)
- Antibiotics: ceftriaxone 2g (usually on the ward); vancomycin IV if MRSA or allergy
- Monitoring
  - Arterial line
  - Central line (surgeons need it after surgery): internal jugular if possible, avoid femoral (near the operative field)
    - For elective cases, may be placed by interventional radiology beforehand, especially if access looks difficult
  - Urine output once the graft is in
- Blood pressure (graft perfusion)
  - Before anastomosis: MAP >70mmHg, higher if baseline BP is higher
  - After anastomosis: MAP 75-85 mmHg
    - The graft has impaired autoregulation; MAP >75 may reduce delayed graft function (DGF)
  - Avoid vasopressors such as ephedrine and phenylephrine; small doses are acceptable
    - Cumulative phenylephrine 500mcg or ephedrine 15mg should prompt a search for the cause of hypotension
  - Noradrenaline may be considered if clinically needed
  - Dopamine is no longer used routinely
- Fluids: avoid both hypovolaemia and overload
  - Consider goal-directed therapy
  - Balanced crystalloids (Hartmann's, Plasma-Lyte); saline's hyperchloraemia constricts renal vessels and lowers graft GFR after reperfusion
  - No gelatin colloids (linked to AKI)
  - Roughly 2000ml to 3000ml over the operation, using judgement to avoid overload
- Reperfusion
  - Before reperfusion give the premix: 340ml NS + 100ml 20% albumin + 62.5ml 20% mannitol
  - Mannitol: osmotic diuretic and free radical scavenger; vasodilates and raises tubular flow, preventing cast obstruction
- Other
  - Normothermia and normoglycaemia
  - Avoid needless transfusion; use leucocyte-reduced products to lower sensitisation
- Renal failure points
  - RSI if aspiration risk is high (uraemia, reflux, gastroparesis); suxamethonium if K <5, otherwise rocuronium
  - Do not give sodium bicarbonate just for a base deficit
  - ROTEM to guide factor and blood product replacement if bleeding is significant (platelet dysfunction)
- Sevoflurane is acceptable
- Analgesia
  - Paracetamol, fentanyl, oxycodone, wound local anaesthetic, elastomeric wound infusion (ON-Q)
  - Consider fentanyl PCA
  - Avoid NSAIDs and morphine
  - Regional options: TAP block (single shot or infusion), surgical preperitoneal wound catheter, intrathecal morphine 200mcg to 300mcg

## Postoperative care
- CXR in PACU to check central line position
- High dependency for graft function and haemodynamics; avoid hypovolaemia and hypotension
- Immunosuppression as directed by nephrology

## Flashcards
- Q: MAP target after the graft anastomosis?
  - A: 75-85 mmHg (before anastomosis >70 mmHg)
- Q: Vasopressor amounts that should prompt a search for the cause of hypotension?
  - A: Cumulative phenylephrine 500 mcg or ephedrine 15 mg
- Q: Contents of the pre-reperfusion diuretic premix?
  - A: 340 ml NS + 100 ml 20% albumin + 62.5 ml 20% mannitol
- Q: Which colloid should not be used in renal transplant?
  - A: Gelatin (linked to AKI)
- Q: Where should the central line go and why?
  - A: Internal jugular; avoid femoral because it is near the surgical site
