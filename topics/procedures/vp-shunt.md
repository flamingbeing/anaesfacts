# VP Shunt and Hydrocephalus Surgery

CSF diversion for hydrocephalus: short GA case where shunt tunnelling is the most stimulating step.

## Overview
- Diverts CSF away from the ventricles in hydrocephalus
- Usually ventriculoperitoneal; can be ventriculo-atrial or ventriculo-pleural
- Flushing system and valves prevent clotting and back pressure
- Often done in children; also in adults

## Procedure
- Occipital burr hole; catheter placed in the lateral ventricle
- Catheter tunnelled subcutaneously up or down the neck and trunk, then into the peritoneal cavity through a small incision
- 2 surgeons can work at the same time
- Quicker than a craniotomy

## Preoperative assessment
- ICP is often raised: assess for it

## Positioning
- Supine, shoulder roll, head turned on a horseshoe
- Head faces away from the anaesthetist

## Monitoring
- Arterial line and central line usually not needed

## Anaesthetic technique
- GA
- Airway: ETT usually; LMA possible
  - ETT may be better: the head is moved during tunnelling and an LMA may be displaced
  - Mind ICP at airway instrumentation
- Maintenance: TIVA
- Antibiotics: cefazolin
- Fluids: isotonic
- Analgesia: fentanyl alone
- Tunnelling is very painful and stimulating
  - Keep the patient paralysed for it, especially with an LMA, to avoid laryngospasm

## Postoperative care
- General ward
- A fall in GCS needs a CT scan to exclude shunt malfunction or subdural haematoma

## Complications
- CSF drained too fast: risk of intracranial haemorrhage
- Shunt infection and revision are common
- Pneumothorax from the trocar during tunnelling

## Flashcards
- Q: Which step of VP shunt insertion is most stimulating, and how is it managed?
  - A: Subcutaneous tunnelling; keep the patient paralysed (especially on an LMA) to avoid laryngospasm.
- Q: Why may an ETT be preferred to an LMA for a VP shunt?
  - A: The head is manipulated during tunnelling and an LMA may be displaced.
- Q: A patient's GCS falls after VP shunt insertion. What is needed?
  - A: CT scan to exclude shunt malfunction or subdural haematoma.
- Q: Which chest complication can occur during shunt tunnelling?
  - A: Pneumothorax from the trocar.
