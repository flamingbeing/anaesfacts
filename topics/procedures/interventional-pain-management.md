# Interventional Pain Management

Procedures for chronic pain (nerve blocks, radiofrequency, implants), their place and limits, and when to stop antithrombotics before them.

## Background
- Pain has a placebo response of about 30%

## Benefits
- Lower doses of regular medicines
  - Fewer systemic side effects; opioid sparing
  - Less tolerance
- Diagnostic value

## Drawbacks
- Invasive, with risk of complications
- Cost
- Not suitable for everyone

## Classification
- By guidance: ultrasound, or X-ray (C-arm with contrast)
- Destructive (radiofrequency ablation, neurolytics) or non-destructive
- Implantable devices
- Spinal or non-spinal
- By pathology
- By location
- Common or uncommon

## Radiofrequency ablation
- Needs insulated needles, a thermocouple and an RF generator
- Conventional RF (destructive)
  - Usually 80-90 degrees for about 2 mins
  - Causes coagulation necrosis, endoneural oedema and Wallerian degeneration
  - Bigger lesion with: longer active tip, larger needle gauge, solid needle, higher temperature, longer time
- Pulsed RF (non-destructive)
  - Maximum 42 degrees; nerve preserved
  - Some neuromodulating effect, mechanism unclear
- Cooled RF (destructive): covers a larger area
- Issues: costly; needs an operating theatre

## X-ray guided procedures
- Contrast
  - Helps localisation and shows a misplaced needle
  - Shows how far a given volume spreads
  - Reveals unintended intravascular injection

## Antithrombotics and anticoagulants (ASRA 2018 interventional procedures)
- Non-aspirin NSAIDs: not needed for heart or brain protection, so stop (timing by half-life)
  - COX-2 inhibitors: no need to stop
- Aspirin
  - Medium to high risk procedures: consider stopping; balance against cardiovascular risk
  - Low risk procedures: continue
- P2Y12 inhibitors (e.g. clopidogrel)
  - Medium to high risk: stop
  - Low risk: consider stopping; balance against cardiovascular risk
  - Clopidogrel: stop 7 days; restart after 12hr
  - Prasugrel: stop 7-10 days; restart at 24hr
  - Ticagrelor: stop 5 days; restart at 24hr
  - Consider LMWH bridging
- Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors (e.g. abciximab): stop 5 days
- Warfarin
  - Stop 5 days; INR must be normal, below 1.2 (stricter than 1.4); restart the next day
  - Consider LMWH bridging
- Unfractionated heparin: stop 6 hrs; restart after 2hrs, or 24hrs if bloody
- LMWH
  - Therapeutic dose: 24hr for high risk, with normal aPTT; restart after 8hrs
  - Prophylactic dose: 12hr; restart after 4hrs, or 12hrs if high risk
- NOACs
  - Apixaban: 3d
  - Rivaroxaban: 3d
  - Dabigatran: 4d; 5-6d in renal disease
  - Consider LMWH bridging
- SSRIs: continue

## Stellate ganglion block
- Sympathetic block for pain in the head, neck, chest or arm from
  - Sympathetically maintained pain (complex regional pain syndrome, reflex sympathetic dystrophy)
  - Causalgia (nerve injury)
  - Herpes zoster
  - Intractable angina
- Improves blood flow in Raynaud's disease or CREST syndrome (limited scleroderma)
- Vasospasm after subarachnoid haemorrhage

## Flashcards
- Q: Temperature and time for conventional versus pulsed RF?
  - A: Conventional 80-90 degrees for about 2 mins; pulsed maximum 42 degrees
- Q: Clopidogrel before a medium or high risk pain procedure (ASRA 2018)?
  - A: Stop 7 days; restart after 12hr
- Q: Warfarin before a pain procedure?
  - A: Stop 5 days; INR below 1.2; restart next day
- Q: Stop times for apixaban and dabigatran?
  - A: Apixaban 3d; dabigatran 4d (5-6d in renal disease)
- Q: Should COX-2 inhibitors or SSRIs be stopped?
  - A: No, continue both
