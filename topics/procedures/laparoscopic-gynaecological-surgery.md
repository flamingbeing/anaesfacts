# Laparoscopic Gynaecological Surgery

Rigid-scope examination and surgery of the pelvic organs in usually fit young adults, with lithotomy, head-down tilt and CO2 pneumoperitoneum.

## Preoperative assessment
- Usually fit young adults
- Consent for suppositories
- NSAIDs before surgery

## Intraoperative management
- Supine, then lithotomy with head-down tilt
- Short-acting non-depolarising relaxant: mivacurium 0.15mg/kg gives about 15min of block
- ProSeal LMA or ETT
- Short-acting opioid (fentanyl)
- Ask the surgeon to use LA
- Watch for perforation of abdominal organs
- Watch for CO2 gas embolism

## Postoperative care
- Morphine may be needed
- Shoulder tip pain is common: expel as much CO2 as possible at the end

## Flashcards
- Q: What mivacurium dose suits a short gynae laparoscopy, and how long does it last?
  - A: 0.15mg/kg, about 15min of block
- Q: How can shoulder tip pain be reduced after gynae laparoscopy?
  - A: Expel as much CO2 as possible at the end
- Q: Which two complications should be watched for intraoperatively?
  - A: Abdominal organ perforation and CO2 gas embolism
