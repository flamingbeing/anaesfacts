# Clavicle Surgery

Fixation of clavicle fractures: GA usually, with regional blocks for postoperative analgesia.

## Technique
- General anaesthesia usually needed
- Regional anaesthesia can be added for postoperative analgesia

## Innervation
- Complex
- Skin over the clavicle: supraclavicular nerve, from the superficial cervical plexus (well established)
- Bone itself: disputed
  - Some say the supraclavicular nerve of the superficial cervical plexus
  - Others say branches of the brachial plexus

## Analgesia options
- Interscalene plus superficial cervical plexus block
- Clavipectoral fascial plane block (newer)

## Clavipectoral fascial plane block
- Ultrasound probe transverse to the clavicle, placed proximal and distal to the fracture
- Inject local anaesthetic between the periosteum and the clavipectoral fascia, both proximal and distal to the fracture
- Low risk

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerve supplies the skin over the clavicle?
  - A: The supraclavicular nerve, from the superficial cervical plexus
- Q: Which block combination covers clavicle surgery?
  - A: Interscalene plus superficial cervical plexus block, or a clavipectoral fascial plane block
- Q: Where is local anaesthetic placed in a clavipectoral fascial plane block?
  - A: Between the periosteum and the clavipectoral fascia, proximal and distal to the fracture
