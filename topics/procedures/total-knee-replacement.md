# Total Knee Replacement

Usually spinal with local infiltration; cefazolin, two doses of TXA and an intra-articular cocktail; postoperative pain is the main problem.

## Summary
- Spinal anaesthesia
- Cefazolin, TXA x2
- Intra-articular cocktail

## Compared with total hip replacement
- Shorter operation
- Less blood loss
- Less risk of cement hypotension
- Postoperative pain can be extreme

## Preoperative assessment
- DVT prophylaxis
  - Start antithrombotic measures on admission to hospital
  - Some orthopaedic surgeons use aspirin instead

## Intraoperative management
- Duration
  - 1-2 hr
  - Check whether the implants are in
- Pain: severe (++++/+++++)
  - Some surgeons want PCA
- Blood loss
  - Minimal with a tourniquet
  - 250-500 ml without
  - Tourniquet commonly used, so intraoperative loss is rarely a problem
  - Drains may lose up to 500 mL (often more) in the 1st hour after surgery
  - Tourniquet use is declining
  - Group and crossmatch usually not needed
- Position: supine

## Anaesthetic technique
- Options
  - Spinal plus local infiltration
  - GA plus local infiltration
  - Epidural or combined spinal-epidural
- Spinal, with or without intrathecal opioid, is preferred; GA is often used
- Trend away from nerve blocks towards spinal plus local infiltration
  - Allows faster mobilisation and earlier discharge
  - Nerve blocks can delay mobilisation considerably
- With GA, surgeons usually ask for TIVA
- Cefazolin
- TXA 1g at the start, then another 1g after the tourniquet is released
- Intra-articular cocktail: ropivacaine, adrenaline, ketorolac, vancomycin (check for contraindications)

## Special equipment
- Tourniquet often used
  - Issues: tourniquet pain, reperfusion
- Cement
  - Consider avoiding cement in severe cardiac disease; discuss with the surgeon beforehand

## Postoperative management
- Pain control
  - Usually the biggest problem and the main factor in choosing the anaesthetic technique
  - Many patients already take opioids, which makes pain harder to manage
  - Surgeons may want PCA; continue until the patient is walking
- Blood loss
  - If drain loss stays brisk after the 1st 500mL, the surgeon often clamps the drains for a while

## Bilateral total knee replacement
- Only for young, fit, motivated patients
  - Elderly patients and those with significant cardiovascular disease are high risk
- Advantage: avoids two admissions and two operations
- Disadvantages: major cardiovascular stress; unpredictable blood loss and fluid needs
- GA plus epidural is probably the most practical technique
- Consider invasive monitoring (arterial line)

## Revision total knee replacement
- As for 1° (primary) knee replacement, but longer: ≥2hr
- More blood loss
  - Without a tourniquet, crossmatch 2U of blood
- Patients are older with more comorbidities

## Flashcards
- Q: What TXA regimen is used for TKR?
  - A: 1g at the start, then another 1g after the tourniquet is let down.
- Q: What is in the intra-articular cocktail?
  - A: Ropivacaine, adrenaline, ketorolac and vancomycin (check contraindications).
- Q: Expected blood loss without a tourniquet?
  - A: 250-500 ml; drains may lose up to 500 mL in the 1st postoperative hour.
- Q: Why has practice moved away from nerve blocks for TKR?
  - A: Spinal plus local infiltration gives faster mobilisation and earlier discharge; blocks can delay mobilisation.
- Q: How much blood should be crossmatched for a revision TKR without a tourniquet?
  - A: 2U.
