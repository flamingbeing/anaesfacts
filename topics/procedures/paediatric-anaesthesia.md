# Paediatric Anaesthesia Overview

Practical checklist for anaesthetising children: age-specific risks, theatre set-up, conduct and blood product doses.

## Key considerations by age
- Neonates and infants
  - Difficult airway
  - Cardiac output is rate dependent
  - Keep warm, wet and sweet: avoid hypothermia, replace insensible losses, prevent hypoglycaemia
- Preschool children: often uncooperative
  - Separation anxiety: inhalational induction, or IV after EMLA
  - Emergence delirium
- Drugs: emergency drugs drawn up, saline flushes
- Equipment: airway, breathing circuit, infusion pump
- Major paediatric surgery
  - Remote injector
  - Albumin 5%
  - Syringe pump for blood
  - Vasopressors at PICU dilutions
- Always consider contraindications to premedication, to inhalational induction and to day surgery

## Preoperative assessment
- Talk to the child
- Can they take paracetamol and parecoxib?
- Asthma, upper respiratory tract infection, fasting time
- Loose teeth
- Is there an IV cannula already?
- Bring a mask to show the child; apply EMLA cream; prepare a video for distraction

## Theatre preparation
- Warm the theatre; consider an underbody forced-air warmer
- Precalculate drug doses, ETT size and depth, and fluids; write them down
- Draw up emergency drugs
- Draw up induction drugs and a saline flush; use a 1 ml syringe
  - Beware tenfold (10x) dose errors when the child is under 10 kg
- Change to a paediatric head ring; tell the anaesthetic assistant the tube size
- Change to a paediatric breathing circuit for children under 20 kg; switch the ventilator to pressure control
- Syringe pump; short paediatric IV extension with priming syringe, or a remote injector
- Change monitor alarm limits

## Intraoperative management
- Inhalational induction
- Fluids: 10 ml/kg over the first hour

## Emergence and recovery
- Tape the hand and disconnect the IV before emergence
- Remove the LMA deep; may extubate deep, in the lateral position, with padded side rails
- SpO2 monitoring only in recovery
- Hand over the analgesia plan to recovery staff

## Nasal intubation
- Use oxymetazoline (Iliadin) nose drops rather than co-phenylcaine spray

## Blood products
- Red cells: weight x 5 ml raises Hb by 1
- Platelets: 5-10 ml/kg
- Prothrombin complex concentrate: 15 IU/kg
- FFP: 10-20 ml/kg
- Cryoprecipitate: 5-10 ml/kg
- Fibrinogen concentrate: 30-50 mg/kg; alternative: body weight / 160 x target increase

## Flashcards
- Q: Red cell volume to raise Hb by 1 in a child?
  - A: Weight (kg) x 5 ml.
- Q: Paediatric doses of FFP and cryoprecipitate?
  - A: FFP 10-20 ml/kg; cryoprecipitate 5-10 ml/kg.
- Q: Fibrinogen concentrate dose in children?
  - A: 30-50 mg/kg.
- Q: Below what weight switch to a paediatric breathing circuit?
  - A: Under 20 kg.
- Q: Below what weight is the tenfold dosing error risk highlighted?
  - A: Under 10 kg.
