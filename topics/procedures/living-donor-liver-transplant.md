# Living Donor Liver Transplant

Partial liver graft from a living donor (LDLT): shorter cold ischaemia for the recipient, and donor safety comes first.

## Recipient considerations
- Cold ischaemia time is much shorter than with a deceased donor graft
  - Less metabolic derangement at reperfusion
- Graft fragility: partial grafts are more prone than whole deceased donor grafts to congestion and hyperperfusion injury when small-for-size

## Living donor management
- Main goal: donor safety
  - Aim for a smooth, atraumatic experience for a healthy volunteer
- Use propofol infusions with care
  - Emergence may be delayed because the reduced liver mass metabolises it

## Flashcards
- Q: Why does an LDLT recipient have less metabolic derangement at reperfusion than a deceased donor recipient?
  - A: Much shorter cold ischaemia time.
- Q: What injury is a small-for-size partial graft prone to?
  - A: Congestion and hyperperfusion injury.
- Q: What is the overriding goal of anaesthesia for the living donor?
  - A: Donor safety, with a smooth atraumatic course.
- Q: Why use propofol infusion with care in the living donor?
  - A: Emergence may be delayed because of reduced hepatic metabolism.
