# Pancreas Transplantation

Long laparotomy for type 1 diabetes: frequent glucose checks, steroids, the diuretic cocktail, and stopping insulin once the pancreas is joined.

## Background
- Indication: type I diabetes and its complications
- 3 types
  - SPK: simultaneous pancreas and kidney (most common)
  - PAK: pancreas after kidney
  - PAT: pancreas alone
- Surgery lasts 5-7hr

## Intraoperative management
- Midline laparotomy
- Check blood glucose closely, every 30min; consider holding IV insulin if the surgeon asks
- Methylprednisolone 1g; consider a repeat dose at 6 hours
- Diuretic cocktail (saline, mannitol and albumin)
- IVC side clamp and iliac artery clamping
  - Iliac unclamping: MAP falls and potassium rises
- At the pancreatic anastomosis
  - Stop insulin
  - Check glucose every 15mins; dextrose infusion often needed

## Postoperative care
- Usually surgical high dependency; consider ICU
- Continue immunosuppression
- Complications: graft thrombosis, pancreatitis, infection, anastomotic leak, immunosuppressant side effects, bladder problems, SIRS and shock

## Flashcards
- Q: Most common type of pancreas transplant?
  - A: Simultaneous pancreas and kidney (SPK)
- Q: What happens to insulin at the pancreatic anastomosis?
  - A: Stop it, and check glucose every 15 min; dextrose infusion often needed
- Q: Steroid dose during pancreas transplant?
  - A: Methylprednisolone 1 g, consider repeat at 6 hours
- Q: Haemodynamic and electrolyte effect of iliac unclamping?
  - A: MAP falls and potassium rises
