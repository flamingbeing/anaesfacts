# Paediatric myringotomy and grommets

Short, usually bilateral ear operation in infants and toddlers; immobile patient, spontaneous breathing via face mask or LMA, laryngospasm risk.

## Overview
- One of the commonest paediatric operations in the UK
- Myringotomy: incision in the tympanic membrane and suction of middle ear secretions
- Often combined with insertion of grommets (tympanostomy tubes)
- Indications: recurrent otitis media, persistent middle ear effusion

## Procedure
- Short; supine with head turned to the side
- Usually bilateral
- Operating microscope: magnified field needs an immobile patient
- Mostly day cases

## Preoperative assessment
- Mostly infants and toddlers
- Respiratory symptoms are common in this group
  - Surgery usually proceeds, but carries a risk of laryngospasm and hypoxia
- A significant minority have a congenital condition, notably Down's syndrome, with extra considerations

## Anaesthetic technique
- Induction as indicated or requested; inhalational induction is common (young age, difficult venous access)
- Airway: face mask or LMA
- Maintenance: volatile in oxygen with air or nitrous oxide
- Spontaneous ventilation is the norm
- Laryngospasm can occur at myringotomy

## Postoperative care
- Pain is unpredictable and variable: from none to an inconsolable child
  - Mainly from trauma to the external auditory meatus, possibly acute middle ear pressure changes
- Simple analgesia (paracetamol, NSAIDs) is sufficient
- Some give paracetamol or ibuprofen as premedication

## Flashcards
- Q: Why must the patient be immobile during myringotomy?
  - A: An operating microscope gives a magnified field
- Q: What airway is usually used for myringotomy and grommets?
  - A: Face mask or LMA with spontaneous ventilation
- Q: Which congenital condition is notably common in children having grommets?
  - A: Down's syndrome
- Q: What analgesia is usually enough after grommets?
  - A: Simple analgesics such as paracetamol and NSAIDs
