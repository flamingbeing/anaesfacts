# Pancreatic Surgery

Patients fall into four groups, from critically ill pancreatitis to functioning islet cell tumours; surgeons may ask for octreotide to reduce pancreatic leak.

## Patient groups
- Acute pancreatitis failing medical treatment: may be very unwell; excision or drainage of necrotic or infected areas
- Complications of chronic pancreatitis, e.g. pseudocyst or abscess
- Pancreatic adenocarcinoma
- Islet cell tumours, hormonally active (60–70%) or inactive
  - Mainly insulinoma and gastrinoma; rarely VIPoma and glucagonoma

## Intraoperative management
- Surgeon may ask for subcutaneous octreotide to reduce pancreatic leak
  - Usually 200mcg, i.e. 2ml (2 vials)
- Usually needs a nasogastric tube

## Flashcards
- Q: Usual octreotide dose requested in pancreatic surgery?
  - A: 200mcg subcutaneously (2ml, 2 vials)
- Q: Why give octreotide?
  - A: To reduce pancreatic leak
- Q: Commonest islet cell tumours?
  - A: Insulinoma and gastrinoma
