# Hernia Repair

Often day-case GA with LMA; obstructed hernias carry aspiration and ischaemic bowel risk.

## Preoperative assessment
- Usually adult men or young children
- Usually day-case
- Obstructed or incarcerated hernia: aspiration risk, possible ischaemic bowel
- Treat causes of raised intra-abdominal pressure before repair to reduce recurrence: COPD and chronic cough, bladder outlet obstruction (BPH), ascites

## Laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair
- Often bilateral
- Technique: LMA commonly used; atracurium
- Orogastric tube
- Position: supine; left arm out, or both arms in
- Antibiotics: cefazolin
- Pneumoperitoneum: mind ventilation
- Duration: 45 min to 2 hr (unilateral or bilateral)
- Blood loss minimal
- Pain moderate: morphine 0.5 mg/kg, paracetamol, parecoxib
- PONV: consider dual antiemetics (laparoscopy)
- Postoperative: 23-hour stay

## Open hernia repair (inguinal, paraumbilical, incisional)
- Technique: LMA with paralysis
  - Consider ETT if the abdomen will be entered
  - Spinal if unwell, but may not cover deep dissection
  - Rarely under LA alone (very difficult)
- Orogastric tube
- Position: supine; either arm out
- Antibiotics: cefazolin
- Reduce hernia, place mesh: keep paralysed until the mesh is in
- Duration: 45 min to 1.5 hr (depends on difficulty, e.g. hernia size)
- Blood loss minimal
- Pain moderate, size-dependent: morphine 0.5-1 mg/kg, paracetamol, parecoxib
- PONV: consider dual antiemetics (bowel handling)
- Postoperative: day surgery

## General perioperative points
- Time 30-60 min
- Pain moderate to severe
- Blood loss not significant
- Position supine
- Techniques
  - GA with LMA; ETT if laparoscopic
  - Spinal
  - Local infiltration with or without sedation
- Inguinal field block: sole technique or adjunct to GA
  - Iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves: block 2 cm caudal and medial to the ASIS
  - Genitofemoral nerve: 1–2 cm above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, deep to the external oblique aponeurosis; can be left to the surgeon to avoid vascular or peritoneal puncture
  - Probably the technique of choice in high-risk patients if the operator is experienced in LA
  - Low-dose propofol infusion can help when done under LA alone

## Postoperative care
- Day-case: send home with analgesia, e.g. NSAID, paracetamol, tramadol 50–100 mg four times daily

## PROSPECT 2019 recommendations
- Paracetamol plus NSAID or COX-2 inhibitor, started before or during surgery and continued after
- Single IV dose of dexamethasone: longer block, less analgesic use, antiemetic
- LA infiltration and/or regional analgesia (ilioinguinal/iliohypogastric block or TAP block)
- LA technique recommended if the surgeon is experienced in it

## Flashcards
- Q: Where are the iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves blocked for hernia repair?
  - A: 2 cm caudal and medial to the ASIS.
- Q: Where is the genitofemoral nerve found for an inguinal field block?
  - A: 1–2 cm above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, deep to the external oblique aponeurosis.
- Q: Until when should an open hernia patient stay paralysed?
  - A: Until the mesh is in.
- Q: What does PROSPECT 2019 recommend for systemic analgesia in hernia repair?
  - A: Paracetamol plus NSAID or COX-2 inhibitor, given pre- or intraoperatively and continued, plus single-dose IV dexamethasone.
- Q: Why address COPD, BPH or ascites before hernia repair?
  - A: Raised intra-abdominal pressure predisposes to recurrence.
