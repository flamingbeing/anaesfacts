# Testicular Surgery

Short scrotal operations, often day case, moderate to severe pain; GA with LMA or regional/local, and watch for vagal responses.

## Overview
- Examples: orchidectomy, orchidopexy, hydrocele marsupialisation, vasectomy, testicular torsion
- Time: 30min -1hr
- Pain: moderate to severe (++ to +++)
- Blood loss: not significant
- Position: supine

## Techniques
- GA with LMA, plus spermatic cord block
- Spinal, or LA infiltration

## Radical orchidectomy
- Usually for testicular cancer; patients often young and fit
- May have had chemotherapy: watch for systemic toxicity
- Bleomycin causes pulmonary toxicity
  - After bleomycin, colloid may cause fewer lung complications than crystalloid
  - Lower inspired oxygen may help
- Supine; GA or regional both acceptable
- Retroperitoneal lymph node dissection may be added
  - On the left, intercostal arteries can be compromised, cutting flow in the artery of Adamkiewicz and causing spinal cord ischaemia

## Preoperative assessment
- Often suitable for day surgery

## Intraoperative management
- Watch for vagal responses; have atropine ready
- Innervation
  - Somatic: ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, pudendal and posterior scrotal nerves (from the posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh); roots L1 to S3
  - Autonomic: sympathetic chain T10–L4, parasympathetic S1–S3
  - Local techniques must cover T10–S3
- Spermatic cord block
  - Adjunct to GA or part of a local technique for scrotal surgery
  - Covers all nerves except the pudendal and posterior scrotal branches; with LA alone, also infiltrate the scrotal skin
  - Best done under direct vision by the surgeon
  - For a local technique: feel the cord where it enters the top of the scrotum and infiltrate 5–10mL of LA around it

## Flashcards
- Q: Which segments must a local technique for scrotal surgery cover?
  - A: T10–S3
- Q: Which nerves does a spermatic cord block miss?
  - A: Pudendal and posterior scrotal branches (so add scrotal skin infiltration)
- Q: Volume for a landmark spermatic cord block?
  - A: 5–10mL of LA around the cord at the top of the scrotum
- Q: Anaesthetic concern after bleomycin?
  - A: Pulmonary toxicity: use lower inspired oxygen; colloid may be better than crystalloid
