# Bullectomy and Emphysema Surgery

Non-anatomical resection of hyperinflated, poorly functioning lung in COPD with resectable bullous disease.

## Background
- Usually done for severe emphysema, i.e. patients who would normally avoid GA at all costs
- The National Emphysema Treatment Trial found benefit for upper lobe disease with low baseline exercise capacity
- An isolated congenital bulla or cyst is managed the same way, but these patients are often much fitter
- Aims:
  1. Reduce the amount of non-functional lung
  2. Improve diaphragm function
  3. Improve right heart function by reducing the tamponade effect

## Preoperative assessment
- Intensive assessment and careful selection:
  1. Very breathless
  2. Heterogeneous lung disease (areas that can be resected)
  3. No smoking for 3 months
  4. Age under 75
  5. No pulmonary hypertension
  6. FEV1 <20% predicted, plus predicted improvement after surgery
- Cardiac assessment: angiography, right heart catheterisation, echo, pulmonary artery pressure
- Perioperative steroid supplementation as required

## Intraoperative management
- Surgical approach:
  - Thoracotomy for unilateral surgery
  - Median sternotomy for bilateral surgery
  - VATS: less pain and fewer physiological changes
- Severe pain
- IPPV via a double-lumen tube; confirm position with bronchoscopy
- Deliberate hypoventilation and permissive hypercapnia
- Ventilation: tidal volume 6 ml/kg, RR 10, I:E 1:4, airway pressure <30 cmH2O
- Disconnect from the ventilator intermittently to let the lungs empty
- TIVA (high A-a gradient)
- Thoracic epidural
- Ventilate cautiously: bullae can burst
- Cautious fluids
- Bronchospasm and sputum can be problems

## Postoperative management
- Extubate early
- Accept a high PaCO2; aim for SpO2 90-92%
- Watch closely for air leaks
- Physiotherapy
- Pulmonary rehabilitation programme

## Complications
- Prolonged air leak is common: 50% of patients leak for more than 7 days
- Mortality 5-10%
- Bronchopleural fistula, pneumothorax
