# Duration of Surgery (Short and Long Cases)

Plan airway, relaxant, temperature, fluids and pressure care by expected case length.

## Very short (under 30min)
- E.g. OGD, surgical evacuation
- If intubating (e.g. RSI), consider suxamethonium or low-dose rocuronium to avoid residual paralysis at emergence

## Short to intermediate (30min-4hrs)
- Standard GA
- Temperature management: forced-air warmer (Bair Hugger), with or without a temperature probe

## Long (over 4hrs)
- Intubate
- Temperature management: forced-air warmer and temperature probe
- Watch fluid balance
  - Urinary catheter with hourly urine output charted
  - If not catheterised, avoid giving too much fluid
  - Chart blood loss
- Mechanical protection
  - Pressure injury risk: make sure padding and positioning are adequate
    - Pad sacrum, scapulae and heels; gel under the ulnar nerve
  - DVT risk: calf pumps
- Ergonomics: get comfortable and move as little as possible
  - Consider infusions (e.g. remifentanil, atracurium)
  - Tidy wires and tubing

## Very long (over 8hrs, e.g. free flap)
- Remifentanil for intraoperative opioid titration
- Arterial line to spare repeated NIBP cuffing; ABG checks (lactate, Hb, glucose)
- Central line for reliable IV access
- Disposition: consider ICU; may wake slowly or be unsafe to extubate at night

## Flashcards
- Q: Which relaxants suit intubation for a case under 30 min?
  - A: Suxamethonium or low-dose rocuronium, to avoid residual paralysis at emergence
- Q: Above what duration should you intubate, catheterise and chart urine output hourly?
  - A: Over 4 hours
- Q: Which pressure points need padding in long cases?
  - A: Sacrum, scapulae, heels; gel under the ulnar nerve
- Q: What extra monitoring and access suit a case over 8 hours?
  - A: Arterial line with ABGs (lactate, Hb, glucose), central line, remifentanil; consider ICU
