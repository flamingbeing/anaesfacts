# Endoscopic Thoracic Sympathectomy

Short thoracoscopic division of the sympathetic chain, mostly for palmar hyperhidrosis in young ASA 1-2 patients.

## Indications
- Main indication: palmar hyperhidrosis (1% of people)
- Also angina and long QT syndrome
- Patients are generally young, ASA 1/2

## Relevant anatomy
- 2 paravertebral sympathetic chains arise from the ventrolateral grey matter of T1-L2
- Short preganglionic fibres; long postganglionic adrenergic fibres to effector sites (sweat glands are cholinergic)
- Lower cervical and upper thoracic ganglia usually fuse to form the stellate ganglion
- Supply:
  - Head and neck: T1-2
  - Upper limb: T2-5
  - Thoracic viscera: T1-4
- The chain runs beneath the parietal pleura, over the necks of the ribs

## Surgery
- Resection, cutting or clipping (clipping is reversible)
- Level chosen by symptoms:
  - Palmar hyperhidrosis: T2-3
  - Axillary hyperhidrosis: T2-4
  - Angina and long QT syndrome: T2-4/5
- 10-15min per side

## Preoperative assessment
- Check whether it is for angina or long QT syndrome

## Intraoperative management
- Airway, 2 options:
  - DLT if the surgeon is slow
  - Standard ETT with or without bronchial blocker if the surgeon is quick
- Small volumes of CO2 are insufflated into the chest during apnoea or with the lung down to see the chain
- Arterial line optional
- 2x skin temperature probes, one on each palm: a sudden rise (usually >2deg C) confirms a successful sympathectomy
- Position: lateral, or beach chair (control the head)
- Be ready for open thoracotomy
- Problems:
  - Bradycardia
  - Severe haemorrhage from rupture of major subclavian vessels

## Postoperative complications
- Compensatory sweating
- Persistent pneumothorax
- Horner's syndrome
