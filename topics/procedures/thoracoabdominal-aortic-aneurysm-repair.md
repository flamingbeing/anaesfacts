# Thoracoabdominal Aortic Aneurysm Repair

Aneurysm crossing the diaphragm through the origins of the visceral and renal arteries, so repair risks spinal cord, renal and bowel ischaemia.

## Anatomy
- Involves the aortic segment giving off the coeliac, SMA and renal arteries
- High spinal cord ischaemia risk, plus renal and bowel ischaemia, as it crosses the diaphragm

## Modified Crawford classification (Safi added the 5th type)
- I: just beyond the left subclavian artery to the coeliac axis (above the renals)
- II: just beyond the left subclavian artery to the aortic bifurcation (includes infrarenal); whole descending thoracic and abdominal aorta
- III: 6th intercostal space to the aortic bifurcation
- IV: diaphragm to the aortic bifurcation
- V: 6th intercostal space to above the renals

## Open repair
- May need thoracotomy or sternotomy plus laparotomy
- Very complex: visceral and renal arteries are reimplanted into the graft

### Intraoperative management
- GA, double-lumen tube with one-lung ventilation
- Cross-clamp effects are more marked
  - Worse hypertension (GTN, esmolol, sodium nitroprusside)
  - Clamp cuts off splanchnic and renal arteries
  - Spinal cord ischaemia from clamping the artery of Adamkiewicz
  - Acidosis: metabolic from the clamp plus respiratory from one-lung ventilation
  - Severe hypotension after unclamping
- Distal perfusion with selective visceral and renal perfusion
  - Blood from the bypass circuit goes straight into the coeliac, SMA and renal arteries via small catheters (Pruitt cannulae)

## Endovascular repair
- TEVAR is an option (see EVAR and TEVAR)

## Flashcards
- Q: Which Crawford type runs from the left subclavian to the aortic bifurcation?
  - A: Type II
- Q: What are Crawford types III and V?
  - A: III: 6th intercostal space to the bifurcation; V: 6th intercostal space to above the renals
- Q: Why is acidosis worse during open TAAA repair?
  - A: Metabolic acidosis from the cross-clamp plus respiratory acidosis from one-lung ventilation
