# Percutaneous Nephrostomy and Nephrolithotomy

Endoscopic removal of a renal stone through a nephrostomy tract, usually for larger stones; prone GA with risks of hypothermia, sepsis and pneumothorax.

## Preoperative assessment
- Usually young, healthy adults, but stones may reflect a metabolic disorder
- May have neurological disability (bladder dysfunction)
- Check renal function

## Intraoperative management
- Prone position
- GA with armoured ETT
- Eye and pressure area care
- Support chest and pelvis so the abdomen can move freely
- Antibiotic prophylaxis
- Hypothermia: large volumes of irrigation fluid
- Nephrostomy often placed near the diaphragm: risk of pneumothorax or hydrothorax
- Renal pelvis may rupture
- Infected stones may cause a septic shower

## Postoperative management
- Pain is variable
- Monitor for:
  - Bacteraemia, often Gram negative
  - Urinary obstruction

## Complications
- Bleeding
- Prone position injuries
- Fluid absorption (TURP-like syndrome)
- Hypothermia
- Pneumothorax or hydrothorax

## Flashcards
- Q: Why is pneumothorax a risk in PCNL?
  - A: The nephrostomy is often placed near the diaphragm
- Q: Why are patients prone to hypothermia?
  - A: Large volumes of irrigation fluid
- Q: What infective complication follows infected stones?
  - A: Septic shower and Gram negative bacteraemia
