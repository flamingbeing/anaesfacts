# Percutaneous ASD and PFO Closure

Cath lab device closure of the interatrial septum: GA with ETT for TEE, heparin, and keep the patient dry because LV preload rises after ASD closure.

## Indications
- There are 2 indications for septal closure
- ASD as a shunt lesion
  - Types: ostium secundum (the only one suitable for percutaneous closure), ostium primum, sinus venosus, unroofed coronary sinus
  - ECG: RBBB with axis deviation (direction depends on type)
- PFO with cryptogenic stroke
  - Septum primum and secundum fail to fuse at one edge of the fossa ovalis
  - Present in up to 27% of people
  - Left to right flow usually limited by a flap; right to left flow limited by the higher left atrial pressure
  - Echo after stroke or TIA to exclude PFO
  - Closure can lower the risk of further stroke from 33% to 7%

## Preoperative assessment
- Dual antiplatelet therapy started before the procedure

## Intraoperative management
- TEE essential, so GA with ETT
- Heparin needed
- After ASD closure LV preload rises (RV preload falls): may cause flash pulmonary oedema if the LV has diastolic dysfunction; keep the patient dry

## Postoperative care
- Lie flat for 2hours until the venous sheath is removed
- Antiplatelets prescribed afterwards

## Complications
- Air embolism
- Arrhythmias: AF and atrial flutter are common
- Thrombus on the device
- Device embolisation
- Pericardial bleeding and tamponade

## Flashcards
- Q: Which ASD type can be closed percutaneously?
  - A: Ostium secundum only
- Q: Why is GA with ETT used?
  - A: TEE is essential during the procedure
- Q: Why keep the patient dry after ASD closure?
  - A: LV preload rises and can cause flash pulmonary oedema if there is LV diastolic dysfunction
- Q: How common is a PFO?
  - A: Up to 27% of people
