# Oesophageal Perforation and Repair

Perforation leads to mediastinitis and sepsis; repair needs RSI, often one-lung ventilation, invasive monitoring and good analgesia.

## Causes
- Iatrogenic (OGD, dilatation)
- Boerhaave's syndrome (barogenic rupture)
- Foreign body

## Consequences
- Mediastinitis
- Systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS)
- Septic shock

## Management options
- Temporising medical management:
  - Nil by mouth and TPN
  - Antibiotic cover
  - Proton pump inhibitor
- Oesophageal stenting:
  - Fewer complications and improving success rates
  - Temporary stent, removed 6-12 weeks later
  - Watch for stent migration
- Oesophageal repair; approach depends on the level of the rupture:
  - Neck: local incision
  - Mid thoracic: thoracoscopic or open
  - Low thoracic: midline abdominal incision or laparoscopic
  - May be closed over a draining T-tube, forming a controlled oesophagocutaneous fistula so it heals without contamination

## Preoperative assessment
- CT chest and abdomen: pneumomediastinum, pleural effusions

## Intraoperative management
- Thoracoscopic approach: one-lung ventilation with a DLT or bronchial blocker
- RSI: aspiration risk
- Avoid coughing and straining, which risk extending the rupture
- Monitoring:
  - Arterial line
  - Consider cardiac output monitoring and goal-directed fluid therapy
- Analgesia: multimodal; thoracic epidural is ideal

## Postoperative management
- Early enteral feeding via nasojejunal tube

## Flashcards
- Q: Name three causes of oesophageal perforation.
  - A: Iatrogenic (OGD or dilatation), Boerhaave's (barogenic) and foreign body.
- Q: When is a temporary oesophageal stent removed?
  - A: 6-12 weeks later; watch for migration meanwhile.
- Q: What airway technique is used for a thoracoscopic repair?
  - A: One-lung ventilation with a DLT or bronchial blocker, after RSI.
- Q: What is the ideal analgesia for oesophageal repair?
  - A: Multimodal analgesia, ideally with a thoracic epidural.
