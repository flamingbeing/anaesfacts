# Breast reduction and augmentation

Usually fit patients under GA, 30 degrees head up; reductions bleed more (crossmatch for large ones), augmentations hurt more with large implants.

## Breast reduction
### Preoperative assessment
- Often severe back and neck pain: check neck extension
- Usually young and fit
- DVT prophylaxis
- Group and hold

### Intraoperative management
- Moderately painful
- Supine, 30 degrees head up
- GA (ETT or LMA with IPPV)
- Absorbent pads under the patient for blood
- Check IV lines still run after positioning

### Postoperative management
- A dose of morphine, then simple analgesia
- Early: haematoma; later: wound infection, dehiscence, fat necrosis
- Larger reductions (>1kg) may need red cells: crossmatch

## Breast augmentation
### Indications
- Reconstruction after mastectomy
- Correcting asymmetry
- Cosmetic bilateral augmentation

### Preoperative assessment
- Usually fit and well; FBC

### Intraoperative management
- Supine, 30 degrees head up, arms out on boards or hands behind buttocks
- GA (ETT or LMA with IPPV)
- Minimal blood loss
- After mastectomy may be combined with a TRAM (transverse rectus abdominis muscle) flap or free flap
- Pocket made at the anterior axillary line, then implant inserted

### Postoperative management
- Larger implants hurt more
- Simple analgesia and NSAIDs
- Early: haematoma; late: infection, capsule formation, rupture

## Flashcards
- Q: Position for breast reduction and augmentation?
  - A: Supine, 30 degrees head up
- Q: When should red cells be crossmatched for breast reduction?
  - A: Larger reductions, over 1kg
- Q: Late complications of breast augmentation?
  - A: Infection, capsule formation, implant rupture
