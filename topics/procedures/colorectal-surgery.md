# Colorectal Surgery

Small and large bowel surgery: plan for emergency vs elective, open vs laparoscopic, bowel oedema, positioning and opioid-sparing analgesia.

## Overview
- Common operations: volvulus, intussusception, perforation, tumour resection

## Key considerations
- Emergency vs elective
  - Emergency: acute abdomen
  - Elective: ERAS, bowel preparation
- Open vs laparoscopic
  - Open: fluid shifts, pain control
  - Laparoscopic: arms tucked in
- Avoid bowel oedema: limit crystalloids; give colloids early
- Minimise postoperative opioids: regional techniques help
- Underlying condition
  - Acute abdomen (as for emergency laparotomy)
  - Inflammatory bowel disease: often on long-term steroids; frequently need stress-dose steroids before induction
  - Malignancy: anaemia from chronic blood loss; higher coagulopathy risk

## Preoperative management
- ERAS protocols
  - Bowel preparation
  - Water and carbohydrate drinks until 2hrs before surgery
- Arrange HDU/ICU bed if needed

## Intraoperative management
- Monitoring and lines
  - Large-bore IV access
  - Arms usually tucked at the sides for laparoscopic surgery; may be out for open surgery
    - Use long extensions if arm access is restricted
    - Consider an arterial line
- Induction
  - RSI if bowel obstruction or regurgitation risk
  - Antibiotics: ceftriaxone (Rocephin) and metronidazole (Flagyl)
- Maintenance
  - Expect lithotomy or Lloyd-Davies with steep head-down tilt
    - Long cases in this position reduce FRC; may need higher FiO2 and PEEP
  - PONV is common after bowel surgery; reduce it with
    - Adequate hydration
    - Multimodal, opioid-limiting analgesia
    - No N2O
    - Antiemetics from different classes

## Postoperative management
- Oxygen overnight, continued as needed to keep SpO2 >95%
  - Always give oxygen with opioid-based analgesia (PCA, opioid infusion)
- Close fluid balance: count drain, ileostomy and NG losses
- After major surgery, hourly urine output for at least 24hr

## Postoperative analgesia options
- IM/SC opioids for smaller operations (appendicectomy, colostomy reversal)
- Opioid PCA: lower abdominal surgery, open cholecystectomy after failed laparoscopy, laparotomy when epidural is contraindicated or declined
  - Add oxygen and hourly sedation and pain scores
- Local infiltration of small wounds
- Regional
  - TAP block
  - Rectus sheath infiltration
  - Erector spinae or paravertebral catheter
  - Low thoracic epidural
- Preperitoneal wound catheter (onQ pump)
- Intrathecal opioids
- Epidural

## Flashcards
- Q: How late can clear carbohydrate drinks be given under ERAS?
  - A: Up to 2hrs before surgery.
- Q: Why may Lloyd-Davies with steep head-down need more FiO2 and PEEP?
  - A: Prolonged head-down reduces FRC.
- Q: Postoperative oxygen target after colorectal surgery?
  - A: SpO2 >95%, overnight and with any opioid-based analgesia.
- Q: How long is hourly urine output measured after major bowel surgery?
  - A: At least 24hr.
