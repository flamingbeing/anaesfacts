# Extrapleural Pneumonectomy

En-bloc removal of lung, part of the same-side diaphragm, parietal pleura and pericardium: long, bloody, arrhythmogenic surgery needing lung isolation and protective ventilation.

## Surgical points
- Resects lung, part of the ipsilateral diaphragm, parietal pleura and pericardium in one block
- Now sometimes combined with hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy, for microscopic tumour control and better survival

## Anaesthetic challenges
- Long operation
- One-lung ventilation physiology and its complications
- Blood loss: unstable haemodynamics, acid-base and electrolyte disturbance
- Temperature swings
- Arrhythmias from direct handling of the heart

## Preoperative preparation
- Thoracic epidural commonly placed
  - Limits lung dysfunction from the large incision
  - Helps early recovery
- Large-bore IV access
- Blood products available in theatre
- Invasive cardiovascular monitoring, as rapid haemodynamic changes and arrhythmias are likely

## Induction and maintenance
- No technique proven superior
- Cardiostable IV induction to limit myocardial depression (for example etomidate, midazolam and fentanyl)
- Aim for extubation on the table
  - Reduces bronchial stump pressure
  - Lowers risk of ventilator-induced lung injury and ventilator-associated pneumonia in the remaining lung
- Short-acting volatile (sevoflurane) plus intermittent epidural local anaesthetic top-ups
  - Spares long-acting opioids, so less respiratory depression and sedation at emergence

## Lung isolation and ventilation
- Double-lumen tube or bronchial blocker
  - For a left EPP with a normal airway, a right-sided DLT is an option
- In lateral decubitus, the dependent lung often behaves restrictively (tumour weight, surgical pressure during dissection)
- Protective ventilation of the dependent lung
  - Tidal volume 5-6 ml/kg
  - Peak airway pressure below 30 cmH2O

## Hypotension
- Can occur at induction, during dissection and on repositioning
- Induction: vasodilating drugs and thoracic epidural sympathetic block; vasoconstrictors often needed
- Dissection: blood loss, insensible losses, compression by tumour or retraction
  - Usually temporary
  - Manage with goal-directed fluids, point-of-care haematocrit and vasopressors
  - Liberal fluids linked to coagulopathy, postoperative acute lung injury and higher mortality
- Emergence and turning supine
  - Risk of cardiac herniation, especially after pericardial resection in right-sided EPP
  - Other causes: tight pericardial patch, mediastinal shift after closure
  - Often multifactorial: blood loss, reduced venous return from compression, vasodilating drugs

## Arrhythmias
- Atrial arrhythmias occur in 44.2% after EPP
- Exclude correctable causes first: pain, dehydration, anaemia, sepsis, electrolyte and metabolic problems
- Then treat with drugs (digoxin has been used)

## Summary
- Lung isolation with protective ventilation
- Neuraxial (epidural) block
- Haemodynamic control
- Close surgeon-anaesthetist teamwork
