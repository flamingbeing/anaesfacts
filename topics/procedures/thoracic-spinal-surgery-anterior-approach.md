# Thoracic Spinal Surgery (Anterior Approach)

Occasional approach for scoliosis repair in children: needs a DLT, neuromonitoring-friendly anaesthesia and ICU care.

## Overview
- Occasionally used for scoliosis repair in children
- Posterior approach is more common (lower morbidity)
- Causes of scoliosis:
  - Idiopathic (most common)
  - Neuromuscular disease
  - Tumour
  - Infection
  - Marfan syndrome
  - Rheumatoid arthritis
  - Injury
- Surgery indicated if Cobb angle is over 40 degrees: improves posture and slows progression of pulmonary dysfunction

## Preoperative assessment
- Idiopathic:
  - PFTs; surgery is tolerated well even with FVC below 32%
  - Echo only if exercise tolerance is reduced
  - 25% have mitral valve prolapse
- Secondary scoliosis needs fuller assessment:
  - Muscular dystrophies: cardiomyopathy, so echo with or without stress echo
  - Neuromuscular disease: bulbar palsy? ventilatory capacity?

## Intraoperative management
- Induction: double-lumen tube for surgical access
- Maintenance
  - Long procedure
  - Large blood loss
  - Avoid hypothermia
  - Keep depth of anaesthesia stable for neurophysiological monitoring
- Emergence
  - ICU
  - Extubate early for neurological assessment
  - Epidural placed by the surgeon
  - Multimodal analgesia plan

## Postoperative care
- Careful fluid management
- One third of total blood loss is usually postoperative
- Ventilatory failure
- SIADH
- Severe pain
- Ileus, superior mesenteric artery syndrome

## Flashcards
- Q: Above what Cobb angle is scoliosis surgery indicated?
  - A: Over 40 degrees
- Q: What proportion of patients with idiopathic scoliosis have mitral valve prolapse?
  - A: 25%
- Q: What airway device is used for anterior thoracic spine surgery?
  - A: Double-lumen tube, for surgical access
- Q: How much of the total blood loss in scoliosis surgery occurs after the operation?
  - A: Usually one third
