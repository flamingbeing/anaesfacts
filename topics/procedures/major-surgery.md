# Major Surgery

Long operations with risks of haemodynamic instability, fluid shifts, blood loss, hypothermia and high pain needs.

## Main considerations
- Prolonged surgery
  - Positioning, urinary catheter, calf pumps
- Risk of haemodynamic instability
  - Lines: IV, arterial line, central venous line
  - Emergency drugs
  - Consider booking a high dependency bed
- Risk of fluid shifts, leading to haemodynamic instability and AKI
  - Urinary catheter with hourly urine charting
- Risk of blood loss, leading to haemodynamic instability and transfusion
  - Preoperative Hb, bleeding risk (platelets, PT/PTT), valid group and crossmatch
  - Good vascular access, arterial line, with or without central line
  - Blood warmer, with or without a Hotline fluid warmer
  - Chart blood loss; trend Hb on arterial blood gases and transfuse as needed
  - Call for blood, check it and keep it on standby in the fridge
  - Consider ROTEM if blood loss is significant
- Risk of surgical complications causing haemodynamic instability (e.g. a nicked blood vessel)
- Risk of hypothermia: temperature management
  - Temperature probe
  - Active warming: forced-air (Bair Hugger) and underbody warmer; consider Hotline
- Extensive surgery leads to high postoperative analgesic needs
  - Consider regional anaesthesia; counsel for PCA

## Before induction
- Insert the largest IV cannula possible (green or grey)
- Insert arterial and central lines either before or after induction
- Set up infusions and emergency drugs
- Set up monitoring: temperature probe, BIS, urinary catheter

## After induction
- Arterial blood gas, ROTEM
- Check blood, keep on standby in the fridge
- Hourly urine and blood loss charting
- With or without esomeprazole (Nexium)

## Postoperative care
- PCA
- High dependency care; consider a surgical ICU bed

## Flashcards
- Q: How is urine output monitored during major surgery?
  - A: Urinary catheter with hourly urine charting.
- Q: What preoperative blood tests are needed before major surgery with blood loss risk?
  - A: Hb, platelets, PT/PTT and a valid group and crossmatch.
- Q: How is ongoing blood loss guided intraoperatively?
  - A: Chart blood loss, trend Hb on arterial blood gases, and consider ROTEM if loss is significant.
- Q: What warming measures are used in major surgery?
  - A: Temperature probe, forced-air and underbody warmers, and consider a Hotline fluid warmer.
