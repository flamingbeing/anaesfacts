# Hysteroscopic Surgery

Short day-case gynaecological procedure; the main hazard is absorption of distension fluid.

## Overview
- Diagnostic or operative
- Operations: D&C, polypectomy, fibroid resection or myomectomy, intrauterine adhesions
- Day-case; short; blood loss usually small, but serious complications can occur
- Position: lithotomy, sometimes Trendelenburg

## Irrigation fluid risks
- Fluid overload
  - Distension medium enters vessels opened by resection
  - Causes volume expansion and electrolyte disturbance, like TURP syndrome
  - Risk factors: infusion pressure, amount of resection, length of surgery
- Hidden haemorrhage
  - Blood is diluted by irrigation fluid, so loss is hard to measure
  - Fluid absorption can mask signs of hypovolaemia
- Hypothermia

## Other intraoperative complications
- Air embolism
- Uterine perforation: check for intra-abdominal or pelvic organ injury by laparoscopy or laparotomy

## Anaesthetic technique
- Options
  - Local infiltration with or without sedation
  - Paracervical block with or without sedation
  - Neuraxial (spinal, epidural)
  - GA
- Diagnostic hysteroscopy: local or paracervical block can suffice
- Operative hysteroscopy: usually needs neuraxial or GA
- GA is the most common technique
- Regional may be better: allows early detection of hyponatraemia and fluid overload symptoms

## Intraoperative management
- Track fluid balance closely: key to preventing overload
- Surgeons may inject intramyometrial vasopressin: can cause hypertension and bradycardia
- Be ready to convert to laparoscopy or laparotomy if complications occur
- Fluid overload may need intubation

## Postoperative care
- Pain is minimal; NSAIDs usually suffice
- Day-case discharge

## Flashcards
- Q: What syndrome does fluid absorption in hysteroscopic surgery resemble?
  - A: TURP syndrome (volume expansion and electrolyte disturbance).
- Q: Risk factors for fluid overload in hysteroscopy?
  - A: Infusion pressure, amount of resection and duration of surgery.
- Q: What can intramyometrial vasopressin cause?
  - A: Hypertension and bradycardia.
- Q: Why may regional anaesthesia be preferred for operative hysteroscopy?
  - A: The awake patient shows early signs of hyponatraemia and fluid overload.
