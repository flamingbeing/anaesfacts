# Bariatric Surgery

Anaesthesia follows obesity principles, plus bougie handling, arm positioning and PONV control.

## Indications (UK)
- BMI >40, or BMI >35 with significant comorbidities
  - and non-surgical measures have failed for >6 months
- BMI >50: surgery as a 1st-line option
- Assessment by a bariatric MDT is required

## Intraoperative management
- As for obesity in general
- Consider an arterial line (not needed for shorter procedures)
- Position with both arms out
- Antibiotic: ceftriaxone
- Withdraw the nasogastric tube and temperature probes before stapling
- Fentanyl plus multimodal analgesia; titrate analgesia in PACU

## Specific operations
- Sleeve gastrectomy
  - A large orogastric bougie sizes the sleeve
    - Insert at the start and hold in the oesophagus at 30cm; advance before stapling; remove without suction
  - Buscopan (hyoscine butylbromide) 20mg, repeatable every 30min, helps bougie passage and stapling; side effect: tachycardia
- Gastric bypass (Roux-en-Y)
  - Large orogastric bougie to form the pouch
- Gastric banding
  - Shortest operation; minimal opioids
  - PONV risk: two antiemetics essential
- Intragastric balloon
  - Often left lateral, under sedation
  - High PONV after insertion

## Postoperative complications
- Anastomotic leak: tachycardia, abdominal pain, fever, tachypnoea

## Flashcards
- Q: UK BMI criteria for bariatric surgery?
  - A: BMI >40, or >35 with significant comorbidities, after >6 months of failed non-surgical measures; BMI >50 as first line.
- Q: Buscopan dose during sleeve gastrectomy?
  - A: 20mg, repeatable every 30min; causes tachycardia.
- Q: What must be withdrawn before stapling?
  - A: Nasogastric tube and temperature probes.
- Q: Signs of an anastomotic leak after bariatric surgery?
  - A: Tachycardia, abdominal pain, fever, tachypnoea.
