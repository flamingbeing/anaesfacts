# Parathyroid Surgery

Removal of overactive parathyroid tissue, usually for primary hyperparathyroidism; shared airway, often renal patients, check calcium after.

## Quick summary
- Patient: often end-stage renal failure
- Technique: armoured tube or north-facing RAE tube with straight extension, head wrap and bite block
  - Smooth extubation to reduce bleeding
  - Often TIVA
  - Monitoring and lines: forced-air warmer, temperature probe
- Position: supine, shoulder roll to extend the neck, slight head up for venous drainage, arms tucked (long extensions)
- Antibiotics: cefazolin
- Procedure: with or without frozen section
  - Duration: 3-4hrs in total
  - Blood loss: under 100ml
  - Pain: moderate
  - Antiemetics: single or dual (PONV causes bleeding; less airway oedema)
- Complications: tube dislodgement, circuit disconnection
- Special points:
  - Shared airway: secure airway, tight connections
  - With or without recurrent laryngeal nerve monitoring: no paralysis (use remifentanil), electrode wrapped around the ETT
  - Calcium levels may be checked intraoperatively
- Postoperative: airway monitoring in PACU; calcium levels

## Background
- Four parathyroid glands control calcium via parathyroid hormone (PTH)
- PTH acts on bone and kidney: serum calcium rises, phosphate falls
  - Bone: osteoclasts release calcium and phosphate
  - Kidney: more phosphate excreted, more calcium reabsorbed
- Commonest indication: primary hyperparathyroidism (PHP) from an adenoma
  - Incidence about 25 per 100 000 in the UK; up to 1 in 500 women over 45 years
  - Hypercalcaemia: fatigue, bone, abdominal, urinary and mental symptoms ("stones, bones, abdominal groans and psychic moans")
  - Also more cardiovascular deaths, impaired glucose tolerance, more fractures, worse quality of life
- Traditional surgery: collar incision, bilateral neck exploration, find all four glands, remove the abnormal one(s)
  - GA as for thyroid surgery; airway compression is rare
  - Duration unpredictable, especially with frozen section or PTH assays, so actively keep warm

## Minimally invasive parathyroidectomy
- Over 80% of PHP is a single adenoma; removing it cures the disease
- Localisation used to be unreliable:
  - Neck ultrasound depends on the operator
  - Thallium-technetium subtraction scanning depends on adenoma size
- Technetium-99 m sestamibi scanning locates size and site correctly in 88% of patients
- Then a minimal access approach is possible, usually through a 2-cm incision over the gland

## Anaesthesia
- GA still common, with a reinforced ETT or LMA
- Increasingly done under local anaesthesia, which suits patients with significant cardiorespiratory disease
  - Options: cervical plexus blocks, or surgical infiltration, with or without sedation
- Cervical plexus blocks: deep, superficial or both
  - Deep block is harder and riskier: dural cuff or vertebral artery injection, phrenic nerve palsy
  - So avoid bilateral deep blocks
  - Superficial alone versus superficial plus deep: no difference in supplemental analgesia, satisfaction, pain scores or later analgesia, except the combined group asked for analgesia sooner
  - Either way, extra local anaesthetic is needed at the upper poles of the thyroid for retraction
- Sedation with infiltration: midazolam with remifentanil, or alfentanil

## Postoperative care
- Check serum calcium at 6 and 24 h
- Treat hypocalcaemia with supplements; persistent hypercalcaemia is rare
- Pain usually mild; oral analgesia
- Avoid NSAIDs with renal impairment
