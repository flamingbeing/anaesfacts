# Lumbar Spine Surgery

Prone posterior lumbar surgery, from short day-case microdiscectomy to long multilevel fusions with blood loss and neuromonitoring.

## Laminectomy, fusion and TLIF
### Procedure
- Corrects spondylolisthesis or spinal stenosis causing pain or instability
- Single or multiple levels
- Posterior approach, prone
  - Laminotomy (part of the lamina removed) or laminectomy (all of it) decompresses the cord or nerve roots
  - If a discectomy is needed, the dura is retracted aside and the disc removed in pieces
  - Multilevel decompression may need stabilisation or fusion (e.g. plates and screws)
- Bone graft may be taken from the pelvis

### Main considerations
- Number of levels drives duration and blood loss
- Spinal cord protection: maintain MAP
- Neuromonitoring: TIVA with remifentanil, BIS
- Multilevel surgery
  - Blood loss can be considerable: ensure a valid group and crossmatch, large-bore IV, consider arterial line, fluid warmer if several levels
  - Consider TXA, cell salvage, hypotensive anaesthesia
  - Pain can be significant (PCA may be needed) but less than cervical surgery
- Temperature probe, forced-air warming, urinary catheter
- Prolonged prone position
  - Prone headrest (e.g. ProneView), pad pressure points, eyes free
  - Secure airway: bite block, consider armoured tube
  - Consider dexamethasone; avoid excess crystalloid
  - Enough staff to turn the patient
- Surgeon may ask for a Valsalva to check venous haemostasis or the dural repair

### Preoperative assessment
- Counsel about prone position; check equipment
- Group and crossmatch

### Intraoperative management
- Position: prone, arms above head on arm boards
- Monitoring and lines: BIS, with or without arterial line (keep transducer on the bed as the table is raised and lowered often)
- GA with ETT
  - TIVA, single dose atracurium
  - TIVA for maintenance to allow neuromonitoring
- Antibiotic: cefazolin
- Fluids: avoid excess to limit oedema
- Can be long and major: blood conservation, keep BP low
- Turning prone
  - Induce on the trolley; secure tube, insert bite block and temperature probe
  - Fit prone headrest, extend circuit with a straight connector
  - Disconnect monitors and ETT, then turn
  - Meanwhile the neurophysiologist places electrodes and the surgeons catheterise
- Image intensifier needed; O-arm sometimes
- Analgesia: usually substantial, e.g. morphine 0.1mg/kg; PCA often needed
- Antiemetic: dexamethasone (also for airway oedema)
- Usually neuromonitoring; check how many levels
- Some teams secure the tube with a tie around the neck

### Postoperative care
- Patients are often in severe pain in recovery
- High dependency usually not needed

### Analgesia (PROSPECT 2020, laminectomy)
- Paracetamol plus an NSAID or COX-2 inhibitor, started before or during surgery and continued after, unless contraindicated
- Local anaesthetic wound infiltration or instillation just before closure
- Opioids as rescue only

## Discectomy and microdiscectomy
- Time 1–2 hr; pain mild to moderate; blood loss not significant; prone
- Microdiscectomy can be a day case
- Position: prone with prone headrest, arms above head
- IV in the right hand
- GA; spinal described in case reports
  - Airway as for spine: armoured ETT; induce on trolley then turn
  - Consider special tape for the ETT; bite block if neuromonitoring
  - Reconnect ETT after turning; check eyes
  - Drugs: propofol and remifentanil (stimulation comes in spikes), atracurium
- TIVA if neuromonitoring (usually none, but check)
- Antibiotic: cefazolin
- Image intensifier only at the start
- Microscope; about 2 hrs; minimally invasive, faster than open discectomy
- Analgesia: morphine, parecoxib
- Antiemetics: two agents
- Day surgery discharge

## Lumbar foraminal block
- Patient can position themselves
  - Cervical: left lateral, head on a foam pad, pillow to hug held by an arm guard; arm reaches down, head bent away from the side to open the space; wires out of the beam
  - Lumbar: prone, arms above head on arm boards
- IV on the right for lumbar; extension lets fentanyl be given from behind the lead shield
- MAC with face mask and EtCO2
  - Keep sedation light: patient must report paraesthesia
  - Options: fentanyl 25-50 mcg; midazolam, ketamine, propofol and fentanyl; or propofol with dexmedetomidine
- No antibiotic, no ventilation, minimal fluid, no analgesia or antiemetic
- Image intensifier; table can be adjusted from behind the lead shield with the remote
- Very quick: most time goes on positioning; injection takes about 5 mins
- Day surgery discharge

## Lumbar spine fracture fixation
- Spine may be unstable; on spinal nursing precautions
- Other major injuries may coexist, especially rib fractures
- Suxamethonium may be contraindicated
- May already be in ICU and ventilated
- Neurological deficit is often irreversible
