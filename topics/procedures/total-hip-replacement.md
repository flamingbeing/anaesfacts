# Total Hip Replacement

Like a hip fracture operation but elective and without the fall; key issues are bone cement implantation syndrome, blood loss and VTE prophylaxis.

## Overview
- Similar to neck of femur surgery without the emergency or the fall; underlying cause may be rheumatoid arthritis
- Spinal preferred, but GA is often used because of duration; a nerve block is less critical
- Main concerns: bone cement implantation syndrome (BCIS) and blood loss
- Group and crossmatch; give tranexamic acid

## Patient factors
- Often elderly
- Start VTE prophylaxis on admission: at least 1% develop DVT even with prophylaxis

## Surgical factors
- Position: **lateral** or **supine**
- Intraoperative antibiotic prophylaxis needed
- Duration: 90-120min
- Bone cement implantation syndrome:
  - **Make sure the patient is well hydrated** before spinal and during cementing
  - Cement triples the risk of PE (3-fold)
  - In **severe cardiac disease, an uncemented prosthesis may be better**: agree with the surgeon **before surgery**

## Blood loss
- Typically **300–500mL** (300-500ml); less with neuraxial techniques; varies widely
- A **similar volume may be lost into drains and tissues after surgery**
- Have a **valid group and crossmatch (unlike TKR)**
- 16G or larger cannula, in the upper arm if lateral
- Active warming clearly reduces blood loss, morbidity and mortality
- **Consider cell salvage** for complex surgery

## Analgesia
- Pain: severe (+++)
- After surgery, **simple oral opioids with regular paracetamol or NSAIDs usually suffice**; caution with NSAIDs in the elderly

## Anaesthetic technique
- Spinal with sedation:
  - **Single-shot spinal**: about 3mL bupivacaine 0.5%, adjusted for size; diamorphine 0.25–0.5mg prolongs analgesia
  - Lateral position sedation: intermittent midazolam or TCI propofol, with oxygen by face mask
  - Midazolam can cause acute delirium in the elderly; occasionally conversion to GA is needed
- Longer cases: **combined spinal-epidural**
  - Postoperative epidural usually means a urinary catheter (also helps fluid balance); best placed during surgery
- If neuraxial block is contraindicated: **lumbar plexus (psoas) block or femoral 3-in-1 block** gives similar analgesia and can supplement GA
- GA with LMA, with or without a block:
  - Supine: LMA with light GA
  - Complex or long operations: GA (not sedation) with or without epidural or block

## Postoperative care
- Supine on the bed with the **legs abducted on a pillow** to prevent dislocation
- Continue VTE prophylaxis
- **Check Hb** at 24hr; treat with transfusion or iron as needed
- **Mobilise at 24–48hr**; an **epidural infusion must stop before mobilising**, so its benefit is mostly early (up to 6hr)

## Bilateral total hip replacement
- Chosen by some surgeons for younger, fit patients
- **Major operation**: select carefully; significant cardiovascular disease raises mortality
- **GA** with epidural is most practical
- **Invasive monitoring**: arterial line, with or without CVP

## Revision total hip replacement
- As for primary, but longer, bloodier and more painful; all **depend on complexity**, so discuss with the surgeon
- Patients older with more comorbidities
- Position: lateral or supine
- Duration: **2-6hr depending on complexity**; at least 2–3hr, often longer
- Blood loss: **about 1L, sometimes much more; group and crossmatch 2U**
  - Use cell salvage wherever possible
  - Transfusion often needed; have two units of crossmatched blood in theatre
- Pain: very severe (++++)
  - Can be a **major problem**; **epidural** infusion useful, **PCA** an alternative
- Mobilisation depends on complexity and strength of the reconstruction
- Technique: GA with or without epidural or nerve block; **too long for a single-shot spinal**
- **Arterial line, and consider a CVP line**, if major bleeding is expected or cardiovascular status warrants

## Flashcards
- Q: Typical blood loss in primary THR?
  - A: 300–500mL, with a similar amount lost after surgery into drains and tissues
- Q: Single-shot spinal for THR?
  - A: About 3mL bupivacaine 0.5%, with diamorphine 0.25–0.5mg if wanted
- Q: By how much does bone cement raise PE risk?
  - A: About 3-fold
- Q: Blood to have available for revision THR?
  - A: 2 units crossmatched; expect about 1L loss
- Q: DVT rate in THR despite prophylaxis?
  - A: At least 1%
