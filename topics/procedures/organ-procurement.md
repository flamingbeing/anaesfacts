# Organ Procurement for Transplantation

Anaesthesia for organ retrieval from brain dead (DBD) and circulatory death (DCD) donors, and the ischaemic time limits for each organ.

## Cold ischaemic time (CIT)
- Storage time: starts at aortic cross-clamp or cold preservation flush, ends when the organ leaves cold storage
- Limits by organ:
  - Heart (most sensitive): 4–6 hours; little cellular reserve, so needs quick reimplantation
  - Lungs: 6–8 hours; endothelial and alveolar injury happen easily
  - Liver: 8–12 hours; longer CIT raises the risk of ischaemic cholangiopathy (bile duct injury)
  - Pancreas: 15 hours; prolonged ischaemia predisposes to post-transplant pancreatitis
  - Kidney (most resilient): 24–36 hours; low metabolic demand when not filtering

## Warm ischaemic time (WIT)
- Time the organ spends at body temperature with its blood supply stopped or reduced
- Starts when the organ warms to body temperature during implantation and ends at completion of the anastomosis (reperfusion)
  - In practice, this is the anastomosis time
- Limits are set by practical surgical constraints:
  - Heart: <45 minutes
  - Lungs: <30-45 minutes per lung
  - Liver: <30-45 min
  - Kidney: <30-45 min

## Donor warm ischaemic time (DWIT)
- Extra, unavoidable period of warm ischaemia in DCD donors before cold perfusion begins
- Agonal phase: from the start of treatment withdrawal to cessation of cardiac output
- Absolute WIT: from cardiac arrest to start of the cold flush
- Functional DWIT (fDWIT): from when donor perfusion falls below a set limit
- fDWIT limits by organ (memory aid: LHP30, K60, L90); actual time depends on how quickly the donor dies
  - Liver: <30 min
  - Heart: <30 min
  - Pancreas: <30 min
  - Kidneys: <60 min (timed from SBP <50 mmHg); beyond this DCD is abandoned
  - Lung: <90 min

## Sequence of ischaemic periods
- Organ blood flow stops: DWIT begins (DCD donors only, because of the no-touch period)
- Cold flush or cross-clamp: CIT begins (organ on ice or machine perfusion)
- Implantation starts and the organ warms: WIT begins
- Reperfusion with recipient blood
- Issues: graft function, reperfusion injury

## Donation after brain death (DBD)

### Preoperative care
- Continue ICU donor management
- May need crossmatched blood

### Rationale for anaesthetic drugs
- Use of anaesthetic drugs during retrieval is debated
- Aim is to block haemodynamic and motor responses driven by spinal cord reflexes
  - Justifies neuromuscular blockers and analgesics
- Opioids alone may not control catecholamine surges from surgical stimulation
  - Volatile agents can be used as vasodilators

### Intraoperative management
- Continue ICU supportive care
  - Invasive monitoring, vasopressor infusions
  - Active warming and temperature monitoring
- Anaesthesia
  - Opioids for haemodynamic stability: spinal sympathetic pathways still respond to noxious stimuli (as in spinal cord injury)
  - Paralyse: spinal reflexes can cause complex movements (Lazarus sign) that interfere with surgery
  - Do not use BIS: medically and legally meaningless

### Procurement
- Heparin 300–400 units/kg via central line about 3-5 minutes before aortic cross-clamp, ready for preservative flush
- Once the aorta is clamped and cold flush starts, the ventilator and monitors are switched off
  - Anaesthetic care ends; the anaesthetist can leave and does not need to remove the tube
- Usual order of removal: heart, lung, pancreas, liver, kidney
  - In fast-track or unstable cases, liver and pancreas may be prioritised as the transplant team requires
- The deceased is then sent to the mortuary

## Donation after circulatory death (DCD)
- Functional warm ischaemic time applies (see above)
- If the agonal period plus no-touch period is too long, the transplant is called off

### Process
- Withdrawal of care (extubation, inotropes stopped)
  - May take place in ICU or in theatre
- No-touch time of 5 mins after a flat arterial line trace
- 2 certifiers certify death
- Death must occur within a set window of 60 mins (the warm ischaemic limit for kidneys)
- Organ retrieval then starts rapidly

## Organ-preserving CPR
- CPR given after cardiac arrest to preserve organs for donation rather than to revive the patient
- Can honour the wish to donate for patients and families
- Aggressive: may injure the patient and cause psychological harm to families and staff

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the cold ischaemic time limits for heart, liver and kidney?
  - A: Heart 4–6 hours, liver 8–12 hours, kidney 24–36 hours.
- Q: What heparin dose is given before aortic cross-clamp in organ retrieval?
  - A: 300–400 units/kg via central line about 3-5 minutes before cross-clamp.
- Q: What is the kidney functional DWIT limit in DCD?
  - A: <60 min, timed from SBP <50 mmHg; beyond this DCD is abandoned.
- Q: How long is the no-touch time in DCD?
  - A: 5 mins after a flat arterial line trace.
- Q: Why are muscle relaxants given to brain dead donors?
  - A: Spinal cord reflexes can cause complex movements (Lazarus sign) that interfere with surgery.
