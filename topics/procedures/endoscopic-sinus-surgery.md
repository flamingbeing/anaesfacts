# Endoscopic Sinus Surgery

Shared airway with blood and secretions; aim for a bloodless field and a smooth emergence (especially after pituitary work).

## Key points
- Shared airway
- Blood and secretions in the airway
- Bloodless field; smooth emergence, especially if pituitary

## Surgery
- Example: functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS)
- Indications: chronic sinusitis, severe epistaxis, tumours of the anterior skull base, pituitary and sinuses, CSF leak repair

## Preoperative assessment
- Patients usually have few comorbidities
- Pituitary tumours and their removal have physiological effects: acromegaly, diabetes insipidus, thyroid dysregulation
- Warn patients they will not be able to breathe through the nose afterwards

## Intraoperative management
- Anaesthesia
  - GA, usually TIVA with remifentanil
  - Volatile or propofol both acceptable; propofol TIVA gives a smoother wake-up with less coughing
  - Real-time CT image guidance may get in the way of a BIS sensor; if used, place it as high as possible
  - Remifentanil keeps BP low, reducing bleeding and improving the surgical view
  - Usually some long-acting opioid as well
- Airway
  - South-facing RAE tube, positioned to the left
  - Rarely, a flexible LMA for pure turbinoplasty, as it is very short
  - Tape the tube to the lower lip, nothing on the upper lip
  - Throat pack, as large volumes of blood and secretions can collect
  - Suction the oropharynx thoroughly before extubation
  - Some pass an orogastric tube to empty blood and secretions before extubation
- Monitoring and access
  - Non-invasive monitoring and one IV cannula usually enough; blood loss is usually modest
  - Complex tumour resection or epistaxis: large-bore access and blood products available
- Can be long
- Antibiotics: co-amoxiclav (Augmentin)
- Position: supine on a head ring, no shoulder roll, 15 degrees head up
- Emergence
  - Smooth wake-up to limit hypertension and bleeding
  - Nose is packed at the end, which is often distressing
  - Tell patients to breathe through the mouth on waking
  - Consider removing the plug at emergence

## Postoperative management
- Pain usually mild: morphine 0.05 mg/kg is enough

## Flashcards
- Q: Which airway is used for FESS and where is it taped?
  - A: South-facing RAE tube to the left, taped to the lower lip, with a throat pack
- Q: Why use remifentanil in sinus surgery?
  - A: It keeps BP low to reduce bleeding and improve the surgical view
- Q: What head position is used for FESS?
  - A: Supine on a head ring, 15 degrees head up, no shoulder roll
- Q: What morphine dose is usually enough after FESS?
  - A: 0.05 mg/kg
