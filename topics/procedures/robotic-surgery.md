# Robotic Surgery

Robotic surgery adds extreme positioning, more hypercapnia, poor patient access and longer operating times.

## Anaesthetic considerations
- Positioning may be more extreme and steeper than in open or laparoscopic surgery (e.g. steep Trendelenburg)
  - Higher risk of the patient sliding off the table
- More hypercapnia: a problem in traumatic brain injury (TBI)
- Difficult access to the patient once the robot is docked
- The robot's arms may injure the patient
- Overall operating time may be longer

## Flashcards
- Q: Which position is typical for robotic pelvic surgery and what is the main safety risk?
  - A: Steep Trendelenburg; the patient may slide off the table
- Q: Why is robotic surgery a concern in TBI?
  - A: It may cause more hypercapnia
- Q: Name two practical problems the robot causes for the anaesthetist.
  - A: Difficult access to the patient and risk of injury from the robot's arms
