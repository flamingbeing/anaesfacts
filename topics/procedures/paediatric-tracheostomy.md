# Paediatric tracheostomy

Shared airway, often difficult intubation; tube sited between the second and third tracheal rings, ETT kept in until the tracheostomy ventilates, and a dislodged tube is managed from above.

## Indications
- Congenital or acquired upper airway obstruction (e.g. Pierre Robin syndrome, severe laryngomalacia)
- Long-term respiratory support (e.g. Ondine's curse, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome)

## Anaesthetic considerations
- Shared airway
- Potentially difficult intubation
- Sited between the second and third tracheal rings
  - Below the first ring to reduce tracheal stenosis risk
  - Too low risks endobronchial intubation

## Preoperative assessment
- Indication drives technique
- Key: cause of airway obstruction and known ease of intubation
- Long-term ventilation group: respiratory function from the notes
- Examine the airway; predict difficulty with airway maintenance and intubation
- Surgeon and anaesthetist should plan together
- ICU bed may be needed

## Anaesthetic technique
- Preferred when possible: intubation, neuromuscular blockade and IPPV
- May arrive from ICU already intubated
- If difficult: gas induction, intubate under deep inhalational anaesthesia
- Can be done breathing spontaneously via face mask or LMA if needed
- Maintenance: volatile in oxygen and air, or 100% oxygen
  - Nitrous oxide usually avoided in case of pneumothorax
- Shoulder roll to fully extend the neck and bring the trachea forward
- Surgeon infiltrates LA with adrenaline (haemostasis, analgesia)
- Opioid bolus: postoperative sedation so the agitated child does not dislodge the tube
- Ventilate with 100% oxygen for a few minutes before tracheal incision (reserve for the ETT-to-tracheostomy change)
- Tracheostomy tube usually 0.5 mm larger than the ETT it replaces
- ETT pulled back but not removed until ventilation via tracheostomy is adequate (can be re-advanced)
- Two stay sutures either side of the incision, taped to the chest: hold the stoma open if the tube must be replaced in the first few days
- Fibrescope through the ETT often used to watch tracheostomy tube insertion; then ETT withdrawn
- Fibrescope through the tracheostomy tube confirms the tip is above the carina
- Remove the shoulder roll and flex the neck before tying tapes, so they are not too loose

## Postoperative care
- Early CXR: position and length, exclude pneumothorax
- Common problems: bleeding, surgical emphysema, decannulation, blockage, pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum
- Accidental decannulation:
  - Manage airway and ventilation from above (face mask or LMA, oral intubation if needed)
  - Replace the tracheostomy tube later under controlled conditions
  - Reinsertion should not be first-line: risk of tracheal trauma and false passage, especially by unskilled staff
- Track takes 7-9 days to mature; constant nursing supervision until then
  - Usually high dependency; occasionally ICU and IPPV
- Upper airway warming and humidification bypassed: needs humidified oxygen and frequent suction

## Flashcards
- Q: At what level is a paediatric tracheostomy sited?
  - A: Between the second and third tracheal rings
- Q: How does the tracheostomy tube size compare with the ETT?
  - A: Usually 0.5 mm larger
- Q: How long does a paediatric tracheostomy track take to mature?
  - A: 7-9 days
- Q: First action if a fresh paediatric tracheostomy dislodges?
  - A: Manage the airway from above (face mask, LMA or oral intubation), not reinsertion
- Q: Why is nitrous oxide usually avoided?
  - A: In case of pneumothorax
