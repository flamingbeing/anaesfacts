# Tenckhoff Catheter Insertion and Removal

Peritoneal dialysis catheter: insertion usually laparoscopic under GA with paralysis; removal often under local with sedation.

## Insertion
### Preoperative assessment
- May have had temporary femoral dialysis catheter beforehand
- Many are vasculopaths, though some are younger

### Surgery
- Usually laparoscopic, consider open
- Very quick: <1 hr

### Technique
- GA needed
- LMA or ETT
- Surgeons usually prefer the patient paralysed

## Removal
- Simpler and quicker than insertion
  - Usually open: old scar reopened, fibrosed subcutaneous cuffs dissected out, catheter pulled
- Indications: peritonitis not responding to antibiotics, catheter malfunction (kinking or migration), successful kidney transplant

### Preoperative assessment
- Sepsis or peritonitis: may be unstable, acidotic or septic; check inflammatory markers and blood cultures
- Dialysis: when was the last session; will temporary haemodialysis access (Permcath or Vas-cath) be needed once PD access is gone?
- Fluid status: peritonitis may cause relative hypovolaemia (inflammation, poor intake), or overload if PD has failed

### Intraoperative management
- Local infiltration with sedation (monitored anaesthesia care): ideal for stable patients
  - Cuff dissection is the most painful step
- GA
  - Indications: anxiety, failed local, suspected deep tunnel infection needing wide debridement
  - Airway: LMA usually enough (short, no laparoscopy)
  - Paralysis: usually not needed unless deeper peritoneal exploration

## Flashcards
- Q: Usual anaesthetic for Tenckhoff insertion?
  - A: GA with LMA or ETT; surgeons usually want paralysis; under 1 hr
- Q: Usual anaesthetic for Tenckhoff removal in a stable patient?
  - A: Local infiltration with sedation
- Q: Most painful part of Tenckhoff removal under local?
  - A: Dissection of the cuffs
