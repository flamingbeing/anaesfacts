# Major lower limb amputation

Removal of necrotic or infected limb tissue (below or above knee), often in sick, septic, anticoagulated vascular patients.

## Preoperative assessment
- Typical patient
  - Frail, bed-bound diabetic with significant cardiovascular disease and several previous revascularisation attempts
  - May be septic from necrotic tissue: relative contraindication to neuraxial
  - Often anticoagulated or on dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for limb ischaemia, limiting neuraxial options
  - Chronic ischaemic pain may mean high baseline opioid needs; regional analgesia gives more predictable postoperative relief
- Urgency
  - Often source control for sepsis (wet gangrene, infected ulcers); cancelling is rarely an option
- Investigations
  - Check sepsis markers

## Intraoperative management
- Position: supine
- Monitoring: consider invasive arterial monitoring if septic or haemodynamically unstable
- Peripheral nerve block
  - BKA: popliteal sciatic plus adductor canal block, especially if anticoagulated
  - More stable haemodynamics, especially in septic shock
  - Usable when anticoagulated
  - AKA is hard under blocks alone: needs 4 nerves blocked (sciatic, femoral, obturator, lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh)
- General anaesthesia may be needed
  - AKA
  - Neuraxial contraindicated (sepsis, anticoagulation or DAPT)
- Spinal anaesthesia with or without sedation
  - Excellent anaesthesia, can be made unilateral
  - May be contraindicated if septic or on DAPT

## Analgesia
- Peripheral nerve block gives good analgesia for up to 24 hours
  - After GA, adding a regional block is still advisable
- Surgeon-placed stump catheter for local anaesthetic infusion (e.g. bupivacaine 0.25% at 5 mL/h)
- Epidural if feasible, or spinal with morphine
- Systemic adjuncts: ketamine, gabapentin

## Blood loss
- Depends on tourniquet use: vascular surgeons often do not use one, orthopaedic surgeons do
- Group and crossmatch

## Postoperative care
- Phantom limb pain affects 60–70% of amputees at some point
- Distinguish it from surgical pain; involve the pain team

## Flashcards
- Q: Which nerve blocks cover a below knee amputation?
  - A: Popliteal sciatic plus adductor canal block.
- Q: Which 4 nerves must be blocked for an AKA under regional alone?
  - A: Sciatic, femoral, obturator and lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh.
- Q: Typical stump catheter infusion?
  - A: Bupivacaine 0.25% at 5 mL/h.
- Q: How common is phantom limb pain after amputation?
  - A: 60–70% of amputees at some point.
