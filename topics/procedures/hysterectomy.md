# Hysterectomy

Removal of the uterus by open, vaginal or laparoscopic route; anaesthesia follows the approach.

## Types
- Total hysterectomy: uterus and cervix, ovaries left
  - The most common type
- Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy: uterus, cervix, tubes and ovaries
- Radical hysterectomy: uterus, cervix, upper vagina, most of the surrounding pelvic tissue, sometimes pelvic lymph nodes
  - For some cancers
- Supracervical (subtotal) hysterectomy: body of uterus only, cervix kept
  - Uncommon; cervical cancer risk remains, so cervical screening continues
  - Chosen by some women who want to keep the cervix

## Approaches
- **Open abdominal**: removal through an abdominal incision of about six to eight inches
  - Mostly used when **tubes and ovaries are also removed**, the **uterus is large** (e.g. fibroids), or disease has spread in the **pelvis** (endometriosis, cancer)
  - Incision vertical (umbilicus to pubis) or transverse (along the top of the pubic hairline)
- **Vaginal**: removal through the vagina
  - Mostly for **uterine prolapse** or when **vaginal repairs** are also needed
  - No external scar
- Laparoscopic
  - **Laparoscope-assisted vaginal hysterectomy (LAVH)**: intra-abdominal part done laparoscopically, the rest (vaginal incision, cervical excision) transvaginally; uterus removed in pieces via the port or the vagina
  - Total laparoscopic hysterectomy: whole operation laparoscopic, specimen delivered vaginally
- Robotic-assisted radical total laparoscopic hysterectomy

## Abdominal hysterectomy
- Removal of the uterus through an abdominal incision (may include ovaries)
- Pelvic lymph node dissection can make it much longer: arterial line, careful fluids

### Preoperative assessment
- Menorrhagia or postmenopausal bleeding: check Hb, group and hold
- Check urea and electrolytes: possible ureteric obstruction
- Anxiety

### Intraoperative management
- Supine, head down so bowel falls away from the uterus
- GA with ETT, IPPV and non-depolarising neuromuscular blocker
- PONV prophylaxis
- Blocks
  - Pfannenstiel (bikini line) incision: bilateral ilioinguinal blocks, TAP blocks or local infiltration
  - Midline incision: epidural or rectus sheath catheters

### Postoperative management
- Simple analgesia plus PCA
- PONV common

## Vaginal hysterectomy
- Degree of prolapse determines surgical difficulty

### Preoperative assessment
- Patients often older and may be frail

### Intraoperative management
- GA with LMA, or spinal
- Lithotomy with legs down
  - Position carefully (osteoarthritis, nerve injury)

## Laparoscopic hysterectomy
- Laparoscopic-assisted or total laparoscopic approach, e.g. to also remove tubes and ovaries
- Intubate (ETT)

### Postoperative management
- Opioids, NSAIDs and local anaesthetic

## Flashcards
- Q: Which blocks suit a Pfannenstiel incision for abdominal hysterectomy?
  - A: Bilateral ilioinguinal blocks, TAP blocks or local infiltration
- Q: What analgesia suits a midline incision?
  - A: Epidural or rectus sheath catheters
- Q: Why check urea and electrolytes before abdominal hysterectomy?
  - A: Possible ureteric obstruction
- Q: What anaesthetic options suit a vaginal hysterectomy?
  - A: GA with LMA, or spinal, in lithotomy
