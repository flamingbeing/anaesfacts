# Thoracic Anaesthesia Overview

Key themes: the underlying disease, lung function testing, one-lung ventilation with lung isolation, and regional analgesia.

## Summary
- Underlying disease
- 3-legged stool of respiratory assessment, ABG
- One-lung ventilation, lung-protective ventilation, postoperative CXR
- Double-lumen tube
- Analgesia: regional

## Operations
- Airway procedures (see ENT)
- Mediastinal surgery
  - Mediastinoscopy and biopsy
  - Mediastinal mass resection
  - Thymectomy
  - Retrosternal goitre
- VATS
  - Pleurodesis for pneumothorax
  - Decortication for empyema
  - Thoracic sympathectomy
  - Lung biopsy
  - Pericardial window
  - Wedge and segmental resections
  - Lobectomy
- Thoracotomy or sternotomy
  - Mediastinal masses
  - Lobectomy
  - Pneumonectomy
  - Oesophagectomy

## Anatomy
- Trachea runs anterior to and slightly right of the oesophagus; adult diameter 2.2-2.4 cm
- Carina lies at the level of the sternal angle
- Right main bronchus: wider, shorter, more vertical; 2-2.5 cm long
- Left main bronchus: 5 cm long
- Branching: main (primary) bronchi, then lobar (secondary), then segmental (tertiary)

## Preoperative assessment
- Airway
  - Double-lumen tube size: based on sex, height and tracheal diameter on CT
- Respiratory function
  - 3-legged stool: PFTs, FEV1, DLCO
  - ABG

## Intraoperative management
- Lung isolation
  - Double-lumen tube
  - Bronchial blocker
  - Single-lumen tube advanced into the chosen main bronchus
- Subglottic tracheal or carinal obstruction from external compression or tumour invasion
- Surgical handling can obstruct venous return: give volume and ask the surgeon to stop compressing the heart
- Arrhythmia: usually AF

## Postoperative care
- Avoid prolonged ventilation where possible, because it:
  - Stresses pulmonary suture lines
  - Increases air leaks
  - Increases infection risk
- Physiotherapy
- Humidified oxygen

## Analgesia
- Depends on the operation: uniportal VATS, multiport VATS or thoracotomy
  - Thoracotomy is very painful, especially more posterior incisions; good analgesia is vital
- Intraoperative
  - Multimodal: paracetamol, NSAIDs (not after pleurodesis), opioids
  - Regional: paravertebral, erector spinae plane, intercostal blocks (by the surgeon), thoracic epidural
  - Surgical cryoablation of intercostal nerves: not effective immediately, so early postoperative analgesia is still needed
- Postoperative
  - Multimodal plus opioids
  - PCA
  - Regional technique with or without a catheter, or epidural

## Flashcards
- Q: Adult tracheal diameter and main bronchus lengths?
  - A: Trachea 2.2-2.4 cm; right main bronchus 2-2.5 cm; left main bronchus 5 cm.
- Q: What is the 3-legged stool?
  - A: Preoperative respiratory assessment using PFTs, FEV1 and DLCO (with ABG).
- Q: What determines double-lumen tube size?
  - A: Sex, height and CT tracheal diameter.
- Q: Why avoid prolonged postoperative ventilation after lung surgery?
  - A: It stresses suture lines and increases air leaks and infection risk.
- Q: When should NSAIDs be avoided in thoracic surgery?
  - A: After pleurodesis.
