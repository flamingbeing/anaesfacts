# Oesophageal Surgery and POEM

Hiatal hernia, reflux and achalasia surgery carries a high aspiration risk: RSI, and in POEM watch for CO2 insufflation complications.

## General points
- Hiatal hernia, achalasia and reflux (GERD) surgery is usually laparoscopic
- Treat as full stomach: RSI
- Severe reflux: consider recurrent aspiration pneumonia and reduced pulmonary reserve

## Peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM) for achalasia
- Done with an OGD scope fitted with a mucosal cap or hood
- Mucosal bleb raised 10–12 cm above the gastro-oesophageal junction (GEJ) with saline plus methylene blue or indigo carmine dye, to show the submucosal space
- Longitudinal mucosal incision with an electrocautery knife
- Submucosal tunnel dissected down to 3 cm below the GEJ
- Starting 2–3 cm distal to the incision, the circular muscle is divided (longitudinal fibres spared) to 2 cm below the GEJ
- Mucosal entry closed with endoscopic clips
- Intraoperative complications mostly from CO2 insufflation:
  - Pneumothorax
  - Capnopericardium
  - Mediastinal emphysema
  - Subcutaneous emphysema
  - Pneumoperitoneum

## Intraoperative management of POEM
- GA with ETT, supine
- Airway:
  - RSI with cricoid pressure: risk of aspirating retained food
  - Cuffed armoured ETT may be preferred: less kinking or obstruction
  - ETT with subglottic suction port tried, no proven benefit
  - Nasal intubation has been used to avoid tube displacement by the scope
  - Tape the ETT to the right side of the mouth so the scope can pass
  - Endoscopist stands at the head, so airway access is poor: secure depth and fixation and protect the eyes before starting
- CO2 problems:
  - Hyperventilate to clear insufflated CO2; if ETCO2 keeps rising, pause the procedure for several minutes
  - Sudden rise in ETCO2 or peak airway pressure: early sign of capnothorax
  - Rising peak pressure can also mean abdominal distension; the surgeon may need percutaneous abdominal decompression
  - Check the neck for crepitus (subcutaneous emphysema)
  - Tension pneumothorax with pneumomediastinum can cause haemodynamic compromise
- Aspiration risk is high at extubation too

## Flashcards
- Q: What induction is used for achalasia and reflux surgery?
  - A: RSI with cricoid pressure, as for a full stomach.
- Q: In POEM, where is the mucosal bleb raised and how far does the myotomy go?
  - A: Bleb 10–12 cm above the GEJ; myotomy to 2 cm below the GEJ.
- Q: What does a sudden rise in ETCO2 or peak airway pressure during POEM suggest?
  - A: Capnothorax.
- Q: Where is the ETT fixed for POEM?
  - A: Right side of the mouth, to let the scope pass.
