# Evacuation of Uterus (ERPOC)

Relax the uterus for exploration, then restore tone to limit bleeding; RSI from 16 weeks.

## Procedures
- Incomplete miscarriage (6-12 weeks)
- Suction termination of pregnancy (TOP) (up to 12 weeks)
- Surgical termination (STOP) via dilatation and curettage (D&C): beyond 12 weeks
- Medical termination (MTOP): beyond 16 weeks, prostaglandin with or without ERPOC
- Retained placenta (may be at term)

## Goals
- First: uterine relaxation to allow manual exploration
  - IV nitroglycerin 100mcg
  - High-concentration volatile
- Then: improve uterine tone to cut blood loss after exploration
  - Avoid high-concentration volatile; deepen with propofol and opioid instead
  - Oxytocin 5IU IV

## Preoperative assessment
- Quantify blood loss so far (vital signs and Hb); may need some resuscitation first
- IV access

## Intraoperative management
- Supine
- LMA if under 16 weeks
- ETT with RSI if over 16 weeks
  - Treat as full stomach from 16 weeks gestation until 48 h post-partum
  - Earlier if symptomatic reflux (GERD)
  - Exact cut-off is uncertain: Chestnut says first trimester; BJA Education (CEACCP) and UpToDate say 18 weeks
  - Short case: suxamethonium if paralysis is needed; fentanyl alone is enough for analgesia
- If RPOC after delivery with an epidural in place, the epidural can be topped up
- Oxytocin; consider tranexamic acid (TXA)

## Postoperative management
- Oral analgesia
- Dual antiemetics

## Flashcards
- Q: What IV dose of nitroglycerin relaxes the uterus for manual exploration?
  - A: 100mcg
- Q: From and until when is a pregnant patient treated as a full stomach?
  - A: From 16 weeks gestation to 48 h post-partum (earlier if symptomatic reflux)
- Q: Airway choice for ERPOC under and over 16 weeks?
  - A: LMA under 16 weeks; ETT with RSI over 16 weeks
- Q: Once exploration is done, how is uterine tone restored?
  - A: Avoid high-concentration volatile (use propofol and opioid) and give oxytocin 5IU IV
