# Obstetric Anaesthesia

Two patients: anaesthesia must protect both the mother and the fetus.

## Maternal considerations
- Difficult airway: difficult intubation, aspiration, desaturation
- Aortocaval compression

## Fetal considerations
- The fetus is the second patient
- Teratogenicity: drugs, radiation
- Maintain uteroplacental blood flow; avoid fetal acidosis and hypoxaemia
  - Fetal heart rate monitoring
- Preterm labour

## General approach
- Regional anaesthesia preferred
- Multidisciplinary approach: involve obstetricians and neonatologists
- Timing of non-urgent surgery: 6 weeks postpartum, or in the 2nd trimester

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the main maternal anaesthetic concerns in pregnancy?
  - A: Difficult airway (difficult intubation, aspiration, desaturation) and aortocaval compression.
- Q: What are the main fetal concerns during surgery in pregnancy?
  - A: Teratogenicity, maintaining uteroplacental blood flow (avoid fetal acidosis and hypoxaemia) and preterm labour.
- Q: When is non-urgent surgery best timed in relation to pregnancy?
  - A: 6 weeks postpartum, or in the 2nd trimester.
- Q: Which anaesthetic technique is preferred in pregnancy?
  - A: Regional anaesthesia.
