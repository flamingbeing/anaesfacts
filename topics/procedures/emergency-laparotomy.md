# Emergency Laparotomy

Anaesthesia for urgent abdominal surgery in an acute abdomen: resuscitate, rapid sequence induction, invasive monitoring, plan for ICU.

## Quick points

- Urgent case
- Aspiration risk from the underlying problem; nasogastric tube
- Septic or dehydrated: resuscitate before induction
- Early albumin to prevent bowel oedema
- PCA
- Temporary abdominal closure possible

## Key considerations

- Major abdominal surgery
  - Fluid shifts, insensible and third-space losses, electrolyte disturbance
  - Hypothermia
  - Blood loss
- Emergency: little time to optimise cardiac status
- Underlying condition
  - Perforated viscus: septic shock
  - Intestinal obstruction: aspiration risk, fluid shifts, electrolytes
  - Colon cancer: obstruction, chronic bleeding and anaemia, cachexia, metastases (lung)

## Preoperative management

- Judge urgency and whether there is time to resuscitate
  - Ongoing haemorrhage: immediate surgery
  - Septic shock: surgery within 3 hours (<3hrs)
  - Severe sepsis: surgery within 6 hours
  - May need ICU admission before surgery to optimise
- Assess degree of organ dysfunction (see sepsis)
- Investigations: FBC, U+E, Mg2+, coagulation, ECG, CXR, group and crossmatch, lactate
- IV resuscitation, with vasopressors and inotropes if needed; prompt IV antibiotics per sepsis guidelines
  - IV fluids can start in reception before induction
- Nasogastric tube for bowel obstruction
- Risk assessment
- Discuss likely morbidity and mortality with the patient and family

## Intraoperative management

- Monitoring and access
  - Arterial line before induction if possible, unless very well; consider CVP
  - Large-bore IV access
  - Temperature probe, forced-air warmer; consider a fluid warmer (major case, transfusion likely)
- Induction
  - Aspirate the nasogastric tube
  - Vasopressors ready
  - Rapid sequence induction, cardiostable induction, e.g. midazolam
- Maintenance
  - Remifentanil for titratability; or lidocaine infusion
  - Monitor blood loss and trend haemoglobin
  - Replace fluid deficit guided by urine output and lactate; goal-directed fluid therapy
  - Correct electrolytes
  - Keep normothermic
  - Watch for septic showers and regurgitation of bowel contents
  - Bowel surgery: avoid bowel oedema; early colloids

## Postoperative management

- Extubation decision
  - Surgical: temporary closure means ICU, stay intubated
  - Patient: haemodynamics, lactate or acidosis, respiratory problems
- Analgesia
  - Surgeons may refuse an On-Q pump (pre-peritoneal catheter) if the field is contaminated
  - PCA
  - Nerve blocks
- ICU care
  - Treat sepsis, continue resuscitation
  - Trend lactate
  - Abdominal compartment syndrome if the abdomen is closed
    - Risk with peritonitis and tissue swelling when the wound is closed
    - Signs: peak inspiratory pressure rises on closure, hypotension; tell the surgeons
    - The wound may need leaving open with delayed closure
  - Consider early TPN

## Flashcards

- Q: Target timing for surgery in septic shock versus severe sepsis?
  - A: Septic shock within 3 hours; severe sepsis within 6 hours
- Q: Timing of surgery for ongoing haemorrhage?
  - A: Immediate
- Q: Signs of abdominal compartment syndrome on closing the abdomen?
  - A: Rising peak inspiratory pressure and hypotension
- Q: When should the patient stay intubated for ICU?
  - A: Temporary abdominal closure, or unstable haemodynamics, high lactate or acidosis, respiratory problems
