# Femoral Endarterectomy

Open removal of plaque from the common femoral artery (CFA) and its bifurcation.

## Overview
- Gold standard for localised CFA disease
- Stenting is often avoided here: the hip moves a lot, so stents risk fracturing

## Preoperative assessment
- Vasculopath: assess as for any patient with vascular disease

## Intraoperative management
- Anaesthetic technique options
  - GA: may need an ETT for longer procedures
  - Neuraxial anaesthesia: excellent if not contraindicated
  - Nerve blocks
    - Ilioinguinal plus genitofemoral nerve block: covers the groin skin incision
    - Deep plexus blocks (e.g. lumbar plexus)
  - Local anaesthesia with monitored anaesthesia care (MAC) for high-risk patients
- Heparinisation
- Clamping, then reperfusion

## Complications
- Groin haematoma or pseudoaneurysm: the most common specific complication
  - May lead to wound dehiscence or infection

## Flashcards
- Q: Why is open endarterectomy preferred over stenting for common femoral artery disease?
  - A: High hip mobility puts stents at risk of fracture.
- Q: Which blocks cover the groin skin incision for femoral endarterectomy?
  - A: Ilioinguinal plus genitofemoral nerve block.
- Q: What is the most common specific complication after femoral endarterectomy?
  - A: Groin haematoma or pseudoaneurysm.
