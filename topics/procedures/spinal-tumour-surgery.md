# Spinal Tumour Surgery

Often difficult spinal surgery (e.g. vertebrectomy) with risk of major blood loss and neurological damage.

## Overview
- Example: vertebrectomy
- Tumours may be primary (1°) or secondary (2°), from any level of the spine

## Key features
- Duration: 2–6+ hr
- Pain: severe to very severe (+++/++++)
- Blood loss: potentially massive; crossmatch 6 units and have clotting factors available
- Position: supine, prone or lateral tilt
- Often difficult surgery with potential for major blood loss and neurological injury

## Flashcards
- Q: How much blood should be crossmatched for spinal tumour surgery?
  - A: 6 units, with clotting factors available
- Q: How long does spinal tumour surgery usually take?
  - A: 2–6+ hours
- Q: What are the two main risks of spinal tumour surgery?
  - A: Major (potentially massive) blood loss and neurological damage
