# Brain tumour surgery and stereotactic biopsy

Control seizures and ICP, document deficits, plan steroid cover, and aim for smooth emergence with early neurological assessment.

## Approaches
- Awake craniotomy: may suit supratentorial tumours near eloquent cortex
- Transsphenoidal surgery: especially pituitary tumours
- Stereotactic surgery
  - Minimally invasive; may be done under LA with sedation
  - The stereotactic frame blocks easy access to the airway
- Tumour-related vasogenic oedema can raise ICP but responds to steroids

## Preoperative assessment
- Seizure control
- Document existing neurological deficits
- Quantify steroid use (for stress dosing)
- Tumour assessment
  - Mass effect and degree of raised ICP
  - Bleeding risk: very vascular tumours, or tumours near venous sinuses

## Intraoperative management
- Operations for metastases are usually quicker and easier
- Dexamethasone 8mg with esomeprazole (Nexium)
- Levetiracetam (Keppra) for seizure control
- Surgeons may ask for mannitol
- Intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring may be used

## Stereotactic brain biopsy
- Preoperative: GCS, seizures, imaging; group and crossmatch
- Position: depends on lesion site; head fixed in a clamp
- Monitoring: no arterial line needed (similar to a burr hole)
- Anaesthesia: GA with ETT
- Maintenance: sevoflurane is fine; paralyse
- Antibiotics: cefazolin
- Fluids: isotonic
- Special: neuroprotection, BP and ICP control, consider levetiracetam
- Access: through a burr hole
- Analgesia: fentanyl; antiemetic: dexamethasone
- Emergence: smooth, awake, early neurological assessment
- Postoperative: neurosurgical high dependency

## Flashcards
- Q: Dexamethasone dose given intraoperatively for brain tumour surgery?
  - A: 8mg, with a proton pump inhibitor
- Q: Does stereotactic brain biopsy need an arterial line?
  - A: No; it is similar to a burr hole
- Q: Airway concern with stereotactic surgery under sedation?
  - A: The stereotactic frame blocks easy airway access
