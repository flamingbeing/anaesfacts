# Arteriovenous Fistula and Graft Creation

Dialysis access in vascular, renal patients: brachial plexus block preferred (sympathectomy helps flow and patency), LA for simple fistulas.

## Procedures
- Radiocephalic (RC) AVF: snuffbox or wrist; most distal and simplest
- Brachiocephalic (BC) AVF: elbow; cephalic vein to brachial artery
- Brachiobasilic (BB) AVF: elbow; vein transposed to a superficial position, so more invasive and two-staged
- AV graft (AVG): synthetic conduit between artery and vein when native veins are inadequate

## Preoperative assessment
- Vasculopaths
- End-stage renal failure
- Confirm the planned site, as it decides the block

## Intraoperative management
- LA alone possible for RC and BC AVF
- Surgeons prefer brachial plexus block
  - Sympathectomy dilates vessels, raising diameter and flow; easier surgery and better early patency and maturation than LA or GA
  - Supraclavicular or axillary, with or without intercostobrachial nerve block
- BB AVF may need GA
- Renal failure: avoid excess fluid

## Flashcards
- Q: Why do surgeons prefer a brachial plexus block for AVF creation?
  - A: Sympathectomy dilates vessels, improving flow, ease of surgery and early patency
- Q: Which AVF types can be done under LA alone?
  - A: Radiocephalic and brachiocephalic
- Q: Which AVF may need GA, and why?
  - A: Brachiobasilic: vein transposition makes it more invasive
