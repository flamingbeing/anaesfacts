# Prostate Brachytherapy

A radioactive source is placed beside the tumour through an applicator; often for patients unfit for surgery, as one or several sessions.

## Procedure
- Duration 1.5–3hr, sometimes longer
- Blood loss usually minimal
- Postoperative pain can be a problem

## Anaesthetic technique
- Light GA
- Sedation, though immobility may be unreliable
- Spinal or epidural; the procedure may outlast a single spinal, so catheters may be needed

## Postoperative care
- Residual radioactivity may mean recovery in an isolated area

## Flashcards
- Q: Usual duration of prostate brachytherapy?
  - A: 1.5–3 hours, sometimes longer
- Q: Drawback of sedation alone?
  - A: Immobility may be unreliable
- Q: Why might a catheter neuraxial technique be chosen over single-shot spinal?
  - A: The procedure may outlast the spinal block
