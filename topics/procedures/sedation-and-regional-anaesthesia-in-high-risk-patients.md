# Sedation and Regional Anaesthesia in High-Risk Patients

Sedation with regional or local anaesthesia can spare high-risk patients a GA, but plan and agree a rescue GA in advance.

## Sedation as an alternative to GA
- Better haemodynamics
  - Sedation and peripheral nerve blocks may cause less haemodynamic instability
  - Spinal can still cause marked, uncontrolled instability
  - A titrated epidural may lessen this
- Operating conditions are less favourable

## Planning for MAC (RA or LA) in high-risk patients
- Talk directly with the surgeon
  - Make clear the patient is high risk
  - Confirm the benefits of surgery outweigh the risks
  - Discuss alternatives: not operating, less invasive options, the consequences of GA
  - Discuss rescue GA (e.g. uncooperative patient or surgical need)
  - Suggest an experienced or senior surgeon
  - Have the discussion early enough to avoid last-minute cancellation
- Counsel the patient and family before surgery
  - Explain the high-risk status
  - Check they understand the alternatives (not operating, GA)
  - Explain the planned RA or LA, rescue GA and when it would be needed
  - Document whether the patient agrees to GA if it becomes necessary
- Optimise the patient for successful MAC
  - Look for any change since the last review
- Consider the need for an anaesthetic assistant and arrange early

## Flashcards
- Q: Which techniques may give the most stable haemodynamics in a high-risk patient?
  - A: Sedation with peripheral nerve blocks (or a titrated epidural rather than a spinal)
- Q: What must be agreed with the family before MAC in a high-risk patient?
  - A: Whether to proceed to rescue GA if it becomes needed, documented
- Q: What should be discussed with the surgeon beforehand?
  - A: High-risk status, risk-benefit, alternatives, rescue GA, and a senior surgeon
