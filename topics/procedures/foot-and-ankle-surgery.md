# Foot and Ankle Surgery

Well suited to regional techniques (popliteal plus saphenous, ankle blocks, spinal), but watch for masked compartment syndrome and tourniquet pain.

## Types of procedure
- Tendon transfers: ≤1hr
- Joint arthrodesis: 1-2hrs
- ORIF: may be an emergency; variable, 1.5–2hr
- Prosthetic joint replacement: 2+ hrs (longer)

## Anaesthetic technique
- Regional anaesthesia alone or combined with GA
  - Beware of masking compartment syndrome; discuss with the surgeon
- Nerve blocks are popular; need both:
  - Sciatic (or popliteal) block
  - Femoral (or saphenous) block
- Spinal anaesthesia is also an option

## General considerations
- Position: supine, prone, or occasionally lateral
- Duration: usually about 2hrs
- Blood loss: minimal, as a tourniquet is often used
- Pain: variable; can be very painful
- Tourniquets are common; plan for tourniquet pain

## Ankle fracture fixation
- After trauma, ORIF may be urgent if distal circulation is compromised
- Full stomach: aspiration risk; RSI is the best option in trauma
- Make sure other significant injuries have been dealt with
- Before a block for ORIF, check there is no concern about postoperative compartment syndrome, which a block would mask
  - General rule: avoid nerve blocks in trauma; local infiltration is useful
- Spinal anaesthesia is a good alternative for ORIF
  - Intrathecal opioid (e.g. diamorphine 0.25–0.5mg) prolongs analgesia

## Ankle tendon transfer or repair
- Duration: shorter, ~1 hour
- Position: supine
  - Ruptured Achilles tendon repair: prone
- Technique
  - GA with LMA plus local infiltration by the surgeon
  - Spinal if supine
  - IPPV if prone

## Foot surgery
- Osteotomies are painful afterwards
- Surgery usually takes 30min to 1hr
- Many are day cases needing early mobilisation and discharge with good analgesia

### Regional anaesthesia
- Blocks help postoperative analgesia (especially osteotomies and nail bed excision) and early mobilisation
- Slow onset: if used without GA, perform them a full 40min before surgery
  - Less experienced operators: use them mainly for postoperative analgesia, with LMA and GA
- Blocks by site:
  - Toe surgery: ring or web-space block, or ankle block
  - Forefoot: ankle block
  - Midfoot: sciatic (or popliteal) block
- Usually done for postoperative analgesia, combined with GA
- Spinal anaesthesia is an alternative in all cases
- No adrenaline in ring or web-space blocks; avoid it in ankle blocks if peripheral circulation is poor

### Tourniquet
- Breakthrough tourniquet pain is common, especially if surgery lasts longer than 45min
- Place the tourniquet as distally as possible to reduce this

## Flashcards
- Q: Which two nerve territories must be blocked for most foot and ankle surgery?
  - A: Sciatic (or popliteal) and femoral (or saphenous)
- Q: How long before surgery should foot blocks be done if used without GA?
  - A: A full 40min
- Q: Which blocks must never contain adrenaline?
  - A: Ring and web-space blocks
- Q: Intrathecal diamorphine dose to prolong analgesia for ankle ORIF?
  - A: 0.25–0.5mg
- Q: After what duration does tourniquet pain become a common problem?
  - A: Surgery longer than 45min
