# Cardiac Anaesthesia

Practical step-by-step conduct of adult cardiac surgery on cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), from premed to ICU handover.

## Preoperative assessment

- CABG: number of vessels; any radial artery harvest (check the consent)
- Valve replacement: which valve, symptoms
- Effort tolerance; fluid overload state
- Investigations
  - 2D echo: EF, PASP, regional wall motion abnormalities, mitral E/A ratio, LVIDd/LVIDs, other valves
  - Carotid ultrasound: any significant stenosis

## Preoperative instructions

- Nil by mouth from 12 midnight; sips of water until 6am (or 9am if second case)
- Valid group and crossmatch; standby 4 units packed cells, 1 unit cryosupernatant plasma (CSP), 1 L FFP
- Postoperative cardiothoracic ICU (CTICU) bed

## Patient preparation

- IV cannula on hand or forearm (16 or 18G)
  - Sedation with midazolam, with or without fentanyl 30 mcg, before lines
  - Peripheral line (non-dominant hand) with a warmed line and a 3-way tap fitted first; cell-saver blood is returned through it
- Arterial line in the left radial artery
  - Use the right radial if the left radial is to be harvested as a conduit
- Right internal jugular CVC
  - Superficial cervical plexus block: 7 ml lignocaine 1% at the midpoint of the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid
  - Go a little higher for mitral valve surgery with bicaval cannulation: aim the tip at the 2nd intercostal space instead of the usual 3rd
  - Fix at 13 cm (male) or 12 cm (female), adjusted for height and neck length
- Keep the central line clean: cover all ports, syringe on the last free port
- Anaesthetic assistant attaches 5-lead ECG, 3-lead TEE ECG, and defibrillator pads (for minimally invasive surgery)
- Scrub side applies 2 diathermy pads and a sacral foam dressing

## Theatre preparation

- Check the machine and a small mask

### Resuscitation drugs

- Adrenaline 10 mcg/ml and 100 mcg/ml
- Phenylephrine 100 mcg/ml in a 100 ml saline bag (or 2 x 50 ml bags)
  - Label and draw up 3-4 x 10 ml syringes on standby
- Ephedrine 5 mg/ml in 6 ml
- Atropine 0.6 mg in 1 ml
- Calcium chloride in a 10 ml syringe
- Heparin: 300 u/kg + 2000 IU, up to 400 u/kg (in case of an emergency crash onto bypass)

### Induction drug tray

- Fentanyl 500 mcg in a 20 ml syringe (a further 500 mcg for high-dose opioid technique)
  - Alternative: alfentanil 1 mg diluted to 10 ml
  - Sicker patients: fentanyl 500 + 500 mcg with midazolam 5 mg
  - Others: alfentanil 1 mg in 10 ml plus fentanyl 500 mcg and midazolam 5 mg
- Propofol in a 50 ml plus a 20 ml syringe (20 ml is often enough)
- Rocuronium 50 mg (or 100 mg in 10 ml)
  - Atracurium neat infusion: 250 mg (25 ml); 500 mg (50 ml) if over 70 kg
- Cefazolin 2 g
- Morphine 10 mg; paracetamol 1 g and tramadol 100 mg as alternatives or additions
- Midazolam 5 mg
- Tranexamic acid (TXA) 1 g or 30 mg/kg (some hand it to the perfusionist to give into the pump)

### Infusions to prepare and check

- Adrenaline
- Milrinone, sodium nitroprusside (SNP)
- Dopamine or dobutamine (practice varies)
- Noradrenaline: formerly 4 mg in 100 ml, now 8 mg in 100 ml
- GTN neat (50 mg in 50 ml)
- Insulin (some start from the beginning)
- Propofol TCI (effect-site or plasma mode; plasma mode with a 1 min induction)
- Remifentanil TCI (e.g. for left main disease; plasma mode with a 1 min induction)
- Atracurium infusion

## Patient on table

- BIS on (some place it after induction)
- 2 L nasal prongs oxygen
- IV: connect to the warmed line; run about 500 ml fluid (saline or Hartmann's)
- Arterial line: baseline ACT and ABG
- CVC
  - Transducer on the brown (14G) lumen
  - Drugs on the 18G lumens: vasopressors/inotropes ("uppers") and vasodilators ("downers") on separate lumens; spare taps can be extended later; turn on and flush to check every pump works
  - Saline drip for patency; label everything
  - Start drug infusions at 0.1 ml/h (usually corresponds to 0.01 mcg/kg/h)
  - Wrap lines in a hand towel
- Difficult IV access: insert a quad-lumen CVC

## Induction

- Preoxygenate
- Midazolam 3-5 mg + fentanyl 200 mcg + propofol 20-50 mg
  - Consider esmolol or alfentanil 2-3 ml boluses to control BP
- Secure the airway and ventilate
- Nasopharyngeal temperature probe: insert, secure, transduce (check the label on screen)
- Consider passing an orogastric tube in and out to decompress the stomach before TEE
- Attach TEE leads to the machine
- Give cefazolin and TXA (30-40 mg/kg)
- Atracurium (10 mg/ml) infusion at 2.5-3 ml/h, or 20 mg boluses hourly
- Tuck arms in; confirm IV still runs and arterial line still aspirates
- Drape bar: over the eyes, level, clear of the nose
- Draping: hold the first drape, then tape the final drape; fold the corner towards the assistant side if radial harvest is planned (stops flapping and contamination)
- Connect the defibrillator cable passed out between the adhered part of the drape
- High-dose opioid technique: give the rest of the fentanyl up to 10 mcg/kg
- Track glucose (hypocount) on ABG
  - Consider insulin if above 10 mmol/L: glucose X mmol/L gives X u/h insulin (only if on adrenaline); without adrenaline run at 1/3 u/h
  - On transfer to ICU reduce insulin to 0.5X u/h based on the last glucose
- Document TEE findings
- Coil infusion lines together and hook to the side of the arm board

## Before bypass

- Fentanyl 250 mcg just before sternotomy
- "Lungs down": switch to bag and/or disconnect the expiratory limb to deflate the lungs during sternotomy, so the pleura is less likely to be opened (pneumothorax)
- "Lungs up": back on the ventilator; a chest drain goes in if the left pleura is breached
- LIMA dissection: tidal volume down to 6 ml/kg with a high rate to keep minute ventilation; PEEP off (usually not needed with an open chest); surgeon asks for table up and tilted away
- Endoscopic radial artery harvest: watch for CO2 absorption and rising EtCO2 (little tissue to absorb it, so most enters the circulation); less of an issue with saphenous vein harvest
- Heparin
  - Given via the central line when the surgeon asks (usually just before clamping and dividing the IMA, so the graft blood is anticoagulated); flush with 20 ml saline
  - Saphenous vein should already be harvested, as heparin causes heavy venous oozing; radial harvest is not a problem
  - Surgeons flush other conduits with GTN and verapamil, later with heparinised blood once on bypass
  - ACT 3-5 min after heparin from the CVC; target ACT above 400 s
  - Alternative: sample from the arterial line, with a 10 ml syringe kept on an extra 3-way tap
- Cannulation
  - Arterial cannula into the ascending aorta, then venous cannula into the right atrial appendage
  - Keep SBP 80-90 for aortic cannulation (consider a small GTN bolus of 0.3-0.5 ml) to avoid dislodging plaque
  - TEE (bicaval view, ascending aorta short and long axis) to confirm cannula position
  - BP can be allowed higher once the aortic cannula is in

## Bypass phase

- Go on bypass once ACT is above 400 s
- Cooling: oesophageal temperature falls first (cardioplegia and cold saline around the heart sit next to the oesophagus); peripheral temperature follows
- Ventilator off; oxygen and sevoflurane off; leave air at 0.5 L/min
- All monitor alarms off; QRS volume to 0
- Turn the BIS to face the perfusionist
  - Extra midazolam 0.5-1 mg, atracurium 20 mg (if no infusion) and morphine (at start of bypass or during rewarming)
  - Propofol infusion at 4-5 mg/kg/h if used
- Cardioplegia cannula, aortic cross-clamp, then cardioplegia by the perfusionist
  - BP falls; keep MAP 60-80
- Check the pacing box and batteries
- Glucose below 10; potassium normal
- Perfusionist keeps ACT above 480 s as a buffer
- ABG every 30 min on pump; record the first and last on-pump gases
  - Venous gas for ScvO2: aim 75-80
  - Too low: tissue perfusion inadequate
  - Too high: tissues not extracting (usually hypothermia)
  - pCO2 guides oxygenator gas (sweep) flow; BP guides pump flow
- MAP not controlled with GTN: consider phentolamine (dilute 10 mg/ml to 1 mg/ml, give 1 ml at a time)
- Consider SNP from 0.1 mcg/kg/min upwards

## Rewarming

- Increase GTN to 5 ml/h for vasodilation (more even warming of the periphery)
- Midazolam 0.5-1 mg
- When peripheral-core temperature gap is below 0.5 °C, stop GTN and increase noradrenaline and adrenaline (prevents excess vasodilation and hypotension)
- Redose relaxant when temperature is above 35 °C or EMG above 30, whichever comes first (20 mg rocuronium or atracurium)
- At 36 °C, 4 ml blood from the perfusionist for TEG platelet mapping
- Order platelets at this point
- Platelets via a new giving set, not warmed (not through the warmed line; switch it off if no other line is free)

## Removal of aortic cross-clamp

- Arrhythmias are common as the clamp comes off
  - Internal defibrillation 6-10 J
  - Check electrolytes; top up magnesium and potassium if needed
  - Lignocaine 100 mg
  - Amiodarone 150 mg, consider infusion in 30 ml water
- Attach pacing wires (blue atrial, white ventricular); pace at 88 bpm, 10 mA
- Surgeons do the proximal anastomoses with a side-biting clamp on the aorta, then check graft flow (some start low-dose adrenaline here)

## Preparing to come off bypass

1. Temperature
2. No arrhythmia; pacing well
3. Electrolytes corrected
4. Heart well filled
5. Alarms back on
6. Ventilate

## Coming off bypass

- Wean slowly, 25-50% at a time; TEE to check contractility
- Heparin reversal: protamine 1 mg per 100 IU heparin (initial dose)
  - Once off pump and when asked, give slowly in 2 ml aliquots via a peripheral line (first pass through the liver; avoids idiosyncratic pulmonary hypertension); flush with 20 ml saline
  - Option: give 80% of the intended dose or 20 mg/kg, check ACT, then consider a top-up based on how far ACT has fallen (avoids protamine overdose)
  - Check ACT 3-10 min after
- Redose drugs
  - TXA 500 mg or 10 mg/kg after protamine (not all redose)
  - Cefazolin 1 g
  - Atracurium if not given earlier
  - Morphine
  - Replace calcium 20 min after coming off bypass (procoagulant)
- Consider platelet panel or ROTEM; FFP or CSP
- Resuscitate: fluids (consider 1 unit gelatin or albumin), return cell-saver blood via the peripheral line (it contains heparin, so oozing may increase), increase inotropes
- Aortic decannulation: keep SBP in the 90s (venous cannula usually comes out first)

## End of surgery

- Confirm with the perfusionist: urine pre and on bypass, cross-clamp time, bypass time
- Start propofol infusion (40 ml in a 50 ml syringe) at 10 ml/h about 30 min before the end
- Titrate sevoflurane down to BP and BIS until off
- Atracurium bolus if no infusion
- Sign controlled drug records only after leaving theatre

### Stimulating points (fentanyl)

- Induction: 200 mcg
- Skin incision and sternotomy: 300 mcg
- Graft harvesting: 50 mcg boluses
- Diathermy of the IMA near the end: give the rest

### Points of hypotension

- Cannulation and decannulation
- Cardioplegia (including top-ups)
- Rewarming
- Coming off bypass

### Fluid management

- Aim for only 500 ml for the case; the pump prime gives a lot of volume
- Gelatin for the second unit (not everyone)
- Consider adrenaline if EF is poor
- Frusemide if needed (e.g. poor urine output)

## Transfer to ICU

Left side:

- Disconnect the rectal temperature wire from the transducer
- Disconnect and remove the nasopharyngeal temperature probe
- Unplug and coil the CVC cable; disconnect CVC tubing from the transducer and cap it
- Pass the arterial cable to the ICU monitor, plug in, zero, transduce
- Move the arterial/CVC pole to the centre pole hole on the far side of the bed
- Peel off TEE leads; disconnect the TEE machine
- Assistant attaches new ECG leads for the ICU monitor and removes the old ones
- Remove eye tape

Right side:

- Disconnect infusions no longer needed (atracurium, insulin)
- Keep other infusions running at 0.1 ml/h
- Move the half pole to the near-side pole hole; check the bag-valve-mask and oxygen cylinder work
- Remove diathermy and defibrillator pads as the patient is turned
- Connect oxygen; confirm bilateral chest rise with bagging
- Transducers at the correct height

## Handover

- Demographics: name, age, drug allergies
- Surgery done
- Past history (cardiovascular and non-cardiac)
- Invasive lines
- Pre-bypass TEE findings
- Cross-clamp and bypass times
- Events: difficult intubation or venous access, arrhythmia, difficulty weaning from bypass, bleeding
- Heparin and protamine given; last ACT
- Other infusions; analgesia
- Fluids in and out (urine, blood loss)
- Blood products (including pump blood)
- Post-bypass TEE
- Last-minute drugs

## Off-pump cardiac surgery

- Indications: aorta unsuitable for clamping (heavy plaque, porcelain aorta); patient cannot tolerate the inflammatory response (SIRS) of CPB
- Fluids: 15-20 ml/kg
- BP
  - Raise it during distal anastomoses to perfuse coronaries via collaterals, especially with the proximal coronary clamped
  - Lower it during proximal anastomoses (avoids aortic dissection with the side clamp)
  - Some do distal-proximal-distal-proximal to shorten ischaemia per vessel, which means repeatedly raising and lowering BP
- Temperature: active warming (fluid warmer, active humidifier)
- Drugs: heparin 150-200 u/kg; TXA not needed; less oozing (no CPB inflammatory response, less heparin)
- Aim HR 60 to reduce movement in the field; noradrenaline or phenylephrine to raise BP
- Stabilisers: "octopus" (2-limbed suction device holding a section of myocardium still); "starfish" (5-limbed suction device lifting the apex for work on the circumflex territory on the posterior surface)

## Ten rules for aortic dissection

1. Think of it: thunderclap or migrating chest pain, marfanoid habitus
2. Examine: pulsatile mass, pulse pressure, BP control
3. Good imaging: CT angiography or TEE
4. Type A is a surgical emergency: mortality rises by about 1% per hour of delay from diagnosis
5. Type B is complicated (malperfusion, refractory hypertension or pain) or uncomplicated
6. Good preoperative medical therapy: control HR and BP; beta-block before vasodilators to avoid reflex tachycardia; aim HR 60 and SBP below 120 mmHg
7. Serial imaging for residual dissection needing medical therapy
8. Good postoperative medical therapy (beta-blockers, ARBs)
9. Related conditions (intramural haematoma, penetrating aortic ulcer): treat most as dissection
10. Very sick patients: postoperative mortality 10-30% with high morbidity; needs multidisciplinary teamwork

## Deep hypothermic circulatory arrest (DHCA): 4 indications

1. Aortic arch surgery (e.g. aortic dissection)
2. Need for a bloodless field
   - Complex congenital heart surgery
   - Tumours invading the IVC
   - Pulmonary thromboembolectomy
   - Large cerebral aneurysms; cerebral AVM repair
3. Aorta cannot be cross-clamped (porcelain aorta, heavy plaque)
4. Heart damaged at sternotomy (redo sternotomy with the RV stuck to the sternum)

## Cardiopulmonary bypass circuit

4 functions:

1. Systemic circulation (heart)
2. Gas exchange (lungs)
3. Still, bloodless field
4. Temperature control (heating and cooling)

Blood path from patient to machine:

1. Venous return cannula; cardiotomy sucker (white); vents: LV vent via the right superior pulmonary vein (green), aortic root vent via the proximal ascending aorta (yellow), drop vent into any chamber
2. Reservoir
3. Roller pumps (arterial, sucker, vent)
4. Temperature control and monitoring
5. Gas exchange (oxygenator)
6. Arterial filter
7. Bubble detector

Then it branches:

- 8A. Main arterial line to the arterial cannula
- 8B. Cardioplegia roller pump (cardioplegia added), dual heat exchanger, bubble trap, cardioplegia cannula

Pump prime: 1.5-2 L for the circuit, Plasma-Lyte or albumin 5%

## Advantages of TEE over TTE in cardiac surgery

Preoperative:

1. Feasibility of mitral valve repair
2. Paravalvular leak
3. Aortic dissection: sensitivity and specificity close to 100%
4. Intracardiac thrombus
5. Intracardiac mass (e.g. atrial myxoma)
6. Infective endocarditis or abscess

Intraoperative:

1. Aorta: guiding IABP or femoral cannula insertion; ascending aortic plaque before cannulation
- Heart: LVEF, regional wall motion, RV function, volume status
- De-airing
- Valve assessment after repair or replacement

## Heparin resistance

- Relative deficiency of antithrombin III (which heparin binds to)
- Treatment: FFP (contains antithrombin III)

## Flashcards

- Q: Initial protamine dose to reverse heparin after bypass?
  - A: 1 mg per 100 IU heparin, given slowly in 2 ml aliquots via a peripheral line
- Q: Heparin dose and target ACT before going on bypass?
  - A: 300 u/kg + 2000 IU (up to 400 u/kg); ACT above 400 s (perfusionist keeps it above 480 s)
- Q: Target ScvO2 on bypass and what a high value suggests?
  - A: 75-80; too high suggests tissues not extracting, usually from hypothermia
- Q: BP target for aortic cannulation?
  - A: SBP 80-90 (small GTN bolus if needed)
- Q: Heparin dose for off-pump CABG?
  - A: 150-200 u/kg, with no TXA needed
