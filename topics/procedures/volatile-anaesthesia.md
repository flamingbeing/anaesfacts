# Volatile-Based Anaesthesia

Maintenance of GA with a volatile agent, usually the default; low fresh gas flows save agent and warm and humidify inspired gas.

## Overview

- Usually the default technique
- Agent choice
  - Sevoflurane: avoid in prolonged surgery and in obese patients
  - Desflurane: avoid in asthma or where tachycardia is unwanted

## Advantages

- End-tidal monitoring may lower the risk of awareness
- Bronchodilation (useful in asthma)
- Cardioprotection through ischaemic preconditioning (useful in cardiac surgery)
  - AHA 2014: benefit shown in cardiac surgery, but no reduction in myocardial ischaemia or MI shown in non-cardiac surgery
- No IV access needed (e.g. paediatric inhalational induction)

## Disadvantages

- Inhibits hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction
- Uncouples cerebral metabolic rate and cerebral blood flow

## Administration

- Volatile agent delivered by vaporiser

## Low-flow anaesthesia

### Definition

- No single agreed definition; implies a carrier gas flow below what a non-absorber breathing system would allow
- Fresh gas flow below minute ventilation
- Or rebreathing fraction above 50%

### Flow classification (Baxter 1997)

- Metabolic flow: 250 mL/min
- Minimal flow: 250-500 mL/min
- Low flow: 500-1000 mL/min
- Medium flow: 1-2 L/min
- High flow: 2-4 L/min
- Very high flow: above 4 L/min

### Benefits

- Better humidity of inspired gas
- Inspired gas temperature rises by up to 6 degrees C
- Less use of gas and volatile agent
  - Lower cost
  - Less environmental pollution from scavenged gas

## Flashcards

  - A: 500-1000 mL/min

  - A: 250-500 mL/min

  - A: More than 50%
