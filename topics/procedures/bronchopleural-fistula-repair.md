# Bronchopleural Fistula Repair

An abnormal connection between the bronchial tree and the pleural space: avoid positive-pressure ventilation and keep spontaneous breathing until the fistula is isolated, and protect the healthy lung.

## Key points
- Plan depends on where the hole is
- Avoid positive-pressure ventilation until the fistula is isolated; place the cuff under bronchoscopic guidance
- Keep spontaneous breathing and avoid paralysis until a DLT or tube is beyond the hole

## Causes
- After pneumonectomy or lobectomy (most common)
- Necrotising pneumonia, lung abscess, empyema

## Clinical features
- Productive cough, often purulent; haemoptysis, fever, dyspnoea
- Subcutaneous emphysema

## Surgical options
- Small fistula: bronchoscopic glue or sutures
- Larger fistula: surgical repair (e.g. muscle flap) by lateral thoracotomy, usually through the old incision
- Rarely tracheal resection and reconstruction

## Preoperative assessment
- CT thorax: size and site of the leak
- Flexible bronchoscopy
- Chest drain to decompress the pleura

## Airway management
- Aims
  - Control ventilation: prevent large leaks through the fistula and tension pneumothorax
  - Protect the good lung from contamination: avoid coughing before the fistula is isolated
- Options
  - Awake fibreoptic intubation, then bronchoscope-guided one-lung ventilation
    - A DLT can be hard because the upper airway is difficult to topicalise enough; consider remifentanil to prevent coughing
    - Endobronchial intubation with a 6mm ETT
    - Bronchial blocker: usually a temporary measure; surgery may not be possible with it
    - Jet ventilation catheter in the normal bronchus
  - Rapid sequence induction if no difficult airway is expected
  - Inhalational induction keeping spontaneous breathing
    - Hard to reach the depth needed for a DLT with sevoflurane alone: add topicalisation
    - Induction is slow with a large air leak
- Keep the patient sitting up with the affected side down so fluid or pus does not flood the good lung
- Watch for tension pneumothorax

## Maintenance
- TIVA, as volatile delivery is unreliable with a leak

## Postoperative care
- Aim to extubate; avoid positive-pressure ventilation

## Flashcards
- Q: Key rule for ventilation before the fistula is isolated?
  - A: Avoid positive-pressure ventilation; keep spontaneous breathing and avoid paralysis
- Q: Positioning to protect the healthy lung?
  - A: Sitting up with the affected side tilted down
- Q: Size of ETT for endobronchial intubation of the normal side?
  - A: 6mm
- Q: Why TIVA?
  - A: Volatile delivery is unreliable through a large air leak
