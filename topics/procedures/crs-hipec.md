# Cytoreductive surgery and HIPEC

Long open case: the CRS phase is about blood and fluid loss; the HIPEC phase is about heat, electrolytes and platinum nephrotoxicity.

## Key issues
- Large incision from xiphoid to pelvis
- CRS phase: blood loss
- HIPEC phase: hyperthermia
  - Hypermetabolism, lactic acidosis
  - Vasodilation and hypotension
  - Coagulopathy
- Electrolytes: dialysis fluid is the chemotherapy carrier, causing hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, raised lactate and hyperglycaemia
- Chemotherapy: platinum agents impair renal function
- After HIPEC only closure remains, so the end comes quickly

## Cytoreductive surgery phase
### Losses
- Depend on the extent of debulking: a few peritoneal nodules, or very extensive surgery lasting 6-8hrs
- Raw peritoneal surfaces bleed a lot during debulking
- Very high insensible losses from exposed peritoneum and bowel
- Usually a slow ooze rather than sudden massive bleeding, but it adds up
- Much is lost into swabs; suction volumes underestimate it

### Fluid management
- Guide by fluid balance, FloTrac SVV and serial ABG (Hb, lactate, base excess), even hourly if worried
- Avoid overload, which causes bowel oedema
- One approach: half crystalloid, half colloid from the start: 1L crystalloid, then 1L albumin, then alternate
  - Giving all the crystalloid early tends to end up third-spaced by the end
- Fluid balance = blood loss + urine output + insensible loss
  - Insensible loss: 0 for laparoscopic, 2ml/kg for open, 4-10 for CRS
  - Excludes fasting deficit and maintenance
  - Aim for about 1-1.5L positive at the end of the case, not zero balance
- Cystectomy with ileal conduit: urine output disappears during the operation

## HIPEC phase
### Temperature
- Timing is everything
- Start pre-cooling 1hr before HIPEC; HIPEC preparation takes only 30mins, so track the surgical stage closely
- Aim for 35.0 before HIPEC starts; otherwise temperature can reach 39 degrees
- Temperatures of 37+ degrees during HIPEC are common and acceptable
- Start warming as soon as HIPEC ends, or temperature rebounds downwards
  - Consider stopping cooling 15mins early if temperature does not look likely to pass 37
- Cooling methods
  - Most important: underbody water blanket (e.g. Blanketrol) at 5 degrees
  - Forced-air blanket at ambient, fluid warmer off, lower theatre temperature
  - Ice packs to neck and axillae

### Electrolytes
- ABG 30min before HIPEC; start correcting K and glucose (especially in diabetics)
  - Aim K 4
  - Give magnesium prophylactically
- ABG midway
  - Diabetics often need insulin by now
  - Lactate rising from 1 to 3 may be acceptable if all else is fine
- Consider an ABG afterwards (doubles as the end-of-case ABG)

### Chemotherapy
- Platinum agents are nephrotoxic
  - Maintain urine output 1.5-2ml/kg/hr
  - Nephroprotection protocols may include mannitol and thiosulfate (antioxidant)

## Anaesthetic technique (one approach)
- Analgesia
  - Spinal morphine, then bilateral rectus sheath catheters
  - Avoid systemic opioids: no PCA, no tramadol
  - Paracetamol; no parecoxib if platinum chemotherapy is given
- Lines: arterial line with FloTrac; CVP now only considered for HIPEC (more stable if HIPEC problems are anticipated); large volume line with fluid warmer

### Rectus sheath catheter technique
- Start with a Tuohy needle
- Advance until a pop (anterior rectus sheath), then until resistance is felt, without pushing through
- Attach a 20ml syringe; inject and advance as the plane opens; keep 5ml LA to test the catheter
- Thread the catheter, attach clip and filter, test; no need to rescan
- Repeat on the other side
- Secure with skin glue and several loops, leaving little slack so it is not pulled out; stick the clip to the upper abdomen
- Dress after the catheters are in

## Flashcards
- Q: When should cooling start before HIPEC, and what is the target temperature?
  - A: 1hr before; aim for 35.0 before HIPEC starts
- Q: Insensible loss allowance for laparoscopic, open and CRS surgery?
  - A: 0, 2ml/kg and 4-10
- Q: Target fluid balance at the end of CRS?
  - A: About 1-1.5L positive
- Q: Target K before HIPEC?
  - A: K 4, with prophylactic magnesium
- Q: Urine output target with platinum HIPEC?
  - A: 1.5-2ml/kg/hr
