# Pulmonary Embolectomy

Surgical removal of pulmonary emboli on cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), usually in a collapsing patient; speed to bypass is everything.

## Preoperative assessment

- Patient often collapsed, with resuscitation ongoing
- A healthy heart needs 50-80% of the pulmonary trunk obstructed before the RV fails
- May already have had thrombolysis
- Urgent CPB is essential for any chance of survival
- Once the decision for surgery is made, move without delay

## Induction

- Rapid induction
- Inotropes as needed
- Heparin 300 IU/kg
- Start CPB as soon as possible

## Maintenance

- Massive airway haemorrhage can cause difficult ventilation
  - Frequent suctioning
  - Double-lumen tube (DLT)

## Post-bypass management

- Heart rate: normal
- Preload: keep well filled
- Contractility: inotropic support likely
- SVR: vasodilators to lower SVR can help
- Pulmonary pressures
  - Pulmonary vasculature likely to be reactive
  - Careful pulmonary vasodilator management is important
  - Adrenaline may be needed to support the RV

## Postoperative management

- Delay heparin after CPB for at least 24 hrs to reduce surgical bleeding

## Special points

- Very high right-sided pressures can open a patent foramen ovale, giving a right-to-left shunt
  - Hypoxia
  - Possible stroke from paradoxical emboli
- Capnography is unreliable at induction because pulmonary blood flow is severely reduced
  - EtCO2 improves dramatically after successful embolectomy

## Flashcards
- Q: What heparin dose is given before CPB for pulmonary embolectomy?
  - A: 300 IU/kg
- Q: How much of the pulmonary trunk must be obstructed before a healthy RV fails?
  - A: 50-80%
- Q: How long should postoperative heparin be delayed after CPB?
  - A: At least 24 hrs
- Q: Why is EtCO2 unreliable at induction?
  - A: Pulmonary blood flow is severely reduced; it improves dramatically after embolectomy
