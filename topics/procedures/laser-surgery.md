# Laser surgery

Laser in theatre: control the fire triad, protect the patient and staff, and pick an airway plan that minimises airway fire.

## Summary
- General laser
  - Fuel (fire triad): avoid pooling of alcohol prep
  - Patient safety: protect skin and eyes
  - Staff safety: laser glasses, masks, warning signs
- Airway laser
  - Tubed or tubeless technique
  - Oxidiser: low FiO2, avoid nitrous oxide
  - Fuel: laser-resistant ETT, saline-filled cuff

## Patient safety
- Eyes
  - Always tape closed and cover with moist swabs
  - For laser work near the eyes, matt metallic protective eye covers can be used
- Skin
  - Burns possible depending on laser type
  - Cover all exposed skin with drapes of absorbent material, not plastic (combustible)
  - Protect tissue next to the lesion with moist pads or swabs
  - Avoid plastic tape (combustible)
- Skin preparation: flammable fluids such as alcohol should not be used
- Instruments: matt-black, non-reflective instruments reduce beam reflection; mirrored surfaces reflect laser light

## Staff safety
- Teaching and training
- Warning signs outside every theatre door; lock doors from inside while the laser is in use
- Laser glasses
  - Retinal or corneal injury possible depending on beam frequency
  - Everyone in theatre wears glasses matched to the laser, with side shields
  - Ordinary spectacles do not reliably protect the peripheral field
- Laser masks
  - The laser plume from tissue contains fine, potentially harmful particles; use smoke evacuation
  - Laser treatment of papillomata can aerosolise virus; wear special masks
- A designated laser safety officer is present whenever the laser is in use
- Standing further away adds little safety: the beam is high energy and does not diverge

## Airway laser surgery
- Laser can ignite plastic and rubber; oxygen-rich gas plus high thermal energy makes airway fire a high risk
- Plan the airway carefully
- Tracheal tube technique
  - Standard unmodified tubes cannot be used (they burn)
  - Use laser-resistant tubes
    - Their cuffs are vulnerable: protect with damp pledgets
    - Alternative: wrap the ETT in foil
  - Fill the cuff with saline rather than air
    - Add methylthioninium chloride (methylene blue) so a cuff puncture is obvious
- Tubeless techniques
  - Jet ventilation, e.g. Venturi (Sanders injector) via the side port of a bronchoscope or laryngoscope
  - Intermittent face mask ventilation with apnoeic periods during lasing
  - Spontaneous ventilation
  - Apnoeic oxygenation, high-flow nasal oxygen

## Reducing airway fire risk
- Oxidiser
  - Oxygen 25% or less
  - Oxygen from a face mask or nasal cannula can collect under drapes and feed a fire
  - Avoid nitrous oxide (supports combustion)
- Fuel
  - Laser-resistant ETT
  - Saline-filled cuffs; double cuffs protect the airway but do not reduce fire risk
- Keep a 50-mL syringe of saline immediately available

## Flashcards
- Q: What oxygen concentration should be used during airway laser surgery?
  - A: 25% or less, and avoid nitrous oxide.
- Q: What should the ETT cuff be filled with for laser surgery, and why add methylene blue?
  - A: Saline; methylene blue makes a cuff puncture obvious.
- Q: What should be immediately available during airway laser surgery?
  - A: A 50-mL syringe filled with saline.
- Q: Why does moving further from the laser add little safety?
  - A: The beam is high energy and non-divergent.
