# Starting a Case and Preparing for Surgery

Step-by-step sequence from the morning machine check to knife-to-skin, for any type of anaesthesia.

## Morning
- Anaesthetic machine check

## Preparing theatre before the case
- Prepare drugs
- Set ventilator settings and monitor alarms
- Repeat the machine check if the breathing circuit has been changed
- Set theatre temperature
- Team brief (pre-operative huddle)
  - Especially for difficult airways, including obstetric cases

## When the patient arrives
- Transfer the patient to the table (or patient moves themselves)
- Take arms out of the gown
- Set up and test the IV drip
- Attach monitors
- Insert any lines needed before induction
- Salbutamol puff if indicated
- Aspirate the nasogastric tube
- Consider a test dose of antibiotics

## General anaesthesia: overview
- Preoxygenate and check equipment and staff (airway, drugs, ventilator)
- Induce
- Intubate or insert LMA and secure it
- Prepare for the operation

## General anaesthesia: induction steps
- Preoxygenate for 3-5 min with the APL valve open; watch end-tidal oxygen
- Induce: fentanyl, then propofol or lignocaine
  - Give until the patient is asleep: call their name, check the eyelash reflex
- Mask ventilate: hold the mask, partly close the APL valve, squeeze the bag
  - Feel compliance; look for chest rise, misting and an ETCO2 trace
  - Decide within 5-10 seconds whether you can ventilate
  - Adjuncts may be needed
- Give the muscle relaxant and turn on volatile to keep the patient asleep
  - Wait for the relaxant to work: 2-3 min for atracurium, 1 min for rocuronium
- Intubate or insert LMA; consider turning off volatile or reducing flow first
  - Laryngoscope in, ETT in, laryngoscope out, inflate cuff
  - Assistant holds the ETT
  - Change gloves
  - Connect ETT to the circuit
- Confirm placement and ventilation: auscultate and check ETCO2
- Reduce flows and FiO2, switch to desflurane, switch to the ventilator
  - Alternative order: ventilator, then FiO2 and flow, then volatile (left to right on the machine)
- Secure the tube
- Housekeeping: check cuff pressure, consider a bite block
- Check blood pressure

## Maintenance once intubated
- Set the ventilator
- Inhalational or IV hypnotic
  - For example, flows of 1 + 1 l/min with sevoflurane 4% until target reached
  - Or keep sevoflurane or desflurane low

## Preparing for the operation
- Protection and positioning
  - Tape the eyes
  - Position the patient; may need to move them down the table
  - Headboard to support the ETT
  - Right angle connector for easier airway access
  - Position arms and head as needed
  - Pad pressure points
  - Supinate the arms
- Temperature
  - Forced-air warmer (Bair Hugger): switch on once draped
- Drugs and fluids
  - Antibiotics
  - Dexamethasone for PONV
  - Paracetamol and parecoxib
  - Start loading morphine
  - Support blood pressure (often falls without surgical stimulus)
- Surgical side: draping, then surgical time-out before incision

## Knife to skin
- Give a dose of fentanyl between finishing draping and time-out

## Documentation
- Do this last

## Other points
- Protective gel can be applied to the mouth

## Other techniques
- Spinal anaesthesia: see spinal technique
- Peripheral nerve block: see general nerve block technique
- Sedation

## Flashcards
- Q: How long should you preoxygenate?
  - A: 3-5 min with the APL valve open, watching end-tidal oxygen
- Q: How quickly should you decide whether mask ventilation is possible?
  - A: Within 5-10 seconds
- Q: How long to wait after atracurium and after rocuronium before intubating?
  - A: Atracurium 2-3 min; rocuronium 1 min
- Q: When is the extra fentanyl dose given before incision?
  - A: Between finishing draping and the surgical time-out
