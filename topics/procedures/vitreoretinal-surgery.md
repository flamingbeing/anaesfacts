# Vitreoretinal Surgery

Long, still surgery under block and/or GA; avoid nitrous oxide with intraocular gas, and watch for the oculocardiac reflex.

## Anaesthetic technique
- Regional block and/or GA (no nitrous oxide); not topical alone
  - Surgery can be long and the patient must stay completely still

## Intraocular gas and nitrous oxide
- No nitrous oxide when gas (SF6 or C3F8) is to be injected, or a gas bubble was placed earlier for retinal tamponade, unless an ophthalmologist documents it has fully absorbed
- Usual absorption: SF6 by 10 days, C3F8 by six weeks
- Blindness has been reported with nitrous oxide given 25 days after SF6 and 41 days after C3F8

## Retinal detachment surgery
- Mainly extraocular, but becomes intraocular if the surgeon drains subretinal fluid, so manage as intraocular surgery
- Globe rotation and traction on extraocular muscles can trigger the oculocardiac reflex: watch for bradycardia and other arrhythmias

## Flashcards
- Q: How long after SF6 or C3F8 can nitrous oxide be dangerous?
  - A: Blindness reported up to 25 days after SF6 and 41 days after C3F8 (usual absorption: 10 days and six weeks)
- Q: Why is topical anaesthesia alone avoided for vitreoretinal surgery?
  - A: Surgery may be long and the patient must not move
- Q: Which reflex should you watch for during retinal detachment surgery?
  - A: Oculocardiac reflex (bradycardia, arrhythmias)
