# Off-Pump and Minimally Invasive CABG

Beating-heart coronary grafting without CPB: haemodynamic instability during grafting, lower heparin dose and active warming are the main issues.

## OPCAB: benefits
- Avoids CPB and its effects on almost every system
  - No SIRS from blood contact with the circuit
  - No embolic stroke or aortic dissection from cannulation or cross-clamp
  - No renal injury from non-pulsatile flow
  - No atelectasis from collapsed lungs on bypass
  - No myocardial ischaemia-reperfusion injury
- Faster recovery and lower cost (no circuit)

## OPCAB: disadvantages
- Risk of incomplete revascularisation and more graft failure than on-pump

## OPCAB: technique
- Median sternotomy
- Heart keeps beating; no CPB or cardioplegia
- Stabilisers (e.g. Octopus suction arms) and CO2 blowers to see the field

## MIDCAB: benefits
- No sternotomy: less pain, better lung mechanics, earlier (on-table) extubation
- No CPB

## MIDCAB: disadvantages
- Technically difficult with poor access
- Needs a double-lumen tube

## MIDCAB: technique
- Small left anterior transverse (keyhole) thoracotomy
  - Double-lumen tube for one-lung ventilation
  - External defibrillator pads mandatory: internal paddles hard to use
- No CPB
- Stabilised with latex sutures and a small compression foot
- Usually a single graft: LIMA to LAD

## Anaesthetic management
- Double-lumen tube and defibrillator pads if thoracotomy
- TOE mandatory

## Haemodynamic instability
- Stay vigilant and communicate with surgeons during grafting
- Causes of hypotension
  - Mechanical compression by stabilisers, reducing filling
  - Positioning: lifting the heart vertically for posterior grafts reduces venous return and causes AV valve regurgitation
  - Valve distortion
  - Myocardial ischaemia, especially during anastomosis
  - Arrhythmias
  - Bleeding
- Strategies
  - Talk to the surgeon: reposition heart or stabilisers
  - Trendelenburg
  - Keep preload higher to offset compression
  - Alpha agonists (noradrenaline preferred) to avoid tachycardia
  - Careful inotropy for contractility
  - Intracoronary shunt for ischaemia; detect with ECG and new regional wall motion abnormality on TOE
  - Keep potassium and magnesium high-normal to prevent arrhythmias
  - Pacing to keep rate and AV synchrony
  - Consider IABP or conversion to CPB
- Myocardial ischaemia during anastomosis: the target coronary may be occluded briefly

## Heart rate
- Keep heart rate low to cut oxygen demand and surgical motion
- Remifentanil or fentanyl; esmolol or adenosine ready for further slowing
- Atrial pacing if too slow; avoid atropine

## Ventilation
- Small tidal volumes with a higher rate to reduce field movement

## Anticoagulation
- Lower heparin dose than on-pump, e.g. 10000 units
  - Target ACT above 250-300
  - Protamine 50 mg usually enough
- If urgent conversion to CPB: give full heparin (400 units/kg) at once

## Temperature
- Aggressive hypothermia prevention: no CPB heat exchanger
  - Forced-air warming and fluid warmers
  - Prewarming
  - Warm theatre
  - Warm irrigation fluid
- Especially important for on-table extubation

## Analgesia
- Regional blocks to allow on-table extubation

## Emergencies
- May need to go on bypass urgently: intractable VF, uncontrolled bleeding or ischaemic collapse
- Perfusionist immediately available

## Flashcards
- Q: What heparin dose and ACT target are used for OPCAB?
  - A: Lower dose, e.g. 10000 units; ACT above 250-300.
- Q: What heparin dose is needed for urgent conversion to CPB?
  - A: Full heparinisation, 400 units/kg.
- Q: Why must external defib pads be placed for MIDCAB?
  - A: The keyhole thoracotomy restricts internal paddle access.
- Q: Which vasopressor is preferred for hypotension during OPCAB, and why?
  - A: Noradrenaline (alpha agonist), to avoid tachycardia.
- Q: What is the usual MIDCAB graft?
  - A: A single LIMA to LAD graft.
