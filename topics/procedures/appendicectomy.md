# Appendicectomy

Usually young fit patients needing RSI; ask whether the appendix has perforated (sepsis) and add a TAP or ilioinguinal block.

## Key points
- RSI
- Perforated? (sepsis risk)

## Patient and surgical factors
- Usually aged 5–20yr and fit, unless perforated, when they may be septic
- Occasionally elderly: may be the first sign of caecal adenocarcinoma needing right hemicolectomy
- NG tube usually not needed
- A drain may be placed; keep nil by mouth if the abdomen is contaminated

## Intraoperative management
- Prophylactic antibiotics: ceftriaxone (Rocephin) and metronidazole (Flagyl)
- Supine; left arm tucked, right arm can be out
- RSI, ETT, IPPV; muscle relaxation is needed
- Analgesia
  - Consider IV NSAID and paracetamol
  - Consider right ilioinguinal nerve block or right TAP block, or ask the surgeon to infiltrate

## Postoperative management
- Regular simple analgesia, opioid as needed, antiemetic
- IV fluids until drinking

## Flashcards
- Q: Typical age range for appendicectomy?
  - A: 5–20yr
- Q: Induction technique for appendicectomy?
  - A: RSI with ETT and IPPV
- Q: Which regional blocks suit open appendicectomy?
  - A: Right ilioinguinal nerve block or right TAP block, or surgical infiltration
