# Remote Site Anaesthesia

Anaesthesia outside the main theatre suite: an unfamiliar, poorly equipped, isolated place with untrained helpers.

## Key problems
- Summary (3P3Es): unfamiliar, untrained, limited, isolated
- Environment: small and unfamiliar
  - Unfamiliar to the anaesthetist; find where key items are kept, e.g. the self-inflating (Ambu) bag
  - Limited space
- Personnel: untrained
  - Clear communication and assigned roles help
- Equipment: limited specialised kit
  - Limited to what you can bring
  - Some equipment may not fit
  - TIVA may be needed
- Emergencies: isolated
  - Far from other anaesthetists; hard to get help
  - Specialised procedures not possible
  - Crash cart or self-inflating bag may be absent
- Patient: poor access
- Procedure-specific factors

## Conduct
- Meticulous preparation
  - Scout the location, including emergency equipment
  - Equipment checklist
  - Staff huddle
- Low threshold to secure the airway
- Extension lines
- Transfers
  - Consider inducing in the main theatre (e.g. difficult airway)
  - Agree a clear route back to the main theatre or ICU if complications arise

## Similar settings
- Emergency (unscheduled) theatre lists
- After-hours surgery

## Flashcards
- Q: Four main problems of remote site anaesthesia?
  - A: Unfamiliar environment, untrained personnel, limited equipment, isolation in emergencies.
- Q: Airway approach at a remote site?
  - A: Low threshold to secure the airway; consider inducing in the main theatre if the airway is difficult.
- Q: Key preparation steps before a remote case?
  - A: Scout the site and its emergency kit, use an equipment checklist, hold a staff huddle.
