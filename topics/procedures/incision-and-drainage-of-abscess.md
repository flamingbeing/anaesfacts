# Incision and Drainage of Abscess

Short procedure with bursts of intense pain: keep the patient deep and well loaded with opioid to avoid laryngospasm.

## Overview
- Painful stimulation can trigger laryngospasm
- Patient may be septic
- Technique: GA with LMA; spontaneous ventilation or IPPV; keep deep
- Position depends on abscess site: lateral (back), prone (neck)
- Antibiotics often given on the ward
- Procedure: incise, curette, cautery, hydrogen peroxide, gauze
- Duration 15-30 min
- Blood loss minimal
- Complications: laryngospasm; delayed return of spontaneous ventilation once the intense stimulus stops
- Pain: short periods of intense pain; no local anaesthetic given, so long-acting opioids often needed; pain can start right from skin cleaning
- Postoperative: general ward

## Surgery
- **Usually perianal or back abscess**, but can be anywhere, with special positioning considerations
  - May need a vacuum (VAC) dressing
- Needle may be used to probe and aspirate
- Curette and finger exploration of the cavity
- Deep cavities may be packed with ribbon gauze

## Main considerations
- **Short periods of intense pain**, especially during curettage and exploration
  - Surgeons usually do not infiltrate local anaesthetic into an infected wound
- **Fast**: 15-30 min

## Preoperative assessment
- Often on the emergency list
- Check for **sepsis: white cell count, procalcitonin, fever, tachycardia or hypotension**

## Intraoperative management
- Technique: usually GA as it is quick
  - Consider spinal or peripheral nerve block if septic (as for wound debridement)
- Monitoring and equipment: routine
- Position depends on abscess site:
  - Perianal: lithotomy
  - Back: **lateral**, use a **ProSeal LMA**
  - **Neck: may be prone, so intubate**
- Airway and ventilation
  - **LMA** usually sufficient
    - Check it is **sitting well, especially after turning lateral or into lithotomy**, as laryngospasm may follow
  - **Switch to pressure support early**, as the procedure is fast
- Induction
  - Routine
  - Give antibiotics if not already given, as surgeons usually require them
- Maintenance
  - **Adequate opioid analgesia**
    - Load morphine early
    - Consider 2x fentanyl doses
    - Alfentanil may be useful
    - Multimodal: paracetamol, parecoxib
  - **Keep the patient deep** to avoid reacting to stimulation
- Emergence
  - Ensure adequate breathing

## Flashcards
- Q: What is the main airway complication during I&D of an abscess?
  - A: Laryngospasm, from intense stimulation at light depth
- Q: Why is local anaesthetic usually not used for abscess drainage?
  - A: Surgeons avoid infiltrating an infected wound
- Q: How long does an I&D of abscess usually take?
  - A: 15-30 min
- Q: What airway is used for a back abscess in the lateral position, and for a prone neck abscess?
  - A: ProSeal LMA for lateral; tracheal tube for prone
