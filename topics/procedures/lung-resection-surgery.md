# Lung Resection Surgery

Elderly smokers with lung cancer: assess fitness with the three-legged stool, then OLV via DLT, restrictive fluids and strong regional analgesia.

## Summary
- Usual diagnosis: lung cancer
- Fitness: the three-legged stool
- Anaesthesia: OLV, DLT, often VATS
- Intermediate-risk surgery: perioperative risk of cardiac death and non-fatal cardiac events 1-5%
- Mortality is high: lobectomy 2-4%, pneumonectomy 6-8%

## Indications
- Lung cancer
  - Non-small cell (NSCLC, 80%): slow growing; 5y survival 40% with surgery, 10% without
  - Small cell (SCLC, 20%): aggressive, usually metastatic at presentation, usually inoperable
- Others: benign tumours, bronchiectasis, TB

## Key considerations
- Surgical
  - Long surgery: fluid and electrolyte balance, VTE prophylaxis, hypothermia
  - Lateral position
  - Possible blood loss
  - Careful fluids to prevent postoperative pulmonary oedema
  - Possible difficult airway
  - Tolerance of OLV: lung isolation, managing hypoxaemia
  - Postoperative complications, post-thoracotomy analgesia, possible postoperative ventilation
- Patient
  - Elderly
  - Current or heavy smokers with smoking-related disease: COPD, pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale
  - Effects of lung cancer and its treatment
  - Cardiovascular fitness, respiratory function, nutrition

## Preoperative assessment

### Typical patient
- Elderly and less fit (except pneumothorax and sympathectomy patients, who are usually younger)
- Smokers
- Often significant comorbidity: IHD, vascular disease, renal impairment

### Underlying disease
- Bronchial carcinoma: local effects, paraneoplastic effects, cachexia
- Pleural effusion
- Empyema: sepsis
- Haemoptysis: RSI
- COPD
- Oesophageal obstruction

### Airway assessment for DLT
- Check CT, CXR or bronchoscopy reports for airway obstruction and tracheal or carinal distortion or compression
- Size the DLT by sex, height and CT tracheal diameter

### Respiratory assessment (major risk; ability to tolerate OLV)
- Cough, sputum and recent change; haemoptysis
- Functional capacity, breathlessness on exertion
- Current treatment: inhalers, home oxygen
- CXR or CT for bullae or effusions; discuss scans with the surgeon
- PFTs (below); CPET for borderline cases

### Cardiovascular assessment (risk of MACE)
- IHD, vascular disease
- RV function, especially in COPD
- ESRF: risk of fluid overload

## Three-legged stool
- Respiratory mechanics: FEV1, ppoFEV1
- Lung parenchymal function: DLCO, ppoDLCO
- Cardiopulmonary reserve: exercise tolerance, 6 minute walk test, CPET

## Pulmonary function tests
- FEV1 and DLCO:
  - Straightforward patient: both >60% of predicted
  - Marginal patient: either 30-60% predicted; calculate ppo values
- FEV1 (spirometry): aim >60% predicted
- Accepted minimum FEV1 by operation:
  - Pneumonectomy: >60% (or >2L)
  - Lobectomy: >40% (or 1.5L)
  - Wedge resection: >30% (or 1L)
- DLCO: aim >60% predicted

### Predicted postoperative (ppo) values
- Based on the fraction of lung removed:
  - Preop value x (5 - lobes resected)/5
  - Preop value x (19 - segments resected)/19
    - RUL 3 segments, RML 2, RLL 5, LUL 4, LLL 5
  - Preop value x (1 - % functional tissue removed/100), guided by V/Q scan
- ppoFEV1:
  - >40% of predicted (about >800mls): can extubate
  - 30-40%: decide on extubation using exercise tolerance, DLCO, V/Q and comorbidities
  - <30%: staged weaning from mechanical ventilation
- ppoDLCO:
  - >40% predicted is listed as an independent predictor of more pulmonary and cardiac complications
  - <30%: hypoxic without supplemental O2

## Cardiopulmonary reserve
- Functional METs
- CPET:
  - Anaerobic threshold >11ml/kg/min
  - VO2 max >15ml/kg/min: limit to lobectomy or wedge; high risk
- Breath hold for 30 sec
- 6 minute walk test (6MWT)
  - Correlates well with VO2 max: <2000feet means <15ml/kg/min
  - Monitor SpO2; high risk unless the drop is <4% and SpO2 stays >90% after

## Other tests
- ABG: hypoxia or hypercarbia on room air is a poor sign
- V/Q scan: regional blood flow to each lung, for more accurate ppoFEV1 and ppoDLCO
  - Normal split: left 45%, right 55%
- FBC: polycythaemia from chronic hypoxia, white cells for infection
- CXR: DLT sizing, gross pathology
- CT: airway and extent of disease

## Suitability for surgery
- Oncological: no distant metastases (M1) and no extensive mediastinal N2/N3 disease (CT, PET, EBUS)
- Surgical: technically resectable, without prohibitive invasion of great vessels or heart
- Physiological: fit for GA and for lung resection (three-legged stool: mechanics, parenchyma, reserve)

## Optimisation
- Lung function:
  - Stop smoking 6-8 weeks before
  - Incentive spirometry
  - Optimise bronchodilators; consider oral steroids
  - Physiotherapy and a home exercise plan
- Nutrition
- MDT and close communication with the surgeon: operation, position, likely complications

## Risk assessment
- Three-legged stool and ppoFEV1
- Thoracic Revised Cardiac Risk Index (ThRCRI):
  - Creatinine > 176.8 μmol/L: 1 point
  - Cerebrovascular disease: 1 point
  - Cardiac ischaemia: 1.5 points
  - Pneumonectomy: 1.5 points
- Risk of MACE: 0 points 2%; 1-1.5 points 6%; 2-2.5 points 11%; >2.5 points 20%
- Other risk factors: cardiac function, AF, COPD, cor pulmonale, stroke, CKD, frailty

## Intraoperative management

### Monitoring and access
- Arterial line: mandatory (serial ABGs during OLV, BP swings with surgical handling)
- CVP: not routine; useful in pneumonectomy to avoid fluid overload (risk of right heart failure)
- Large-bore IV access, ideally in the non-dependent arm (avoids positional ischaemia and tissued drips)

### Positioning
- Lateral decubitus, table broken, elbows flexed so the forearms lie parallel to the face, upper arm in a gutter support

### One-lung ventilation
- Left-sided DLT: standard
- Right-sided DLT: only for left main bronchus surgery or distortion
- Bronchial blocker: difficult airway, previous contralateral lung surgery, children
- Lung-protective ventilation:
  - Tidal volume 6 ml/kg (ideal body weight)
  - PEEP 5–10
  - Peak pressure <30
- Restrictive, goal-directed fluids: excess fluid increases post-pneumonectomy pulmonary oedema (high mortality)

### Analgesia
- Regional:
  - Thoracic epidural: gold standard for open thoracotomy
  - Paravertebral block: equivalent for VATS or limited thoracotomy
  - ESP block
  - Intercostal block by the surgeon
- Multimodal and opioid-sparing (to avoid respiratory depression): ketamine, paracetamol, magnesium

### Resection
- Bronchial suture line is leak-tested under saline with manual inflation to 40cmH2O

## Postoperative care
- Extubation:
  - ppoFEV1 >40%: extubate in theatre
  - ppoFEV1 <30%: planned ventilation in ICU
    - Change the DLT to a single-lumen ETT
    - Wean in SICU over a few hours
- Good analgesia to limit postoperative respiratory dysfunction
- Judicious fluids to prevent pulmonary oedema

## Complications
- Early
  - Cardiac: supraventricular arrhythmias (especially AF), MI, acute RV failure or pulmonary oedema, cardiac herniation through a pericardial defect (can cause VF)
  - Respiratory: acute respiratory failure, ARDS or ALI, PE, lung torsion or infarction
  - Others: haemorrhage, airway trauma, nerve injury (recurrent laryngeal, vagus, phrenic)
- Late
  - Respiratory: pneumonia, empyema, effusion, haemothorax, chylothorax, bronchopleural fistula
  - Post-pneumonectomy syndrome: mediastinal shift stretches and compresses the left main bronchus between the pulmonary artery in front and the aorta and spine behind; commoner after right pneumonectomy
  - Others: stroke, MI, DVT, PE

## Causes of tachypnoea in PACU
- Respiratory: pneumothorax, lung collapse or atelectasis, COPD exacerbation, airway obstruction (bleeding, oedema), pulmonary oedema, aspiration
- Cardiac: MI, anaemia
- Others: sepsis, pain

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the three legs of the pre-thoracotomy assessment stool?
  - A: Respiratory mechanics (FEV1), lung parenchymal function (DLCO), cardiopulmonary reserve (exercise testing, CPET)
- Q: How is ppoFEV1 calculated by segments?
  - A: Preop value x (19 - segments resected)/19
- Q: What ppoFEV1 allows extubation in theatre after lung resection?
  - A: >40% of predicted (about >800mls); <30% needs planned ventilation
- Q: What minimum FEV1 is accepted for pneumonectomy, lobectomy and wedge resection?
  - A: >60% (or >2L), >40% (or 1.5L), >30% (or 1L)
- Q: What ventilation settings are used during OLV?
  - A: Tidal volume 6 ml/kg IBW, PEEP 5–10, peak pressure <30
