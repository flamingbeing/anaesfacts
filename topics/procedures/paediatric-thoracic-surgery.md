# Paediatric Thoracic Surgery and One Lung Ventilation

Lung isolation choices by age, airway sizing and anaesthesia for thoracotomy and thoracoscopy in children.

## Lung isolation options
- Conventional ETT in the trachea: surgeon retracts the operated lung with swabs and retractors in open surgery
- Double lumen tube (DLT)
  - Preferred at 8–18 yr
  - Smallest DLT made is 26F, usable only over 8 years old
- Bronchial blocker
  - Preferred at 2–8 yr (coaxial technique, through the ETT)
  - Preferred at 6 months–2 yr (parallel technique, outside the ETT)
  - Paediatric 5Fr blocker has no central lumen
    - Minimum ETT size 5.0mm
    - Maximum cuff volume 3ml
  - For blocker and bronchoscope to fit through the ETT together: (OD blocker + OD bronchoscope) / ID tube < 0.9
- Selective endobronchial intubation
  - Preferred at 0–6 months
  - Easier on the right; a cuff protruding above the carina can block the other main bronchus

## Paediatric bronchoscopy
- OD bronchoscope / ID tube < 0.7 to allow ventilation around the scope
- Vathin scopes: 2.2 (no suction channel) and 3.2
  - The 2.2 is the smallest fibreoptic scope and can be used down to a 3.0 ETT
  - Through a 2.5 ETT the ratio is 2.2/2.5, i.e. 0.88: below 0.9 so it fits, but above 0.7 so no ventilation is possible

## Airway size
- The trachea is elliptical: transverse diameter larger than anteroposterior
- Size devices on the smaller (anteroposterior) diameter
- Left and right main bronchus diameters are about 0.66 and 0.86 of the tracheal diameter respectively

## Indications for thoracotomy and thoracoscopy
- Closed cardiac procedures: PDA ligation, coarctation repair, modified Blalock-Taussig shunt
- Tracheo-oesophageal fistula repair
- Congenital cystic adenomatoid malformation resection
  - Incidence 1:5000 births; may compress surrounding lung; low malignant risk
- Congenital lobar emphysema: resected when it compresses adjacent lung (worsened by IPPV pressure)
- Bronchiectasis
- Mediastinal masses: biopsy or resection
- Empyema drainage, decortication
- Anterior approach for scoliosis
- Lung sequestration
  - Large blood supply can cause left to right shunt to the IVC and high output cardiac failure
  - Associated with diaphragmatic hernia

## Complications
- Stump leak, hypoventilation, hypoxia, intrapulmonary bleeding, haemothorax

## Physiology in children
- Many procedures now thoracoscopic with a CO2 capnothorax
- Oxygen consumption in neonates and infants is double the adult value (7 mL/kg/min vs 3 mL/kg/min)
- Closing volume exceeds FRC up to age 6-8 years
- Immature hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction, compliant chest wall and volatile agents mean the upper (operated) lung may get more perfusion than the dependent lung, increasing shunt
- Prevent heat loss in open surgery: warm theatre, fluids and gases, warming mattress; hypothermia rarely a problem with thoracoscopy
- Look for associated cardiac disease

## Preoperative assessment
- Likely pathology; previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy
- Review CXR, CT or MRI
- Assess risk of lung soiling, bleeding and poor postoperative respiratory function; possible need for postoperative IPPV
- FBC, U&E, crossmatch
- Arterial or capillary gas if hypoxic
- Formal lung function tests are difficult under 8-10 years of age
- Avoid sedative premedication if hypoxic and/or hypercarbic

## Anaesthetic technique
- Induction usually as parent or child prefers; inhalational sometimes necessary (e.g. mediastinal mass biopsy or resection)
- ETT, DLT or bronchial blocker, position confirmed with fibreoptic scope
- IPPV
- Maintenance: volatile in oxygen and air
  - Remifentanil infusion 0.1-0.25 mcg/kg/min reduces volatile need and helps cardiovascular stability
- Low threshold for arterial line, central line and urinary catheter (by age, size, pathology, expected course)
- Lateral position, operative side up
- Hand ventilation with a T-piece may be better for part or all of the case if compliance changes, or with hypoxia or hypercarbia
- High FiO2 often needed during one lung anaesthesia
- Periodic re-expansion and two-lung ventilation may be needed to clear CO2 or improve oxygenation
- Neonatal thoracoscopy causes hypercarbia; capnothorax can cause hypotension needing a fluid bolus and/or inotrope

## Analgesia
- Thoracotomy: opioid bolus (morphine 100-150 mcg/kg IV or fentanyl 1-5 mcg/kg) plus a regional technique:
  - Thoracic epidural
  - Intercostal blocks by the surgeon at closure: at least two nerves above and below the incision
  - Paravertebral block
  - Interpleural catheter (epidural catheter placed by the surgeon at closure and tunnelled)
- Thoracoscopy: opioid plus port-site infiltration with local anaesthetic and adrenaline
- NSAID in both, unless contraindicated

## Postoperative care
- HDU care
- Oxygen as needed; early chest physiotherapy
- Maintenance fluids until feeding
- Chest drains can hurt as much as the incision
- Analgesia options: epidural infusion of local anaesthetic plus opioid, or a regional block from theatre (intercostal, paravertebral, interpleural) with IV morphine infusion, PCA or NCA
- Regular NSAID is very useful in most

## Flashcards
- Q: Smallest DLT made and minimum age?
  - A: 26F, only in children over 8 years.
- Q: Preferred lung isolation method by age?
  - A: 0–6 months endobronchial intubation; 6 months–2 yr blocker (parallel); 2–8 yr blocker (coaxial); 8–18 yr DLT.
- Q: Which size ratio must be met to ventilate around a bronchoscope in an ETT?
  - A: OD bronchoscope / ID tube < 0.7.
- Q: Oxygen consumption in neonates vs adults?
  - A: 7 mL/kg/min vs 3 mL/kg/min.
- Q: Remifentanil infusion rate for paediatric thoracic surgery?
  - A: 0.1-0.25 mcg/kg/min.
