# Surgery for Complications of Pancreatitis

Operations such as necrosectomy for infected necrosis or uncontrolled haemorrhage in pancreatitis; patients are often septic and dry.

## Indications

- Infected pancreatic necrosis
- Haemorrhagic pancreatitis not responding to blood product resuscitation and correction of coagulopathy

## Anaesthetic issues

- Respiratory compromise and sepsis are common
- Severe dehydration and electrolyte disturbance, especially hypocalcaemia
- Invasive monitoring decided case by case

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the indications for surgery in pancreatitis?
  - A: Infected pancreatic necrosis, and haemorrhagic pancreatitis not responding to blood products and correction of coagulopathy
- Q: Which electrolyte disturbance is especially common in severe pancreatitis?
  - A: Hypocalcaemia
- Q: What systemic problems do these patients often bring to theatre?
  - A: Respiratory compromise, sepsis and severe dehydration
