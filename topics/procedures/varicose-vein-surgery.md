# Varicose Vein Surgery

Removal of lower limb varicose veins in mostly fit patients; usually LMA with spontaneous breathing.

## Procedure
- Removal of tortuous lower limb veins
- High tie and strip: removal of the long saphenous vein, sometimes both legs
- Short saphenous vein surgery: vein tied off in the popliteal fossa

## Preoperative assessment
- Usually young and fit (Oxford Handbook)
- Wide age range, young adults to elderly; average age 48 years, but generally healthy (Manual of Surgical Procedures)
- With a venous ulcer, base preoperative antibiotics on culture results

## Duration
- 30 min to 3 hr (Oxford Handbook)
- 2-3 hr (Manual of Surgical Procedures)
- Bilateral surgery is common: 30–60 min per incision
- Redo surgery is common and can be very long

## Blood loss
- 50-250 ml (Manual of Surgical Procedures)
- Up to 1000 ml (Oxford Handbook)
- Multiple avulsions are usually added to the main operation; these tiny incisions can bleed heavily
- Leg elevation reduces blood loss

## Position
- Supine
- May be prone for short saphenous surgery
- Combined long and short saphenous surgery (two incisions on one leg) may need turning mid-operation

## Anaesthetic technique
- LMA with spontaneous ventilation for most
  - Suitable in selected slim patients without aspiration risk
- ETT with IPPV if prone
- Regional anaesthesia possible
  - Spinal
  - Surgical field block plus ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerve blocks (needs gentle surgical handling)
- Monitored anaesthesia care or local anaesthesia also possible

## Postoperative analgesia
- Long-acting opioids not needed
- NSAIDs plus local anaesthetic into the groin wound give good analgesia

## Flashcards
- Q: What blood loss can varicose vein surgery cause?
  - A: 50-250 ml typically; up to 1000 ml.
- Q: How long does bilateral surgery take per incision?
  - A: 30–60 min.
- Q: What airway is used if the patient is prone?
  - A: ETT with IPPV.
- Q: What gives good postoperative analgesia?
  - A: NSAIDs plus local anaesthetic into the groin wound; no long-acting opioids needed.
