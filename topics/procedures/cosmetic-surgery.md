# Cosmetic Surgery

Abdominoplasty, liposuction, facelift and prominent ear correction: watch local anaesthetic dose, fluid balance, temperature and smooth emergence.

## Abdominoplasty
- Also called lipectomy; fourth most common cosmetic procedure
- Indications: cosmetic (tummy tuck), after abdominal trauma, after bariatric surgery or extreme weight loss, ventral hernia, after infection or abdominal wall dehiscence
- Preoperative: often obese, with OSA
- Intraoperative
  - GA with ETT
  - Temperature probe and forced-air warming
  - Consider remifentanil, arterial line and BIS
- Postoperative: PCA

## Liposuction: techniques
- Vacuum aspiration of subcutaneous fat through a small incision with a blunt cannula
- Four techniques differ in how much fluid is infiltrated, and so in blood loss as a share of aspirate
  - Dry: no infiltration; blood loss 20-45% of aspirate
  - Wet: 200-300 ml wetting solution per area, whatever the aspirate; blood loss 4-30%
  - Superwet: 1 ml injected per ml of expected aspirate; blood loss about 1%
  - Tumescent: 3-4 ml injected per ml of expected aspirate, making tissue tense and firm; blood loss about 1%
- Variants: ultrasound-assisted, laser-assisted

## Liposuction: preoperative assessment
- Usually well
- Some are obese

## Liposuction: anaesthesia
- Local infiltration alone, with IV sedation, or GA with LMA
  - Local or MAC suits small-volume liposuction
  - GA recommended for large volumes or by patient choice
  - Lumbar epidural or spinal possible for abdominal liposuction
- Tumescent technique (most common): large volume of very dilute local anaesthetic with adrenaline infiltrated into the fat compartment
  - Lidocaine 35-55 mg/kg can be used, as absorption is slow and peak levels are delayed
    - Subcutaneous fat has low blood flow
    - Adrenaline gives long, intense vasoconstriction
    - Lidocaine is lipophilic and taken up by fat cells
    - Dilution lowers and delays the plasma peak further
  - Local anaesthesia lasts up to 18 hours, so little postoperative analgesia is needed
  - Vasoconstriction limits blood loss (about 1% of aspirate), bruising and soreness
  - Infiltrated fluid absorbed over 48 hours
    - Adrenaline peaks 5-6 hours after the procedure
    - Lidocaine peaks 12-14 hours after the procedure
    - So LAST risk persists for hours after surgery

## Large-volume liposuction (over 4 L)
- Haemodynamic changes
  - Adrenaline: tachycardia
  - Blood loss: can be large, depending on technique
  - Volume overload: insert a urinary catheter to track fluid status and avoid bladder overdistension; care in cardiac, renal or liver disease
- Hypothermia: large exposed area and large volumes of cold infiltrate
- LAST
- Other complications: pneumoperitoneum, bowel injury, pneumothorax, haemothorax

## Liposuction: postoperative management
- Pressure dressings
- Encourage oral fluids
- Monitor urine output
- Check Hb
- Bruising can be marked
- Simple analgesics
- Morbidity linked to large aspirate (over 2 L) and high lidocaine dose

## Facelift (rhytidectomy)
- Surgical reduction of facial folds and wrinkles
- Preoperative
  - Age 45-65 years
  - Usually fit and well
  - High expectations; may have had previous procedures
  - Stop NSAIDs 2 weeks (2/52) before surgery
- Intraoperative
  - Lasts 3-4 hours
  - Supine, 30 degrees head up
  - Facial nerve blocks
  - GA with LMA or ETT and IPPV; nasal north-facing RAE useful
    - Discuss with the surgeon which airway they prefer
  - Hypotensive technique (SBP 70-80) may reduce blood loss
  - Remifentanil TIVA
  - Incisions in hidden areas; skin mobilised and put under traction
  - Antiemetics
  - Warming blanket
- Postoperative
  - Avoid coughing at extubation (LMA and propofol ideal)
  - Morphine around extubation; little pain later
  - Avoid shivering (pethidine)
  - Paracetamol and NSAIDs
  - Severe pain suggests haematoma

## Correction of prominent ears
- Preoperative
  - May be one side
  - Day case
  - Usually children aged 4-10 years
  - Children may be anxious and self-conscious (teased at school)
- Intraoperative
  - Supine, 30 degrees head up
  - Flexible LMA, spontaneous ventilation
  - PONV common, so propofol TIVA works well
  - NSAIDs
  - Local infiltration by the surgeon
  - Crystalloid 20 ml/kg
- Postoperative
  - Paracetamol and ibuprofen
  - Tight dressing takes time at the end
  - Complication: early haematoma

## Flashcards
- Q: What maximum lidocaine dose can be used in tumescent liposuction?
  - A: 35-55 mg/kg
- Q: When do plasma lidocaine levels peak after tumescent infiltration?
  - A: 12-14 hours after the procedure
- Q: What is the blood loss with dry versus tumescent liposuction?
  - A: Dry 20-45% of aspirate; tumescent about 1%
- Q: Above what aspirate volume is liposuction called large volume?
  - A: Over 4 L
- Q: When should NSAIDs be stopped before a facelift?
  - A: 2 weeks before surgery
