# Extracorporeal Shock Wave Lithotripsy

Non-invasive fragmentation of renal and ureteric stones by focused acoustic shock waves; usually sedation or none, GA for children.

## Overview
- A lithotripter sends acoustic waves that create stress in the stone and fracture it
- Used for stones <20mm in the upper tract
- Needs an unobstructed system to flush fragments out
- Now uncommon to need anaesthesia or sedation
- Shock waves timed badly (during cardiac repolarisation) can cause arrhythmias; triggering 20 ms after the R wave, when the heart is refractory, reduces this

## Contraindications
- Pregnancy
- Bleeding risk
- Active infection

## Preoperative management
- Premedication: diclofenac, pethidine
- Review previous anaesthetic records
- Pacemaker care
- Often remote-site anaesthesia and day stay

## Intraoperative management
- Sedation for adults (short-acting opioid such as remifentanil usually enough); GA for children
- GA in adults if a long procedure is planned or the patient is restless
- Position: supine or prone depending on stone site; lateral with arms above the head also used
- Antibiotic prophylaxis
- Stones located by ultrasound or image intensifier, then shock wave focused on them
- Arrhythmias: time shock waves to the ECG refractory period
  - Glycopyrrolate can raise heart rate, so more shocks per minute and a shorter procedure
- Epidural: use loss of resistance to saline, as shock wave energy is released at air-water interfaces
- IV hydration; diuretics may help flush fragments
- Antiemetics: GI bruising can cause PONV

## Postoperative management
- Pain is minimal; simple analgesia
- Nausea and bradycardia from excess vagal tone
- Haematuria
- Subcapsular renal haematoma, especially in hypertensive patients

## Flashcards
- Q: What stone size suits ESWL?
  - A: Stones <20mm in the upper tract, with an unobstructed system.
- Q: How are shock-wave arrhythmias reduced?
  - A: Trigger the shock 20 ms after the R wave, in the refractory period.
- Q: Why use loss of resistance to saline when siting an epidural for ESWL?
  - A: Shock wave energy is released at air-water interfaces.
- Q: Name three contraindications to ESWL.
  - A: Pregnancy, bleeding risk and active infection.
