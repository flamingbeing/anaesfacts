# Nephrectomy

Radical or partial kidney removal: watch for IVC tumour extension, large blood loss and severe pain.

## Surgery
- Radical nephrectomy removes the kidney, fascia, adrenal gland and upper ureter
- Indications: malignancy, transplantation, cystic disease, severe stone disease
- In about 5% of patients the tumour extends into the vena cava
  - Full or partial IVC occlusion can reduce venous return
  - The IVC may need temporary clamping during resection, sometimes needing vasopressors
  - Cardiopulmonary bypass is rarely needed for extensive IVC infiltration
- Left nephrectomy: ligation of the left gonadal vein can cause left testicular swelling and pain
- Laparoscopic nephrectomy (retroperitoneal insufflation) is mainly for organ donation or small tumours (partial nephrectomy)

## Preoperative assessment
- Why the kidney is being removed
- Comorbidities: hypertension, diabetes, renovascular disease, paraneoplastic problems (10-40%)
- Hb: renal tumours can cause anaemia without blood loss
- U+E: renal failure, inappropriate ADH secretion from the tumour
- CXR: metastases, effusions
- Laparoscopic or open (open is now very rare)
- Predicted postoperative GFR is about current GFR/2
- Ask the surgeon how invasive the tumour looks (IVC involvement); prepare for massive blood loss

## Positioning
- Supine for the transabdominal approach (tumour surgery: paramedian or transverse laparotomy)
- Kidney position for the retroperitoneal approach (donor surgery: loin incision)
  - Lateral, table broken, patient extended over the kidney rest
  - Compression of the legs and IVC can reduce venous return
  - Pressure care matters

## Intraoperative management
- GA with ETT and IPPV, with or without thoracic epidural, rectus sheath catheter or wound catheter (combined epidural-GA is common)
- Blood loss varies: blood products ready, large IV access, invasive monitoring if indicated
- Be ready for large fluid shifts and large-volume blood loss
- Use the epidural cautiously until bleeding is controlled

## Postoperative care
- Pain is severe:
  - Epidural needs to cover up to T7/8
  - PCA
  - Wound catheters
  - Intercostal blocks: analgesia for several hours
  - NSAIDs
- Complications: bleeding, pneumothorax, PE, pain, vagal reactions

## Partial nephrectomy
- Increasingly used for well-localised tumours or a single kidney (only 1 kidney)
- Blood loss can be large as haemostasis is difficult
- Some surgeons request, before renal artery clamping:
  - Mannitol 12.5g, furosemide 10mg, with or without heparin 3000 IUs
  - Cooling with ice
- Aim: maintain perfusion and reduce ischaemia

## Flashcards
- Q: In what proportion of renal tumours does the tumour extend into the vena cava?
  - A: About 5%
- Q: How do you estimate postoperative GFR after nephrectomy?
  - A: About half the current GFR (current/2)
- Q: What drugs may be given before renal artery clamping in partial nephrectomy?
  - A: Mannitol 12.5g, furosemide 10mg, with or without heparin 3000 IUs
- Q: What dermatomal level must an epidural reach for nephrectomy?
  - A: Up to T7/8
- Q: Why might a patient with a renal tumour be anaemic without bleeding?
  - A: Renal tumours can cause anaemia directly
