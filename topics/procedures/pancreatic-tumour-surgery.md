# Pancreatic Tumour Surgery

Long major laparotomy with bleeding and leak risk: GA (often TIVA), arterial line, large-bore access, a planned analgesic technique and high dependency care after.

## Surgical background

- 5-year survival 15% – 25% (versus 1% – 5% without cancer-directed treatment)
- Acceptable operative (30-day) mortality <5% in the US
- Most perioperative deaths are from systemic (non-surgical) complications
- Operations: Whipple, distal pancreatectomy, total pancreatectomy, regional pancreatectomy

## Course of an open Whipple

- Laparotomy: large midline incision with lateral extension (L shape), or subcostal
- Mobilise and judge resectability (extensive or metastatic tumour is not resected)
- Resection (1st part)
  - Kocher manoeuvre mobilises the pancreatic head
  - Common bile duct divided, gallbladder removed, vessels (e.g. SMV) freed
  - Pancreas, proximal duodenum or distal stomach and jejunum (beyond the ligament of Treitz) divided; specimen removed en bloc
- Reconstruction (2nd part): gastrojejunostomy, hepaticojejunostomy, pancreaticojejunostomy
- Drains nearly always left (high leak risk)

## Preoperative assessment

- Anaemia common (anaemia of chronic disease)
- Electrolyte disturbance common with pain or vomiting: check
- Usually malnourished
- Major surgery: group and crossmatch, blood products on standby; book high dependency or surgical ICU

## Intraoperative management

- GA
  - RSI if the tumour is obstructing
  - Usually TIVA with propofol and remifentanil
- Analgesia: place before surgery
  - Open and laparoscopic need different plans: ask the surgeon how likely conversion to open is
  - Adjuncts: dexmedetomidine, magnesium, ketamine
- Monitoring and lines
  - Large peripheral IV through a fluid warmer, with or without a rapid infuser
  - Arterial line (frequent bloods, especially glucose)
  - With or without CVC (or external jugular line)
  - Urinary catheter, temperature probe, BIS
  - Nasogastric tube (pulled back during anastomosis), used after surgery
- Fluids and blood
  - High bleeding risk: good IV access and blood immediately available
  - Long operations with insensible and 3rd space losses
  - Expected blood loss: Whipple 500-750 ml; total pancreatectomy 750-1000 ml; regional pancreatectomy 750-1500 ml
  - Consider cardiac output monitoring (e.g. FloTrac)
- Temperature: probe, fluid warmer, forced-air and table warmer
- Hypocalcaemia from release of pancreatic lipase
- Octreotide 200 mcg SC before the pancreas is cut, to reduce pancreatic leak

## Postoperative management

- Analgesia
  - PCA
  - Spinal morphine 200-300 mcg
  - Epidural at T6-8 (inferior angle of scapula is about T7)
    - Consider 0.5% bupivacaine during surgery, then 0.125% – 0.25% after
    - If bleeding is a major concern, hold local anaesthetic until near the end
  - Rectus sheath catheters
- Disposition: surgical high dependency as standard; may stay intubated in surgical ICU depending on fluid shifts and cardiorespiratory state
- Complication: pancreatic leak; check drain amylase

## Flashcards

- Q: Three anastomoses in a Whipple reconstruction?
  - A: Pancreaticojejunostomy, hepaticojejunostomy, gastrojejunostomy
- Q: Octreotide dose and timing in pancreatic surgery?
  - A: 200 mcg SC before the pancreas is cut, to reduce leak
- Q: Epidural level for open pancreatic surgery?
  - A: T6-8
- Q: Typical blood loss for a Whipple?
  - A: 500-750 ml
- Q: How is a pancreatic leak detected?
  - A: Drain amylase
