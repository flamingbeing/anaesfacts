# Intraoperative fluid management

Working out intraoperative fluid needs: traditional calculation, restrictive (zero-balance) and goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT).

## Overview
- Calculated approach: deficits + maintenance + ongoing losses + third spacing
- GDFT targets: urine output, lactate, SVV and cardiac output
- Blood loss replacement with crystalloid at 3:1 (see haemorrhage topic)

## Goal: euvolaemia
- Avoid intravascular depletion (hypovolaemia)
  - Optimise organ perfusion (mainly cardiac preload) for adequate tissue oxygenation
    - DO2 = CO x CaO2, where CaO2 = 1.34 x Hb x SaO2 + 0.0031 x PaO2
  - Maintain renal output
- Avoid fluid overload (hypervolaemia)
  - Avoid pulmonary oedema

## Evaluation
- 3 questions
  - 1: Does the patient need fluid?
  - 2: Will the patient respond to it?
  - 3: When to start and when to stop?
- 3 concepts
  - Volume status: total body water (TBW) or blood volume?
  - Volume need: is giving IV fluid clinically relevant?
  - Volume responsiveness: cardiac output rises by more than 15 % after 500 ml

## Phases of therapy

### Initial therapy: replacing preoperative losses
- Fasting deficit from physiological output
  - Maintenance rate (ml/h) multiplied by time since last oral intake
  - But intravascular volume appears to stay normal even after a 10 h overnight fast (Lamke)
- Pathological losses: bowel obstruction, burns
  - Mechanical bowel preparation may cause GI fluid loss and lower preoperative intravascular volume

### Maintenance therapy: intraoperative maintenance and replacement
- General maintenance, 4/2/1 rule (includes urine output)
  - 4 ml/kg/h for the first 10 kg
  - 2 ml/kg/h for the next 10 kg
  - 1 ml/kg/h for each kg after the first 20 kg
- Insensible losses
  - Current view: basal evaporation 0.5 ml/kg/h, rising only to 1 ml/kg/h in major abdominal surgery (Lamke)
  - Long operations, especially with an open abdomen, can lead to bowel oedema and fluid sequestration
- Classic third space
  - Described as a non-functional extracellular volume (nfECV): peritoneal cavity, bowel and injured tissue, normally holding little or no fluid, thought to be lost from extracellular exchange
  - Never measured directly and its location is unclear; it probably does not exist clinically
  - Fluid instead shifts within the functional extracellular compartment, from intravascular to interstitial
  - Classic third space estimates by tissue trauma
    - Minimal: 0–2 ml/kg
    - Moderate: 2–4 ml/kg
    - Severe: 4–8 ml/kg (or more)
- Intraoperative blood loss
  - Gauze, suction bottle, hidden losses under drapes and on the floor
  - Replace with crystalloid (1:1.5), colloid (1:1) or packed red cells

## Liberal (traditional) approach

### Deficit plus maintenance
- Fasting time
- Grades of dehydration: mild less than 5%, moderate 5–10%, severe more than 10%
  - Losses can be greater, especially into the GI tract and third space
- 4-2-1 rule; maintenance typically about 1.5 ml/kg/h

### Blood loss
- 3 ml of crystalloid for every 1 ml of blood lost
- Colloid 1:1
- Consider Plasmalyte or normal saline (no calcium) when given alongside blood
- Classes of shock: less than 15%, 15–30%, 30–40%, more than 40% blood loss

### Third space and insensible losses
- Minimal tissue trauma (e.g. herniorrhaphy): 2–4 ml/kg/h
- Moderate tissue trauma (e.g. cholecystectomy): 4–6 ml/kg/h
- Severe tissue trauma (e.g. bowel resection): 6–8 ml/kg/h
- Fever increases losses

## Restrictive approach
- Insensible losses: 0.5 ml/kg/h, or 1 ml/kg/h with an open abdomen

### Zero-balance approach
- Replace only what is lost during surgery
- Balanced crystalloid at 1 to 3 ml/kg/h to cover sensible and insensible losses
- Extra fluid for blood loss: optimal crystalloid:blood ratio about 1.5:1.0 and colloid:blood 1:1, until the red cell transfusion threshold is reached
- Do not replace non-anatomical "third space" losses: no benefit and possible harm
- No crystalloid preload before neuraxial block or induction
- Treat anaesthetic- or block-related hypotension with vasopressors such as phenylephrine or ephedrine
- In patients who are hypovolaemic, a total volume slightly above zero balance is appropriate

## Goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT)
- Static targets
  - MAP, HR: late indicators
  - CVP, PCWP: poor indicators
  - Urine output more than 0.5 ml/kg/h: affected by stress response and ADH
  - Cardiac output monitoring
    - Pulse contour analysis
    - Pulmonary artery catheter: thermodilution, lithium dilution
    - Oesophageal Doppler
    - TTE
  - Central venous oximetry
- Fluid challenge
- Dynamic: SVV, PPV
- Laboratory markers
  - Haemoconcentration
  - Lactate
  - Central venous oxygen saturation

## Choosing an approach by type of surgery

### Minimally or moderately invasive surgery
- A liberal approach is linked to less PONV and less postoperative pain
- For most adults having short, minimally or moderately invasive surgery with early mobilisation and no major fluid shifts or bleeding: consider 1 to 2 l of balanced crystalloid
  - Usually given during surgery over 30 minutes to two hours

### Major surgery
- Restrictive or goal-directed approaches give fewer perioperative complications (and possibly lower mortality) than liberal or fixed-volume regimens that risk overload
- Restrictive (zero-balance): consider for major surgery with expected blood loss less than 500 ml when there is no invasive dynamic monitoring (arterial line, oesophageal Doppler or TOE)
  - Downside: hypovolaemia may be missed, and the cause of hypotension (bleeding versus anaesthetic effect) can be hard to tell
- Goal-directed: consider for high-risk patients having major surgery, especially if significant blood loss (e.g. more than 500 ml) or fluid shifts are expected
  - Needs one or more invasive dynamic haemodynamic measures

## Paediatric fluids
- Neonates and infants often need maintenance dextrose to prevent hypoglycaemia, as glycogen stores are small
  - E.g. D5% with 0.45% saline, or D5% with 0.9% saline
- Maintenance by Holliday–Segar: 4/2/1 rule
- Resuscitation bolus: 10 ml/kg
- Assessing dehydration
  - Mucous membranes, skin turgor or tenting, capillary refill
  - Grade by percentage weight loss (5%, 10%, 15%)

## Flashcards
- Q: Maintenance fluid by the 4/2/1 rule?
  - A: 4 ml/kg/h for the first 10 kg, 2 ml/kg/h for the next 10 kg, 1 ml/kg/h for each kg above 20 kg
- Q: Basal insensible evaporative loss?
  - A: 0.5 ml/kg/h, rising to only 1 ml/kg/h with an open abdomen
- Q: Definition of fluid responsiveness used here?
  - A: Cardiac output rises by more than 15% after 500 ml
- Q: Fluid for short minimally invasive surgery in adults?
  - A: 1 to 2 l balanced crystalloid over 30 minutes to two hours
- Q: When to consider goal-directed therapy?
  - A: High-risk patients having major surgery with expected blood loss more than 500 ml or major fluid shifts
