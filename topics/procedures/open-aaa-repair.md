# Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm and Open Repair

Highest-risk vascular surgery: manage cross-clamp and unclamp haemodynamics, blood loss and organ protection; in rupture, keep the tamponade until the surgeon is ready.

## Key considerations: elective open repair
- Vascular patients with widespread disease
- Heparinisation
- Cross-clamp: haemodynamic control and reperfusion
- Organ protection, especially renal

## Key considerations: ruptured AAA
- Vascular patients; **emergency**: brief history, arterial line around induction
- **Resuscitate without provoking more bleeding**
  - Target SBP 90mmHg
  - Avoid coughing, straining and large movements
- **Prepare for blood loss**
  - **Surgeons scrubbed and abdomen draped before induction** (tamponade)
  - **Haemostatic resuscitation**: IV access, rapid infuser (Belmont), blood ready for **massive transfusion**
  - **Collapse on induction is likely**: relaxed abdominal muscles release the tamponade; induce only when scrubbed, draped and blood available
  - **Coagulopathy** and the lethal diamond
- Heparinisation
- **Cross-clamp**: hypertension then hypotension; have vasopressors and vasodilators ready; CVC
- Organ protection: renal; abdominal compartment syndrome

## Risk
- Aortic surgery is the highest vascular risk tier
- Mortality: elective 7%, emergency >50%
- Much higher with uncontrolled cardiovascular disease

## Risk stratification
- Cardiac:
  - **CPET is the gold standard** for open and EVAR cases, assessing reserve (VO2 max)
  - Alternatives: stress echo, myocardial perfusion scan (thallium), MUGA
  - Basics: **ECG and TTE**
  - Emergencies may go ahead before dynamic tests are possible
- Respiratory:
  - Baseline SpO2 and **room-air ABG in COPD** or heavy smokers
  - **Lung function tests** to predict weaning from ventilation after surgery

## The aneurysm
- Issues: vascular comorbidity; preventing rupture during surgery
- Risk factors:
  - **Male** predominance 4:1
  - **Smoking** is the biggest risk factor: 90% are current or former smokers
  - Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, family history, inflammatory vasculitis, trauma
  - Rarer: **Marfan syndrome** (fibrillin-1 defect) and vascular (type IV) Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (type III procollagen)
- Location:
  - 80-85% infrarenal
  - 15-20% juxtarenal or suprarenal: more complex, needs fenestrated grafts

## Indications for surgery
- **Symptoms**: **any symptomatic AAA** (back, abdominal or flank pain or tenderness), whatever its size; treat as impending rupture
  - Most are asymptomatic
- **Size**:
  - **Men >5.5 cm**
  - Women >5.0 cm (rupture at smaller diameters; smaller normal aorta)
  - Annual rupture risk at 55mm: 18% in women, 12% in men; rises steeply with size
  - Aneurysm **defined as >30mm**
- **Rapid growth**: **>1.0 cm** in **1 year**
- Shape: saccular aneurysms may be repaired earlier than fusiform ones, being less stable

## Elective open repair
- Aneurysm sac excised and replaced with a synthetic tube or bifurcated (trouser) graft
- Mortality <7%
- Surgical issues: suprarenal versus infrarenal clamp; clamp and unclamp haemodynamics; atheroembolism to the legs; postoperative pain

## Preoperative assessment
- **Vascular comorbidity**: elderly smokers with IHD, valve disease and renal impairment
  - Often smoking-related lung disease
  - **Consider cardiac stress testing**

## Intraoperative management
- GA with ETT and IPPV; supine with arms out
- **With or without epidural** (T6-T11)
  - For: best analgesia; best protection from respiratory complications
  - Against: high failure rate; coagulopathy (haematoma 1:1000); other options exist; unclear what to do after a bloody tap (postpone or proceed)
  - Consider holding local anaesthetic boluses until the aortic repair is done, so unclamping hypotension is easier to treat with intact sympathetic tone
- Monitoring and equipment:
  - 5 lead ECG
  - **Arterial line before induction and CVP**
  - Active warming, but **no forced-air warmer on the legs** (worsens ischaemia while clamped)
  - Urinary catheter for urine output
- Blood loss can be large; mean 1L
  - **Large-bore** access and rapid infusion lines
  - **Rapid infuser (Level 1 or Belmont)**
  - Group and crossmatch with **blood in the theatre fridge**
  - **Cell salvage**
- Haemodynamics:
  - **Vasoconstrictors** ready: phenylephrine, metaraminol, ephedrine, noradrenaline
  - **Vasodilators** ready: GTN, remifentanil, labetalol, SNP
  - Cardiostable induction
- **Organ protection**: keep MAP up for **spinal cord and renal perfusion**; **maintain spinal cord perfusion pressure**

## Phases of surgery
- **Before clamping**:
  - **Reduce preload and afterload**: MAP low-normal; deepen anaesthesia or start **GTN or SNP**
  - **Heparin** 3000-5000units before clamping
- **During clamping** (usually 30-60mins):
  - **Treat sudden hypertension**: deepen volatile or TIVA, beta-blocker, GTN or epidural local anaesthetic
  - **Support the LV**: **restrict fluids** (avoid heart failure); watch ST segments
  - Whole-body **respiratory alkalosis** with **metabolic acidosis in the ischaemic legs**; keeping minute ventilation up leaves an alkalosis that buffers the acid load at release
- **Before unclamping**:
  - **Pre-emptive volume and vasopressors**: fluid load, stop vasodilators, start vasopressors as the clamp comes off
  - **Increase ventilation** for the acid load
  - **ABG**: **correct** anaemia, acidosis and **calcium**
- **After unclamping**:
  - **Slow, coordinated clamp release** with the surgeon
    - **Refractory hypotension: reapply the clamp briefly**
  - **Vasopressor boluses**, e.g. adrenaline 10mcg
  - Lighten anaesthesia: reduce volatile and opioids
  - Correct K+ and pH (**calcium, bicarbonate**)
  - Keep watching end-organ function: urine output, bowel
- **Correct coagulopathy**: ACT, TEG, replace deficits
  - Platelets <100
  - ACT normal <140s
  - Consider protamine
- Emergence: **PCA** often needed; extubate if safe
- Postoperative: ICU

## Emergency repair (rupture or dissection)
- Ruptured sac excised and replaced with a tube or trouser graft
- Overall mortality 75%:
  - 50% die before reaching hospital
  - 25% die in hospital
  - Operative survival 50%
- True anaesthetic and surgical emergency
- Preoperative:
  - Confirm with ultrasound, or CT if stable
  - **Target SBP 90mmHg** (LITFL) or 80-100mmHg (UpToDate)
  - Small IV morphine increments for pain
  - **No coughing, straining or excess movement**
  - As in dissection, reduce dP/dt
- Theatre preparation:
  - As for major trauma
  - **Activate massive transfusion; ROTEM in theatre**
  - **Large-bore IV and rapid infuser (Belmont)**
  - **Arterial line** before induction, or during it if no time; **vasopressors** ready
  - Central access after induction (CVC or PA catheter sheath)
  - **Prep, drape and scrub before induction**: abdominal muscle relaxation removes tamponade and can cause collapse
- Induction:
  - RSI with ETT and IPPV
  - **Cardiostable**, e.g. ketamine and high-dose rocuronium
  - **Surgeon cuts as soon as the tube is confirmed**
  - Keep one IV running with fluid and blood products
- Maintenance: massive haemorrhage management; **avoid the lethal diamond**; **ROTEM**-guided
- **Clamp on and off**: GTN; **slow** release
- Postoperative:
  - ICU
  - **Abdominal compartment syndrome risk**: massive resuscitation causes bowel oedema; if closure needs high pressure, leave the abdomen open with a temporary dressing

## Physiology of aortic cross-clamping
- Used in thoracic and abdominal aortic surgery, sometimes in trauma resuscitation
- Effect size by level: supracoeliac > suprarenal-infracoeliac > infrarenal
- Haemodynamic: hypertension
  - **Higher SVR** from impedance to aortic flow
  - Blood shifts from splanchnic beds to the upper body (**preload rises**: CVP and PCWP)
  - **Arterial pressure rises**: the most dramatic and consistent effect
- Heart:
  - **Afterload** rises
  - **Preload** rises from **passive vessel recoil** and **catecholamine vasoconstriction** below the clamp, pushing blood above it
    - Smaller effect with infrarenal clamps, as the compliant splanchnic veins absorb the volume
  - **Wall stress** and myocardial oxygen use rise
  - Cardiac output falls
- Organs below the clamp:
  - Perfusion pressure and flow fall: **spinal cord ischaemia, AKI, bowel ischaemia**
  - **Sympathetic and renin-angiotensin activation lower renal blood flow** even with infrarenal clamps
  - Anaerobic metabolism leads to **lactic metabolic acidosis**

## Physiology of unclamping
- Haemodynamic: hypotension and shock
  - **Reactive hyperaemia**: distal vasodilation sharply lowers SVR
  - **Capillary leak** from ischaemia-reperfusion: relative hypovolaemia
  - **Myocardial depression**: acidotic, hyperkalaemic blood and myocardial depressant factors washed out of the gut
- Metabolic: **lactic acidosis**, **hyperkalaemia**; transient rise in CO2 output and O2 consumption
- Severity depends on:
  - **Clamp level** (most important)
    - **Supracoeliac**: worst; splanchnic circulation excluded, big volume shift upwards, severe renal and visceral ischaemia
    - **Infracoeliac or suprarenal**: intermediate
    - **Infrarenal**: least; splanchnic bed buffers preload and afterload
  - **Clamp time**: ischaemia and metabolic debt grow with time
  - Patient's **cardiovascular reserve**

## Acute heart failure after clamping
- Clamp raises SVR, so **LV afterload rises**
- Signs:
  - **Hypotension** or cardiogenic shock after clamping
  - Rising airway pressures, pink frothy sputum (**acute pulmonary oedema**)
  - Rising CVP and PAOP
  - New ST-T changes
  - TOE: global hypokinesia, regional wall motion abnormality, MR
  - Cardiac output monitor (**FloTrac**)
- Management:
  - Tell the surgeon; the clamp may need moving distally
  - **Inotrope (adrenaline)** for the LV
    - Add **short-acting vasodilators (GTN, SNP)** to offload the LV
    - Inodilator (**milrinone**, **dobutamine**)
    - Vasopressors with caution
  - **Restrict IV fluids**
  - Optimise **PEEP**
  - Balance supply and demand: **Hb >8-9**, low-normal HR
  - Consider an intra-aortic balloon pump
- Persistent changes: 12 lead ECG, cardiology review, urgent angiography if STEMI (NSTEMI more likely)

## Flashcards
- Q: Size thresholds for elective AAA repair?
  - A: Men >5.5 cm, women >5.0 cm, or growth >1.0 cm in 1 year, or any symptoms
- Q: Target SBP in ruptured AAA before control?
  - A: 90mmHg (80-100mmHg in some sources)
- Q: Heparin dose before clamping?
  - A: 3000-5000units
- Q: Why prep, drape and scrub before inducing a ruptured AAA?
  - A: Muscle relaxation removes abdominal tamponade and can cause collapse
- Q: Which clamp level causes the greatest physiological insult?
  - A: Supracoeliac
