# Decortication of Empyema

Removal of pus and thick organised fibrinous pleura: septic patient, one-lung ventilation via DLT, VATS, possible major blood loss.

## Key points
- Underlying disease: sepsis
- One-lung ventilation with a double-lumen tube; usually VATS
- Blood loss

## Surgery
- Surgical removal of pus and the organised, thick fibrinous pleural membrane
- Causes
  - Parapneumonic effusion: 50%
  - Haemothorax
  - Iatrogenic: chest drains, after surgery, oesophageal perforation
- Stages of pneumonia-related empyema
  - Simple parapneumonic effusion: exudate with normal pH
  - Fibropurulent stage: bacterial invasion, pH falls
  - Complicated parapneumonic effusion: clear fluid but low pH
  - Empyema: frank pus
- Steps
  - Break down adhesions and explore
  - Remove infected tissue
  - Wash with H2O2 and iodine
  - Pleural peel
  - Fully re-expand the lung
  - Obliterate the infected pleural space
  - A rib may be resected
- Timing: no consensus; BTS suggests surgery for sepsis with a pleural collection persisting for 5-7 days
  - Operate earlier if iatrogenic

## Preoperative assessment
- Quantify the degree of sepsis and organ dysfunction
- Look for a bronchopleural fistula (pus draining into the airways)
  - Positive pressure ventilation may cause a tension pneumothorax
  - Soiling of the other lung causes serious morbidity
  - So isolate the lung before positive pressure ventilation

## Intraoperative management
- VATS
- Arterial line usually, unless very fit and well
- Bleeding can be significant: 500-2000 ml
- Often quite painful
- Massive air leak can occur

## Postoperative management
- High dependency if septic
- Complications
  - Persistent air leak
  - Severe lung parenchymal damage, which may need lobectomy

## Flashcards
- Q: What proportion of empyemas follow parapneumonic effusions?
  - A: 50%
- Q: When does BTS suggest surgery for empyema?
  - A: Sepsis with a pleural collection ongoing for 5-7 days
- Q: Why must the lung be isolated before positive pressure ventilation with a bronchopleural fistula?
  - A: Risk of tension pneumothorax and soiling of the healthy lung
- Q: How much blood can be lost in decortication?
  - A: 500-2000 ml
