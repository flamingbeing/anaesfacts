# Aortic Valve Replacement

Most common valve replacement; manage the dominant lesion: stenosis "slow and tight", regurgitation "full, fast and forward".

## Key points
- 1cm
- Stenosis: slow and tight

## Background
- Most common valve replacement
  - Severe AS: 2% of people aged 65-85; 4% over 85
- Valve choice
  - Younger patients: mechanical valve; lasts longer but needs warfarin
  - Older patients: homograft (biological tissue); no anticoagulation but lasts about 15 years
- Surgical steps
  - Excise diseased valve
  - Place sutures in the annulus, then through the sewing ring
  - Seat and secure the prosthesis
  - Close the aorta with a continuous suture
- Mixed stenosis and regurgitation: manage the dominant lesion

## Haemodynamic goals: stenosis
- Slow and tight; keep sinus rhythm and perfusion
- Heart rate: avoid extremes, especially tachycardia
- Sinus rhythm: atrial kick gives about 20-40% of filling of the stiff ventricle
- Preload: maintain to help LV filling
- SVR: maintain with alpha agonists, e.g. noradrenaline
- Maintain myocardial perfusion

## Haemodynamic goals: regurgitation
- Full, fast and forward
- Faster heart rate shortens diastole, so less regurgitation
  - Cardiac output is rate dependent: aim for about 90/min
- Anaesthesia lowers SVR, which reduces regurgitant fraction and favours forward flow

## Intraoperative management
- TOE is essential before and after bypass
  - Confirm prosthesis function and seating
  - Exclude paravalvular leak
  - Assess LV and RV function and filling

## Post-bypass management
- Pacing
  - AV node damage is common; pacing often needed
  - Dual-chamber (DDD) pacing is ideal to keep AV synchrony and atrial kick
- Blood pressure
  - Keep SBP below 120 mmHg to protect the aortic root suture line
- Mechanical support
  - IABP is absolutely contraindicated in native aortic regurgitation (worsens regurgitation)
  - May be used after AVR if the new valve is competent

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the haemodynamic goals for aortic stenosis?
  - A: Slow and tight: avoid tachycardia, keep sinus rhythm, preload and SVR.
- Q: How much LV filling can atrial kick provide in AS?
  - A: About 20-40%.
- Q: What systolic BP limit is advised after AVR, and why?
  - A: Below 120 mmHg, to protect the aortic root suture line.
- Q: Which pacing mode is ideal after AVR?
  - A: Dual-chamber (DDD), to keep AV synchrony.
- Q: Target heart rate for aortic regurgitation?
  - A: About 90/min.
