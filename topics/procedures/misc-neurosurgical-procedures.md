# Miscellaneous Neurosurgical Procedures

Anaesthetic points for stereotactic brain biopsy and extracranial-intracranial (EC-IC) bypass.

## Stereotactic biopsy
- Usually quick: about 1.5hrs of operating time, not counting localisation at the start
- Withhold antibiotics and dexamethasone until the frozen section is back, in case the lesion is an abscess or lymphoma

## EC-IC bypass: indications
- Increases or maintains cerebral blood flow, or allows occlusion of an intracranial aneurysm that cannot be reached surgically
  - Moyamoya disease
  - Intracranial aneurysms that cannot be treated directly and need the proximal artery fully occluded

## EC-IC bypass: surgical technique
- Through a craniotomy
- Donor is usually the superficial temporal artery; other external carotid branches can be used
- Temporalis muscle or omentum are alternatives when an external carotid branch is unsuitable
- Two ways to revascularise:
  - Direct: donor vessel sewn to an intracranial vessel, usually a middle cerebral artery branch
  - Indirect: donor tissue laid on the brain surface without anastomosis; new vessels grow in over time

## EC-IC bypass: anaesthetic management
- Controlled hypertension (MAP 90-110) to keep cerebral perfusion pressure up
- For a direct anastomosis:
  - Some centres use mild hypothermia (33-34 °C) for cerebral protection
  - Induce burst suppression (thiopentone or propofol 1mg/kg) just before the cerebral artery is cross-clamped
- Intraoperative SSEP and EEG monitoring
- Risk of cerebral hyperperfusion after surgery

## Flashcards
- Q: Why withhold antibiotics and dexamethasone during stereotactic brain biopsy?
  - A: Until the frozen section is back, in case the lesion is an abscess or lymphoma
- Q: What MAP target is used during EC-IC bypass?
  - A: Controlled hypertension, MAP 90-110, to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure
- Q: What is done just before cerebral artery cross-clamping in a direct EC-IC bypass?
  - A: Burst suppression with thiopentone or propofol 1mg/kg; some centres also use mild hypothermia (33-34 °C)
- Q: What postoperative complication is specific to EC-IC bypass?
  - A: Cerebral hyperperfusion
