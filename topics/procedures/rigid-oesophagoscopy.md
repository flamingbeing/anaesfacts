# Rigid Oesophagoscopy

Shared-airway procedure with high aspiration risk: needs a cuffed ETT, full muscle relaxation and blunting of a strong sympathetic response.

## Indications

- Assessment
- Foreign body removal
- Oesophageal stricture: dilatation, stenting
- Malignancy: biopsy, debulking
- Achalasia: botulinum toxin injection or pneumatic dilatation

## Considerations

- Airway protection (high aspiration risk)
- Shared airway
- Very stimulating

## Intraoperative management

- Cuffed ETT is mandatory (unlike rigid bronchoscopy)
  - The bulky rigid scope can compress the posterior trachea
  - High risk of regurgitation of oesophageal contents
- Full muscle relaxation: any movement or bucking greatly increases the risk of oesophageal perforation
- Blunt the sympathetic response with short-acting opioids (e.g. remifentanil)

## Postoperative management

- High suspicion for oesophageal perforation: surgical emphysema, chest pain, unexplained tachycardia

## Flashcards

- Q: Airway device for rigid oesophagoscopy?
  - A: Cuffed ETT, mandatory (unlike rigid bronchoscopy)
- Q: Why keep the patient fully paralysed?
  - A: Movement or bucking greatly increases the risk of oesophageal perforation
- Q: Signs of oesophageal perforation afterwards?
  - A: Surgical emphysema, chest pain, unexplained tachycardia
