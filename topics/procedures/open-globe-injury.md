# Open globe and penetrating eye injury

Usually emergency eye surgery: keep intraocular pressure (IOP) down, avoid coughing and suxamethonium, and manage like a neuro case (BP, head up, CO2, PEEP).

## Key principles
- Avoid suxamethonium
- Smooth anaesthetic with no coughing
- Neuro-style care: BP control, head up, CO2 control, limit PEEP
- Usually an emergency: delay risks extrusion of eye contents (vitreous, lens), haemorrhage, infection and endophthalmitis

## Preoperative assessment
- IV sedation if very distressed, to keep control
- Children: premedication
- Full stomach: if the eye cannot be saved, surgery is not urgent and can wait
- Exclude cervical spine injury

## Intraoperative management
- Retrobulbar blocks contraindicated
- Care with the face mask during preoxygenation (no pressure on the eye)
- Rapid sequence induction, modified to:
  - Avoid suxamethonium (raises IOP, effect lasts 15 min); alternatives:
    - Intubate without relaxant using remifentanil
    - Rocuronium
    - Precurarisation
  - Blunt the pressor response to intubation
    - IV lidocaine 1 mg/kg
    - IV esmolol
- Avoid ketamine
- Paralyse to allow microsurgery and lower IOP
- Keep IOP low
  - Control BP
  - Avoid hypercarbia
  - Head up, neck neutral, no ties around the neck
  - Avoid excessive PEEP
- Drugs that lower IOP: acetazolamide, mannitol (also parasympatholytics, sympathomimetics, beta blockers)
- Smooth emergence: remifentanil, lidocaine, LMA exchange, deep extubation
  - Good PONV prophylaxis
  - Good analgesia

## Factors affecting intraocular pressure
- Increase IOP
  - Coughing and straining
  - External pressure on the eye
  - Obstructed venous drainage
  - Suxamethonium, ketamine
- Decrease IOP
  - Etomidate lowers IOP by 50%
  - Other agents that lower BP, e.g. thiopental, propofol

## Postoperative care
- Simple analgesia

## Flashcards
- Q: Why avoid suxamethonium in open globe injury, and for how long does its IOP effect last?
  - A: It raises intraocular pressure; the effect lasts about 15 min.
- Q: Name alternatives to suxamethonium for RSI in open globe injury.
  - A: Rocuronium, remifentanil intubation without relaxant, or precurarisation.
- Q: What IV lidocaine dose blunts the pressor response at intubation?
  - A: 1 mg/kg.
- Q: Which regional technique is contraindicated in open globe injury?
  - A: Retrobulbar block.
- Q: By how much does etomidate lower IOP?
  - A: By 50%.
