# Chest Trauma Surgery

Most chest trauma does not need thoracotomy; key surgical injuries are aortic, diaphragmatic, oesophageal and tracheobronchial.

## General principles
- Under 30% of patients with thoracic trauma need a thoracotomy
- Chest drain output of 1.5 L, or over 200 ml/hr: thoracotomy
- Good venous access essential; exsanguination is unacceptable
- Look for tension pneumothorax
- Massive air leak suggests tracheobronchial injury

## Aortic disruption
- CXR signs:
  - Wide mediastinum
  - Loss of the aortic knob
  - Trachea deviated to the right
  - No gap between aorta and pulmonary artery
  - Depressed left main bronchus
  - Oesophagus deviated to the right
  - Paratracheal stripe
  - Apical cap
  - Left haemothorax
  - 1st or 2nd rib fractures
- CT if stable
- TOE in theatre if unstable

## Ruptured diaphragm
- Varied presentation:
  - Chronic missed injury
  - Intestinal obstruction from herniated bowel
- Check bloods before surgery
- Repair is not an emergency
- Lateral thoracotomy or thoracoabdominal approach
- Manage as for fundoplication
- DLT and one-lung ventilation help surgical access
- NG tube to decompress the stomach

## Ruptured oesophagus
- Presentation: surgical emphysema, pleural effusions
- Causes:
  - Trauma
  - Forceful abdominal straining and vomiting (Boerhaave syndrome)
  - Iatrogenic (endoscopy)
  - Foreign bodies
- Sequelae: mediastinitis, then sepsis, SIRS, shock and multi-organ failure
- Aim of surgery: drain and stop further contamination
- Frail patients: conservative trial with NG suction and chest drain
- Optimise in ICU before surgery
- Anaesthetic plan as for oesophagectomy
- Surgery:
  - Upper oesophagus: right thoracotomy
  - Lower oesophagus: left thoracotomy
  - Primary closure or oesophagectomy
- AF very common
- Early jejunostomy or parenteral feeding
- High risk for many days after surgery

## Tracheobronchial injury
- Most die before reaching hospital
- 100% oxygen and bilateral chest drains
- Adequate positive pressure ventilation may be impossible with a single lumen tube
  - Fibreoptic-guided DLT into the contralateral bronchus
  - Or a single lumen ETT guided past the tear with a bronchoscope, then cuff inflated
- Thoracotomy for repair once the airway is secure
- Carinal disruption needs cardiopulmonary bypass during repair

## Flashcards
- Q: What chest drain output indicates thoracotomy in chest trauma?
  - A: 1.5 L, or over 200 ml/hr
- Q: What proportion of thoracic trauma patients need thoracotomy?
  - A: Under 30%
- Q: Which side is the trachea deviated to in traumatic aortic disruption?
  - A: Right
- Q: How can the airway be secured in tracheobronchial injury?
  - A: Fibreoptic-guided DLT into the contralateral bronchus, or single lumen ETT guided past the tear
- Q: What does carinal disruption need for repair?
  - A: Cardiopulmonary bypass
