# GI Endoscopy

Short, highly variable stimulation in a remote site; OGD and ERCP add a shared airway and aspiration risk.

## Key points
- OGD: shared airway, aspiration risk, remote location
- Common procedures: colonoscopy, upper endoscopy (OGD), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)
- Endoscopists want a patient who does not gag or move
- Haemostatic powders may be used: Hemospray, or Endoclot (weaker)

## Routine anaesthetic for OGD and colonoscopy
- Sedation
  - Preoperative assessment and consent
  - IV access
- Short-acting drugs (likely day surgery)
- Nasal prongs with capnography
- Local anaesthetic spray to the mouth

## Preoperative assessment
- Aspirin can usually continue; stop clopidogrel (dual antiplatelet therapy)

## Intraoperative management
- Sedation or GA
- GA if upper GI bleed with risk of haematemesis and aspiration

### Sedation
- Hypnosis and amnesia: midazolam, propofol, etomidate or ketamine, chosen for the patient
- Opioids (fentanyl, remifentanil) blunt gag, cough and pain during scope insertion and painful steps such as stenting and dilation
  - Remifentanil causes marked respiratory depression and, with propofol, a synergistic fall in blood pressure
- Shared airway: in OGD and ERCP the scope passes through the mouth, so mask ventilation is not possible without stopping the procedure; keep the patient breathing spontaneously

### General anaesthesia
- Intubate; RSI is the usual technique
- Relaxant: suxamethonium, or rocuronium with sugammadex if the case is expected to be short

### Other concerns
- Cases are usually brief (5–30 min) and often day case
- Shared airway with poor access during the procedure
- Position:
  - OGD: lateral
  - ERCP: usually prone
- Stimulation swings widely (e.g. scope insertion), so depth must be adjusted almost instantly; use short-acting agents
- Endoscopist may ask for IV glucagon 1mg or hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan) 20mg

## Postoperative care
- Pain is usually minimal apart from bloating from insufflation

## Flashcards
- Q: Typical duration of GI endoscopy cases?
  - A: 5–30 min, often as outpatients
- Q: Usual position for ERCP?
  - A: Prone
- Q: Which antispasmodic drugs and doses might the endoscopist request?
  - A: IV glucagon 1mg or Buscopan 20mg
- Q: When is GA indicated for GI endoscopy?
  - A: Upper GI bleed with risk of haematemesis and aspiration; RSI is the technique of choice
- Q: What is the concern with remifentanil plus propofol for sedation?
  - A: Profound respiratory depression and synergistic hypotension
