# Gastric POCUS

Gastric ultrasound: transducer choice, scanning technique, landmarks, positioning and Perlas grading.

## Qualitative assessment (Perlas grading)
- Grade 0 (low risk): antrum appears empty, small and collapsed (flat or "target" sign) in both supine and RLD positions

## Transducer choice
- Adults: low-frequency curved array transducer (2–5 MHz) gives adequate depth
- A high-frequency linear transducer may be used for paediatric or very lean patients

## Scanning technique
- Scan in the sagittal or parasagittal plane in the epigastrium

## Anatomical landmarks
- The gastric antrum lies anterior to the pancreas and the abdominal aorta/inferior vena cava
- The gastric antrum lies immediately posterior/inferior to the left lobe of the liver

## Patient positioning
- Always image the patient in both the supine and right lateral decubitus (RLD) positions
- The RLD position shifts gastric contents preferentially into the antrum, maximising diagnostic sensitivity for low-volume fluid or hidden solids
