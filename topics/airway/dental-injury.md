# Dental Injury and Loose Teeth

Commonest source of complaints and claims against anaesthetists; prevent with planning, and save avulsed teeth fast.

## Summary
- Loose teeth: dental review, remove if needed, counsel; suture or protect, use VL, avoid OPA and LMA
- Dental injury: find the tooth, replant immediately or store in saline, get a dental consult

## Incidence
- High medicolegal risk: the most common cause of complaints and claims against anaesthetists
- True incidence unknown; estimated 0.02% to 0.1% of GAs

## Risk factors
- Anaesthetic:
  - Difficult airway
  - Direct laryngoscopy and intubation
    - Mouth-opening "scissors" manoeuvre
    - Double-lumen tubes
  - Biting during emergence
  - Forceful removal of airway devices
- Dental:
  - Restorative dental work
  - Existing dental disease
  - Isolated teeth
  - Mixed dentition (children aged 5-12 years old), when primary and permanent teeth are both present

## Management of dental injury
- Find avulsed or broken teeth
  - Laryngoscopy and retrieval of teeth, prostheses or fragments with Magill's forceps
  - If not found: chest X-ray to look for aspiration into lung or oesophagus
  - Consider bronchoscopy
- Avulsed permanent tooth (serious; prompt care decides tooth survival):
  - Treatment of choice: replant within 5 minutes, pressing the tooth into its socket for several minutes
    - If awake, have the patient bite on gauze to hold it in place
    - May be unsuitable if immunocompromised or with severe periodontal disease (risk of bacterial seeding)
  - If not replanted within 5 minutes, keep it in a storage medium, never dry: e.g. saline or cold fresh milk
    - Survival depends on the periodontal ligament (PDL) cells on the root, which depend on the medium and on "dry time" outside the mouth or a medium
    - Tissue or cell culture transport media are best but rarely in theatre; cold saline or milk (osmolality balanced) are alternatives
- Crown fractures or broken prostheses: put fragments in a storage medium and give them to the patient for urgent dental review
- Haemostasis: gauze over the socket
- Immediate dental consult

## Primary (milk) teeth
- An avulsed primary tooth needs no treatment
- Do not replant it: this can damage the permanent tooth erupting beneath

## Preoperative assessment of loose teeth
- Palpate teeth manually
- Dental consultation
  - Remove very loose primary teeth: high risk of falling out and being aspirated
- Counsel about dislodgement and aspiration
- Document

## Intraoperative management of loose teeth
- Secure mobile teeth with a suture or bridging if indicated, to stop them falling into the airway
  - Use a protective barrier (dental guard or tape) against the laryngoscope blade
- Avoid an OPA: its hard edge can catch and dislodge the tooth on insertion or biting
- Video laryngoscopy: less forceful lifting and less use of the upper teeth as a fulcrum than direct laryngoscopy
  - Intubation may be preferred to an LMA to avoid LMA pressure on the tooth
- Confirm and document teeth intact after intubation and after extubation

## Postoperative management of loose teeth
- Remove the LMA deep

## Flashcards
- Q: Incidence of dental injury under GA?
  - A: About 0.02% to 0.1% of GAs.
- Q: Best management of an avulsed permanent tooth?
  - A: Replant within 5 minutes, pressing it into the socket for several minutes.
- Q: How should an avulsed tooth be stored if it cannot be replanted?
  - A: In a storage medium such as saline or cold fresh milk, never dry.
- Q: Should an avulsed primary tooth be replanted?
  - A: No; it can damage the permanent tooth beneath.
