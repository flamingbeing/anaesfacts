# Patient Movement During Anaesthesia

Movement under GA usually means the patient is too light or not fully paralysed.

## Why it matters
- Gets in the way of surgery
  - Especially in delicate operations
- Can come before hypertension, laryngospasm or other responses to stimulation

## Causes during general anaesthesia
- Inadequate depth of anaesthesia
- Incomplete neuromuscular block

## Management
- Deepen anaesthesia
  - Raise the inhalational agent concentration
  - Or give an IV bolus, e.g. propofol
- Give a muscle relaxant

## Prevention
- Regular atracurium top-ups or an atracurium infusion

## Flashcards
- Q: What are the two usual causes of patient movement under GA?
  - A: Inadequate anaesthesia or incomplete paralysis.
- Q: How can you deepen anaesthesia quickly when a patient moves?
  - A: Increase the volatile concentration or give an IV bolus such as propofol.
- Q: Which airway event can movement herald?
  - A: Laryngospasm (also hypertension).
