# Green Anaesthesia and Sustainability

Biggest anaesthetic wins: drop desflurane and piped N2O, use low flow or TIVA, cut waste and reuse where safe.

## Key ideas
- 5Rs framework
- Global warming potential (GWP)

## Volatile agents
- Halogenated fluorocarbons: potent greenhouse gases with high GWP and long atmospheric lifetimes
  - Desflurane: highest, GWP100 = 2,540; lasts 14 years
  - Isoflurane: moderate, GWP100 = 510
  - Sevoflurane: lowest, GWP100 = 130; lasts 1.1 years
- Nitrous oxide: major warming contributor and the main medical cause of ozone depletion
  - GWP 264; atmospheric lifetime 123 years
  - 90% of the NHS anaesthetic carbon footprint is N2O; two-thirds of that is Entonox in maternity
  - Piped gas leaks (manifolds, terminal outlets) waste large amounts before reaching patients
- Mitigation
  - Minimise desflurane and N2O; prefer sevoflurane or TIVA
  - Low-flow anaesthesia: fresh gas <1 L/min
  - Capture technology is limited: one study recaptured only 25% of desflurane

## Propofol
- Main footprint is packaging (glass, plastic) and disposal: packaging can be over 58% of the total
- Toxic to aquatic life: incinerate leftovers (sharps bin), never pour down the drain
- Persistence, Bioaccumulation and Toxicity (PBT) score 6 out of 9: harm is aquatic, not atmospheric
- Mitigation: recycle packaging; dispose of the drug correctly

## Drug waste
- Over-drawing "just in case" drugs (e.g. suxamethonium, atropine) that are later discarded

## Equipment waste
- Reusable LMAs, gowns and drapes usually have a lower lifecycle footprint than single-use, even with sterilisation
- Up to 40% of anaesthetic waste is recyclable

## Electricity
- Switch off AGSS and forced-air warmers when not in use: meaningful CO2 savings

## 5Rs framework
- Rethink: is GA needed, or can regional or neuraxial be used?
- Reduce: low-flow anaesthesia, <1 L/min (or <0.5 L/min on modern machines), greatly cuts volatile use
- Reuse: validated reusables (laryngoscope handles, some circuit parts) where infection control allows
- Recycle: separate clean non-infectious waste (cardboard, vial caps, paper), up to 40% of theatre waste
- Research: audit your department's carbon footprint

## Barriers to implementation
- Clinical trade-offs
  - Reluctance to stop desflurane in high-risk cases, e.g. morbid obesity
  - No equivalent to Entonox in maternity
  - N2O still needed for paediatric cases
- Infection control
  - Pushback on reusables (LMAs, gowns) over prion (CJD) and surgical site infection, despite evidence of comparable safety
- Workload
  - TIVA takes longer to set up: pumps, lines, processed EEG (BIS, Entropy)
  - Without piped N2O, cylinders must be carefully checked
- Equipment: enough syringe pumps for TIVA
- Education: recyclable versus non-recyclable plastics
- Cost: reusables have high upfront cost

## SSA Green Perioperative Checklist

### Pharmaceuticals
1. Volatiles: reduce or decommission desflurane; low-flow sevoflurane (<1 L/min)
2. N2O: decommission pipelines; use only with clear clinical benefit
3. TIVA: use it; dispose of propofol in clinical waste; do not over-prepare drugs
4. Local or regional: consider it; room air for light sedation (e.g. cataract) where adequate
5. Oxygen: titrate FiO2 to SpO2 >94%; no routine recovery oxygen if N2O was not used
6. Gas flows: low or ultra-low (2–4 L/min during TIVA) to balance cost and absorber life
7. Oral drugs: prefer oral (e.g. paracetamol, etoricoxib): cheaper and lower carbon
8. Evidence-based, value-based care: no blanket antibiotics, antiemetics or PPIs
9. Research: long-acting (e.g. palonosetron) or concentrated (e.g. 2% propofol) drugs to cut emissions
10. Drug management: no routine pre-drawn emergency drugs; use prefilled syringes or split ampoules at list start

### Consumables and equipment
1. Circuits: reuse for up to 7 days with a new HMEF for every case
2. CO2 absorber: change only when exhausted; NaOH-free absorbent and reusable canisters
3. Airway: reusable laryngoscope blades, LMAs, face masks
4. Vascular access: reuse syringes on the same patient; fewer 3-way taps; simple arterial cannulas rather than Seldinger kits
5. Warming: resistive mattresses or blankets over forced air; avoid flannel blankets
6. Gloves only for contact precautions or body fluid risk; infected cases at the end of the list
7. Reusable cloth caps, drapes, scrubs, slide sheets, drug trays
8. Repair damaged equipment; schedule maintenance

### Waste
1. Keep biohazard waste to a minimum; lightly blood-stained items to general waste if policy allows
2. Repurpose sterile wrap as gowns or absorbent pads
3. Work with recycling vendors
4. Donate unused or expired items to under-resourced countries, or use for training or art

### Theatre electricity and water
1. Out-of-hours eco or set-back mode for air exchanges (automated)
2. Switch off AGSS after hours (automated)
3. Ambient temperature >20°C, relative humidity 30–60%, except in specific situations
4. Power-on and power-off checklists for lights, computers, anaesthetic machines
5. Alcohol hand rub instead of water-based surgical scrub: less water and skin irritation

### Intensive care
1. Avoid unnecessary ICU admissions
2. Avoid futile treatment that does not match patient goals or value-based care
3. Bundles that shorten stay; no blanket prophylaxis (antibiotics, PPIs, VTE) in low-risk patients
4. Do not replace clean central lines less than a week old
5. Review drug charts often; switch to oral when possible
6. Natural light rather than artificial light for reorientation
7. Stock fewer items in isolation rooms; keep supply carts outside
8. Bag clean bag-valve-masks and airway devices in plastic for reuse
9. Consider mesh nebulisers instead of metered-dose inhalers
10. Join green dialysis programmes if available

### Obstetrics
1. Consider N2O cracking or destruction units in labour wards
2. Include the environmental impact of analgesia options in patient leaflets
3. Reduce epidural and nerve catheter migration to avoid re-siting

### Pain and regional anaesthesia
1. Do not mandate gowns for single-shot procedures (including spinals) unless teaching or difficulty expected
2. Single-shot peripheral blocks may not need a full sterile pack if asepsis is kept
3. Acute: consider music or hypnosis for blocks and minor procedures
4. Chronic: physiotherapy, CBT, tai chi and home exercise in place of drugs
5. Remote follow-up for spinal cord stimulators and other devices

### Preoperative assessment
1. Referral criteria for who needs the pre-op clinic
2. Telemedicine to save patient travel
3. Antimicrobial wipes instead of couch paper
4. Cut unnecessary or repeated tests; set validity periods (e.g. no routine bloods or ECG for ASA 1/2 cataract surgery)
5. Standard workflows to aid decisions and reduce unwarranted referrals
6. Discuss early when surgery is of doubtful benefit or high risk
7. Structured frailty screening for prehabilitation, to reduce complications and stay

### Catering
1. Reusable cutlery and crockery; no polystyrene
2. Buffet service to reduce waste
3. Compost food waste or use food digesters

### Other efforts
- Collaboration within and between hospitals, with education centres and industry
- Education, clinical improvement, research
- Fewer conferences and less travel
