# Adolescents and Teenagers

Anaesthetic issues in teenagers: anxiety, privacy, IV access, airway, obesity, PONV and consent.

## Anxiety and privacy

- High risk of preoperative anxiety
  - Anxiolytics (midazolam), distraction, involve them in the plan
- Respect privacy; keep dignity during positioning and exposure

## Intravenous access

- Often a major source of distress
- EMLA or Ametop cream, or buffered lidocaine, if time allows

## Airway

- Laryngospasm risk still higher than in adults
- Large tonsils and adenoids may cause obstruction
- May still need smaller tracheal tubes

## Drugs

- Can move to adult dosing

## Obesity

- Rising prevalence
- Screen for undiagnosed obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)

## PONV

- Important risk group, especially girls and high-risk surgery (ENT, strabismus)
- Multimodal prophylaxis: dexamethasone, ondansetron

## Consent and autonomy

- Decision-making capacity is developing
- Consider Gillick competence if parents are absent
- In practice follow local policy on age of consent (usually 21); minors may assent

## Flashcards

- Q: Two common PONV prophylaxis drugs for adolescents?
  - A: Dexamethasone and ondansetron
- Q: Which adolescents are at highest PONV risk?
  - A: Females, and those having ENT or strabismus surgery
- Q: Undiagnosed condition to screen for in obese teenagers?
  - A: Obstructive sleep apnoea
- Q: Options to reduce distress from IV cannulation?
  - A: EMLA or Ametop cream, or buffered lidocaine
