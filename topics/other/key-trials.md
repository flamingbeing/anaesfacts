# Key Anaesthesia and ICU Trials

One-line takeaways of landmark trials by specialty, plus the UK National Audit Projects.

## Sepsis and infection
- EGDT (Rivers) trial: early goal-directed therapy for severe sepsis and septic shock
  - Was the best evidence for EGDT with a strong benefit; led to modified but widespread use of sepsis care bundles
  - ProCESS, ARISE and ProMISe later found no benefit or harm of EGDT over usual care
- EMShockNet, 2010: lactate clearance vs ScvO2 as early sepsis goals
  - Non-inferiority study: using lactate clearance instead of continuous ScvO2 (plus CVP and MAP) did not raise mortality
- CORTICUS, 2008: hydrocortisone in septic shock
  - Shock reversed faster but mortality did not fall in general septic shock
  - HYPRESS and ADRENAL agreed: steroids not for every septic patient
  - Unresolved whether non-responders to fluids and vasopressors benefit
  - ADRENAL: faster weaning from ventilation and shorter ICU stay with steroids
- CLASSIC: in adult ICU septic shock, restricting IV fluids did not reduce deaths at 90 days vs standard fluids
- VASST, 2008: vasopressin vs noradrenaline in septic shock
  - No mortality benefit from adding vasopressin when MAP is kept adequate with standard vasopressors
  - Did not study vasopressor-refractory shock
- PneumA, 2003: 8 vs 15 days of antibiotics for ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP)
  - Same mortality with 8 days as with 15 days, except when gram stain suggested Pseudomonas aeruginosa
- PRORATA, 2010: procalcitonin to guide antibiotics in ICU
  - Stopping antibiotics when procalcitonin <0.25-0.5 mcg/L did not raise mortality and cut antibiotic use by 3 days on average
- 3SITES: subclavian lines had fewer bloodstream infections and DVTs than jugular or femoral, offset by more mechanical complications (including pneumothorax)
- VITAMINS, 2020: vitamin C, thiamine and hydrocortisone does lead to faster septic shock resolution than hydrocortisone alone; debunks a 2017 study suggesting benefit
- Also listed (no summary in notes)
  - Bronchoalveolar lavage vs endotracheal aspirate for diagnosing VAP
  - Combination vs monotherapy for empiric treatment of suspected VAP
  - Nasopharyngeal and oropharyngeal decontamination after cardiac surgery
  - Keystone ICU project: reducing catheter-related bloodstream infections

## Neurology
- INTERACT-2, 2015: systolic target <140 mm Hg within 1 hour lowered death or severe disability vs <180 mm Hg; various oral and IV agents
- ATACH-2, 2018: target 110 to 139 mm Hg did not improve disability or mortality vs standard 140 to 179 mm Hg; mainly IV nicardipine
- CRASH: methylprednisolone for 48 hours after TBI increases mortality
- CRASH-3: TXA is beneficial in TBI
- DECRA: early bifrontotemporoparietal decompressive craniectomy in diffuse TBI lowered ICP but did not improve functional outcome
- RESCUEicp: craniectomy for TBI with refractory intracranial hypertension gave lower mortality but more vegetative state, lower severe disability and upper severe disability than medical care
- STITCH: in traumatic intracerebral haemorrhage, early surgery (evacuation within 12 h of randomisation) reduces mortality
- HACA, 2002: after cardiac arrest from VF or pulseless VT with ROSC, mild hypothermia (32-34° C) improved neurological outcome and mortality at six months
  - TTM (2013) and TTM2 (2021): in comatose adults after out-of-hospital arrest, hypothermia did not improve survival, function or quality of life vs normothermia; more arrhythmias with hypothermia
- Also listed: ICP monitoring in severe TBI; four treatments compared for generalised convulsive status epilepticus

## Sedation and analgesia
- Daily sedation interruption, 2000: daily sedation holidays cut ventilation, ICU and hospital days vs physician-directed holidays
  - ABC trial, 2008: built on the 2000 study by pairing daily sedation holidays with spontaneous breathing trials ("wake up and breathe"); less ventilator time and fewer ICU days
- SEDCOM, 2009: dexmedetomidine gave less ventilator time, less delirium, less tachycardia and hypertension, but more bradycardia
  - SPICE III (2019): dexmedetomidine as main sedative vs usual care had more adverse events and no mortality benefit

## Cardiovascular, resuscitation and shock
- SAFE: no overall difference between 4% albumin and 0.9% saline for resuscitation; avoid 4% albumin in head injury
- SMART: Plasmalyte vs normal saline reduced a composite of death, new renal replacement or persistent renal dysfunction at 30 days
- PLUS: PL-148 vs 0.9% saline did not reduce 90-day mortality or AKI
- PAC-Man, 2005: largest pulmonary artery catheter trial; no clear benefit or harm in critically ill patients
- SOAP II, 2010: dopamine vs noradrenaline in shock
  - The first SOAP study linked dopamine with higher mortality
  - SOAP II: same all-cause mortality but more arrhythmias with dopamine
  - Sepsis guidelines now advise noradrenaline as first-line vasopressor
- SHOCK, 1999: early revascularisation (PCI or CABG) improved survival vs medical stabilisation
  - ACC/AHA MI guidelines now advise emergency revascularisation in cardiogenic shock if younger than 75 years
- IABP-SHOCK II, 2012: IABP did not improve outcome in MI with cardiogenic shock
- Dispatcher-assisted CPR: compression-only did not improve overall survival; trend to benefit in key subgroups
- Also listed: DANAMI-2 (angioplasty vs fibrinolysis in STEMI)

## Respiratory and ARDS
- ARDSnet (ARMA), 2000: ventilate ALI/ARDS with tidal volumes of 6 ml/kg and plateau pressure limited to 30 cm water
- FACTT, 2006: in ALI/ARDS, a conservative fluid strategy (CVP <4 mmHg) improved lung function and cut ventilator and ICU days vs liberal
- ACURASYS, 2010: early neuromuscular blockade significantly lowered mortality in severe ARDS
- PROSEVA, 2013: prone positioning in severe ARDS lowers mortality without more adverse events
- TracMan: no difference between early (around day 4) and late (day 10) tracheostomy
- OSCILLATE, 2013: early high-frequency oscillation in moderate to severe early ARDS may increase hospital mortality
- PReVENT: in ventilated ICU patients without ARDS expected to stay intubated beyond 24 hours of randomisation, low tidal volumes were no better than intermediate for ventilator-free days alive at day 28
- DEXA-ARDS, 2020: dexamethasone in ARDS (77% due to pneumonia or sepsis) sped liberation from ventilation and reduced mortality
  - Established ARDS: P/F ratio <200 at 24 h after onset on PEEP of 10 cm H2O or more and FiO2 of 0.5 or more
- Also listed: CESAR (ECMO vs conventional ventilation); ExPress (PEEP setting in ALI/ARDS); steroids for persistent ARDS; oesophageal pressure-guided ventilation; four weaning methods compared; NIV for COPD exacerbations

## Gastrointestinal and nutrition
- Doig 2013: early parenteral nutrition in patients with a short-term relative contraindication to enteral feeding did not change 60 day mortality or infection vs starting feeding at ~3 days; no need to rush early PN
- POP-UP, 2016, then SUP-ICU, 2018: evidence against routine PPIs in ICU; may still suit patients at very high GI bleeding risk
- Also listed: GI bleeding risk factors in critical illness; EPaNIC (early vs late PN); EDEN (trophic vs full enteral feeding in ALI); early vs on-demand nasoenteric feeding in acute pancreatitis; REDOXS (glutamine and antioxidants)

## Renal
- ATN, 2008: intensive renal replacement did not improve renal or other organ function and caused more hypotension
- BICAR-ICU, 2018: sodium bicarbonate for severe metabolic acidosis (pH <7.2) did not improve mortality at day 28 or organ failure (≥1) at day 7; benefit in the prespecified AKI subgroup
- Also listed: low-dose dopamine in early renal dysfunction; CVVHDF vs intermittent haemodialysis in multi-organ failure

## Haematology
- TRICC, 1999: transfusion threshold of 7-9g/dl is safe in critically ill patients
  - Less blood used than with the older >9g/dl threshold, with fewer costs and complications
  - Caution in active cardiac ischaemia
- TRISS, 2014: septic shock transfused at Hb 7 g/dL vs 9 g/dL had similar 90 days mortality with 50% fewer units
- CRASH-2: TXA safely reduced death in bleeding trauma patients
- Also listed: PROPPR (plasma, platelets and red cells 1:1:1 vs 1:1:2 in severe trauma)

## Endocrine
- NICE-SUGAR: glucose target below 10.0 mmol/L (180 mg/dL) had lower mortality than 4.5 to 6.0 mmol/L (81 to 108 mg/dL); the tighter target is not advised in critically ill adults
  - Overturned the Leuven surgical trial (2001), which favoured tight control

## Musculoskeletal and rehabilitation
- Also listed: daily sedation interruption combined with physical and occupational therapy in ventilated patients

## Obstetrics
- Collaborative Eclampsia Trial (1995): magnesium is the best anticonvulsant in eclampsia
- Magpie: magnesium sulphate in pre-eclampsia cut eclamptic seizures by about 58%; NNT 91

## Perioperative
- Factorial PONV trial, 2004 (six interventions)
  - Ondansetron, dexamethasone and droperidol each cut relative PONV risk by ~25%, with no synergy
  - Propofol maintenance vs volatile: PONV down by 19%
  - Avoiding nitrous oxide: PONV down by 12%
- RELIEF, 2018: in higher-risk major abdominal surgery, restrictive fluids did not improve disability-free survival vs liberal, and caused more AKI
- POISE: perioperative beta-blockers

## National Audit Projects (NAP)
- UK audits of rare but serious anaesthetic complications
- NAP1 and NAP2: supervisory role of consultant anaesthetists; role of morbidity and mortality meetings
- NAP3 (2009): major complications of central neuraxial block
- NAP 4 (2013): major complications of airway management
  - Inhalational induction for a difficult airway performs poorly
- NAP 5 (2014): accidental awareness during general anaesthesia (UK and Ireland)
  - Awareness risk 1:19000
- NAP 6 (2018): perioperative life-threatening allergic reactions
  - Chlorhexidine and patent blue dye ranked 3rd and 4th as causes
- NAP 7 (2023): perioperative cardiac arrest
- NAP 8: complications of regional anaesthesia

## Flashcards
- Q: ARDSnet tidal volume and plateau pressure limits?
  - A: 6 ml/kg; plateau pressure 30 cm water.
- Q: TRICC transfusion threshold?
  - A: 7-9g/dl, instead of the older >9g/dl.
- Q: NICE-SUGAR glucose target?
  - A: Below 10.0 mmol/L (180 mg/dL); the tighter 4.5 to 6.0 mmol/L had higher mortality.
- Q: PONV risk reduction from ondansetron, dexamethasone or droperidol in the 2004 factorial trial?
  - A: About 25% each, no synergy; propofol 19%, avoiding nitrous oxide 12%.
- Q: Effect of magnesium in the Magpie trial?
  - A: About 58% fewer eclamptic seizures; NNT 91.
